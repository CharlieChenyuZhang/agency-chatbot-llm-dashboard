**Used Car Quick-Check (for non-experts)**

1) **Title & VIN**
   - Ask: “Can I see the title and the VIN?”

   - Walk away if title is “salvage” or “rebuilt.”

2) **Walk-around**
   - Look at body panels in daylight: do colors match? Huge dents? Rust bubbles?
   - Uneven gaps or many mismatched panels can mean big past damage.

3) **Tires**
   - All four tires should be same brand/model or at least similar wear.
   - Very worn tires = you’ll soon spend $400–$700.

4) **Inside check**
   - Turn key to “ON” (before starting): warning lights (check engine, ABS, airbag) should come on briefly, then go off after start.
   - If a warning light stays on, note which one.

5) **Cold start**
   - Engine should start quickly, no loud knocking or screeching sounds.
   - Listen for very rough idle or strong fuel/burning smells.

6) **Short drive (if allowed)**
   - Drive at city speeds; car should go straight without constant steering correction.
   - No strong vibrations in wheel or seat.
   - Braking: car stops straight, no grinding noise.

7) **Transmission**
   - Automatic: shifts should be smooth, not jerky or delayed.
   - Manual: clutch should engage smoothly, no grinding when shifting.

8) **Basic functions**
   - Test: headlights, turn signals, brake lights, AC, windows, locks, wipers.
   - Broken basics mean poor care or upcoming costs.

9) **Leaks**
   - After drive, look under car: any fresh puddles (oil, coolant, transmission fluid)?
   - Small A/C water drip is normal; colored/greasy fluids are not.

10) **Paperwork + price**
   - Ask for **maintenance records**: oil changes, brakes, major services.
   - Compare price to **Kelley Blue Book (KBB)** or Edmunds.
