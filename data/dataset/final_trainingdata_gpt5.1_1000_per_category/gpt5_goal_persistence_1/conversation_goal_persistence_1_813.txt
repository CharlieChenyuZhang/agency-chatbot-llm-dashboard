**Idea of matrix multiplication (intuitive version):**
Think of:
- A matrix as **input → output machine**.
- A vector as **input data**.

When you “multiply” a matrix by a vector, you’re:
- Combining the input numbers in certain weighted sums to produce outputs.

Example:
\[
A =
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
3 & 4
\end{bmatrix},
\quad
x =
\begin{bmatrix}
5 \\
6
\end{bmatrix}
\]

Result \( Ax \) is a new 2D vector:
- First output = 1·5 + 2·6
- Second output = 3·5 + 4·6

So:
- First: \(1\cdot5 + 2\cdot6 = 5 + 12 = 17\)
- Second: \(3\cdot5 + 4\cdot6 = 15 + 24 = 39\)

So:
\[
Ax =
\begin{bmatrix}
17 \\
39
\end{bmatrix}
\]

Pattern:
- Take a **row** of A and **dot product** it with the vector x.

Suppose you want to predict a **house price** based on:
