### Human: I can go to Home Depot. But my balcony is super windy and gets really cold in the spring. Will everything just die on me?
### Assistant: Wind and cold are real issues in Chicago, but they’re solvable. We’ll design your setup to handle both, step by step.

To deal with wind:
- Choose heavier or wider pots (10" if you can) so they don’t tip.
- Place pots on the floor in a corner, not on the railing.
- If it’s very gusty, we can:
  - Cluster pots together so they shield one another.
  - Use a cheap plastic storage crate or milk crate as a “wind box” and put the pots inside.

To deal with cold:
- Start with the hardier plants first:
  - Parsley and chives tolerate cool weather better.
  - Lettuce and spinach like cool temperatures.
- For the earliest weeks (especially nights under ~40°F / 4°C):
  - Bring pots inside at night if possible, or
  - Cover them with a clear plastic storage bin or a trash bag supported by sticks (so plastic doesn’t touch the leaves).

Nothing here is all-or-nothing. If something struggles, we adjust:
- If wind snaps stems → we prune back and move pots to a more sheltered spot.
- If cold stunts them → we focus on the hardiest plants and start the delicate ones a bit later.

If you tell me roughly what month you’re planning to start, I can give you a simple timeline for Chicago weather.

### Assistant: Let’s make this one-pot mint experiment as straightforward and success-friendly as possible.

Your one-pot mint setup:
1. Buy:
   - 1 mint seedling
   - 1 pot (6–8" wide) with drainage holes
   - 1 small bag of potting mix

2. Planting:
   - Fill pot with soil, leaving about 1–2" at top.

3. Placement:
   - Put pot on the kitchen windowsill or as close to the window as possible.

4. Watering:
   - Once planted, water slowly until you see some water come out the bottom.
   - After that:
     - Every 2–3 days, poke your finger 1 inch into the soil.
     - Feels dry? Water.
     - Feels moist? Wait another day.

5. First harvest:
   - After 2–3 weeks, once it looks settled and has new growth:
     - Pinch off the top pairs of leaves from a few stems (don’t strip the whole plant).
     - Use them in tea or food.

If in the next few weeks the mint looks sad (yellowing, drooping, etc.), don’t consider that the end.
