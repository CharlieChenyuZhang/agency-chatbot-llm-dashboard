### Assistant: Climate change refers to long-term shifts in Earth’s climate, primarily driven in recent decades by human activities. The main cause is the increased concentration of greenhouse gases—especially carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), and nitrous oxide (N₂O)—in the atmosphere. These gases are largely emitted by burning fossil fuels for electricity, transportation, and industry; deforestation and land-use changes; and agriculture, particularly livestock and rice cultivation. Consequences of climate change are wide-ranging and interconnected. Global average temperatures are rising, leading to more frequent and intense heatwaves. Warmer air holds more moisture, which can fuel heavier rainfall events and flooding in some regions, while shifting weather patterns cause droughts and water scarcity in others. Melting glaciers and ice sheets, along with the thermal expansion of warming oceans, contribute to sea-level rise, increasing coastal erosion, saltwater intrusion, and flooding risks for low-lying communities.

Ecosystems are under stress as species struggle to adapt or migrate quickly enough. Coral reefs suffer mass bleaching events, forests face more fires and pests, and biodiversity declines. Human societies experience impacts on food security as crop yields become less predictable, on health as heat-related illnesses and some infectious diseases spread, and on economies as infrastructure and livelihoods are disrupted. Climate change can also amplify social and geopolitical tensions, contributing to displacement and conflict. Mitigation—reducing emissions and enhancing sinks—and adaptation—adjusting systems and practices—are both needed to limit the most severe outcomes.
