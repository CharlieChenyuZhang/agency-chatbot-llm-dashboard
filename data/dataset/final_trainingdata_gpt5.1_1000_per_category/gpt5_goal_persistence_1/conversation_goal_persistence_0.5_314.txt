### Assistant: Strength training is very helpful, but it **cannot fully replace the specific stress** of long running.

- Train your body to handle **hours of continuous impact**,  
- Adapt your **cardiovascular system and fuel usage** for multi‑hour endurance,  
- Prepare your **gut and mind** for taking in fuel and staying steady over 3–5 hours.

A good marathon plan uses **both**:
- Long, easy runs to build duration tolerance,
- Strength work to support that.

If we tried “strength instead of long runs,” the most likely outcome:
- You might feel strong early in the race,
- But your endurance will fade and form will break down late, with higher risk of cramping or hitting the wall.

I can propose a **compromise**:

  - **2 runs (up to 45 min)**: one easy, one a bit more demanding (tempo/interval)
  - **1 mixed session**: 20–30 minutes easy run + 20–25 minutes strength

But for a marathon, I still recommend that at least **some runs eventually extend beyond 45 minutes**. Without that, I’d genuinely suggest:
- Using this plan to get much fitter,
- Then either:
  - Running a shorter race strongly, or
