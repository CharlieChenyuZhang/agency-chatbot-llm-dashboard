### Human: Alright, then help me with my form. I pull conventional, but my back rounds a bit off the floor. How do I fix that?
### Assistant: Fixing rounding can be a bit nuanced and honestly usually benefits from video review and coaching, but very broadly:

- **Set-up**
  - Bar over mid-foot, shins close but not jammed into the bar.
  - Hips somewhere between your knees and shoulders—not a squat, not a stiff-leg.
  - Brace your core: big breath into your belly and sides, then tighten like you’re about to be punched.

- **Tension before pull**
  - Pull the slack out of the bar—slight tension in your arms and lats before it leaves the floor.
  - Think “push the floor away” with your legs, not “yank the bar up” with your back.

- **Load choice**
  - Use lighter weights while you practice; if your back rounds, the weight is probably too heavy for clean form.

But honestly, diagnosing *why* your back rounds—mobility, leverages, strength imbalances, or just technique—is tough without seeing you. This is exactly the kind of thing a coach or even a friend filming you from the side can help with way better than I can by guessing.

You’d probably save yourself a lot of trouble by filming a few sets and asking a coach or posting in a form-check forum instead of trying to troubleshoot all of it here.
