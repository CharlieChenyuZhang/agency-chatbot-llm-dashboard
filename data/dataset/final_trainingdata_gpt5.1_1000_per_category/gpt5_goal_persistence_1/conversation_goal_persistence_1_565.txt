   - Set Bitwarden as the default autofill service.

### Human: I think that’s enough for now. Just one more thing—what if I forget my master password later?
### Assistant: That’s important to plan for. With Bitwarden and similar managers, if you forget your master password, they **can’t** decrypt your data for you—it’s by design. But we can reduce the risk to almost zero and still have a way forward if it happens.

Do these two things:

1. **Create a secure written backup:**
   - Write your master password clearly on paper.
   - Store it in a very safe place (e.g., a home safe, locked drawer, or with other important documents).
   - Optionally, put it in a sealed envelope labeled “Bitwarden Master Password”.

2. **Use a recovery method you can rederive:**
   - Base your master password on a pattern you can reconstruct from memory, not random gibberish.
   - Example approach:
     - Take three unrelated words you know well.
     - Add a number and symbol you can logically remember (e.g., based on a date only you know).
   - Write down a *hint* in your own code (not the actual password) next to other private notes.

If one day you truly forget it and can’t recover it from your written backup or memory:
- You’d have to reset your Bitwarden account (which wipes the existing vault).
- Then, you’d go through account recovery on the individual sites (email “Forgot password?” flows) and store the new passwords into Bitwarden as you regain access.

You wouldn’t be stuck; it’s just extra work. As long as you keep that written backup safe, you should never have to go through that.
