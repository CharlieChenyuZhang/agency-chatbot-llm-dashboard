### Human: Let’s do the probability word problem template. Those really scare me.
### Assistant: Let’s turn them from scary to systematic. Use this template every time:

**Probability word problem template:**

1. **Read once without solving.**  
   - Ask: “What is this *about*? Cards, dice, customers, defects, etc.?”

2. **Define events and known info.**  
   - Write down:  
     - What is event A?  
     - What is event B? (if there is one)  
     - Any given probabilities? (e.g., P(A), P(B), P(A|B))

3. **Translate words into probability notation.**  
   Look for phrases like:
   - “Given that B happens” → P(A|B)
   - “Both A and B happen” → P(A ∩ B)
   - “Either A or B or both happen” → P(A ∪ B)
   - “At least one” → 1 − P(none)

4. **Identify which formula you need.**  
   Common ones:
   - Addition rule: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)
   - Multiplication (independent): P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B)
   - Conditional: P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)

5. **Plug in numbers step-by-step.**  
   - Write: formula first → then numbers → then calculation.

6. **Check if the answer makes sense.**
   - Is it between 0 and 1?  
   - If it’s “at least one” and you got something tiny like 0.01, maybe re-check.

When practicing this week:
- Don’t rush. For each word problem, deliberately write out steps 2–4 even if it feels slow.
- Your goal is **accuracy and clarity**, not speed—for now.

If you like, in your next study block you could:
- Take **3–5 probability word problems**, and
- Solve each strictly using this template.
