**Step 9: Minimally invasive mitigation strategy**

Goal: Shrink the time that the hot `inventory` row is locked, and handle contention more gracefully.

**Option A: Reorder operations to lock inventory later and for shorter time**

Currently, you likely do something like:

1. Begin transaction.
2. Insert order.
3. Insert order items.
4. Update inventory (locks row).
5. Commit.

If the earlier steps (2–3) take non-trivial time, the lock on inventory is held longer.

**Refinement:**

- Do as much work as possible *before* updating inventory.
- Ensure that between `UPDATE inventory` and `COMMIT`, there’s minimal additional work.

Pseudo-flow:

```sql
BEGIN;

-- Insert order + items first (no inventory lock yet)
INSERT INTO orders ...;
INSERT INTO order_items ...;

-- Just before commit, update inventory
UPDATE inventory
SET quantity = quantity - $1
WHERE product_id = $2
  AND quantity >= $1; -- optional guard

COMMIT;
```

Verify in code that no heavy operations occur after inventory update and before commit (no additional selects/updates, no network calls).

If your DB driver surfaces specific error codes (e.g., serialization failure, deadlock detected, or lock timeout), you can:
