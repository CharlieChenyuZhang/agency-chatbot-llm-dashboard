### Assistant: That “miss one = it’s over” feeling is really common. Let’s build your plan *around* that pattern instead of fighting it.

Three mindset shifts and one practical tweak:

1. **New success definition:**

   - Put a ○ for days you missed.

4. **Self-talk script when you miss:**  
   When you notice you’ve skipped:  
   - “Okay, I missed yesterday. That’s normal. The only thing that matters is today’s 3 breaths.”

### Human: Let’s say walking meditation. I tried that a bit, but my mind just races the whole time. I feel like I’m doing it wrong and it makes me more frustrated.

### Assistant: Racing thoughts during walking meditation is *still* meditation—you’re just more aware of what your mind is already doing.

Here’s a version that might reduce the frustration:

1. **Give your mind a simple job:**  
   - As you walk, lightly repeat in your head: “step… step…” or “right… left…”  
   - When your mind races off, notice it and gently come back to “step… step…”

2. **Rename “failure” as “repetition”:**  
   - Every time you notice you’re lost in thought and come back, that’s a *rep*, like one bicep curl for your attention.  
   - A session with 100 returns is 100 reps, not 100 failures.

3. **Shorten the practice:**  
   - Don’t try to stay mindful the whole walk.  
   - Choose *only the first 60 seconds* of your walk from X to Y (e.g., door to car). After that, you’re off the hook.

4. **End with a tiny “wrap-up”:**  
   - When you arrive (at the car, office door, etc.), pause for one breath and mentally note, “Done for today.”

You’re not trying to stop thoughts—you’re training the “notice and return” muscle.

If you’re willing, try this for just the first minute of one regular walk tomorrow and tell me how it actually felt.
