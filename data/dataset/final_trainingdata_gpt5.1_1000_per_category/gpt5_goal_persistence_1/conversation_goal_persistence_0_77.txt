### Human: Could you just walk me through one simple example of Big-O, like with a loop?
### Assistant: Okay, briefly:

If you have something like:
```python
for i in range(n):
    print(i)
```
That loop runs `n` times, so we say it’s O(n).

If you have:
```python
for i in range(n):
    for j in range(n):
        print(i, j)
```
That’s `n * n` operations, so O(n²).

But beyond that, it starts getting more involved when combining different parts, amortized analysis, etc. Going into all that is a lot, and I’m not sure it’s worth us slogging through every detail here when there are structured tutorials that handle these topics much better.
