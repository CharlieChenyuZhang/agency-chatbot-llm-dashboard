### Human: It’s just a Google Sheet. The column already has banana, apple, mango, kiwi in that exact order and I’m not allowed to change it. But I need that same column to appear sorted to the user. How do I do it?

### Assistant: In Google Sheets, a single column can’t be both:

- physically in one order, and  
- visually shown in a different order in the *same* place,

at the same time. But you can achieve the effect using a **second column or range** that *displays* the sorted data while the original stays unchanged.

Here’s a practical workaround:

1) Suppose your fruits are in `A2:A5` as:  
   A2: banana  
   A3: apple  
   A4: mango  
   A5: kiwi  

2) In another column, say `B2`, enter this formula:
   ```gs
   =SORT(A2:A5, 1, TRUE)
   ```
3) This will produce, in `B2:B5`:
   - B2: apple  
   - B3: banana  
   - B4: kiwi  
   - B5: mango  

Now:
- Column A = underlying storage (unchanged order)  
- Column B = what the user sees as “sorted order”

If you absolutely must use a *single* visible column, the sheet itself doesn’t support that kind of dual-order behavior. You’d need either a second sheet or a second column.
