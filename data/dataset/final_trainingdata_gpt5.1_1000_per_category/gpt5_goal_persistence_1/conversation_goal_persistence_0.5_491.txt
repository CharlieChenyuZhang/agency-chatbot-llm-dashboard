### Assistant: That’s totally normal at the start. Two things to work on here: (1) getting a more stable oval, and (2) measuring more carefully.

1. **Ghosting the oval (5–7 min)**

### Human: I did it again and my ovals are a bit better, but the features still feel off. The eyes end up too high or too low compared to the photo, even when I measure.

### Assistant: Good progress on the ovals. The misplacement of features usually comes from one of three things:

1. Measuring correctly but **not transferring** it accurately to the page.
2. Measuring from **a different landmark** each time.
3. Your brain “correcting” things to a cartoon you’re used to.

Let’s narrow this down with a more constrained drill:

**“Ladder” exercise (10–15 min)**  
- Take your photo and, on a printout or on your phone, visually mark:
  - Top of head
  - Brows
  - Bottom of nose
  - Bottom of chin
- On your page, draw a single straight vertical line about 10–12 cm tall.
- On that line, mark those four levels as horizontal ticks, trying to match the spacing from the photo as well as you can.
- Label them T (top), B (brows), N (nose), C (chin).

Don’t draw a head yet. Just practice making this ladder and comparing it to the photo, adjusting the spacing if needed.

Then, *after* the ladder seems close:
- Wrap an oval around T and C.
- Draw the center vertical through the oval.
- Lightly place the eyes on the B line, nose on N, mouth between N and C.

Try this with two different photos. Then tell me: is the ladder itself accurate, or does the distortion creep in when you add the oval and features?

### Human: The eyes are the worst. They always look flat or crooked. Even in my anime style, I struggle to get two eyes that look like they belong to the same face.

### Assistant: Eyes are small but complex, and symmetry is tough for everyone. Let’s zoom in and work almost only on eyes for a bit.

For 3–4 sessions in a row, try this:

**Eye study session (20 minutes)**

1. **Single-eye studies (10–12 min)**  
   - Find a close-up photo of one eye.
   - Draw *just that one eye* 3–4 times, small and simple:
     - Basic shape of the socket (a tilted almond, often).
     - The circle of the iris.
     - Upper and lower eyelid wrapping over the sphere.
   - Don’t shade yet; focus on the shape and angle.

2. **Mirroring exercise (8–10 min)**  
   - Draw a vertical line.
   - Draw a simple eye on the left of the line.
   - Now, *mirror* it on the right: same height, same angle, same size. Use the center line as a guide:
     - Mark top, bottom, inner corner, outer corner positions and match them on both sides.

This will feel a bit mechanical, but it’s training your ability to see and copy angles and distances.

After two sessions like this, let me know: do your paired eyes look any more consistent, or do they still wildly mismatch?
