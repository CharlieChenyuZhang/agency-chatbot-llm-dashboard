### Human: I usually open with status updates. I ask, “Any blockers?” and then a couple of people talk a lot, the rest stay quiet. When it’s silent, I just move on quickly because it feels awkward.

### Assistant: That’s a very common pattern, and we can absolutely work with it.

   - “What’s one thing that could slow you down this week?”
   - “Is there anything you’re unsure about that we could clarify together?”

2) Give people an easier prompt. Instead of “Anything on your mind?” try: “What’s one thing you need from me or the team this week?” Many people find it easier to answer a concrete question.
3) Follow up 1:1 with the quieter person. You can say: “I noticed I put you on the spot in the meeting—sorry if that felt uncomfortable. I really value your perspective. Is there a better way for me to make space for your ideas?”

If the 1:1 feels hard, I can help you draft the exact words. Want me to help you script that conversation?

### Human: Okay. I’m not quitting yet. Can you give me a simple 30-day plan to keep improving as a leader without burning out?

### Assistant: Yes. Here’s a compact 30-day plan that fits alongside your regular work. Think “small consistent moves,” not “huge overhaul.”

Week 1: Listen and observe
- Do 20-minute 1:1s with each team member (if you haven’t already).
- Ask everyone the same three questions:
  1) What’s going well for you?
  2) What’s frustrating?
  3) What’s one thing I could do to support you better?
- At the end of each day, take 5 minutes to write: one thing you did as a leader that you’re glad about, one thing you’d handle differently next time.

Week 2: Improve communication
- In each team meeting, clearly state:
  1) What we’re deciding.
  2) What input I need and by when.
  3) What we’ve decided and why.
- Practice pausing 5 seconds after asking questions so people can think.
- Once this week, send a short summary after a meeting: decisions, next steps, owners, deadlines.

Week 3: Decisions and expectations
- For every medium/large decision, write down your reasoning and share a brief version with the team.
- Clarify expectations with each person in a 1:1: “Over the next 3 months, success for you looks like… Does that feel clear and realistic to you?”
- Ask them what they need from you to hit those expectations.

Week 4: Reflect and adjust
- Ask the team for quick feedback: “On a scale of 1–10, how supported do you feel by me as your lead? What’s one thing that would move your number up by 1?”
- Review your notes from the month. Write down:
  1) 3 leadership wins you’re proud of.
  2) 2 patterns you want to keep improving.
  3) 1 concrete focus for the next month.

Throughout all 30 days:
- Keep your daily 5-minute reflection.
- Don’t aim for perfect completion; aim for “mostly done and learning.”

If you’d like, we can check in at the end of each “week” (even hypothetically) and I’ll help you adjust the next steps based on how it really went.
