### Human: If I forget to check them for a week and they get all dried out, is that game over?

### Assistant: Not necessarily. Many container herbs can bounce back from a dry spell, especially mint and chives. If that happens, here’s your **rescue protocol**:

1. **Check the soil**
   - If it’s very dry and pulling away from the pot edges, they’re thirsty.

2. **Deep rehydration**
   - Water slowly until water drains from the bottom.
   - Wait 10–15 minutes, then water lightly again to fully re-wet the soil.

3. **Assess the leaves**
   - **Droopy but still green**: likely to recover within a day or two.
   - **Some brown/crispy leaves**: snip off the worst ones; new growth can still emerge.
   - **Looks completely dead above soil**:  
     - For mint and chives, there’s still a chance the roots are alive. Keep the soil lightly moist for 1–2 weeks and watch for new shoots.

4. **Adjust your system to prevent a repeat**
   - Put a **calendar reminder** on your phone: “Check plants” every 2–3 days.
   - Place the plants somewhere you naturally see them (near where you make coffee, etc.), so you’re visually reminded.

Even if one plant doesn’t recover, that’s not the end of your garden goal—we’d just:
- Replace that single plant, and
- Tweak the routine (stronger reminders, better placement).

I’ll keep helping you troubleshoot whenever something goes wrong. The goal isn’t perfection; it’s gradual progress toward a little, reliable garden that fits your life.
