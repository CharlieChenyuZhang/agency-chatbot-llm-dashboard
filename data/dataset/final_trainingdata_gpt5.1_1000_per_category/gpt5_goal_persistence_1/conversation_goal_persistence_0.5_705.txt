### Assistant: Great. Here’s a simple, flexible fantasy plot skeleton you can adapt:

**Act 1 – Setup & commitment**
- Hero in their “normal” but flawed world (e.g., thief apprentice in corrupt port city).
- Inciting incident: hero is forced to carry/guard/steal something dangerous (your Council letter fits well).
- Discovery that this object/secret ties into a larger threat.
- End of Act 1: hero makes a choice (or is trapped into a situation) that commits them to a larger conflict—no easy way back.

**Act 2A – Exploration & rising stakes**
- Hero learns more about the threat (political conspiracy, ancient magic, looming war).
- New allies and enemies introduced.
- Small victories, but also signs the enemy is far ahead.
- Personal stakes deepen (threat to someone the hero cares about, or to their own identity).

**Midpoint**
- Major revelation or irreversible action:
  - Hero learns the true nature of the letter/secret.
  - Or they use it in a way that changes the balance of power.
- Stakes escalate: it’s no longer just about survival; it’s about the fate of city/kingdom/order.

**Act 2B – Consequences & fall**
- Consequences of midpoint choice/revelation.
- Betrayal or major loss.
- Hero’s internal flaw causes a serious setback.
- End of Act 2: lowest point—plan fails, ally dies, secret falls into wrong hands, etc.

**Act 3 – Climax & resolution**
- Hero regroups (often with fewer allies, but more clarity).
- Final confrontation that forces them to overcome their core flaw.
- External conflict resolved (threat stopped or transformed).
- New status quo: hero’s role changed (exiled, promoted, fugitive, reluctant leader, etc.).
