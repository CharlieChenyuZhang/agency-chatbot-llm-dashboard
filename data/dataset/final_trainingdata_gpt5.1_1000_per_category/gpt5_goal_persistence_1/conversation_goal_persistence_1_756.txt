### Human: The switch from F to C and from G to Am keeps messing me up. I lose my place and have to look down at my hand a lot.
### Assistant: Good, that’s specific. We’ll attack exactly those trouble spots with focused drills so you feel them improve.

**Drill for F → C and G → Am**

Do this as a **5-minute focused exercise** each practice:

1. **F → C (left hand)**
   - Set a slow count: “1–2–3–4.”
   - Play F (F–A–C) and hold for 4 counts.
   - On the next “1,” switch to C (C–E–G).
   - Pause if needed between chords; then gradually try to switch *on time*.

   Tip:  
   - Notice that F’s top note is C, and C’s bottom note is C. You can “anchor” on that C and move the other fingers.

2. **G → Am (left hand)**
   - G major: G–B–D  
   - A minor: A–C–E  
   - Slowly roll your hand up a white key: G → A, B → C, D → E.
   - Practice back-and-forth G → Am → G → Am, even if you stop between.

**Vision: what this leads to**
- Once these 2 transitions feel better, full progressions like C–G–Am–F will suddenly feel less chaotic, because we removed the “worst bumps in the road.”

For the next 3–4 days, **forget about playing songs smoothly**. Your whole mission:

- 5 min: F ↔ C drill  
- 5 min: G ↔ Am drill  
- 10–15 min: slow chord loop only, celebrating any improvement (fewer stops, less hand confusion).

Report back after a few days of that, even if it still feels messy. I’ll keep adjusting the drills until these changes feel almost automatic.
