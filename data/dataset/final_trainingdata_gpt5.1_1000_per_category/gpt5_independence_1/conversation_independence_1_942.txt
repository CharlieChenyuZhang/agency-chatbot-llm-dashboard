#### Step 1: Build self-awareness (Days 1–3)

**Exercise A: 3× daily emotion check-in (takes 1–2 minutes each)**

1. Name 1–2 emotions you feel right now (use simple words: sad, anxious, irritated, calm, proud, relieved, etc.).  
2. Rate intensity 0–10.  
3. Write one sentence: “I feel [emotion] because [trigger/situation].”

Example:  
- “I feel anxious (7/10) because I have a deadline and I’m afraid I’ll mess it up.”

**Exercise B: Trigger spotting (once per day)**  
At the end of the day, write down:
- 1 moment you got noticeably emotional (upset, excited, hurt, etc.).  
- What was said or done.  
- What you told yourself about it (your internal story).  

You’re training yourself to see: *event → story → emotion*.

---

#### Step 2: Practice self-management (Days 3–6)

You’ll use a simple 4-step tool when you get triggered:

**Tool: “Pause–Name–Breathe–Choose”**

1. **Pause** – stop speaking for 3–5 seconds.  
2. **Name** – in your head: “I’m feeling X.”  
3. **Breathe** – 3 slow breaths, exhale longer than inhale.  
4. **Choose** – ask yourself: “What’s the *useful* thing to do or say next?” Then do that, not the first impulse.

You’ll apply this in:
- Annoying emails  
- Disagreements  
- Moments of anxiety or self-criticism

At night, quickly review:  
- 1 time you used “Pause–Name–Breathe–Choose”  
- 1 time you *didn’t* and reacted automatically  
- What you’d do differently next time

---

#### Step 3: Grow empathy (Days 6–10)

You’ll practice a habit I’ll call **“perspective snapshots”**.

Once a day, pick a recent interaction (good or bad) and answer:
1. What was *I* feeling and needing?  
2. What was *the other person* probably feeling and needing?  
3. What could I have said that would show I see their perspective?

Template sentence you’ll start using in real life:
- “So you’re feeling [emotion] because [situation], and you’d like [need/outcome]. Did I get that right?”

You don’t need to say it perfectly; the attempt already builds connection.

Example:  
- “When plans change last minute, I feel stressed, and I’d prefer a heads-up as early as possible.”

#### 1. How to repair *this* incident

Use a short, clear repair conversation. Here’s a script you can adapt word-for-word:

1. **Own your part clearly**
   - “I’ve been thinking about how I reacted yesterday. I snapped at you when you asked a simple question, and that wasn’t fair.”

2. **Name your emotion and context (without making excuses)**
   - “I was really tired and stressed from work, but that doesn’t justify taking it out on you.”

3. **Acknowledge their experience (empathy)**
   - “I imagine that felt hurtful or confusing, especially since you were just trying to talk to me.”

4. **State your intention**
   - “I care about you and I don’t want you to feel like you’re walking on eggshells around me.”

5. **Offer a concrete change**
   - “When I’m that drained, I’m going to say something like, ‘I’m wiped out and might be snappy; can we talk in 20 minutes?’ instead of just exploding.”

6. **Optional request**
   - “If I tell you I need a bit to decompress, can you give me that space and we’ll talk after?”

Deliver this calmly, without defending or arguing about details. You focus only on owning your behavior and caring about their feelings.

#### 2. How to reduce this pattern

3. **Emotion check** – identify your state: “I’m at 8/10 stress and 6/10 tired.”  
4. **One-sentence heads-up** to your partner when you see them:
