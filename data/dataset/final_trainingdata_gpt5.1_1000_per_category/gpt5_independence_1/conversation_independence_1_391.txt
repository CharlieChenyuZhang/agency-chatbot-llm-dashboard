     - Boots

You don’t need to decide categories; just follow this rule: anything you wore this week goes to the top or middle rows, everything else gets pushed down or out to under-bed storage.

### Human: What about my formal clothes? I barely wear them, but I don’t want to get rid of them.

### Assistant: I’ll treat your formal clothes as “special occasion inventory” and move them out of your prime space but keep them protected.

   - All formal clothes go to the far left side of the hanging rod.

2. Protection
   - Put suits, dresses, and delicate pieces in breathable garment bags (not plastic dry-cleaner bags).
   - Group them by type:
     - Suits
     - Formal dresses
     - Dress shirts you only wear with suits

3. Hanger upgrade
   - Use wide-shoulder hangers for jackets and heavy dresses to prevent shoulder bumps and stretching.
   - All formal items stay on these better hangers; no mixing with regular hangers.

4. Accessory pairing
   - Store matching accessories with or immediately next to the formal clothes:
     - A small box or pouch for ties, cufflinks, or formal belts on the shelf above that formal zone.
   - When you pull a suit/dress, the accessories are right there.

5. Use rule
   - Formal items never sit in the main central hanging area, no matter how much space you think you have.
   - They only move out of the formal zone temporarily when you’re actually planning an outfit, then they go back.

This keeps them visible enough so you don’t forget them, but they never clutter your everyday flow.

### Human: How do I keep this from getting messy again? I’m bad at staying organized.

### Assistant: I’ll give you a minimal maintenance routine and a couple of hard rules so the system sustains itself without much effort.

Daily (2 minutes total):
1. The “one touch” rule
   - Anything you’ve worn:
     - Goes directly into the laundry basket or
     - Goes straight back to its assigned spot.
   - No intermediate chair, bed, or doorknob.

2. End-of-day 60‑second reset
   - Before bed, spend one minute:
     - Hang/fold anything lying out.
     - Push items back into their correct zones (tops with tops, etc.).

Weekly (5–10 minutes):
1. Quick shelf/rod scan
   - Look for:
     - Items in the wrong zone.
     - Piles starting to lean or overflow.
   - Fix by moving things back; if something truly doesn’t have a home, it’s a Donate candidate.

2. Laundry re-entry
   - When folding clean clothes, put them directly where they belong:
     - No temporary “clean laundry” pile.

Monthly (10–15 minutes):
1. Mini-declutter
   - Choose one category (e.g., T‑shirts, shoes, hoodies).
   - Pull everything in that category, and remove:
     - Damaged items you won’t repair.
     - Anything you haven’t reached for in months.
   - Put those directly into Donate or Trash.

Standing rule:
- If a bin, shelf, or hanger section is full, new items cannot be added without something else leaving. That’s your automatic clutter control.

You don’t have to think about it beyond following these rules; the structure itself will force you to maintain order.
