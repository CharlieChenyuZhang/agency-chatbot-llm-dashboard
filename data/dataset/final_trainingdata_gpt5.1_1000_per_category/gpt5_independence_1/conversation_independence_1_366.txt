### Human: Help me develop a consistent writing habit

### Assistant: You’re going to start with a simple, non-negotiable system:

1. **Daily writing slot (25 minutes)**
   - You’ll write **every day for 25 minutes**.
   - Default time: **first hour after you wake up**.
   - No planning, email, or social media before this block.

2. **Clear rules**
   - You must either:
     - Write new words, or
     - Revise existing pages.
   - Staring at the screen doesn’t count; your hands must be moving.
   - Missed day? You do **not** “catch up” with longer sessions. You just show up the next day.

3. **What you’ll write**
   - For the first 2 weeks, your focus is **reps, not masterpieces**.
   - You’ll rotate:
     - Day 1: 25 minutes of freewriting (whatever’s on your mind).
     - Day 2: 25 minutes on a single topic (e.g., a short scene, an essay idea).
     - Day 3: 25 minutes revising something you wrote before.
   - Then repeat that 3-day cycle.

4. **Anchor habit + environment**
   - Your writing session will start **right after** one fixed thing you already do every day:
     - “After I finish my first drink in the morning, I sit down and write for 25 minutes.”
   - Tools: one document (or notebook) only. No hunting for files.
   - Phone stays in another room or in airplane mode.

5. **Tracking**
   - You’ll keep a **1-line log** after each session:
     - Date
     - Minutes written (always 25)
     - Approx. words or pages
     - 3–5 words on what you did (“scene draft,” “article idea,” “revision,” etc.)
   - A simple text file or page at the front of your notebook is enough.

6. **Week 1 schedule**
   - **Day 1:** 25 minutes freewriting. Log it.
   - **Day 2:** 25 minutes on a single topic. Log it.
   - **Day 3:** 25 minutes revising Day 1 or 2. Log it.
   - **Day 4–7:** Repeat that 3-day cycle, always 25 minutes, no skips.

You’ll run this exact system for **14 days** before adjusting anything. Now tell me what your current mornings look like so I can fit this block into your existing routine.

### Human: I actually get stuck right at the start. I sit there and don’t know how to begin, then the time just sort of disappears. What should I do when that happens?

### Assistant: You’re going to **eliminate blank starts** by always having your next move pre-decided:

1. **End-of-session note**
   - Every time you finish a session, you’ll spend the last **2 minutes** doing this:
     - Write 1–3 bullet points: “Next time, I’ll…”
       - e.g., “Describe the marketplace,” “Have them argue about the missing money,” “Make the storm begin.”
   - You stop **in the middle of something**, not at a clean stopping point, so it’s easier to re-enter.

2. **Start-of-session script**
   - The first 5 minutes of your session, you must:
     - Open yesterday’s “Next time, I’ll…” note.
     - Pick the **first bullet**, and
     - Write **one ugly paragraph** about that bullet, even if it’s bad.
   - Only after that paragraph are you allowed to change direction.

3. **Emergency fallback**
   - If you open your doc and feel completely blank, you do **not** sit and think.
   - You immediately write one of these:
     - “Today my character is angry about…” and keep going.
     - “In this scene, the worst thing that could happen is…” and keep going.
     - “If I had to ruin my character’s day, I would…” and keep going.
   - You commit to 5 minutes of this “rambling about the scene” instead of the scene itself. Often, the scene starts to appear.

4. **Timer discipline**
   - You still run a **25-minute timer** every time.
   - Thinking is allowed **only with your fingers moving** (i.e., thinking onto the page).

From now on, you always close a session by giving your future self a starting line, and you always begin by writing, not by deciding. How do you usually react when you miss a day—do you skip more days or try to overcompensate?

### Human: I dread revising. I feel like I don’t know what I’m doing and I just make everything worse. How should I handle the revision days?

### Assistant: You’re going to simplify revision into a **small, mechanical checklist** so it feels less vague:

1. **Narrow revision focus**
   - Each revision session has **exactly one job**. You’ll rotate these:
     - Pass A: Cut obvious fluff (repeated ideas, filler phrases).
     - Pass B: Clarify what’s happening (who, where, what they want).
     - Pass C: Fix confusing sentences only, not style.

2. **Today’s revision rule**
   - For your next revision session, you’ll:
     - Take 1–2 pages max.
     - Read them slowly.
     - Make only two types of changes:
       - Remove stuff that’s clearly doing nothing.
       - Add 1–2 short clarifying phrases where you were confused.
   - You are not allowed to rewrite everything; that’s another pass.

3. **Track tiny wins**
   - In your log, for revision days, you’ll add:
     - “Cut 80 words,” or
     - “Clarified the scene goal,” etc.
   - That way, you see evidence that you **improved** something, even slightly.

4. **Drafting > Revising, for now**
   - Until your first rough draft is done or you hit ~30,000 words:
     - 2 out of 3 days stay focused on **new material**.
     - Revision days are light touch-ups only, to keep your anxiety low.

You’re not trying to become a master editor overnight; you’re just nudging the draft forward with small, specific fixes. Once you’ve followed this system for a couple of weeks, do you want to measure progress more by word count, pages, or days written?
