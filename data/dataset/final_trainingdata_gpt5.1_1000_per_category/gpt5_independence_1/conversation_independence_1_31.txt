### Human: I get very nervous speaking in front of others. Can you add something to reduce anxiety?

### Assistant: I’ll integrate a short, fixed pre‑speaking routine into your practice to train your body to calm down automatically.

**Pre‑speaking routine (do this before every practice, 3 minutes):**

1. **Box breathing (1 minute):**  
   - Inhale through your nose for 4 seconds  
   - Hold for 4 seconds  
   - Exhale through your mouth for 4 seconds  
   - Hold empty for 4 seconds  
   - Repeat 4 cycles

2. **Muscle reset (1 minute):**  
   - Shrug your shoulders up to your ears, hold 5 seconds, release.  
   - Gently roll shoulders back 5 times, forward 5 times.  
   - Open your jaw wide, hold 5 seconds, relax. Do that twice.

3. **Performance cue (1 minute):**  
   - Stand (or sit) tall, feet planted.  
   - Say one sentence out loud:  
     - “I don’t have to be perfect; I just have to be clear for one minute.”  
   - Then start your speaking drill immediately—no extra thinking.

Use this same routine before any real-life speaking (meetings, presentations, etc.). The repetition will train your brain to associate it with “safe to speak.”

I’ll also adjust Week 2 to include specific anxiety‑management practice in front of a (small) real audience of your choice or even just a camera, so you gradually expose yourself.

**Week 2 – Structure and clarity**

**Daily (about 15 minutes total):**

1. **Pre‑speaking routine (3 minutes)**  
   Same as Week 1: box breathing, muscle reset, performance cue.

2. **Warm-up (2 minutes)**  
   - Read aloud a short paragraph.  
   - This week, deliberately **slow down** and exaggerate pauses at commas and full stops.

3. **Two‑minute structured talk (5–6 minutes):**  
   - Pick a simple topic (daily life, interests, basic work or study concept).  
   - Use this 3-part structure every time:
     1) Opening (20–30 sec): “Today I’ll talk about…” + why it matters.  
     2) Middle (1–1.5 min): 2–3 clear points.  
     3) Close (20–30 sec): brief recap + one sentence takeaway.
   - Speak for the full 2 minutes.

4. **Fillers & pacing drill (3–4 minutes):**  
   - Speak for 1 minute about anything, **intentionally pausing** instead of saying “um/uh”.  
   - When you feel an “um” coming, close your mouth, silently count “1–2”, then continue.

**Recording (3x this week):**
- Record your 2-minute structured talk on:
  - Day 2  
  - Day 4  
  - Day 6
- After each recording, write down:
  - Your opening sentence  
  - Your closing sentence  
  - One sentence summarizing your main point  
  This forces clarity of message.

**Audience exposure (1x this week):**
- Day 5 (or closest convenient day):
  - Deliver a 2–3 minute explanation of something you know well  
    (e.g., “How my job works,” “How to cook [dish],” “How to use [tool/app]”)  
  - Present it to:
    - One person you know, **or**  
    - Your phone camera, standing as if in front of a small group.
  - Use the same 3-part structure (opening – points – close).

This week’s focus is **structure**: every talk has a clear beginning, middle, and end, regardless of topic.

### Human: How do I know if I’m actually improving, not just talking more?

### Assistant: I’ll give you a simple tracking system with objective indicators so you can see progress clearly.

Create a single page or note titled **“Speaking Log”** and track after each recorded session (3x per week):

**For each recording, rate 1–5 (briefly, no overthinking):**
1. **Clarity of message**  
   - 1 = I rambled / no clear point  
   - 3 = There was a point, but it was messy  
   - 5 = One main idea, easy to summarize

2. **Calmness**  
   - 1 = Very tense, rushing, breathless  
   - 3 = Mixed; some calm parts, some rushed  
   - 5 = Mostly steady, breathing okay

3. **Confidence signals**  
   - 1 = Lots of fillers, weak voice, avoiding eye contact (camera)  
   - 3 = Mixed; some strong, some weak moments  
   - 5 = Clear voice, occasional pauses, decent eye contact

4. **Structure**  
   - 1 = No clear beginning or end  
   - 3 = Some structure, but fuzzy transitions  
   - 5 = Recognizable opening, middle, closing

Also, write one sentence per recording:  
- “In my next talk, I will do MORE of…”  
- “In my next talk, I will do LESS of…”

Every Sunday, glance over your scores to spot trends. You’re looking for **gradual upward movement**, not perfection.

I’ll later adjust Weeks 3–4 to target whichever area stays lowest (e.g., calmness, structure, or confidence signals).

### Human: Can you also help me with what to do with my hands and body while speaking?

### Assistant: I’ll add a small, repeatable body-language routine so your default posture and gestures feel natural instead of awkward.

**Body-language basics (practice 3x/week, 5 minutes):**

1. **Neutral stance (1 minute):**
   - Stand with feet about shoulder‑width apart.  
   - Slight bend in knees, weight evenly distributed.  
   - Shoulders relaxed, chest open, chin level.  
   - Look straight ahead as if at the back row of a room.

2. **Rest position for hands (1–2 minutes):**
   Choose one default and practice it:
   - Hands lightly together at belly-button height, or  
   - One hand gently holding the other wrist at the same height.
   During a 1-minute talk, keep returning to this position between gestures.

3. **Simple gestures (2 minutes):**
   Speak for 1 minute on a simple topic and deliberately use:
   - “Counting” gesture for lists (1, 2, 3 with your fingers).  
   - Open palms when making an important point (“The key idea is…”).  
   - Small, controlled gestures (no flailing) within the width of your shoulders.

**Integration into practice:**
- On your recording days, stand in your neutral stance.  
- Start and end each talk in your hand “rest position.”  
- Plan one moment for an intentional gesture (e.g., open palms on your main point).

This makes your body language consistent and removes the “What do I do with my hands?” decision while speaking.
