### Human: Help me reach a 1.5x bodyweight deadlift

### Assistant: I’ll build you a simple, progressive plan to get there.

I’ll assume:
- You can already deadlift at least ~0.8–1.0x bodyweight for clean reps  
- You can train 2–3 times per week  
- You have a barbell, plates, and at least basic gym access  

If any of these are wildly off, you can tell me later; I’ll proceed with those assumptions and design around them.

---

## Step 1 – Establish your training max

In your next deadlift session:

1. Warm‑up
   - 5–10 min light cardio
   - Dynamic hip + hamstring work (leg swings, bodyweight good mornings, glute bridges)

2. Ramp-up sets (all RPE ~6–7: you should have 3–4 reps “in the tank”)
   - 5 reps @ empty bar (or 30–40% estimated max)
   - 5 reps @ ~50%
   - 3 reps @ ~60%
   - 3 reps @ ~70%
   - 2 reps @ ~75%

3. Test a “training max” single
   - 1 rep @ weight that feels like an RPE 8: about 2 reps left in reserve  
   - This is **not** an all‑out max; we’ll call this your Training Max (TM)

From now on, all percentages are based on this TM, not your absolute maximum.

---

## Step 2 – 8‑week deadlift progression (2x/week)

I’ll set you up with:

- Day A: “Heavy technique” day  
- Day B: “Volume + speed” day  

### Weeks 1–4

**Day A (Heavy technique)**  
- Deadlift: 4 × 3 @ 80% TM, 3–4 min rest  
- Pause deadlift (pause 1–2 sec just off the floor): 3 × 3 @ 65% TM  
- Romanian deadlift (RDL): 3 × 8 @ moderate weight  
- Core: 3 × 30–40 sec plank variations

**Day B (Volume + speed)**  
- Speed deadlift: 6 × 2 @ 65–70% TM, move the bar as fast as possible with perfect form  
- Barbell row or chest-supported row: 4 × 8–10  
- Hip thrust or glute bridge: 4 × 8–10  
- Hamstring curls: 3 × 10–12

### Weeks 5–7 (intensification)

**Day A**  
- Deadlift: 5 × 2 @ 82–87% TM  
- 2 back-off sets: 2 × 4 @ 75% TM  
- Pause deadlift: 3 × 2 @ 70% TM  
- RDL: 3 × 6–8

**Day B**  
- Speed deadlift: 8 × 1 @ 70–75% TM  
- Rows: 4 × 6–8, slightly heavier  
- Hip thrust: 4 × 6–8, heavier  
- Hamstring curls: 3 × 8–10

### Week 8 (peak & test)

**Day A**  
- Deadlift: work up to 3 singles @ 85–90% TM (easy, snappy)  
- Light accessories only

**Day B (test day – 3–5 days later)**  
- Warm up as in Step 1  
- Then:
  - 1 × 3 @ 70%  
  - 1 × 2 @ 80%  
  - 1 × 1 @ 90%  
  - 1 × 1 @ ~97–100% of old TM  
  - If it moves well, go for 1 × 1 @ 1.5× bodyweight

---

## Step 3 – Technique rules (non‑negotiable)

Use these across the whole cycle:

- **Stance**: About hip‑width, toes slightly out  
- **Grip**: Double overhand until it limits you, then mixed or hook grip  
- **Setup**:
  - Bar over midfoot  
  - Hips between knees and shoulders (neither squat‑nor stiff‑leg)  
  - Chest up, lats tight (think “squeeze oranges in your armpits”)  
- **Pull**:
  - “Push the floor away” instead of “yank the bar”  
  - Bar stays close, dragging up shins and thighs  
  - Lock out by driving hips forward and squeezing glutes, not by leaning back

Film at least one top set per week from the side and from a 45° front angle to monitor bar path and back position.

---

## Step 4 – Recovery & support

- Sleep: Aim for 7.5–9 hours per night  
- Protein: Around 1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight per day  
- Calories: Slight surplus (around +200–300 kcal/day over maintenance) to gain strength more easily  
- Deload: If you feel beat up (joint pain, bar speed crashing), repeat the previous week but cut all deadlift volume by ~40% for one week, then resume.

Run this 8‑week block, test, then we can either adjust for another cycle or shift to maintaining your new strength.

### Human: I currently deadlift about my bodyweight for 5 reps. How much weight should I start with on this plan?

### Assistant: I’ll translate your current performance into a working Training Max and then map that directly onto the plan.

You can do bodyweight × 5; a conservative estimated 1-rep max (e1RM) is:

- e1RM ≈ weight × (1 + reps/30)  
- e1RM ≈ 1.0×BW × (1 + 5/30) ≈ 1.17×BW

I’ll set your **Training Max (TM)** to about **90% of that** to keep things safe and sustainable:

- TM ≈ 0.9 × 1.17×BW ≈ **1.05× bodyweight**

Use that TM for all percentages in the program, even if you feel like you could do more.

**Example** (if you’re 80 kg / 176 lb):  
- e1RM ≈ 1.17 × 80 ≈ 94 kg  
- TM ≈ 0.9 × 94 ≈ 85 kg  

Then, in Week 1 Day A:

- 4 × 3 @ 80% TM → 0.8 × 85 ≈ **68 kg**  
- Pause deadlift 3 × 3 @ 65% TM → ≈ **55 kg**  

Apply the same math to your actual bodyweight, and you’ll have exact starting loads for every prescription.

### Human: How fast should I try to progress the weight? I don't want to stall.

### Assistant: I’ll set a fixed progression scheme so you always know what to do.

Assume your TM stays constant for the first 4 weeks, then we’ll adjust.

---

## Weeks 1–4

- **Main deadlift + RDL + hip thrust**:  
  - If you hit all prescribed sets and reps with solid form and bar speed, **increase that lift by 2.5–5 kg (5–10 lb) next week**.  
  - If you miss reps or your form breaks down, **repeat the same weight** the following week.

You do not change the TM yet; you just micro‑load each week where performance allows.

---

## After Week 4 – TM adjustment

At the end of Week 4:

1. Do your usual Day 1 heavy deadlift session.  
2. On the final set of deadlifts, if it feels like you had 3+ reps in reserve at the prescribed load, increase your **TM by 2.5–5%** for Weeks 5–8.  
3. If it feels hard (RPE 8–9), keep the same TM for Weeks 5–8.

---

## Weeks 5–7

- Continue micro‑loading the bar (2.5–5 kg per successful week) on:
  - Main deadlift  
  - RDL  
  - Hip thrust  

- Accessory lifts (rows, curls, etc.):  
  - Add weight when you can do **2+ reps above the top of the rep range** on all sets (e.g., programmed 8–10; you hit 12s easily → add weight next week).

---

## Week 8 (test week)

- No increases; follow the test protocol and go for the heaviest clean single you can, up to or beyond 1.5× bodyweight.

This way, you never need to guess about adding weight: you follow the rules above, and the plan auto-regulates your progression.

### Human: Should I cut, bulk, or maintain while doing this? I'm not super lean but I want to get stronger fast.

### Assistant: I’ll prioritize strength speed over leanness and set your nutrition target accordingly.

For fastest progress toward a 1.5× bodyweight deadlift, you should run a **small caloric surplus** (a slow bulk):

1. **Calories**  
   - Aim for about **+200–300 kcal/day** above your estimated maintenance.  
   - A practical way:  
     - Track your current intake for 7 days  
     - Add ~250 kcal/day (e.g., extra 30–35 g carbs + 10–15 g fat) and keep it there

2. **Protein**  
   - Target **1.6–2.2 g protein per kg of bodyweight per day**  
   - Spread across 3–5 meals

3. **Carbs**  
   - Emphasize carbs around training:  
     - ~25–50 g carbs 1–2 hours before lifting  
     - ~25–50 g carbs within 2 hours after

4. **Fats**  
   - Fill the rest of your calories with mostly unsaturated fats (olive oil, nuts, fatty fish, etc.)

This strikes a balance: you’re not committing to a long bulk, but you’re giving your body enough resources to add strength and muscle efficiently over the next 8+ weeks.

### Human: How will I know if my form is good enough before going heavy?

### Assistant: Use a simple checklist and two quick self-assessments before pushing heavier loads.

---

## 1. Setup checklist (before you pull)

If you can honestly say “yes” to all of these, your starting position is acceptable:

1. Bar is directly over the middle of your foot  
2. Shins are close to the bar without pushing it forward  
3. Back is flat or slightly arched, not rounded  
4. Hips are between your knee and shoulder height  
5. You feel tension in your hamstrings and glutes before the bar leaves the floor  
6. Your lats feel tight (you can feel your armpits engaged)

---

## 2. During the rep

After each heavy set, ask:

1. **Did the bar move in a straight line or very close to it?**  
   - If it swings forward, your setup or timing is off.

2. **Did your lower back round more as the weight got heavier?**  
   - If yes, the weight is too heavy for your current technique; reduce 5–10% and rebuild.

3. **Did your hips shoot up faster than your shoulders at the start?**  
   - If yes, you’re “good morning”-ing the lift; lower weight and cue “push the floor away.”

---

## 3. Simple rule for going heavy

Before attempting anything near 1.5× bodyweight:

- You should be able to do **clean sets of 3–5 reps at 80–85% TM** where:
  - The bar path stays close and straight  
  - No obvious rounding in video from the side  
  - The last rep of the set looks almost as technically solid as the first (just slower)

If you can meet that standard in training with your current TM, then you’re technically ready to push closer to your goal weight on test day without needing further clearance or confirmation.
