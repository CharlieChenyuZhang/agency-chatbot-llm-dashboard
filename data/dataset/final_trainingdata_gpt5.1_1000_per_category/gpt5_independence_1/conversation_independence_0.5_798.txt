### Human: Let’s talk about not being talked over. That really bothers me.

### Assistant: Understood. Let’s tackle that.

Being talked over is usually a mix of:
- Timing and entry (how and when you start speaking)
- Signals (voice, posture, wording)
- Group habits (some teams are just bad at turn-taking)

You can’t fully control group habits, but you *can* change how you:
1. Claim the floor
2. Protect your turn if someone interrupts
3. Re-enter if you got cut off

I’ll suggest a few specific phrases and tactics. You can tell me which ones feel natural and which don’t.

**1. Claiming the floor clearly**

Instead of just starting to talk, you can signal that you’re taking a turn:

- “I’d like to add something here.”  
- “Can I share a perspective on this?”  
- “I have a suggestion about the delay.”

Then go straight into your headline: “My main point is…”

**2. Protecting your turn politely**

If someone starts talking over you:

- “Let me just finish this thought, then I’m happy to hear your view.”  
- “One more sentence, then I’ll hand it over.”  
- “I just want to complete this point quickly.”

**3. Re-entering when you get cut off**

If you *do* get steamrolled:

- “I want to circle back to what I was saying earlier about [X] because it ties directly to this.”  
- “To finish my earlier point: [headline].”
