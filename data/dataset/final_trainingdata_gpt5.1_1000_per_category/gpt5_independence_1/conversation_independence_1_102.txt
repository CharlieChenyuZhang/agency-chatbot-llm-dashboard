## Museum Quick Reference

**Miller Institute for Contemporary Art (Miller ICA)**  
Non-collecting contemporary art institute on CMU’s campus, featuring experimental exhibitions, artist projects, and public programs that often highlight emerging and mid-career artists. Great for seeing what’s current and conceptually driven.

**Carnegie Museum of Art**  
One of the first museums of contemporary art in the United States, with strong holdings in painting, sculpture, photography, and design. Combines historic works with ambitious temporary exhibitions in a large, traditional museum setting.

**The Frick Art Museum (The Frick Pittsburgh)**  
An intimate museum on the historic Frick estate, focusing on European paintings, works on paper, and decorative arts. Offers a quieter, more contemplative experience compared to the larger Carnegie.

**Contemporary Craft**  
A space dedicated to contemporary craft, including ceramics, fiber, glass, metal, wood, and mixed-media works. Exhibitions emphasize material innovation, social themes, and hands-on engagement with craft disciplines.

**August Wilson African American Cultural Center**  
A multidisciplinary arts center celebrating African American and African diasporic culture through visual art, performance, and community programs. Gallery exhibitions often foreground Black artists and narratives in a downtown setting.

**The Andy Warhol Museum**  
The largest museum in North America dedicated to a single artist, showcasing Andy Warhol’s work across painting, sculpture, film, photography, and archives. Offers a deep dive into Pop Art, celebrity culture, and Warhol’s influence on contemporary art.

**Mattress Factory Museum of Contemporary Art**  
A museum known for immersive, site-specific installations housed in repurposed buildings in the Mexican War Streets neighborhood. Ideal if you enjoy experiential contemporary art that transforms whole rooms and environments.

**Silver Eye Center for Photography**  
A nonprofit contemporary photography space presenting exhibitions by emerging and established photographers. Focuses on innovative photographic practices and often includes artist talks and public programs.
