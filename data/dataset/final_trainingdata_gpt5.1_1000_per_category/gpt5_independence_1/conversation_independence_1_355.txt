**Overall goal (10 days)**

**Daily structure (template)**

   - Quick review of yesterday’s hardest material.

2. **Focused learning block 1 (40 min) + 5‑min break**

3. **Focused learning block 2 (40 min) + 5‑min break**  
   - Learn new material (Topic B or deeper on Topic A).  
   - Turn what you just learned into flashcards right away.

4. **Spaced-repetition review block (40 min)**  
   - Review flashcards using spaced repetition.  
   - Priority order:  
     1. Due reviews (older cards scheduled for today)  
     2. Yesterday’s new cards  
     3. Today’s new cards (quick pass)

5. **Reflection & planning (10 min)**  
   - Write:  
     - 3 things you solidified  
     - 2 things still fuzzy  
     - 1 thing you’ll hit first tomorrow

**Spaced repetition schedule for new cards**

Whenever you create a new card on Day *D*:

- First review: same day (end of session)  
- Second review: Day D+1  
- Third review: Day D+3  
- Fourth review: Day D+7  
- Fifth review: Day D+14 (post‑sprint maintenance)

If you’re using an SRS app (like Anki), set new cards per day to something manageable (e.g., 20–40) and let the app handle exact intervals; just commit to doing *all due reviews* daily.

---

**10‑day sprint blueprint**

- **Days 1–2: Setup + Foundation**
  - Create your flashcard system (app deck or physical cards box).
  - Identify 3 priority topics.
  - Add 20–30 cards per day.
  - Start review habit (at least 15–20 min of review).

- **Days 3–5: Expansion**
  - Add 30–40 new cards per day.
  - Keep review to max 30–40 min; if reviews explode, reduce new cards.
  - Start mixing topics in reviews to practice recall switching.

- **Days 6–8: Consolidation**
  - Shift emphasis from new cards to reviews.
  - New cards: 15–25 per day.
  - Use active recall mini-tests at the end of each day (no notes).

- **Days 9–10: Exam simulation & cleanup**
  - Reduce new cards sharply (0–10 per day).
  - Do 1–2 longer mixed review blocks (simulate exam conditions).
  - Tag or mark “weak” cards and see them twice these days.

---

**How to write effective spaced-repetition cards**

Use **clear, testable prompts**:

- Bad: “Photosynthesis”  
- Good: “What are the reactants and products of the light‑dependent reactions of photosynthesis?”

Use these patterns:

- **Definition:**  
  - Q: “What is [term]?”  
  - A: short, precise definition.
- **Concept -> example:**  
  - Q: “Give an example of [concept] and explain why it fits.”
- **Process steps:**  
  - Q: “List the 4 main steps of [process] in order.”
- **Cause → effect / If → then:**  
  - Q: “If [condition], what happens to [variable] and why?”

Aim for **1 idea per card**. If you feel like you’re cramming, split it.

---

**Daily minimums (non‑negotiables in the sprint)**

- Do **all due reviews** every day (even if you skip new material).
- Do at least **one 25–40 min focused block** (no multitasking).
- Capture **new cards the same day** you learn something important.

You now have:

- A fixed sprint length (10 days)
- A repeating daily structure
- A simple spaced-repetition schedule and card style
- Rules for balancing new learning vs review

### Human: Can you write 10 high-quality flashcards for me on cell respiration following the format you described?

### Assistant: I’ll create 10 clear, testable cards on cellular respiration, each with one main idea.

1. **Overall equation**  
   - Q: What is the overall balanced chemical equation for aerobic cellular respiration (reactants and products)?  
   - A: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + ATP (energy).

2. **Purpose of cellular respiration**  
   - Q: What is the main purpose of cellular respiration in cells?  
   - A: To break down glucose (and other fuels) to produce ATP, which provides usable energy for cellular processes.

3. **Location of stages**  
   - Q: Where in the cell do glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and the electron transport chain occur?  
   - A: Glycolysis: cytoplasm; Krebs cycle: mitochondrial matrix; ETC: inner mitochondrial membrane (cristae).

4. **Inputs/outputs of glycolysis**  
   - Q: What are the main inputs and outputs of glycolysis per molecule of glucose?  
   - A: Inputs: 1 glucose, 2 ATP, NAD⁺; Outputs: 2 pyruvate, net 2 ATP, 2 NADH.

5. **Role of NADH and FADH₂**  
   - Q: What roles do NADH and FADH₂ play in cellular respiration?  
   - A: They act as electron carriers, transporting high-energy electrons from glycolysis and the Krebs cycle to the electron transport chain.

6. **Krebs cycle purpose**  
   - Q: What is the main purpose of the Krebs cycle (citric acid cycle)?  
   - A: To fully oxidize acetyl-CoA to CO₂ and produce high-energy electron carriers (NADH, FADH₂) and a small amount of ATP.

7. **Role of oxygen**  
   - Q: What is the role of oxygen in the electron transport chain?  
   - A: Oxygen is the final electron acceptor; it combines with electrons and protons (H⁺) to form water, allowing the chain to keep running.

8. **How ATP is produced in ETC**  
   - Q: How does the electron transport chain generate ATP?  
   - A: Electrons flow through ETC proteins, pumping H⁺ across the membrane to create a gradient; H⁺ flows back through ATP synthase, which uses that energy to make ATP (chemiosmosis/oxidative phosphorylation).

9. **Aerobic vs anaerobic respiration**  
   - Q: How does aerobic respiration differ from anaerobic respiration (fermentation) in terms of ATP yield?  
   - A: Aerobic respiration produces much more ATP (~30–32 ATP per glucose), while anaerobic respiration (fermentation) produces only 2 ATP per glucose (from glycolysis alone).

10. **Fate of pyruvate with and without oxygen**  
    - Q: What happens to pyruvate when oxygen is present vs absent?  
    - A: With O₂: pyruvate enters mitochondria, converted to acetyl-CoA and enters Krebs cycle. Without O₂: pyruvate undergoes fermentation (e.g., lactic acid or alcoholic fermentation) to regenerate NAD⁺ for glycolysis.

You can drop these directly into your flashcard system and then expand with more detailed or example-based cards as needed.
