**7‑day PM interview prep loop**

Each day: ~45–60 minutes.

**Day 1 – “Tell me about yourself” + career change story**

Work from this skeleton:

> “I started my career in [field] where I worked on [type of work]. Over time, I found myself drawn to problems like [how to improve a product/process, interpreting data, etc.].  
>  
> In my last role at [Company], I [brief high‑level impact]. The part I enjoyed most was [product‑like work]. That led me to learn more about product management—[short list of what you did: reading, courses, mini projects].  
>  
> Now I’m looking to bring my experience with [customers/data/cross‑team collaboration] into an Associate Product Manager role, and I’m particularly interested in [Company/role] because [short reason].”

Practice saying this out loud until it flows naturally in 60–90 seconds.

**Day 2 – Product sense / design questions**

Common prompts:

- “How would you improve our product?”  
- “Design a product for commuters / remote workers / new parents.”  
- “What’s your favourite product and how would you improve it?”

Use this structure:

1. Clarify the **user**: who they are.  
2. Clarify the **problem**: what they’re struggling with.  
3. Propose **2–3 ideas**, not 10.  
4. For each idea, mention a **metric** (e.g., activation, retention, conversion, NPS).  

Prepare one detailed example for a product you actually use in London (e.g., Monzo, Revolut, Deliveroo, Citymapper).

**Day 3 – Execution / prioritisation**

Typical questions:

- “You have 10 feature requests and only capacity for 2; how do you choose?”  
- “What would you do if engineering said they can’t deliver a planned feature?”

Use a simple response:

> “I’d start by understanding the impact of each option:  
> – Who is the user?  
> – How many people are affected?  
> – What’s the business impact (revenue, cost, retention)?  
>  
> I’d then use a lightweight framework such as impact vs. effort and make trade‑offs explicit with the team and stakeholders. For example, if Feature A impacts a core user journey and is low effort, it probably takes priority over a nice‑to‑have for a small set of users. I’d turn that into a recommendation, share the reasoning, and adjust based on feedback.”

Prepare one concrete story from your past role where you prioritised tasks or projects.

**Day 4 – Behavioural questions (STAR stories)**

Prepare at least 3 stories in STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for:

- Handling a difficult stakeholder or colleague.  
- Improving a process or fixing a broken workflow.  
- Making a decision with imperfect information.

Example skeleton:

> “In my role at [Company], we faced [Situation]. My responsibility was [Task]. I did [Action: 3–4 specific steps]. As a result, [Result: metric or qualitative outcome].”

**Day 5 – Analytical / metrics**

Expect questions like:

- “How would you measure success for [feature]?”  
- “What metrics would you look at to see if [product] is doing well?”

Use:

> “I’d define a primary metric that represents user value, for example [retention, engagement, conversion], and a few guardrail metrics like [churn, complaints, operational load]. For a new feature, I’d look at adoption (how many people use it), repeated usage, and any impact on our core funnel.”

Prepare one mini example: pick any feature and define:

- Primary metric (e.g., weekly active users of the feature).  
- Supporting metrics (e.g., completion rate, time to complete).

**Day 6 – Company‑specific prep**

- Read the company’s site and product pages.

  - Who their users are.  
  - What problem they solve.  
  - 1–2 ideas to improve their product and how you’d measure success.

**5. “How would you prioritise features if you only have capacity to ship one this quarter?”**

> – What problem does it solve?

>
