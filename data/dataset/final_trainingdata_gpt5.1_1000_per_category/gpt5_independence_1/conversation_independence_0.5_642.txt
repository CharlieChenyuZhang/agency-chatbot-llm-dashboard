On getting stuck, there are a few patterns and corresponding tactics. I’ll propose a small set; you can tell me which ones match your experience, and we’ll refine.

Common “stuck” patterns and what to do:

1. **“I don’t know where to start”**  
   - Tactic: *Constrain the first step absurdly small.*  
     - e.g., “First, I’ll restate the problem in one sentence and list two hypotheses.”  
     - Or, “I’ll only try to reproduce the bug for 15 minutes.”

2. **“Too many possible directions” (analysis paralysis)**  
   - Tactic: *Prioritize by impact × effort.*  
     - Rank 3–5 options by:
       - How likely they are to be right
       - How fast they are to test  
     - Commit to testing the top one for a fixed time (e.g., 20 minutes), then reassess.

3. **“I keep looping on the same ideas”**  
   - Tactic: *Change the representation.*  
     - Draw a simple diagram or flow, or explain the problem out loud (or in writing) as if to a teammate.  
     - Often this surfaces assumptions you can test.

4. **“Mental fatigue / frustration”**  
   - Tactic: *Short, deliberate break with a rule.*  
     - Eg: “10-minute break, then I come back and must either:
       - state a new hypothesis,
       - or explicitly ask for help with what I’ve already tried.”
