In finance, compounding is obvious:

- You invest.  
- The investment earns.  
- Then the earnings start earning.

In careers, it’s less visible—but even more powerful.

Career compounding is when:

- Each project makes you more valuable for the next one.  
- Each skill you build increases the value of every other skill.  
- Each relationship expands what’s possible for you.

Most people never reach this state.

Not because they’re not trying.

Because their effort is scattered across things that *don’t* compound.

---

### The trap: linear effort, random direction

Here’s what most driven people do (I did this too):

- Say yes to every “good opportunity.”  
- Collect general experience without a clear direction.  
- Hope that if they just keep working hard, “it’ll all work out.”

The result?

You get linear returns at best.

Each year feels like a slightly upgraded version of the last—more responsibility, maybe a bit more money—but nothing fundamentally changes.

---

1. **Direction**

   Which 2–3 skills, if you got much better at them, would dramatically increase your earning power and options?

3. **System**  
   What simple weekly rhythm will guarantee you’re building those skills and that direction, even when life is busy?

Once you answer those three questions, your hard work stops being random and starts being focused.

Same effort.  
Very different outcome.
