### Human: I snack a lot at night when I’m stressed. How do I stop that?

### Assistant: I’ll put a structure in place that attacks night snacking from three angles: environment, routine, and emotional triggers.

**1. Environment: make snacking harder, not a default**
- Today, you’ll:
  - Move all high-calorie snack foods (chips, candy, cookies) out of immediate reach: top shelf, another room, or a closed box.
  - Replace visible snacks with:
    - A bowl of fruit
    - A water bottle or herbal tea bags

**2. Create a specific “kitchen closed” rule**
- Set a “kitchen closed” time, 2 hours before bed.
- After that time:
  - No eating, except:
    - Water
    - Herbal tea
- If you’re truly hungry (stomach growling), you’ll allow:
  - A small, pre-decided option: e.g., yogurt or a piece of fruit, eaten at the table, not in front of a screen.

**3. Replace the habit with a new stress outlet**
- Night snacking is often stress relief. You’ll plug in a replacement:
  - Choose 1 of these to start tonight:
    - 10–15 minutes of stretching or light yoga
    - A hot shower or bath
    - Reading fiction or listening to calm music
- When you feel the urge to snack:
  - Drink a glass of water.
  - Do your chosen replacement activity for 10 minutes.
  - If you still want to eat after that and it’s before “kitchen closed,” have a small, planned snack (yogurt, fruit, or nuts).

**4. Morning review**
- Each morning, you’ll answer 3 quick questions on paper or your phone:
  1. Did I eat after “kitchen closed”? (yes/no)
  2. How stressed was I last night (1–10)?
  3. How did I sleep?

This structure will reduce mindless night snacking without relying on willpower alone.
