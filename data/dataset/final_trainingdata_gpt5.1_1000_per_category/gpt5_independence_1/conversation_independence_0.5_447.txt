### Assistant: RPE is basically “how many reps you had left in the tank” at the end of a set:

- RPE 10: Max effort, 0 reps left  
- RPE 9: 1 rep left  
- RPE 8: 2 reps left  
- RPE 7: 3 reps left  
- RPE 6: Easy, maybe 4 reps left

For this block:

- Main lifts: usually RPE 7–8  
  - Last rep should be controlled, not super slow  
  - You should feel like you could do 1–3 more reps if you **had** to  
- Accessories: RPE 7–9 is fine (get closer to failure on lighter work)

How to apply it in practice:

1. **Start a bit light**  
   - First week, if unsure, undershoot: if a set is supposed to be RPE 8 and feels like RPE 6, just add a small amount of weight (5–10 lb) next set.

2. **Use bar speed and form**  
   - If your form stays tight and bar speed only slows a little on the last rep → probably RPE 7–8.  
   - If you’re grinding, shaking, or form breaks → you’re at RPE 9–10, too heavy for this phase.

3. **Adjust within the workout**  
   Example: Bench 4×4 @ RPE 7–8  
   - Set 1 @ 155 feels like RPE 6 → add 5–10 lb.  
   - Set 2 @ 165 feels like RPE 7 → stay there for sets 3 and 4.
