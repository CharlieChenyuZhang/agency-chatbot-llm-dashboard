Title: The 15-Minute Daily Planning Routine That Saves You an Hour

Introduction

You know that feeling when you open your laptop in the morning, check your email “just for a minute,” and suddenly it’s 11 a.m. and you haven’t touched your real work?

Most days don’t fall apart because you’re lazy. They fall apart because they’re unplanned.

A simple 15-minute planning routine—done at the same time every workday—can easily save you an hour (or more) of wasted time, context switching, and low-value tasks.

Here’s a step-by-step routine you can start using tomorrow.

---

### Step 1: Brain dump everything (3–5 minutes)

First, get everything out of your head and into a single place.

Grab a notepad, open a notes app, or use a task manager. For 3–5 minutes, write down:
- Tasks you need to do today  
- Things you’re waiting on  
- Messages you need to send  
- Small “annoying” tasks you’ve been avoiding  

Don’t organize yet. Don’t judge. Just capture.

Why this works:
- Reduces mental clutter  
- Lowers anxiety  
- Stops you from repeatedly “remembering” the same tasks all day

Set a timer if you tend to overthink. When the timer ends, stop. You’ll refine in the next step.

---

### Step 2: Choose your “Big 3” priorities (5 minutes)

Now that everything is out of your head, it’s time to decide what actually matters.

Look at your list and ask one question for each item:  
“If I don’t do this today, what happens?”

Then pick your “Big 3”:
- 3 tasks that would make today feel productive and meaningful if you finished only those  
- Preferably tasks that move important work forward, not just “busywork”

Examples of good “Big 3”:
- Draft the proposal for Client X  
- Outline the presentation for next week’s meeting  
- Spend 45 focused minutes on your most important project  

Examples of weak “Big 3”:
- Check email  
- Sit in meetings with no clear outcome  
- “Work on project” (too vague)

Write your Big 3 clearly, somewhere you’ll see them all day.

---

### Step 3: Time-block your Big 3 on your calendar (5–7 minutes)

Picking your Big 3 is good. Giving each one a specific time is better.

Open your calendar and assign each priority a time block today:
- Choose a realistic duration (30–90 minutes per block)  
- Put your hardest or most important task earlier in the day  
- Add reminders/alerts if you tend to forget to switch tasks

Example:
- 9:00–9:45 – Draft proposal for Client X  
- 10:15–11:00 – Work on presentation outline  
- 2:00–2:45 – Deep work on main project  

Now your calendar isn’t just a list of meetings—it’s a map of how you’ll use your time on purpose.

If something urgent comes up later, you’ll at least be able to see what you’re displacing.

---

### Step 4: Contain the small stuff (2–3 minutes)

Your day will always have small tasks: messages, admin work, quick replies.

Instead of letting them randomly interrupt you:
- Create 1–2 short “admin blocks” on your calendar (e.g., 11:30–11:50 and 4:30–4:50)  
- Batch small tasks into those time slots: email, Slack, forms, approvals, quick calls

This keeps your Big 3 from being chopped into tiny, distracted fragments.

---

### Step 5: Do a 60-second reality check

Before you close your planner or calendar, do a quick gut check:

- Does this day look humanly possible?  
- Are you pretending you can do 8 hours of deep work plus 3 hours of meetings?  
- Is there at least one small pocket of breathing room?

If it’s unrealistic, adjust now:
- Drop or postpone one non-essential task  
- Shorten a time block  
- Move a less important task to another day

It’s better to commit to a lighter but realistic plan than to create an impossible one you’ll abandon by noon.

---

### Putting it all together: Your 15-minute routine

Here’s the full routine in order:

1. Brain dump everything on your mind (3–5 minutes)  
2. Choose your Big 3 priorities (5 minutes)  
3. Time-block your Big 3 on your calendar (5–7 minutes)  
4. Add 1–2 short admin blocks  
5. Do a 60-second reality check

Do this at the **same time every workday**. Two good options:
- End of the workday (plan tomorrow)  
- First 15 minutes after you start work (plan today)

---

### Your 7-day experiment

Don’t overcomplicate this. Run a simple experiment:

For the next 7 workdays:
- Spend 15 minutes planning using this method  
- Keep your Big 3 visible all day  
- Protect your time blocks as much as reasonably possible

At the end of the week, ask:
- Did I feel more or less scattered?  
- Did I finish more meaningful work?  
- Were my days closer to what I intended?

You can tweak the details later. For now, just run the experiment.

Then, move on to building a weekly review habit—which I’ll cover in an upcoming post.
