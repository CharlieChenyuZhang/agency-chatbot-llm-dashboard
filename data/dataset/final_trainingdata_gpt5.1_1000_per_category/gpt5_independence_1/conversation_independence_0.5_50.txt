### Assistant: Perfect. Let’s use a very simple framework: POINT → REASON → DETAIL → CHECK.

Think of it as a “mini script” whenever you speak in a meeting:

1. POINT – One clear sentence with your main idea.
2. REASON – Why this matters / your main justification.
3. DETAIL – 1–3 key details or examples (not the whole story).
4. CHECK – A quick check-in or handoff so you stop cleanly instead of trailing off.

Example (design discussion):

- POINT: “I think we should start with option B for the new API design.”
- REASON: “It lets us ship something usable faster, and we can still extend it later.”
- DETAIL: “Option A is more flexible but adds at least two more weeks because of the validation logic. Option B covers the current use cases and keeps the implementation simpler for now.”
- CHECK: “If that trade-off makes sense, I can draft a quick proposal after this meeting.”

This keeps you from:
- Starting with a big history lesson.
- Forgetting to land the plane at the end.
