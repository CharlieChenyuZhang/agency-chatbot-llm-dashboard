### Human: Help me solve this complex problem

### Assistant: I’ll treat “complex problem” as: you’re facing something big, messy, and unclear, and you want a systematic way to handle it. I’ll lay out a general method, then illustrate it with a concrete example so you can see how to use it.

General method for any complex problem:

1. Define the real problem  
   - Write one sentence that starts with: “The core problem is…”  
   - Identify why this matters (impact on time, money, health, relationships, or emotions).

2. Set a clear target state  
   - One sentence that starts with: “Success looks like…”  
   - Make it specific, measurable, and time-bound.

3. Map constraints and assets  
   - Constraints: time, money, skills, obligations, rules.  
   - Assets: skills, people you know, tools, money, reputation, past experience.

4. Break into sub-problems  
   - Turn the big problem into 3–7 smaller ones.  
   - For each: “To reach success, I need to solve: X.”

5. Design experiments, not “perfect plans”  
   - For each sub-problem, define a 1–2 week experiment with:  
     - a small action,  
     - a success metric,  
     - a timebox.

6. Execute–measure–adjust loop  
   - Do the experiment.  
   - Measure results against your metric.  
   - Keep, tweak, or discard based on results.

Concrete example: planning a career change

1. Core problem  
   - “The core problem is: I’m stuck in a job I don’t want long term, and I have no clear path or system to change careers without risking my income.”

2. Success state  
   - “Success looks like: within 9 months, I’m in a new role in data analytics with at least 90% of my current salary and clear growth potential.”

3. Constraints vs assets  
   - Constraints: full-time job, limited portfolio, no formal degree in the field.  
   - Assets: strong Excel skills, some SQL basics, 2 hours per weekday evening, savings to cover 1 course.

4. Sub-problems  
   - (1) Decide the exact target role.  
   - (2) Close skill gaps to entry-level requirements.  
   - (3) Build a visible portfolio.  
   - (4) Get interviews.  
   - (5) Perform well in interviews.

5. Example experiments  
   - For (1): In 2 weeks, do 10 informational interviews and analyze 30 job descriptions to pick one specific target title.  
   - For (2): In 4 weeks, complete one structured online course and ship 2 small projects.  
   - For (3): In 2 weeks, publish one project on GitHub and a short write-up on LinkedIn.

6. Loop  
   - After each 2–4 week block, review: what moved you closer to “success” and what didn’t, then reallocate effort accordingly.

You can plug any complex situation into this structure—life, work, business, or personal.
