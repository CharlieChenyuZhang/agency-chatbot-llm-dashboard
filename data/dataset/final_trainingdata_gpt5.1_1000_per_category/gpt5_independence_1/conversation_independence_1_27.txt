Post 1 – Silent killers of engagement (Week 1, Mon)

Hook:
“The biggest threat to employee engagement in 2025 isn’t apathy. It’s silence.”

Body:
Over the last year, talking to HR leaders across dozens of companies, I keep seeing the same 5 “silent killers” of engagement:

1) Calendar-only 1:1s  
Managers treat 1:1s like status updates, not coaching conversations. Employees stop bringing real issues because nothing changes.

2) Feedback with no follow-through  
Pulse surveys go out. Dashboards light up. Then… nothing. People learn that being honest is just extra work with no upside.

3) Managers stuck in firefighting mode  
Leaders are so busy reacting to issues that they never get to the patterns underneath them. The same problems reappear every quarter.

4) No clear owner for engagement  
Is it HR? Is it managers? Is it leadership? When “everyone” owns it, no one does—and initiatives quietly die.

5) Celebrating outputs, not outcomes  
We celebrate “launched a new program” instead of “reduced regrettable attrition by 15%.” People feel the difference.

The hard part: most of these don’t show up in traditional HR dashboards until the damage is already done.

The good part: they’re all fixable with clearer ownership, better feedback loops, and faster follow-through.

If you’re an HR leader, which of these is the most challenging in your org right now?

Post 2 – Broken annual surveys (Week 2, Wed)

Hook:
“Annual engagement surveys are like taking one screenshot of a movie and calling it the whole story.”

Body:
I still see companies betting their entire engagement strategy on a single, 1x/year survey.

Here’s why that’s broken in 2025:

- The pace of change is too fast  
Reorgs, new tools, hybrid policies—things shift monthly. Waiting 12 months to see how people feel is essentially flying blind.

- Employees game the system  
People learn which questions “matter” and answer defensively: “If I say this, will it trigger more meetings? More ‘initiatives’?”

- By the time results are shared, they’re stale  
I’ve seen survey decks presented 90 days after the survey closed. Half the managers have new teams by then.

What works better in practice:

1) Smaller, more frequent pulses  
Ask fewer questions more often. You don’t need 60 questions; you need 6 you’ll actually act on.

2) Manager-level insight, not generic scores  
Help managers see patterns in their own teams, not just “company-wide 78/100.”

3) Closed-loop communication  
Tell employees: “Here’s what we heard. Here’s what we’re doing. Here’s what we’re not doing (and why).”

Tools (including ours) can help automate the data collection and follow-up…  
…but the mindset shift is the real unlock: from “once-a-year report” to “continuous conversation.”

How are you currently gathering engagement data—annual, quarterly, or something more continuous?

Post 3 – 30-minute weekly ritual for HR leaders (Week 2, Thu)

Hook:
“If you only have 30 minutes a week to stay ahead of engagement issues, spend them like this.”

Body:
HR leaders don’t need more dashboards. They need faster signal.

Here’s a 30-minute weekly ritual I’ve seen work across multiple teams:

Minutes 0–10: Scan leading indicators  
- Look at a short list of metrics (e.g., eNPS trend, recent turnover in key teams, 1:1 completion rates).  
- Don’t get lost in charts—focus on “What changed vs last week?”

Minutes 10–20: Identify 1 conversation to start

- “Which team or manager do I need a real conversation with this week?”  
- “What question would surface the truth fastest?”

Then send one message:  
“Hey [Manager], I noticed [pattern]. Can we spend 15 minutes this week understanding what’s really going on for your team?”

Minutes 20–30: Close the loop  
- Pick 1 piece of feedback from employees that’s been sitting too long.  
- Decide: act now, communicate why not, or consciously deprioritize.  
- Tell people what you decided. Silence is what kills trust.

You don’t need a huge transformation to get ahead of engagement problems.  
You need a small, consistent ritual that keeps you close to what your people are actually experiencing.

If you don’t have a weekly ritual like this today, this is your sign to start one next week.
