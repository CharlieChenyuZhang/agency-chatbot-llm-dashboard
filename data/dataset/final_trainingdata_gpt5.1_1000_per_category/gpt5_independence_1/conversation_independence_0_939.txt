**Assignee vs Reviewer in GitLab Merge Requests**

1. **Reviewer – who gives feedback and approvals**
   - A **Reviewer** is typically someone who:
     - Reads and evaluates the code or changes.
     - Leaves comments, suggestions, and discussions.
     - May have the ability to **approve** the MR (depending on project settings).
   - Process-wise, reviewers are the **people asked to review**; they’re not necessarily the one responsible for merging.

2. **Assignee – who is currently responsible for the MR**
   - The **Assignee** is usually:
     - The person **responsible for driving the MR to completion**.
     - Typically the **author** or the **developer** who will address review comments.
     - In some workflows, the assignee is also the person who will **press the Merge button** once all approvals are done (if permissions allow).
   - There is usually **one primary assignee**, whereas there may be multiple reviewers.

3. **Typical workflow using both roles**
   - The **author/assignee** opens the MR and sets one or more **reviewers**.
   - Reviewers:
     - Review the changes.
     - Comment or request changes.
     - Approve once satisfied.
   - The **assignee**:
     - Responds to feedback, updates the code, and pushes new commits.
     - Ensures all required approvals are collected.
     - Finally merges the MR (or hands it off to someone who can).

4. **How this maps to GitLab behavior**
   - GitLab can enforce **approval rules** (e.g., “At least 2 approvals required”).
   - Approvals are usually tied more to **reviewers** (who can click “Approve”) than to the assignee.
   - The assignee remains the person shown as “responsible” in lists and dashboards.
