## 2. Physical setup: what to do step by step

**Step 1: Clear and stage**
1. Clear one flat surface (floor, bed, table).
2. Take all your books off the shelves. Stack them anywhere for now.

**Step 2: First rough split**
Create four physical zones (even just on the floor):

- Zone A: Fiction & poetry  
- Zone B: Nonfiction  
- Zone C: Oversized / large format (won’t fit upright on normal shelves)  
- Zone D: Special / rare / sentimental

Now, go book by book and drop each into a zone:
- Novels, short stories, plays, poetry → Zone A  
- Anything factual, instructional, biographical, essay collections → Zone B  
- Big art books, atlases, big photo books → Zone C  
- Signed, first editions, childhood favorites you can’t replace → also tag as Zone D (they can live inside A, B, or C but flagged as special)

**Step 3: Break nonfiction into categories**
Take Zone B and split into these piles:

- History  
- Science & tech  
- Philosophy & religion  
- Social sciences (psychology, politics, sociology, economics)  
- Biography & memoir  
- Arts & culture (music, film, criticism, design)  
- Practical (business, self-help, productivity, cookbooks, manuals, how‑to)  
- Reference (dictionaries, style manuals, language, encyclopedias)

If a book fits multiple, I’ll decide this rule:  
- If it’s about a person → Biography & memoir  
- If its main goal is to teach a life/practical skill → Practical  
- Otherwise → whichever subject area is most obvious (e.g., “history of physics” → Science & tech)

**Step 4: Break fiction into categories**
From Zone A, create:

- General / contemporary fiction  
- Classics (pre‑1970, canon authors, or clearly “classic”)  
- Mystery / thriller / crime  
- Sci‑fi & fantasy  
- YA (marketed as YA or clearly teen‑targeted)  
- Poetry / drama

Borderline cases go here by default:
- Literary vs general → General  
- Historical novels → General unless very mystery/sci‑fi heavy  
- Magic realism → Sci‑fi & fantasy

**Step 5: Alphabetize**
Within every pile (e.g., “Nonfiction → History” or “Fiction → Sci‑fi & fantasy”):

1. Sort by **author’s last name** A → Z.  
2. If multiple books by the same author, sort them by series order if obvious, else by publication year, else alphabetically by title.

**Step 6: Assign shelves**
Now I’ll map shelves:

- Top shelves: **Fiction & poetry** (Zone A)  
- Middle shelves (easy reach): **Nonfiction** (Zone B) → most used categories go here (History, Science, Social sciences, Practical)  
- Lower shelves:  
  - Oversized (Zone C)  
  - Heavier books (reference, big manuals)  
- Special items (Zone D):  
  - Either one dedicated “special shelf”, or  
  - Face-out displays sprinkled across top shelves for your favorites

Place books on shelves following your category groups, left to right, top to bottom in this order:

1. Fiction block  
2. Nonfiction block  
3. Reference / bulky / oversized block  
4. Special display/rare

## 3. Simple catalog you can maintain

We’ll create a quick spreadsheet (Google Sheets / Excel / Numbers). Use these columns:

1. ID (I’ll define this: F001, NF072, etc.)  
2. Title  
3. Author (Last, First)  
4. Type (Fiction / Nonfiction / Oversized / Special)  
5. Category (from the lists above)  
6. Location (e.g., “Shelf 2, left”, or “Fiction - SciFi, middle shelf”)  
7. Status (Unread / In progress / Finished / To donate)  
8. Format (Hardcover / Paperback / Ebook / Audiobook if you track those too)  
9. Notes (signed, gift from X, first edition, etc.)

**ID scheme I’m choosing:**
- Fiction: F001, F002, F003…  
- Nonfiction: N001, N002, N003…  
- Oversized: O001, O002…  
- Special: S001, S002… (these can overlap with A/B/C, but flagged as special)

As you shelve alphabetically, log each book once. This gives you a searchable catalog forever.

## 4. Handling unread books and culling

I’ll impose this rule to keep the collection from bloating:

- **Unread shelf/section:**  
  - Reserve one small area (e.g., rightmost 1–2 shelves in Fiction and Nonfiction) for **Unread** books.  
  - Once you finish a book, it moves into the main alphabetized area.

- **Culling rules:**  
  - If you haven’t touched a non-reference book in **5+ years**, and it has no sentimental or rare value → it goes to a **“To donate”** box.  
  - Duplicates → keep the nicer copy, box the rest.  
  - Outdated tech/business books older than 10 years → default to donation/recycling, unless still clearly relevant.

Add the “To donate” books to the catalog but set **Status = To donate** so you know what left your shelves.

---

## 5. Daily use habits

Once everything’s in place:

- When you finish a book:
  - Update Status in the catalog (e.g., Unread → Finished).
  - Return it to the correct alphabetized spot.

- When you buy a new book:
  - Immediately log it in the catalog.
  - Put it into the Unread section in the correct Type/Category.

This way, the system stays clean with minimal effort.
