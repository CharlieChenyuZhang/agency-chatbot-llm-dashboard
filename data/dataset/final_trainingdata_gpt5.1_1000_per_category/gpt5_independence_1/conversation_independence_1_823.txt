### Human: Plan a study sprint with spaced repetition

### Assistant: Here’s a concrete 7‑day study sprint that uses spaced repetition. I’ll assume you’re preparing for one major subject (you can swap in your own topic names):

**Overall structure (7 days)**  
- Daily time: ~2 hours  
- Tools: a spaced-repetition app (e.g., Anki) + short focused sessions  
- Pattern: Learn → Review (spaced) → Test → Refine

---

**Day 1 – Setup + First Learning Pass (2 hours)**  
1. **Decide core topics (10 minutes)**  
   - Example:  
     - Topic 1: Key definitions / formulas  
     - Topic 2: Core concepts / theorems  
     - Topic 3: Typical problems / examples  

2. **Create initial flashcards (60 minutes)**  
   - Aim: 60–80 cards total across your topics.  
   - Use simple formats:  
     - “Question → Answer”  
     - “Cloze deletion” (fill-in-the-blank for definitions / formulas)  
   - Keep each card atomic: one idea per card.

3. **First active study session (40 minutes)**  
   - Use your SRS app’s “new cards” session.  
   - Go through all new cards once.  
   - Rate difficulty honestly (Easy/Good/Hard) so the app sets intervals.

4. **Short recall test (10 minutes)**  
   - Close everything and write from memory:  
     - 5–10 key formulas  
     - 5 key concepts  
   - Compare with your cards; add 5–10 new cards for anything missing.

---

**Days 2–3 – Heavy Review + Light New Material (2 hours/day)**  

**Each day:**

1. **Spaced review block (45–60 minutes)**  
   - Do *only* “Due” cards first.  
   - Target: clear your review queue for the day.  
   - If any card feels vague, rewrite it to be clearer or split it into 2 cards.

2. **Focused practice (45 minutes)**  
   - Solve 5–10 problems/questions related to your topics.  
   - For each mistake or hesitation, create 1–2 new cards that:  
     - Capture the *error pattern* (“I forgot to distribute the negative sign”)  
     - Capture the *correct reasoning*.

3. **Quick recap (10–15 minutes)**  
   - Summarize the day in 5–8 bullet points from memory.  
   - Turn weak spots from the recap into 3–5 additional cards.

---

**Days 4–5 – Mixed Practice + Consolidation (2 hours/day)**  

**Each day:**

1. **Spaced review block (45 minutes)**  
   - Again, clear all “Due” cards.  
   - Suspend or tag cards that are consistently too easy (you already know them very well).

2. **Timed mixed practice (50–60 minutes)**  
   - Simulate exam conditions:  
     - Set a 25–30 minute timer, do mixed questions from all topics.  
     - Short break (5 minutes).  
     - Another 25–30 minute block.  
   - After each block, immediately create or adjust cards based on:  
     - Anything you got wrong  
     - Anything that felt slow to recall.

3. **Mini “teach-back” (10–15 minutes)**  
   - Speak out loud (or write) as if teaching someone:  
     - “Here’s how to approach [Topic X] problems…”  
   - Any gap or confusion becomes a new card.

---

**Days 6–7 – Exam Simulation + High-Yield Review (2 hours/day)**  

**Day 6:**
1. **Spaced review (30–40 minutes)**  
   - Do all due cards.  
   - Mark chronically hard cards as “extra” and show them more often.

2. **Full-length practice test (60–70 minutes)**  
   - Use a past paper or long set of questions.  
   - No notes, no pausing. Treat it like the real exam.

3. **Targeted card creation (20–30 minutes)**  
   - For each missed / slow question:  
     - Create 1–3 cards that encode:  
       - The *trigger*: what kind of question this is  
       - The *method*: steps to solve  
       - The *pitfall*: what you did wrong this time  

**Day 7:**
1. **High-yield review (60 minutes)**  
   - Filter your deck:  
     - Show “Hard” or recently-added cards first.  
   - Rapid-fire review; if a card is still confusing, simplify or split it.

2. **Final quick test (30 minutes)**  
   - Short mixed test with your weakest topics.  
   - No notes. Time yourself strictly.

3. **Ultra-condensed cheat sheet (30 minutes)**  
   - On one sheet (or one doc page), write from memory:  
     - Top 10 formulas / rules  
     - Top 10 “if you see X, do Y” patterns  
   - Only afterward, compare with your cards/notes and fill any gaps.

---

**Daily routine (same every day)**  
- Study at the same time each day.  
- Use 25–30 minute focus blocks with 5-minute breaks.  
- Always: Reviews → Practice → Card updates.

You can plug in your exact subject and exam date, and this 7-day sprint will still work: just change what goes on the cards and what problems you practice.

### Human: I’m studying for an organic chemistry exam in two weeks. Can you adapt this plan to that?

### Assistant: I’ll turn this into a 14‑day organic chemistry sprint aimed at a major exam. I’ll assume topics like mechanisms, functional groups, spectroscopy, stereochemistry, and reactions.

**Global structure (14 days)**  
- Time: ~2–3 hours/day  
- Primary tool: SRS app (e.g., Anki) + problem sets / past exams  
- Focus: reaction patterns, mechanisms, recognition (spectra, structures), and common traps

---

#### Days 1–2: Foundations + Deck Setup

**Content focus:**  
- Functional groups & basic properties  
- Acid–base concepts (pKa trends, stability)  
- Resonance and electron-pushing (curved arrows)  
- Nomenclature refresh (only what you actually need)

**Daily plan (2–3 hours):**  
1. **Concept sweep (40–60 minutes)**  
   - Skim your textbook/notes for these topics.  
   - Don’t aim to memorize yet; just collect key ideas.

2. **Flashcard creation (45–60 minutes)**  
   Prioritize these card types:  
   - “ID” cards:  
     - Front: structure, “Name the functional group and predict relative reactivity in [context].”  
     - Back: name + short explanation.  
   - “Why” cards:  
     - Front: “Why is this acid stronger than that one?”  
     - Back: resonance/induction/hybridization reasoning.  
   - “Arrow-pushing mini-cards”:  
     - Front: show a partial mechanism, ask “Where does the arrow go next and why?”  

   Aim for ~80–100 cards over 2 days.

3. **Initial review session (30–40 minutes)**  
   - Go through new cards.  
   - Mark anything that feels like pure memorization without understanding—rewrite those to include reasoning.

4. **Short problem set (30–40 minutes)**  
   - 5–10 problems on acid–base, resonance, identifying functional groups.  
   - Every mistake → 1–2 new cards.

---

#### Days 3–5: Core Mechanisms & Reaction Families

**Content focus:**  
- Substitution: SN1 vs SN2  
- Elimination: E1 vs E2  
- Stereochemistry (R/S, E/Z, stereospecific outcomes)  
- Basic addition reactions (to alkenes, alkynes)

**Each day (2–3 hours):**  
1. **Spaced review first (30–45 minutes)**  
   - Clear all due cards.  
   - Split any “too big” mechanism cards into smaller steps.

2. **Mechanism blocks (60–75 minutes)**  
   For each mechanism family, build:  
   - Decision cards:  
     - Front: “Given this substrate/solvent/base, which mechanism dominates (SN1/SN2/E1/E2)?”  
     - Back: explanation about substrate structure, base strength, solvent.  
   - Step cards:  
     - Front: “Next step in this SN1 mechanism?”  
     - Back: carbocation formation, nucleophile attack, etc.  
   - Stereochemistry cards:  
     - Front: show reactant + conditions, “Draw/configure the product(s) and describe stereochemical outcome.”  

3. **Targeted problem sets (30–45 minutes)**  
   - Mixed SN1/SN2/E1/E2 and stereochemistry problems.  
   - For each: identify *why* one pathway is favored. Encode that reasoning into new cards.

4. **Mini “teach” recap (10–15 minutes)**  
   - Out loud: “Here’s how I decide between SN1, SN2, E1, E2…”  
   - Anything fuzzy → new card.

---

#### Days 6–8: Major Reaction Patterns & Reagents

**Content focus:**  
- Key reaction types:  
  - Electrophilic addition to alkenes/alkynes (hydrohalogenation, hydration, halogenation, etc.)  
  - Oxidation/reduction (e.g., PCC vs Jones, NaBH4 vs LiAlH4)  
  - Common reagents and what they “do”

**Each day (2–3 hours):**  
1. **Spaced review (30–40 minutes)** – always first.  

2. **Reaction pattern mapping (45–60 minutes)**  
   - Make “If you see X, think Y” cards:  
     - Front: “Reagent: PCC on a 1° alcohol → product type?”  
     - Back: aldehyde (+ short explanation).  
   - “Forward” and “reverse” cards:  
     - Forward: reactant + reagents → predict product.  
     - Reverse: product + reagents → draw possible starting material.  
   - “Compare & contrast” cards:  
     - Front: “NaBH4 vs LiAlH4 on esters/aldehydes/ketones – what happens?”  
     - Back: succinct table-style explanation.

3. **Synthesis-style mini problems (45–60 minutes)**  
   - 1–3 step syntheses.  
   - For each step, ask:  
     - Which functional group change is happening?  
     - Which reagent class accomplishes that?  
   - Turn these into *sequence cards*:  
     - Front: “Convert alkene → vicinal diol (syn). Which sequence of reagents?”  
     - Back: Osmium tetroxide (OsO4) + workup, etc.

4. **End-of-day summary (10–15 minutes)**  
   - Build a one-page “reaction map” from memory.  
   - Anything you can’t remember → new cards.

---

#### Days 9–10: Spectroscopy & Structure Elucidation

**Content focus:**  
- IR: main peaks and what they mean  
- 1H NMR & 13C NMR: chemical shift ranges, splitting patterns, integration  
- Mass spec basics (if tested)

**Each day (2–3 hours):**  
1. **Spaced review (30–40 minutes)**  

2. **Spectroscopy cards (60 minutes)**  
   - IR cards:  
     - Front: “ IR: sharp peak ~1710 cm⁻¹ + broad 3200–3600 cm⁻¹. Likely groups?”  
     - Back: carbonyl + O–H (e.g., carboxylic acid), with quick note.  
   - NMR cards:  
     - Front: “1H NMR: singlet at ~9–10 ppm. What environment?”  
     - Back: aldehydic proton.  
     - Front: “Triplet around 1 ppm, integration 3H, next to CH2. What fragment?”  
     - Back: terminal CH3–CH2– fragment.  

3. **Structure puzzles (45–60 minutes)**  
   - Use combined IR + NMR problems.  
   - For each completed problem, create *pattern* cards:  
     - Front: “Key spectral clues → type of molecule?”  
     - Back: short explanation of how you inferred it.

4. **Quick mixed review (10–15 minutes)**  
   - Randomly mix mechanism + spectroscopy cards to keep both fresh.

---

#### Days 11–12: Full Mixed Practice + Weak-Spot Attack

**Content focus:**  
- All topics mixed under exam-like conditions.

**Each day (2–3 hours):**  
1. **Spaced review (30–40 minutes)**  
   - Focus especially on “Hard” and recently-added cards.  

2. **Timed mixed sets (60–75 minutes)**  
   - 2 blocks of 25–30 minutes with 5-minute break.  
   - Mix: mechanisms, synthesis, spectroscopy, acid–base, stereochemistry.  
   - After each block, categorize errors:  
     - “Didn’t know reaction”  
     - “Knew reaction but misapplied mechanism”  
     - “Drawing or stereochemistry sloppiness”  
   - Each category gets its own group of new/refined cards.

3. **Weak-spot drill (30–45 minutes)**  
   - Pick top 2 weakest areas (e.g., multi-step synthesis, NMR).  
   - Do 5–10 problems only in those areas.  
   - Build additional decision-tree cards:  
     - Front: “Goal: convert alcohol → alkene. What are two routes and when prefer each?”  
     - Back: dehydration vs via substitution/elimination etc.

---

#### Days 13–14: Final Exam Simulation + High-Yield Review

**Day 13 (2–3 hours):**  
1. **Short review (20–30 minutes)** – clear due cards.  

2. **Full-length practice exam (60–90 minutes)**  
   - Use a past paper or a comprehensive set mimicking your exam format.  
   - No notes, strict timing.

3. **Post-exam analysis (45–60 minutes)**  
   - For each missed / slow question, write:  
     - What I *thought* was happening.  
     - What was *actually* happening.  
     - The key pattern I need to remember.  
   - Turn each into 1–3 targeted cards.

**Day 14 (2–3 hours):**  
1. **High-yield SRS pass (60–75 minutes)**  
   - Filter: “Hard” + recently-missed patterns from Day 13.  
   - Rapid cycles; simplify any confusing cards.

2. **One-page master sheets (45–60 minutes)**  
   From memory, create:  
   - Reaction summary sheet (major reaction families + reagents).  
   - Spectroscopy summary (key IR peaks, NMR ranges).  
   - Mechanism decision sheet (SN1/SN2/E1/E2, typical additions).  

3. **Final light review (15–20 minutes)**  
   - Flip through your master sheets and a handful of representative problems.  
   - No new cards now—only reinforce existing patterns.

Follow this schedule day by day; keep doing reviews first, then practice, then card updates. That will give you strong coverage for an organic exam in two weeks.
