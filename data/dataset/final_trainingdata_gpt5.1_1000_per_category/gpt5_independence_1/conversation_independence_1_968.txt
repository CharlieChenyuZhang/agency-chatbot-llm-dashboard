### Human: Select a microphone and configure audio chain

### Assistant: I’ll set you up with a clean, reliable vocal recording chain suitable for a typical home environment (spoken word, podcasting, streaming, and light singing).

**Microphone:**  
- **Shure SM7B** – Dynamic cardioid mic  
  - Reason: Excellent rejection of room noise, forgiving in untreated rooms, broadcast-standard tone, low sibilance.

**Audio Interface / Preamp:**  
- **Audient EVO 4** (or similar clean interface with strong preamps)  
  - Reason: Quiet, plenty of gain for the SM7B, simple operation, USB connection.

**Optional Inline Preamp (if needed):**  
- **Cloudlifter CL-1** (or FetHead)  
  - Reason: Adds clean gain to avoid cranking the interface preamp, keeping noise low.

**Physical Setup:**  
- Boom arm or sturdy stand with:
  - Shock mount for SM7B
  - Pop filter (or use built-in SM7B foam plus a secondary nylon pop filter if plosives are strong)
- Place mic about:
  - 10–15 cm (4–6") from your mouth
  - Capsule slightly off-axis (5–15°) to reduce plosives
  - Mic at mouth or slightly above mouth level

**Gain Staging (Recording Level):**  
- Set interface gain so that:
  - Normal speaking peaks hit around **-12 to -9 dBFS**
  - Loudest moments stay safely below **-6 dBFS**
- If using a Cloudlifter, keep interface gain more moderate; let the inline preamp do most of the lifting.

**Digital Audio Workstation (DAW):**  
- Use any of these: Reaper, Audacity (free), or Logic/Pro Tools, etc.  
  - I’ll assume **Reaper** for this chain.

**In-DAW Processing Chain (Insert Order):**

1. **High-Pass Filter (EQ)**
   - HPF at **70–80 Hz** (voice-only)  
   - Steeper slope (18–24 dB/oct) to clean rumble and desk/handling noise.

2. **Subtractive EQ**
   - Cut low-mid muddiness:  
     - **-2 to -4 dB around 200–350 Hz**, fairly wide Q  
   - Tame boxiness if present:  
     - **-2 to -3 dB around 500–800 Hz**  
   - If harshness/sibilance around **4–7 kHz**, a gentle **-2 to -3 dB** dip.

3. **Compression**
   - Ratio: **3:1 to 4:1**  
   - Attack: **10–20 ms** (let transients through a bit)  
   - Release: **60–120 ms** (smooth return)  
   - Aim for **3–6 dB gain reduction** on average speech.

4. **De-Esser**
   - Target band: **5–8 kHz**  
   - Start around **6 kHz**, adjust as needed  
   - Gentle reduction: **2–5 dB** on sibilant consonants.

5. **Tone-Shaping EQ (Post-Compression)**
   - Presence boost for clarity:  
     - **+1 to +3 dB around 3–5 kHz**  
   - Optional air/sheen:  
     - **+1 to +2 dB around 10–12 kHz** if it doesn’t get harsh.

6. **Limiter (On Master or Vocal Bus)**
   - Ceiling: **-1 dBFS**  
   - Only catching peaks: **1–3 dB** max GR on the loudest moments.

**Monitoring Setup:**
- Closed-back headphones to avoid bleed when recording.
- Direct monitoring via the interface to minimize latency; blend knob toward “Input” side.

This gives you a robust, broadcast-quality chain:  
**Shure SM7B → (Cloudlifter) → Audient EVO 4 → DAW (HPF → EQ → Compressor → De-Esser → EQ → Limiter).**

### Human: What if my room is really echoey and untreated?

### Assistant: I’ll adapt the setup to better handle a very reflective, untreated room without asking you to treat the space first.

**Microphone Change:**  
I’ll keep you on a **dynamic cardioid mic**, but I’ll switch the specific choice to optimize rejection and price:

- **Sennheiser e935** or **Shure Beta 58A**
  - Reason: Tight cardioid/supercardioid pattern, strong off-axis rejection, closer working distance, more forgiving of bad rooms than many condensers.

**Placement Adjustments:**
- Distance:  
  - Move closer: **5–8 cm (2–3")** from the grille.  
- Angle:  
  - Even more off-axis: **15–20°** to reduce plosives and harshness.
- Orientation:
  - Point the mic so its **null** faces the loudest or most reflective area (e.g., toward a bare wall or noisy computer).
- Quick makeshift treatment around the mic (not the whole room):  
  - Place a thick blanket or duvet behind you (so your voice doesn’t bounce straight back into the mic).  
  - If possible, put something soft and dense (cushions, hanging blanket) directly behind the mic, out of frame.

**Processing Tweaks for Echoey Room:**

1. **Stronger High-Pass Filter**
   - HPF at **90–100 Hz**, especially for spoken word, to clear low rumble that reflective rooms exaggerate.

2. **Room-Resonance EQ**
   - Narrow cuts in the low-mid range (e.g., **120–350 Hz**) if you hear “boomy” ringing.  
   - Typical starting cuts:  
     - **-3 to -5 dB at 150–200 Hz** (narrow-ish Q) for boom  
     - **-2 to -4 dB at 400–600 Hz** for boxiness

3. **Gate or Expander**
   - Add before compression to reduce room tone between phrases:
     - **Expander** rather than a hard gate:  
       - Ratio: **1.5:1 to 2:1**  
       - Threshold: Just below your quietest speech  
       - Attack: **5–10 ms**  
       - Release: **150–300 ms**  
   - This shortens the decay of room echo between words without chopping ends off.

4. **Compression and De-Essing**
   - Keep compression moderate (3–5 dB GR); too much will pull up the room sound.  
   - De-esser stays similar; just be cautious boosting highs later, as that can emphasize room tone.

5. **Brightness Control**
   - Be conservative with high boosts (above 6–8 kHz), because they bring out hiss and reflections.

The updated chain becomes:  
**Dynamic handheld mic (e935/Beta 58A) → Interface → DAW (HPF → Room-EQ cuts → Expander → Compressor → De-Esser → Light EQ → Limiter).**

### Human: I also want to record acoustic guitar with the same setup. How would you adjust things?

### Assistant: I’ll extend the rig so you can record both voice and acoustic guitar cleanly, still keeping it practical.

**Microphone Strategy:**
- Keep the **dynamic vocal mic** for your voice.
- Add one **small-diaphragm condenser** for guitar:
  - Example: **RØDE NT5** or **Audio-Technica AT2021**  
  - Reason: Better detail and transient response on acoustic instruments.

**Interface Requirement:**
- Use an interface with **at least 2 XLR inputs** (e.g., Audient EVO 4 already has 2).  
- Input 1: Vocal mic  
- Input 2: Guitar mic

**Guitar Mic Placement:**
- Point mic at the **12th fret**, about **20–30 cm (8–12")** away.
- Angle slightly toward the sound hole, but not directly at it (to avoid boominess).
- Keep the guitar mic aimed away from your mouth as much as possible to control bleed if recording voice and guitar simultaneously.

**Recording Approaches:**

1. **Tracking Separately (Best Quality):**
   - Record a vocal track first with the dynamic mic.
   - Then record guitar alone with the condenser.
   - This gives maximum control and minimal bleed.

2. **Tracking Together (Live Performance Feel):**
   - Input 1: Vocal chain (dynamic mic).
   - Input 2: Guitar chain (SDC condenser).
   - Physically separate the mics as much as feasible:
     - Vocal mic closer to your mouth.
     - Guitar mic lower, pointing at the guitar, with its null pointed away from your mouth where possible.

**Guitar Processing Chain:**

1. **High-Pass Filter**
   - Start at **70–80 Hz**, adjust to taste. Go higher (up to 100–120 Hz) if the guitar is too boomy.

2. **Subtractive EQ**
   - Boominess:  
     - **-2 to -5 dB around 100–200 Hz**  
   - Boxiness:  
     - **-2 to -4 dB around 400–600 Hz**  
   - Harshness (especially pick noise):  
     - **-2 to -3 dB around 2–4 kHz** if needed.

3. **Compression**
   - Ratio: **2:1 to 3:1**  
   - Attack: **20–30 ms** (keep transients natural)  
   - Release: **80–150 ms**  
   - GR: **2–4 dB** on peaks to smooth dynamics.

4. **Optional Light Reverb**
   - Short plate or room:
     - Decay: **0.8–1.5 s**  
     - Mix: **10–20%**  
   - Keep it subtle if your room is already echoey.

You’ll end up with:

- **Voice:** Dynamic mic → Interface In 1 → Vocal chain (as previously defined).  
- **Guitar:** SDC condenser → Interface In 2 → Guitar chain (HPF → EQ → Compressor → optional Reverb).
