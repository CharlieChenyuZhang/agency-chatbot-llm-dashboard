### Human: Organize my closet

### Assistant: I’ll take over the whole process and give you a step‑by‑step plan, a layout, and a maintenance routine so you can just execute without having to decide anything.

Here’s the approach I’m choosing:
1. Declutter in a fixed order.
2. Assign every category a home.
3. Set up a simple, low‑maintenance system.

#### 1. Declutter (in this exact sequence)
Work left to right through the closet so you always know where you stopped.

Pile order and rules:
- **Pile 1: Obvious trash**
  - Remove: damaged beyond repair, missing zippers/buttons you won’t fix, single socks, yellowed or badly stained items, stretched‑out basics.
  - Action: Bag these and put directly into trash.

- **Pile 2: Donate**
  - Remove: items that fit but you never reach for, duplicates (e.g., third black hoodie), “aspirational” styles you’ve ignored for 6+ months, gifts you keep out of guilt.
  - Action: Fill 1–2 bags, place them by the door for donation drop‑off.

- **Pile 3: Seasonal off‑rotation**
  - Example: heavy winter coats if you’re in a warm season now, or swimwear if you’re in a cold season now.
  - Action: These will go into a separate bin or high shelf that I’ll assign later.

- **Pile 4: Keep (current season, fits, used at least monthly)**
  - Anything that stays in daily / weekly rotation.

As you go item by item, apply this decision rule:
- Have you worn it in the last 3–6 months (season‑adjusted)?
  - Yes → Keep/Seasonal
  - No → Donate unless it’s formalwear or specialty gear (e.g., suit, costume, hiking jacket).
- Does it fit you now and make you feel okay or better in it?
  - No → Donate.

You do not need to debate every edge case; if you hesitate more than 10 seconds, it goes to Donate.

#### 2. Categories I’m deciding for you
I’ll structure your closet around these standard groups:

1. **Everyday tops** (T‑shirts, casual shirts, blouses)
2. **Everyday bottoms** (jeans, casual pants, shorts, skirts)
3. **Work / smart clothes** (button‑downs, blazers, dress pants, pencil skirts)
4. **Outerwear** (coats, jackets, hoodies)
5. **Occasion / formal** (dresses, suits, dress shirts, ties)
6. **Loungewear / athleisure**
7. **Activewear** (gym clothes, leggings, sports bras)
8. **Underwear / socks**
9. **Sleepwear**
10. **Shoes**
11. **Accessories** (belts, scarves, hats, bags)

Sort your “Keep” and “Seasonal” piles into these categories on the bed or floor.

#### 3. Layout I’m assigning
I’ll assume a standard reach‑in closet with:
- One main hanging rod
- A shelf above
- Floor space below
- Optional side walls

If your closet is larger, just expand each area; the logic still holds.

**Hanging rod (priority items):**
- Left → Right:
  1. Outerwear
  2. Work / smart clothes
  3. Occasion / formal
  4. Everyday tops (shirts, blouses, then light sweaters)
- Use the same hanger type for everything you hang. If you don’t have matching hangers, just standardize by material: all plastic for everyday, all wooden for heavy items.

**Folded items (shelves / bins):**
- **Top shelf:**
  - Seasonal off‑rotation (in 1–2 labeled bins: “Off‑Season Tops”, “Off‑Season Bottoms/Outer”).
- **Eye‑level shelf or most accessible area:**
  - Everyday bottoms: stacked by type (jeans in one stack, other pants in another).
  - Sweaters that stretch on hangers: folded and stacked.
- **Lower shelf / lower bins:**
  - Loungewear / athleisure in one bin.
  - Activewear in another (rolled or file‑folded so you can see everything).

**Floor space:**
- Left: shoes you use weekly on a rack or in a single row.
- Back/center: rarely used shoes in boxes (stacked, labels facing outward).
- Right: 1 laundry basket/hamper.

**Drawers or small bins (if no drawers, use small boxes):**
- Drawer/bin 1: Underwear
- Drawer/bin 2: Socks
- Drawer/bin 3: Sleepwear
- Drawer/bin 4: Accessories (sub‑group with smaller boxes inside if needed: belts, scarves, small bags).

#### 4. Exact organizing rules
- Hang:
  - Blazers, dress shirts, dresses, coats, jackets, structured tops, easily wrinkled fabrics.
- Fold:
  - T‑shirts, knits, sweaters, jeans, workout wear, loungewear.

Inside each category:
- Order by **type** first (e.g., T‑shirts → button‑downs → blouses).
- Then by **color** from dark to light or light to dark; I’ll choose **dark → light** for you:
  - Black → navy → dark colors → medium → light → white.

#### 5. Quick labeling
Use whatever you have (tape + pen, sticky notes, or just mental labels), but I’ll define the zones:

- Shelf labels:
  - “Jeans & Pants”
  - “Sweaters”
  - “Lounge”
  - “Workout”
  - “Off‑Season Tops”
  - “Off‑Season Bottoms/Outer”
- Bin labels:
  - “Underwear”
  - “Socks”
  - “Sleep”
  - “Accessories”

#### 6. Maintenance routine I’m imposing
- **Weekly (5 minutes):**
  - Return stray items to their category zones.
  - Empty hamper into laundry area.
- **Monthly (10–15 minutes):**
  - Pull out 5 items you haven’t worn that month.
  - Decide: wear in the coming week or move to Donate.

Start by emptying one side of the closet and working through the declutter and recategorization; then move to the other side. Follow the order above as written so you don’t have to decide what to do next at any point.

5. Underwear, socks, sleepwear, accessories into drawers/bins in the fixed order already chosen.

You can follow this as a checklist from top to bottom and the closet will end up fully zoned without you needing to redesign anything.
