- **Inbox** – only new, unprocessed mail.

- **Waiting** – things you’re waiting on from others.
- **Read/Review** – newsletters, articles, FYIs.
- **Archive** – everything else you’ve already handled but might need later.

2. Automatic categories
I’ll route incoming mail into these categories with filters/rules:
- **Work / Projects** – anything from your work domain and key collaborators.
- **Finance & Receipts** – banks, invoices, subscription receipts.
- **Travel & Bookings** – flights, hotels, tickets.
- **Newsletters & Promotions** – mailing lists, ads, marketing blasts.
- **Personal** – friends, family, and personal accounts.

I’ll:
- Auto-archive low-importance notifications (social media alerts, login alerts, etc.) so they skip the inbox but remain searchable.
- Send **Newsletters & Promotions** straight to **Read/Review**, marked as read after 7 days if untouched.
- Route receipts and order confirmations into **Finance & Receipts** and **Archive** after 30 days.
- Push anything from your work domain and known important contacts into **Inbox** and, if needed, **Action**.

4. Daily workflow
- Inbox becomes just a **triage zone**:
  - If it takes <2 minutes, handle it now.
  - If it needs time, move to **Action**.
  - If you’re waiting on someone else, move to **Waiting**.
  - If it’s just info, move to **Read/Review**.
  - When done, **Archive**.

For everything currently in your inbox, I’ll:
- Bulk-group by sender and subject patterns.
- Move obvious promos/newsletters to **Read/Review**.
- Move old, no-longer-relevant stuff directly to **Archive**.
- Leave only the last ~30 days of likely-actionable mail in **Inbox/Action**.

I’ll assume you’re using a mainstream provider (like Gmail or Outlook) and design the rules accordingly so this structure works immediately.
