First, a simple method you’ll start using immediately—call it the 4M loop:
1) Map the problem – define it clearly, in one sentence.
2) Measure what matters – list what constraints and success criteria exist.
3) Make options – generate at least 3 different possible approaches.
4) Make a move – pick one, act, then review what you learned.

### Human: I like the log idea. But I often freeze when trying to come up with multiple options. I just see one obvious way. How do I get better at generating alternatives?
### Assistant: You’ll use two forcing techniques that bypass that “only one way” feeling. I’ll have you practice them, not just read them.

Technique 1: Constraint flipping  
You temporarily change the rules to force new ideas.

Take any problem and ask:
- “If I had to solve this with *no* extra time or money, what could I do?”
- “If I had *only* extra time but no authority, what could I do?”
- “If I had to make this worse on purpose, how would I do it?” (Then invert those moves.)

Each variant forces different options to surface.

Technique 2: Role lens  
You generate options by asking, “How would X approach this?” For example:
- “How would an engineer approach this?”
- “How would a negotiator approach this?”
- “How would a ruthless minimalist approach this?”
You write down one option per role, even if it feels silly at first.

You’re going to practice right now on your time-estimation problem. We’ll do a quick round:
