### Assistant: Absolutely, I’ll keep it concise and focused just on cabinet painting. I’ll assume you’re doing it over several days, but you can group steps as needed.

**Cabinet Painting Checklist (Warm Modern, Greige)**

1. **Prep & Label**
   - Take clear photos of cabinet layout.  
   - Label each door and its matching location with tape (numbering system).  
   - Remove doors, drawers, and hardware. Store screws/hinges in labeled bags.

2. **Clean**
   - Wipe all surfaces (doors, drawer fronts, cabinet frames) with a degreaser.  
   - Let dry completely.

3. **Sand**
   - Lightly sand with fine-grit (e.g., 220) to scuff the surface.  
   - Wipe or vacuum dust thoroughly.

4. **Protect Surroundings**
   - Tape off walls, counters, and inside cabinet edges as needed.  
   - Lay down drop cloths.

5. **Prime**
   - Stir primer well.  
   - Apply a thin, even coat to doors, drawers, and frames.  
   - Let dry fully per product instructions.  
   - Lightly sand any rough spots and wipe clean.

6. **Paint – First Coat**
   - Stir paint thoroughly.  
   - Use a brush for grooves/edges, a small foam roller for flat areas.  
   - Apply a thin, even coat. Avoid heavy build-up on edges.  
   - Let dry completely.

7. **Paint – Second Coat (and Third if Needed)**
   - Lightly sand any imperfections between coats; wipe dust.  
   - Apply second coat.  
   - Check coverage; if needed, apply a third thin coat after drying.

8. **Cure**
   - Let paint cure as recommended (often at least 24–48 hours before handling much).  
   - Avoid stacking or leaning doors tightly together while curing.

9. **Reassemble**
   - Carefully reattach hinges and doors, following your labels.  
   - Install new hardware (using a template for consistent placement).

These are the essential steps; no extras. If you’d like, I can also suggest a simple labeling system and a way to batch the work (e.g., uppers vs. lowers on different days), but this should be enough to get you through it cleanly.
