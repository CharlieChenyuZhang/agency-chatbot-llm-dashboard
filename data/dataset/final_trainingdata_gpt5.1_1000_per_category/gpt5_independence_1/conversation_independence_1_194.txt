## 1. Hook (1–2 lines)

Goal: stop the scroll for B2B SaaS founders.

Use one of these hook patterns every time:

- **Pattern A – Call out + mistake:**  
  - “Early-stage B2B SaaS founders: your positioning is probably slowing you down.”
- **Pattern B – Result + curiosity:**  
  - “We changed 17 words on a SaaS site and demo conversions jumped 32% in 30 days.”
- **Pattern C – Hot take:**  
  - “‘We help companies grow’ is not positioning. It’s background noise.”
- **Pattern D – Direct benefit:**  
  - “Here’s a 10-minute exercise to fix your homepage headline this week.”

Pick 1 pattern and write 1–2 lines. That’s it.

---

## 2. Context (1–3 lines)

Goal: show them you understand their world.

Use:

- “Here’s what I keep seeing…”  
- “If you’re under $2M ARR, this is probably happening:”  
- “Most SaaS founders I talk to are dealing with this:”

Example:  
“Most founders I work with have decent products, but their websites read like they’re selling to everyone and no one at the same time.”

---

## 3. Pain / Stakes (2–4 lines)

Goal: make the problem feel expensive and urgent.

Use:

- “The cost of this is bigger than ‘low conversion rates.’ It shows up as…”  
- Bullet 2–3 concrete symptoms:
  - Unqualified demos
  - Longer sales cycles
  - Price sensitivity

Example:  
“And the cost isn’t just fewer demo requests.  
You attract the wrong buyers.  
You get hammered on price.  
And your ‘maybe later’ pipeline keeps growing while revenue doesn’t.”

---

## 4. Insight / Shift (2–4 lines)

Goal: introduce your core idea: a new way of seeing the problem.

Use:

- “The real issue isn’t [surface problem]. It’s [root problem].”  
- “Once you stop doing X and start doing Y, things change.”

Example:  
“The real issue usually isn’t traffic or leads.  
It’s that your positioning is fuzzy.  
If buyers can’t quickly answer ‘Is this for me?’ they just leave.”

---

## 5. Steps / Example (3–7 bullets or short paragraphs)

Goal: make the insight actionable. This is where you teach.

Pick one of these formats:

- **Format A – Step-by-step**
  1. Identify your best-fit customer in one sentence
  2. Write down their #1 painful problem in their own words
  3. Describe the outcome they want in one clear sentence
  4. Combine into a simple positioning line

- **Format B – Before / After examples**
  - Before: “Analytics for modern teams.”  
    After: “Product analytics for B2B SaaS teams who care about expansion revenue, not vanity signups.”
  - Before: “The all-in-one HR platform.”  
    After: “HR operations for remote-first teams who are done with spreadsheet chaos.”

- **Format C – Checklist**
  - “You’re clear enough when:
    - A founder in your ICP can say ‘This is for me’ in 5 seconds.
    - Your sales calls start with specific problems, not ‘So, what do you do?’
    - You can explain what you do without using the word ‘platform.’”

Always choose ONE format per post.

---

## 6. Objection / Caveat (1–3 lines)

Goal: handle the “yeah, but…” in their heads.

Use one of these lines:

- “This doesn’t mean you’ll be stuck in this niche forever.”  
- “No, this isn’t about clever copy. It only works if the product is actually good.”  
- “You can still sell to other segments; you’re just *speaking* to one clearly.”

Example:  
“And no, this doesn’t mean you’re locked into this niche forever.  
It means you’re choosing where your strongest story lives *right now*.”

---

## 7. Close / CTA (1–2 lines)

Goal: tell them what to do or how to engage.

Rotate between:

- **Engagement CTAs** (for reach):  
  - “If this hit a nerve, comment ‘POSITIONING’ and I’ll send you the template.”  
  - “Save this for your next website rewrite.”
- **Action CTAs** (for implementation):  
  - “Block 30 minutes today, open your homepage, and run it through this checklist.”
- **Lead CTAs** (light touch):  
  - “If your positioning feels like it could describe 10 other SaaS products, it’s worth fixing. That’s what I help founders do.”
