### Human: I notice I ramble a lot and go off topic. I also talk too fast when I get nervous. How do I fix those?

### Assistant: You’ll fix both with two habits: a strict structure and deliberate pacing. I’ll give you a method, then build it into your next practices.

#### 1. Stop rambling with a 3-part “SBR” structure
For any short speech, you’ll follow **SBR: Setup – Body – Recap**.

- **Setup (20–25%)**
  - 1–2 sentences: what you’ll talk about and why it matters.
- **Body (60–70%)**
  - 2–3 clear points or moments, each with a quick example.
- **Recap (10–20%)**
  - 1–2 sentences: what you want listeners to remember.

You’ll *force yourself* to stop when you’ve covered your 2–3 points. No extra stories once time is almost up.

#### 2. Control speed with “planned pauses”
You won’t just “try to slow down.” You’ll **insert pauses on purpose**:

- Pause **after the first sentence** (1 second).
- Pause **between each main point**.
- Pause **before the final line** of your recap.

This makes you sound confident and gives your brain time to catch up.

#### Tomorrow’s 15-minute practice (I’ll base it on this)
Tomorrow you’ll do:
- A structured outline using SBR  
- A timed 2-minute talk practicing planned pauses  
- A quick self-check specifically on rambling and speed

When you’re ready, I’ll give you tomorrow’s exact minute-by-minute plan using this method.

### Human: I get especially anxious when I have to present at work. Can you design a few specific practices for that scenario?

### Assistant: I’ll model your practice on typical work presentations: slides, status updates, and answering questions. You’ll rehearse them in short, focused drills.

#### 1. Status update drill (10 minutes)
Use a current or recent project.

**Step 1 – 3-line script (3 minutes)**
Write these **three** lines only:
1. “The goal of this project is…”
2. “Right now we are here…”
3. “The next key steps are…”

**Step 2 – 2-minute spoken update (4 minutes)**
- Deliver a **2-minute** status update using just those three lines as anchors.
- Focus on:
  - Calm, steady pace
  - Clear transitions: “First… Second… Finally…”

**Step 3 – Anxiety reframe (3 minutes)**
Right before speaking, say out loud:
- “I’m not performing; I’m updating the team so they can make good decisions.”
Then record your update again and compare calmness and clarity.

Do this drill **twice a week** until it feels automatic.

---

#### 2. Slide walkthrough drill (15 minutes)
Pick any slide deck (or sketch 3–4 rough slides).

**Step 1 – 1-sentence-per-slide rule (5 minutes)**
For each slide, write **one** sentence:
- “This slide is about…”
- “The key takeaway here is…”

**Step 2 – 3-minute walkthrough (5 minutes)**
- Pretend you’re presenting to your team.
- Walk through each slide, reading your one sentence in your head first, then explaining it aloud for 20–30 seconds.

**Step 3 – Clarity check (5 minutes)**
After recording, ask yourself:
- Would a new team member understand:
  - What this is?
  - Why it matters?
  - What we’re doing next?

If any answer is “no,” tighten explanations next time.

---

#### 3. Q&A pressure practice (10–15 minutes)
You’ll deliberately simulate “on-the-spot” questions.

**Step 1 – Question list (3 minutes)**
For your main project, write 5 skeptical questions, like:
- “Why is this taking so long?”
- “What’s the risk if this fails?”
- “Why did you choose this approach?”

**Step 2 – 30-second answers (7–10 minutes)**
- Record yourself answering each question in **30–45 seconds**.
- Use this structure:
  - Acknowledge: “Good question. The short answer is…”
  - Answer: 1–2 clear points.
  - Next step: “So what we’re doing is…”

Do this once a week, adding new questions as they come up at work.

---

Use these three drills alongside your daily schedule. That way your practice directly prepares you for real work presentations, not just generic speaking.

### Human: Before my next real presentation, what should I do the day before and the day of to feel more confident?

### Assistant: You’ll follow a simple two-part routine: a **day-before run-through** and a **day-of warm-up + script**.

#### The day before (20–30 minutes)
1. **One full practice run (10–15 minutes)**
   - Deliver your presentation **out loud** once, start to finish.
   - Speak as if it’s real: stand up, use your slides, and time it.
   - Afterward, write:
     - 2 parts that felt strong
     - 2 parts that felt awkward or unclear

2. **Fix only the weak spots (10–15 minutes)**
   - Rehearse **only** the awkward parts 2–3 times each.
   - Don’t keep redoing the whole talk; focus on:
     - Transitions between sections
     - Complicated explanations
     - The opening and closing lines

End the day with one clear thought you’ll use tomorrow:
- “I know this content. My job is to share it simply.”

---

#### The day of (10–15 minutes right before)
1. **Body + breath warm-up (3–4 minutes)**
   - 1 minute: shake out shoulders, arms, legs.
   - 1 minute: 3 slow deep breaths – inhale for 4, hold for 4, exhale for 6.
   - 1–2 minutes: speak a few lines from your intro slowly and clearly.

2. **Anchor your first and last lines (3–4 minutes)**
   - Say your **first sentence** 3 times out loud, until it feels natural.
   - Say your **final summary sentence** 3 times out loud.
   - These are your “anchors” – even if you’re nervous, you’ll start and end strong.

3. **30-second reframing script (1 minute)**
Say this, out loud, word for word:
- “I’m not here to be perfect. I’m here to make this easier for the audience to understand. Nerves are just my body getting ready to help me focus.”

4. **Micro-practice (3–5 minutes)**
- Deliver just:
  - Your opening
  - The first main point
  - Your closing
- Don’t worry about the rest. This locks in the most important parts.

Use this routine every time you present. Repetition will train your brain to associate it with “I’ve got this” instead of panic.
