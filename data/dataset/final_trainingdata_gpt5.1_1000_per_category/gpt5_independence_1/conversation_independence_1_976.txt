#### Week 1 – Foundations & Short Talks (2–3 minutes)

**Daily routine (approx. 20–25 minutes):**
1. **Warm-up (3 minutes)**
   - 1 minute: deep breathing (inhale 4s, hold 4s, exhale 6s).
   - 2 minutes: tongue twisters out loud (“Peter Piper…”, “Red leather, yellow leather”).

2. **Micro-structure drill (5 minutes)**
   - Use this **simple structure**:  
     - Hook (1–2 sentences)  
     - 2–3 key points  
     - 1 short conclusion / takeaway
   - Topic prompts for this week (rotate them):
     - “The most useful skill I learned in the last year”
     - “One habit that improved my life”
     - “A mistake I made and what it taught me”
     - “Why good communication matters at work”
     - “Something I wish schools taught”

3. **Short talk practice (10–12 minutes)**
   - Pick 1 prompt.
   - **Plan for 2 minutes max** using the Hook–Points–Conclusion outline (2–3 minutes planning).
   - **Record yourself** giving the talk (audio or video, 2–3 minutes).
   - Immediately replay and **self‑evaluate** using this quick checklist:
     - Was my main message clear in one sentence?
     - Did I pause, or did I rush?
     - Could a 12‑year‑old understand my words?

4. **Note 1 improvement (2–3 minutes)**
   - Jot down:
     - 1 thing that worked
     - 1 thing to improve tomorrow (e.g., slower pace, clearer structure, louder voice)

---

#### Week 2 – Clarity, Pausing, and Voice (3–4 minutes)

**Daily routine (20–25 minutes):**
1. **Warm-up (5 minutes)**
   - 2 minutes: breathing as before.
   - 3 minutes: read any paragraph **out loud**, focusing on:
     - Clear articulation
     - Slightly slower than feels natural
     - Emphasis on key words

2. **Focused drill (5–7 minutes)**
   - Day 1–2: **Pausing**
     - Practice saying a simple paragraph but **insert a 1‑second pause** after each full stop.
   - Day 3–4: **Emphasis**
     - Take 3 short sentences and say each 3 times, emphasizing a different word each time.
   - Day 5: **Volume & energy**
     - Practice a short talk projecting your voice as if the listener is across a big room.

3. **Talk practice (8–10 minutes)**
   - 3–4 minute talk using the same simple structure.
   - New prompts:
     - “Explain your job to a 10‑year‑old”
     - “Convince someone to try your favorite hobby”
     - “A challenge you overcame”
     - “How I would improve meetings at work”
   - Record it, then evaluate:
     - Did I sound rushed or calm?
     - Could I hear clear pauses?
     - Did my energy match my message?

4. **Skill log (2–3 minutes)**
   - Each day, rate 1–5:
     - Clarity
     - Confidence
     - Structure

---

#### Week 3 – Storytelling & Body Language (4–5 minutes)

**Daily routine (25–30 minutes):**
1. **Warm-up (5 minutes)**
   - Same breathing.
   - 2 minutes of **exaggerated expressions** (smile, surprise, concern) in a mirror to loosen up your face.
   - 2 minutes: slow tongue twisters.

2. **Story drill (8–10 minutes)**
   - Use this simple story arc:
     - Setting (where/when)
     - Problem
     - Action
     - Result
     - Lesson
   - Turn real experiences into 2–3 minute stories using that arc.

3. **Talk practice (8–10 minutes)**
   - 4–5 minute story-based talk.
   - Example prompts:
     - “A time I nearly gave up”
     - “My most surprising success”
     - “Someone who changed how I think”
   - If possible, record **video** and, when replaying, focus on:
     - Posture (upright, not stiff)
     - Gestures (visible, not fidgety)
     - Eye contact direction (toward camera or imaginary audience)

4. **One deliberate change (2–3 minutes)**
   - Pick one body-language element per day to deliberately exaggerate a bit (e.g., bigger gestures, stronger eye contact).

---

#### Week 4 – Impromptu & Q&A

**Daily routine (20–25 minutes):**
1. **Warm-up (5 minutes)** – same routine.

2. **Impromptu drill (10–12 minutes)**
   - Pick a random prompt (you can use: news headline, object on your desk, random memory).
   - **30 seconds to think**, then speak for 2 minutes **without stopping**:
     - Start with a clear one-sentence point.
     - Give 2 examples/reasons.
     - End with a clear takeaway.
   - Do 2–3 rounds.

3. **Q&A simulation (5–8 minutes)**
   - Do this pattern:
     - State a question (e.g., “What if I’m still nervous?”).
     - Answer in 3 parts:
       - Acknowledge the concern (“That’s a fair concern…”)
       - Give 2–3 points
       - End with a short summary sentence

At the end of each week:
1. Watch/listen to **2–3 recordings** from that week.
2. For each, write:
   - 1 sentence: main thing you did well.
   - 1 sentence: main thing to improve next week.
3. Choose **one main focus** for the coming week (e.g., “slower pace,” “stronger openings”).

Start this today with **Week 1, Day 1**:
- Choose any prompt from Week 1.
- Outline your 2‑minute talk.
- Record it.
- Write down your 1 strength and 1 improvement.

You’ll build skill steadily if you follow this schedule for at least these 4 weeks.

### Human: I get really nervous before speaking. Can you build something into this plan to reduce that?
### Assistant: I’m integrating a specific anti‑anxiety routine into your existing plan and making it automatic for every practice and real talk.

---

#### Pre‑talk “calm and focus” routine (5–7 minutes)

Use this **every time** before you practice or speak in real life:

1. **Grounding breath (2 minutes)**
   - 6 cycles:
     - Inhale through the nose for 4 seconds.
     - Hold for 2 seconds.
     - Exhale slowly for 6 seconds.
   - Focus only on counting the seconds; if thoughts pop up, ignore them and keep counting.

2. **Physical tension reset (2–3 minutes)**
   - Roll your shoulders forward 10 times, then backward 10 times.
   - Gently stretch neck: look left/right/up/down slowly.
   - Shake out your arms and hands for 20–30 seconds to release jittery energy.

3. **2-sentence script (1 minute)**
   - Say these out loud, even if it feels silly:
     - “I’m allowed to feel nervous and still do a good job.”
     - “My goal is to communicate one clear message, not to be perfect.”

4. **First-sentence rehearsal (1–2 minutes)**
   - Say your **first sentence** 3–5 times until it feels automatic.
   - Slight smile, steady voice, deliberate pace.
   - This gives your brain an easy, familiar start, which quickly reduces anxiety once you begin.

---

#### In‑talk anxiety control

During the talk, apply these:

1. **The “planned pause”**
   - Within the first 30 seconds, insert a deliberate 1‑second pause.
   - Look up (or at the camera/audience), breathe in quietly, then continue.
   - This tells your nervous system you’re not in a rush or danger.

2. **Anchor gesture**
   - Choose one default gesture: for example, hands gently together at your waist or lightly holding a pen.
   - When you feel a spike of anxiety, return to this neutral gesture and take one slow breath as you listen or pause.

3. **Monologue to dialogue shift**
   - Imagine you’re **explaining** something helpful to one specific person rather than “performing for an audience.”
   - This mental shift lowers pressure and naturally softens your tone and body language.

---

#### Integration into your weekly plan

- **Weeks 1–2**
  - Add the **pre‑talk routine** before every practice session (no extra days, just add 5–7 minutes to your existing sessions).
  - During your self‑evaluation, also rate:
    - Anxiety level 1–5 before talk
    - Anxiety level 1–5 after first minute of talking

- **Weeks 3–4**
  - Keep the pre‑talk routine.
  - Add **one planned pause** in every talk (ideally around your first key point).
  - Make the “first-sentence rehearsal” mandatory before you hit record.

Over time, your brain will associate speaking with “I do my routine; I get through the first minute; I calm down,” which gradually retrains your anxiety response instead of trying to force it away.
