**Title:** 5 Common Plumbing Emergencies and What You Can Do Before Calling a Plumber

**Intro**

Plumbing problems always seem to happen at the worst possible time—late at night, right before guests arrive, or in the middle of a busy workday. While many issues require a professional plumber, there are simple steps you can take right away to reduce damage and stay safe until help arrives. In this article, we’ll walk through five common plumbing emergencies and what you can safely do before calling a professional.

---

**1. Burst Pipe or Heavy Leak**

If you see water spraying or quickly pooling on the floor, you may have a burst pipe or major leak. This situation can cause serious damage to walls, floors, and belongings if it’s not handled quickly.

What you can do:

- Find your main water shut-off valve and turn it off to stop the flow of water.  
- If it’s safe, unplug nearby electrical items and move furniture or valuables out of the affected area.  
- Use towels, buckets, or a mop to control the water as best you can and prevent it from spreading.

After you’ve stopped the flow of water, call a professional plumber right away. The sooner a plumber can inspect and repair the issue, the more likely you are to avoid expensive long-term damage.

---

**2. Overflowing Toilet**

An overflowing toilet is messy, stressful, and something most homeowners face at some point. Fortunately, there are a few quick actions you can take to keep things under control.

What you can do:

- Lift the tank lid and push the flapper (the rubber piece at the bottom) down to stop water from flowing into the bowl.  
- Turn off the water supply valve behind or beside the toilet to stop the water entirely.  
- Use a plunger to try to clear the blockage. Make sure there is enough water in the bowl to cover the plunger for a better seal.

If the toilet continues to overflow or you can’t clear the clog, avoid flushing again. At that point, it’s best to call a plumber to prevent a bigger mess and potential water damage.

---

**3. Clogged or Slow-Draining Sink**

A sink that won’t drain or drains very slowly is a common problem in kitchens and bathrooms. It can be caused by food, grease, hair, or other debris building up in the pipes.

What you can do:

- Remove any visible debris from the drain opening.  
- Avoid using chemical drain cleaners, as they can damage pipes over time.  
- Try a plunger or a simple drain snake to break up the clog.  
- Run hot (not boiling) water down the drain for a few minutes to help clear grease buildup.

If the sink remains clogged or backs up repeatedly, there may be a deeper issue in the plumbing system that requires professional attention.

---

**4. No Hot Water**

Losing hot water can be frustrating, especially in the middle of a shower. The cause can range from a tripped breaker to a problem with the water heater itself.

What you can do:

- Check other hot water taps in the house to see if the issue is isolated or affects the entire home.  
- Look at your electrical panel to see if a breaker for the water heater has tripped and reset it if needed.  
- If you have a gas water heater, make sure the pilot light is on, but do not attempt repairs if you smell gas or feel unsure.

If you still have no hot water after these checks, call a professional plumber or water heater specialist. Water heater repairs can be dangerous if handled incorrectly.

---

**5. Sudden Drop in Water Pressure**

A sudden drop in water pressure—especially if it affects the whole house—can be a sign of a leak, buildup in the pipes, or an issue with the local water supply.

What you can do:

- Test multiple faucets to see if the problem is isolated or affects the entire home.  
- Check for visible leaks around sinks, toilets, and appliances.  
- If only one fixture is affected, remove and clean the aerator (the small screen at the end of the faucet).

If the pressure drop is sudden and widespread, or you find signs of a leak, call a plumber to inspect the system. Ignoring the issue can lead to bigger problems later.
