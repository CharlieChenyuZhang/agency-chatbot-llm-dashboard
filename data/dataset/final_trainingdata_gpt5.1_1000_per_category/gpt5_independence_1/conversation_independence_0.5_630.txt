**Part B – Derivative as limit of average rate**

Take s(t) = t².

1) Average speed from t = 1 to t = 2:  
   s(1) = 1² = 1  
   s(2) = 2² = 4  
   Average speed = (4 − 1) / (2 − 1) = 3

2) Average speed from t = 1 to t = 1.1:  
   s(1.1) = (1.1)² = 1.21  
   Average = (1.21 − 1) / (1.1 − 1) = 0.21 / 0.1 = 2.1

3) Average speed from t = 1 to t = 1.01:  
   s(1.01) = (1.01)² = 1.0201  
   Average = (1.0201 − 1) / (1.01 − 1) = 0.0201 / 0.01 = 2.01

As the time interval shrinks towards 0, the average speeds 3, 2.1, 2.01,… are getting closer to 2. So the **instantaneous speed at t = 1** is 2. That’s the derivative s′(1).

We could formally write:
- s′(1) = lim\_{h→0} [(1 + h)² − 1²] / h = 2.

**Key idea:** the derivative is “what the average rate of change is trying to become” as the interval gets tiny.

**Part C – Vectors**

Let v = (2, 1), w = (−1, 2).

1) v + w = (2 + (−1), 1 + 2) = (1, 3)  
   Geometrically: you place w’s tail at v’s head; the sum is the arrow from origin to the new head.

2) 2v = (2·2, 2·1) = (4, 2)  
   Geometrically: same direction as v, twice as long.

3) Dot product v · w = 2·(−1) + 1·2 = −2 + 2 = 0
