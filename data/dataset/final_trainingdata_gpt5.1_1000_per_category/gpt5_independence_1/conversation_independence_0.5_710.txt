1. **Basic prevention (I’d do these by default):**
   - **Mulch:** After your plants are in, add a **2–3 inch layer of mulch** (shredded leaves, straw, or store-bought mulch) around plants. This helps keep soil moist and can reduce some pests.
   - **Healthy spacing:** Stick to the spacing we discussed so air can move; cramped, wet plants attract disease.

2. **What to watch for (common early problems):**
   - **Chewed leaves / holes:** Often caterpillars or beetles.
   - **Tiny bugs on leaves (especially undersides):** Aphids, whiteflies.
   - **Wilting even when soil is moist:** Could be root issues or disease.
   - **Fine webbing:** Spider mites.

3. **Low-impact responses (organic-friendly):**
   - **Hand removal:** If you see a few caterpillars or beetles, just pick them off and drop them in soapy water.
   - **Strong water spray:** For light aphid infestations, a firm spray with the hose can knock many off.
   - **Neem oil or insecticidal soap:**  
     - Both are common organic options for aphids, whiteflies, etc.
     - You spray them on the leaves (especially undersides) in the **evening** or early morning.
     - Always test on a small part of the plant first and follow the label.

4. **Physical barriers (especially for cucumbers and greens):**
   - **Row cover fabric:** A light fabric you drape over hoops to physically block many pests while letting light and water through.

### Assistant: Here’s a simple, Austin-friendly cheat sheet you can screenshot or jot down:

1. **Small green/black bugs clustered on stems or leaf undersides (aphids):**
   - **Do first:** Blast them off with a strong spray of water.
   - **If they keep coming back:** Use **insecticidal soap** or **neem oil** every 5–7 days until under control.

2. **Big chunks missing from leaves, often with little black droppings (caterpillars):**
   - **Do:** Inspect leaves carefully, especially undersides, and **hand-pick** caterpillars. Drop them into soapy water.
   - **If severe:** Neem oil can help, but hand-picking is usually enough for a small garden.

3. **Leaves look “skeletonized” (veins left, tissue eaten), especially on cucumbers/greens:**
   - Likely beetles or caterpillars.
   - **Do:** Hand-pick any beetles you see. Consider **row cover** over young plants.
   - Use neem or insecticidal soap if beetles are widespread.

4. **Sticky leaves, ants crawling on plants:**
   - Often aphids or other sap-sucking insects producing honeydew.
   - **Do:** Treat as aphids (water spray, then insecticidal soap/neem if needed).
   - Ants often farm aphids; controlling aphids reduces ants.

5. **Fine webbing + tiny dots on leaves, often in hot, dry weather (spider mites):**
   - **Do:** Spray underside of leaves with water regularly to knock them off and raise humidity.
   - Follow up with neem oil if it stays bad.

6. **Sudden wilting of one plant, stem near soil looks dark or mushy:**
   - Could be root rot or disease.
   - **Do:** Remove the worst-affected plant to protect others. Avoid overwatering and splashing soil on leaves.
