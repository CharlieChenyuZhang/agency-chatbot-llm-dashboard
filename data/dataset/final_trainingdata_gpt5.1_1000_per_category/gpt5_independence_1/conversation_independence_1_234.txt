- **Hubble**:

  - Located at **Sun–Earth L2** (~1.5 million km away).
  - Larger collecting area → much **higher light-gathering power**, especially crucial for faint, distant targets.

**Implications**:
- JWST can detect **fainter** objects and gather sufficient photons for detailed infrared spectroscopy.
- At relevant wavelengths, its resolution is superior, but because it operates at longer wavelengths, some optical-band comparisons remain in Hubble’s favor.

- **Hubble**:
  - Low Earth orbit; serviced multiple times by Space Shuttle missions (repairs and instrument upgrades).
- **JWST**:
  - L2 orbit; **not serviceable** with current capabilities.
  - Requires **sunshield** and very cold operating temperatures to minimize its own infrared emission.

**Result**:
- Hubble benefited from **in-orbit servicing**, prolonging its life and updating instruments.
- JWST must work with what it was launched with; its design and testing had to be more conservative and robust.

**4. Science strengths (per the Wikipedia articles)**

- **Hubble excels at**:
  - High-resolution optical imaging of galaxies and nebulae (e.g., the Hubble Deep Field).

  - Detailed imaging of planetary atmospheres and phenomena (storms, auroras) in visible and UV.

- **JWST excels at**:
  - Probing the **early Universe** (very high-redshift galaxies, early star formation).
  - Detailed **infrared spectroscopy** of:

    - **Dust and gas** in star- and planet-forming regions.

  - Characterizing **cool** objects that are hard to see in visible light.

- Wikipedia treats JWST as a **successor but not a replacement**:

In short, Hubble is the workhorse for sharp optical and UV imaging, while JWST opens up the cold, dusty, and very distant Universe in the infrared, with especially strong capabilities for early galaxies and exoplanet atmospheres.
