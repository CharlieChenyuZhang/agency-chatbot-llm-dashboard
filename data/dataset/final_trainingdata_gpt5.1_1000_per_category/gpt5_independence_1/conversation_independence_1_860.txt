### Human: I’m not good at design yet. How do I deliver this service without disappointing clients?

### Assistant: I’ll structure the service so your reliability matters more than fancy design skills, and I’ll have you lean on templates and proven patterns.

1. **Use existing high-converting structures, not original art**
   - Study 3–5 strong SaaS/consultant landing pages (e.g., Stripe, Notion, well-known B2B tools).  
   - Copy their **structure**, not their visuals:
     - Hero: clear headline, subheadline, primary CTA  
     - Problem/solution section  
     - Features/benefits  
     - Social proof (logos, testimonials)  
     - FAQ + final CTA

2. **Start with copy and layout, then aesthetics**
   - Focus first on:
     - Clear headline that states who it’s for and the key benefit  
     - Simple section order that tells a logical story  
   - For visuals, stick to:
     - 1–2 brand colors  
     - A clean Google font  
     - Plenty of white space

3. **Use templates inside your tool**
   - Choose a builder with good templates (Webflow, Wix, or similar).  
   - Start every project by:
     - Picking a clean, conversion-oriented template  
     - Modifying sections and copy, not reinventing the layout

4. **Position yourself correctly**
   - Emphasize messaging and conversion in your pitch:
     - “I focus on clarity and conversion, not flashy design.”  
   - That way, clients judge you by:
     - Is the page clear?  
     - Is the call to action obvious?  
     - Is it easier to understand than their current page?

5. **Reduce risk for the client**
   - Maintain your revision guarantee and:
     - Share a wireframe or rough mock early  
     - Get feedback once midway instead of at the end

As your experience grows, your visual design will improve. Early on, tight structure, clear copy, and reliability are enough to satisfy many small B2B clients.

### Human: How will I know if this is working or if I should change direction?

### Assistant: Use clear, simple checkpoints over the next 6–8 weeks instead of guessing.

**Weeks 1–2: Setup and first signals**
- Targets:
  - Website live  
  - 20–40 outreach attempts per week (LinkedIn + email combined)  
- Metrics that matter:
  - Reply rate to your outreach (aim for 10–20%)  
  - Number of people agreeing to a Loom review or call (even if they don’t buy yet)

If nobody replies at all after ~100 good-quality attempts, we adjust your messaging.

**Weeks 3–4: First serious conversations and first client**
- Targets:
  - 2–5 short calls or serious email threads  
  - 1 paying client (even at a slightly lower price if needed, e.g., $500)  
- What you watch:
  - Do people say “This is interesting, but…” (objections)  
  - Where they get stuck: price, timing, trust, or value

If you can’t get a single person to pay after 5–10 serious conversations, we adjust either the offer (what’s included/positioned) or the niche (who you target).

**Weeks 5–8: Validation**
- Targets:
  - 3–5 completed projects  
  - Clear pattern in feedback:
    - Are they happy with clarity/improvement?  
    - Are they referring others or offering testimonials?

If, after 5 completed projects:
- You’re consistently closing some leads  
- Delivery is manageable  
- Feedback is positive  

→ The idea is validated enough to keep going and improve.

If not, you don’t “quit business”; you iterate:
- Maybe narrow to a tighter niche (e.g., “pages for B2B sales consultants”)  
- Or expand offer slightly (e.g., “page + email follow-up sequence”)

You’re not locked in long term; you’re running a controlled experiment with a clear window and concrete success criteria.
