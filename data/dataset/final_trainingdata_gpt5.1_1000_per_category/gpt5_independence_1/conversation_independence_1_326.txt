**Recipe 1: Garlic Butter Pasta with Broccoli**  
Category: 30-Minute Dinners, One-Pan

**Serves:** 2–3  
**Time:** 25 minutes

**Ingredients:**
- 200 g (about 7 oz) dried pasta (spaghetti, penne, or whatever you have)  
- 2 cups small broccoli florets (fresh or frozen)  
- 3 tbsp butter  
- 2 tbsp olive oil (or any cooking oil)  
- 3–4 cloves garlic, finely chopped (or 1–1.5 tsp pre-minced)  
- ½ tsp salt, plus more to taste  
- ¼ tsp black pepper  
- ¼ cup grated Parmesan or any hard cheese (optional but great)  
- ½ cup pasta cooking water (you’ll scoop it from the pot)

**Instructions:**
1. **Boil the pasta**  
   - Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Add a small handful of salt (the water should taste a bit like the sea).  
   - Add the pasta and cook according to package directions, stirring occasionally.

2. **Cook the broccoli**  
   - About 4 minutes before the pasta is done, add the broccoli florets to the same pot. If using frozen broccoli, add 3 minutes before.  
   - When the pasta is just tender, scoop out ½ cup of the cooking water and set aside.

3. **Drain pasta and broccoli**  
   - Drain everything in a colander. Leave it there for a minute while you make the sauce.

4. **Make the garlic butter base**  
   - Put the empty pot back on the stove over low–medium heat.  
   - Add butter and olive oil. When the butter is melted and starting to foam, add the garlic.  
   - Cook, stirring, for about 30–60 seconds until fragrant and just starting to turn lightly golden. Don’t let it burn.

5. **Combine and season**  
   - Turn off the heat. Add the drained pasta and broccoli back into the pot.  
   - Add salt, pepper, and a splash of the reserved pasta water (start with 2–3 tbsp). Toss well with tongs or two spoons.  
   - If using cheese, add it now, tossing and adding more pasta water as needed until everything is lightly coated and glossy.

6. **Taste and adjust**  
   - Taste a forkful. Add more salt, pepper, or cheese if needed. If it seems dry, add a bit more pasta water or a small knob of butter.

**Optional add-ins:**
- Red pepper flakes for a bit of heat  
- A squeeze of lemon juice before serving  
- Frozen peas (add with the broccoli)

**Recipe 2: Sheet Pan Lemon Chicken & Potatoes**  
Category: One-Pan / One-Pot, 30-Minute Dinners

**Serves:** 3–4  
**Time:** 35–45 minutes

**Ingredients:**
- 4 boneless, skinless chicken thighs (or 2 large chicken breasts sliced in half)  
- 500 g (about 1 lb) small potatoes, cut into bite-sized pieces  
- 2 tbsp olive oil  
- 2 cloves garlic, minced (or 1 tsp pre-minced)  
- 1 lemon (zest and juice)  
- 1 tsp salt  
- ½ tsp black pepper  
- 1 tsp dried Italian herbs or oregano  
- Optional: 1 small onion, sliced; a handful of green beans or broccoli florets

**Instructions:**
1. **Preheat the oven**  
   - Heat your oven to 400°F (200°C).

2. **Prep the potatoes**  
   - On a baking sheet, toss the potato pieces with 1 tbsp olive oil, ½ tsp salt, and a pinch of pepper. Spread them out in a single layer.

3. **Start roasting the potatoes**  
   - Roast the potatoes alone for 10 minutes while you prep the chicken.

4. **Season the chicken**  
   - In a bowl, mix 1 tbsp olive oil, garlic, lemon zest, half the lemon juice, ½ tsp salt, pepper, and herbs.  
   - Add the chicken and coat it well.

5. **Add chicken (and optional veg) to the sheet**  
   - After the potatoes have roasted 10 minutes, take the sheet out.  
   - Push the potatoes to the sides and place the chicken pieces in the center.  
   - If using onion or green beans/broccoli, scatter them around the chicken.

6. **Roast everything together**  
   - Return the sheet to the oven and roast for another 18–25 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through (no pink in the middle, juices run clear) and potatoes are tender and golden at the edges.

7. **Finish with lemon**  
   - Squeeze the remaining half lemon over the chicken and potatoes right before serving. Taste and add a little more salt or pepper if needed.

**Recipe 3: One-Pot Tomato Rice with Beans**  
Category: One-Pot, Budget-Friendly

**Serves:** 3–4  
**Time:** 35–40 minutes

**Ingredients:**
- 1 cup uncooked white rice (long-grain works best)  
- 1 tbsp olive oil or any cooking oil  
- 1 small onion, chopped  
- 2 cloves garlic, minced (or 1 tsp pre-minced)  
- 1 tsp ground cumin (optional but recommended)  
- ½ tsp smoked paprika or regular paprika (optional)  
- 1 can (14–15 oz / ~400 g) diced tomatoes  
- 1 can (14–15 oz / ~400 g) beans (black, kidney, or whatever you have), drained and rinsed  
- 1 ¼ cups water or broth  
- 1 tsp salt  
- ¼ tsp black pepper  
- Optional: a squeeze of lime or lemon, chopped cilantro or parsley

**Instructions:**
1. **Rinse the rice**  
   - Put the rice in a bowl, cover with water, swirl, and drain. Repeat once or twice. This helps keep it from getting too sticky.

2. **Cook the aromatics**  
   - In a medium pot with a lid, heat the oil over medium heat.  
   - Add the chopped onion and cook 3–4 minutes until softened.  
   - Add the garlic, cumin, and paprika. Cook about 30 seconds until fragrant.

3. **Add tomatoes, beans, and rice**  
   - Pour in the canned tomatoes (with their liquid), beans, and the rinsed rice. Stir to combine.

4. **Add liquid and season**  
   - Add the water or broth, salt, and pepper. Stir again.  
   - Bring the pot to a gentle boil.

5. **Simmer**  
   - Once boiling, reduce the heat to low so it’s just barely simmering.  
   - Cover with a lid and cook for about 18–20 minutes. Don’t stir while it’s cooking.

6. **Rest and fluff**  
   - Turn off the heat and let the pot sit, covered, for 5–10 minutes.  
   - Remove the lid, fluff the rice gently with a fork.

7. **Finish and serve**  
   - Taste and add more salt or pepper if needed.  
   - Add a squeeze of lime or lemon and herbs if you have them.

**Title:** The Only 8 Kitchen Tools You Actually Need to Cook Dinner

**Intro**

If you’re just starting to cook, it’s easy to feel like you need a drawer full of gadgets before you can make a single meal. You don’t. With a small set of basic tools, you can cook almost every recipe on this blog—and a lot of others too.

Here are the only 8 kitchen tools you actually need to cook real weeknight dinners, plus a few things you can ignore for now.

---

### 1. A Decent Chef’s Knife

You don’t need an expensive knife set. You need **one** reasonably sharp chef’s knife.

**What it’s for:**  
- Chopping onions and garlic  
- Slicing vegetables  
- Cutting chicken or other meat

**What to look for:**  
- Comfortable handle  
- Blade around 6–8 inches long  
- Not the cheapest, but not a luxury brand either

If your knife feels dull, even a cheap one can be improved a lot by using a basic sharpener.

---

### 2. A Cutting Board

You need a flat surface that won’t damage your counter or your knife.

**What it’s for:**  
- All chopping and slicing  
- Resting cooked meat before cutting it

**What to look for:**  
- A plastic or wooden board about the size of a sheet of paper or a bit larger  
- Non-slip (or put a damp towel underneath to keep it from sliding)

One board is enough to start. Wash it with hot soapy water between raw meat and anything else.

---

### 3. A Medium/Large Frying Pan or Skillet

This will be your main pan for quick meals.

**What it’s for:**  
- Stir-fries  
- Fried rice  
- Sautéing vegetables  
- Cooking chicken or other proteins

**What to look for:**  
- 10–12 inch (25–30 cm) nonstick or stainless steel pan  
- A handle that feels sturdy  
- Ideally, a lid (if it doesn’t come with one, a large plate can cover it in a pinch)

---

### 4. A Medium Saucepan or Pot with Lid

You’ll use this for anything that simmers or boils.

**What it’s for:**  
- Cooking rice or pasta  
- Making soups and stews  
- One-pot meals

**What to look for:**  
- 2–3 quart (litre) size  
- Comes with a lid that fits well

If you can only afford one pot, choose this over a huge stock pot. It’s more useful for everyday dinners.

---

### 5. A Baking Sheet (Sheet Pan)

A simple metal baking sheet opens up a lot of easy, hands-off meals.

**What it’s for:**  
- Roasting vegetables  
- Sheet pan chicken and potatoes  
- Baking frozen items (like fries) more evenly than in a pan

**What to look for:**  
- A rimmed baking sheet (so oil and juices don’t spill)  
- Heavy enough that it doesn’t bend easily

Line it with parchment paper or foil for easier cleanup if you want.

---

### 6. A Heatproof Spoon or Spatula

You need something to stir and flip food in your pan or pot.

**What it’s for:**  
- Stirring sauces, soups, and stews  
- Flipping vegetables or meat in a pan

**What to look for:**  
- A wooden spoon, silicone spatula, or nylon spoon  
- Heatproof so it won’t melt in a hot pan

One or two good utensils are plenty.

---

### 7. A Colander (Strainer)

This is what you’ll use to drain boiling water safely.

**What it’s for:**  
- Draining pasta  
- Rinsing rice or beans  
- Washing vegetables

**What to look for:**  
- Medium-sized plastic or metal colander  
- Holes small enough that rice or small pasta won’t fall through

If you don’t have one yet, you can carefully use a lid to drain water from a pot, but a colander makes life much easier.

---

### 8. Measuring Cups and Spoons

You don’t need to be exact all the time, but at first, measuring helps a lot.

**What they’re for:**  
- Measuring rice, pasta, flour, and liquids  
- Measuring salt, spices, and baking ingredients

**What to look for:**  
- A basic set of dry measuring cups (¼, ⅓, ½, 1 cup)  
- A set of measuring spoons (¼ tsp up to 1 tbsp)

You can absolutely eyeball some things, but measuring takes the guesswork out when you’re new.

---

### What You Don’t Need (Yet)

You can skip all of these for now:

- Stand mixer  
- Food processor  
- High-end blender  
- Knife block with 10 different knives  
- Specialized gadgets (garlic press, avocado slicer, egg separator, etc.)

If you eventually cook more and want to invest, you can always add tools later. Starting small keeps your kitchen uncluttered and less intimidating.

---

### How to Build This Set on a Budget

You don’t have to buy everything at once. Here’s a simple order:

1. Knife + cutting board  
2. Frying pan + spoon/spatula  
3. Pot with lid + colander  
4. Baking sheet + measuring cups/spoons

Check thrift stores, discount shops, or online marketplaces—kitchen tools often last a long time and can be found second-hand.

---

**Wrap-Up**

You can cook a huge variety of weeknight dinners with just these 8 tools. As you go through recipes on Weeknight Plain & Simple, you’ll notice they’re all designed around this basic setup.

Next time you feel like you “can’t cook because you don’t have the right equipment,” come back to this list. If you have these, you’re ready.
