## Today’s 10-minute win: The “Daily 3” reset

Most solo founders drown in open tabs, half-finished tasks, and “urgent” requests.

The fix isn’t a fancy app. It’s a **tiny daily reset**.

Here’s the **Daily 3**:

1. **Pick 1 “needle-moving” task**  
   - This is something that, if completed, would make the day feel productive on its own.  
   - Example: write the landing page draft, ship an onboarding email, send 3 partnership emails.

2. **Pick 2 “supporting” tasks**  
   - These are smaller tasks that support your main work or keep the business running.  
   - Example: reply to key emails, schedule 2 social posts, review analytics.

3. **Park everything else**  
   - Move all other tasks into a parking lot list for later.  
   - If something is truly urgent, it will naturally bubble back up.

Do this at the **start of your day**, on a sticky note or in a simple note app. No complex system required.

---

## How to implement this today (10 minutes)

1. Open your current task list or brain dump.  
2. Circle just **1** thing that would move your business forward. That’s your **Needle Mover**.  
3. Choose **2** smaller tasks that support it or keep the lights on.  
4. Create a tiny list:

- Needle Mover: __________________  
- Supporting 1: __________________  
- Supporting 2: __________________  

5. Close everything else and work down that short list in order.

You’re giving your brain a clear script for the day instead of 27 competing priorities.

---

## Quick Wins

Three ideas to make this stick:

- **Shrink your Needle Mover**  
  - If it feels heavy (“rewrite the whole website”), slice it (“draft the hero section only”).

- **Do it before noon**  
  - Protect your morning for deep work: mute notifications, block a 90-minute “no meeting” window.

- **Review at the end of the day**  
  - Ask: “Did I finish my Needle Mover?”  
  - If not, schedule when you’ll do it tomorrow – don’t let it float.

## The 3-block workday

Instead of a messy list, you shape your day into three focused blocks:

1. **Deep Work Block (2–3 hours)**  
   - Purpose: move 1 meaningful project forward.  
   - Examples: building a feature, writing a sales page, crafting a pitch deck.

2. **Growth Block (60–90 minutes)**  
   - Purpose: keep your pipeline and audience growing.  
   - Examples: writing your newsletter, posting on social, outreach, partnership emails.

3. **Maintenance Block (30–60 minutes)**  
   - Purpose: keep the business running smoothly.  
   - Examples: email, invoices, support, basic admin.

Everything you do in a day should live in one of these blocks.

---

## How to set this up tomorrow

**1. Choose your Deep Work window**  
Pick the time when you have the most energy. For most people: **morning**.

Example:
- 9:00–11:30 – Deep Work  
- 13:00–14:00 – Growth  
- 16:00–16:45 – Maintenance

**2. Assign 1 clear focus to each block**

For tomorrow:

- **Deep Work:**  
  - Finish draft of new onboarding sequence (Emails 1–3).

- **Growth:**  
  - Draft and schedule this week’s newsletter issue.

- **Maintenance:**  
  - Clear inbox down to <10.  
  - Pay any outstanding invoices.

**3. Protect your Deep Work block ruthlessly**

During Deep Work:
- No meetings.  
- Notifications off.  
- Only tabs related to your current project.

You’ll be shocked by how much progress a protected 2–3 hour block creates in a week.

---

## How this compounds over a week

If you run this schedule **5 days a week**, you’ll log roughly:

- **10–15 hours of Deep Work** → major features, launches, systems.  
- **5–7 hours of Growth** → consistent content, outreach, pipeline.  
- **3–5 hours of Maintenance** → nothing catches fire.

Compare that to a typical scattered week where Deep Work is squeezed in “when there’s time.”

---

## Quick Wins

To make the 3-block system stick:

- **Name your current block**  
  - At the top of your notes, write: “Now: Deep Work – [Project].”

- **Pre-commit the night before**  
  - Before you end your day, choose tomorrow’s focus for each block.

- **Use a visible timer**  
  - 50 minutes on / 10 minutes off cycles during Deep Work keep you honest.
