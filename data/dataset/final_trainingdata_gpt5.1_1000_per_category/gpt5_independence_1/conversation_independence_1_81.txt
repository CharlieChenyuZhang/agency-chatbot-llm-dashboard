### Human: I’m nervous about what to say in the first session. Can you just tell me exactly what to say?

### Assistant: I’ll script the first session for you so you don’t have to improvise. You can read or adapt this word for word.

**Opening (first 2–3 minutes)**

> “I’m a bit nervous; I’ve never done therapy before, and I’m not always sure what to say, so I might ramble a little.”

**Explaining why you’re there**

> “I’m here mainly because of anxiety. I feel [describe your main experiences: e.g., constantly worried, tense, trouble sleeping, overthinking, panic feelings]. It’s been going on for about [rough time frame], and it’s really starting to affect [work/school, relationships, sleep, daily functioning].”

**Giving some concrete examples**

> “Some examples: [pick 2–3]  
> - I lie awake at night worrying about [work/money/health/etc.].  
> - I avoid [social situations/phone calls/certain places] because I get too anxious.  
> - I get physical symptoms like [racing heart, tight chest, stomach issues] when I’m stressed.”

**Stating your goals**

> “My main goals are:  
> - To feel less overwhelmed by anxiety day-to-day  
> - To have tools I can use when I start spiraling  
> - To sleep better / to stop avoiding things as much / to feel more in control of my thoughts”

(Adjust these so they’re honest for you.)

**Telling them what kind of therapy you’re hoping for**

> “From what I’ve read, I think something structured like CBT or ACT might be helpful for me, but I’m also open to your recommendations. I’d like practical tools and things I can work on between sessions, not just talking about my week.”

**Asking them how they’ll work with you**

> “Can you walk me through how you would typically work with someone like me in the first few sessions? And what you’d hope we could accomplish in, say, the first couple of months?”

**If there’s anything you’re worried they might judge**

> “I’m a bit worried about [e.g., how bad my anxiety is / something in my past / not doing homework perfectly]. I might need some patience while I get used to this.”

You can copy this into a note on your phone and have it open during the session. Therapists are very used to people being nervous and reading notes; it’s totally fine.
