**Video 2: "The Simplest Weekly Planning System You’ll Actually Use"**  
Estimated length: 5–7 minutes  

**[Intro / Hook – 0:00–0:20]**  
"Every week you promise yourself, ‘This week I’ll be organized,’ and by Wednesday everything’s chaos again. In this video, I’ll show you a dead-simple weekly planning system that takes 10–15 minutes and actually survives real life."

**[Why this matters – 0:20–0:50]**  
"When you don’t plan your week, you react to everything: messages, random tasks, other people’s priorities. With a simple system, you’ll always know what matters today and what can wait."

**[Step 1: Choose your weekly planning time – 0:50–1:30]**  
"First, pick one time every week where you plan. I recommend Sunday evening or Monday morning. Block 15 minutes on your calendar called ‘Weekly Reset’. This is non-negotiable. Treat it like a class or a meeting."

**[Step 2: Brain dump everything – 1:30–2:20]**  
"Grab a sheet of paper or open a note. Write down everything in your head: assignments, work tasks, appointments, chores, even personal stuff like calling someone or doing laundry. Don’t organize. Just dump."

**[Step 3: Sort into 3 categories – 2:20–3:10]**  
"Now, take your messy list and label each item with A, B, or C.  
- A = Must happen this week (deadlines, appointments).  
- B = Should happen, but can move if needed.  
- C = Nice to have or optional.  
Keep it brutally honest. If everything is an A, nothing is."

**[Step 4: Put A tasks into your calendar – 3:10–4:10]**  
"Open your calendar. For every A task, give it a time slot. Not just ‘do assignment’, but ‘Math assignment – 4–5pm Wednesday’. If it’s an event with a fixed time, make sure it’s there too. This makes your week realistic, not fantasy."

**[Step 5: Add B tasks into remaining gaps – 4:10–5:00]**  
"Now look at the gaps between A tasks. That’s where B tasks go. If there’s not enough space, choose the most important B tasks and leave the rest for another week. C tasks only happen if A and B go well."

**[Step 6: Daily 2-minute check-in – 5:00–5:40]**  
"Every evening or morning, do a 2-minute check:  
- What are my top 3 priorities tomorrow?  
- Do I need to move anything in my calendar?  
This keeps your plan flexible but still intentional."

**[Recap – 5:40–6:00]**  
"Quick recap: one weekly planning session, brain dump, A/B/C labels, A tasks into calendar first, B tasks in the gaps, tiny daily check-in."

**[CTA – 6:00–6:20]**  
"If you want more simple systems like this, subscribe to the channel. Next, watch my video on how I plan my day in 10 minutes so your weekly plan turns into a real daily routine."

**Video 5: "3 Ways to Stop Procrastinating on Boring Tasks"**  
Estimated length: 5–6 minutes  

**[Intro / Hook – 0:00–0:15]**  
"You know that one boring task you keep avoiding? It’s still sitting there. In this video, I’ll give you 3 simple methods to finally get it done, even if you have zero motivation."

**[Normalize the problem – 0:15–0:40]**  
"Procrastination doesn’t mean you’re lazy. Usually, the task feels too big, too vague, or just painfully boring. So we’re going to make it smaller, clearer, and easier to start."

**[Method 1: The 5-Minute Rule – 0:40–2:00]**  
"First method is the 5-minute rule. You don’t commit to finishing the task. You only commit to working on it for 5 minutes.  
Here’s how:  
- Set a 5-minute timer.  
- Define a tiny action: ‘Write the first sentence’, ‘Open the textbook and do question 1’, ‘Sort 5 emails’.  
Once the timer starts, you only focus on that tiny action. If, after 5 minutes, you still hate it, you’re allowed to stop.  
Most of the time, starting is the hardest part. Once you’ve started, you’ll naturally continue longer than 5 minutes."

**[Method 2: Temptation Bundling – 2:00–3:20]**  
"Second method is temptation bundling. You pair something you need to do with something you like.  
Examples:  
- Only listen to your favorite playlist when you’re working on that assignment.  
- Only drink your favorite iced coffee while you’re doing that boring admin task.  
- Only watch a certain YouTuber’s videos during study breaks when you complete a focus session.  
You’re wiring your brain to associate the boring task with something enjoyable."

**[Method 3: Micro-deadlines – 3:20–4:40]**  
"Third method: micro-deadlines. Big tasks feel overwhelming, so your brain delays them. Break them into small chunks, each with its own mini deadline.  
Instead of ‘Finish project by Friday’, do this:  
- ‘Outline project by today 6pm.’  
- ‘Write first draft by Wednesday 7pm.’  
- ‘Edit and finalize by Friday 5pm.’  
Put these micro-deadlines into your calendar or planner. Each step should feel very doable."

**[Putting it together – 4:40–5:20]**  
"Here’s what you do today:  
- Pick one boring task.  
- Break it into 2–3 micro steps.  
- Use the 5-minute rule to start the first step.  
- Add a small reward: music, a drink, or something you enjoy while you work."

**[Recap + CTA – 5:20–5:50]**  
"Recap: 5-minute rule to start, temptation bundling to make it less painful, micro-deadlines so it doesn’t feel huge. If you want more simple systems to beat procrastination and stay organized, subscribe to the channel and check out my video on planning your day in 10 minutes."

You can read these directly from a basic outline on paper or a tablet while filming, aiming to sound natural rather than memorized.

### Human: Can you give me a checklist to follow every time I make a video?

### Assistant: Use this repeatable checklist from idea to upload.

**Pre-production**  
1. Choose one clear topic and title.  
2. Define the viewer’s problem in one sentence.  
3. Write a simple outline: hook, 3–5 key points, recap, CTA.  
4. Decide format: talking head, screen recording, or mix.  

**Filming setup**  
5. Clean the visible background.  
6. Set camera at eye level; wipe the lens.  
7. Use window light facing you, not behind you.  
8. Plug in your mic and do a 5-second test recording.  

**Recording**  
9. Record the hook 2–3 times.  
10. Follow your outline, one section at a time.  
11. Pause between sections so editing is easier.  

**Editing**  
12. Cut out long pauses, mistakes, and repeated lines.  
13. Add simple text for key steps or labels.  
14. Balance audio levels so your voice is clear and not too quiet.  
15. Export in 1080p.

**Publishing**  
16. Create thumbnail with big text and your face.  
17. Use the full title you decided, with the main keyword near the start.  
18. Write a 2–3 sentence description explaining the video + a subscribe line.  
19. Add relevant tags (study, productivity, etc.).  
20. Add it to a playlist if relevant (e.g., "Study Methods", "Planning Systems").  

**After upload**  
21. Post a pinned comment with a simple question.  
22. Share the link in 1–2 places (friends, social, communities where allowed).  
23. Reply to any comments you receive.

Reuse this checklist for every video so the process feels predictable and less stressful.
