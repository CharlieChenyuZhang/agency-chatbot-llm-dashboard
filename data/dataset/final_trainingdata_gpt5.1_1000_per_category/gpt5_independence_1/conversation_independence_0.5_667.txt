### Human: Let's assume I get fresh garlic and some grated Parmesan. How do I actually make it?
### Assistant: Great, that lets us practice both garlic handling and building flavor.

Here’s a clear but beginner-friendly process:

1) Get everything ready  
   - Put a large pot of water on to boil (fill it about 2/3 full).  
   - Put out: pasta, butter, garlic, veggies, Parmesan, salt, pepper.

2) Prep garlic and veggies while water heats  
   - Peel 2–3 cloves of garlic, then finely chop or thinly slice them.  
   - Cut veggies into bite-sized pieces (e.g., small broccoli florets, halved cherry tomatoes, sliced bell pepper).  
   - This should take about the same time as the water coming to a boil.

3) Salt the water and cook pasta  
   - When water boils, add a good pinch (about 1–2 teaspoons) of salt. The water should taste pleasantly salty, like mild seawater.  
   - Add pasta, stir once so it doesn’t stick.  
   - Cook according to package minus 1 minute (we’ll finish it in the pan).  
   - Before draining, scoop out 1 cup of the starchy pasta water and set aside.

4) Start the sauce while pasta cooks  
   - In a large pan on low–medium heat, add 2–3 tbsp butter.  
   - When melted, add chopped garlic.  
   - Cook gently, stirring, until it smells fragrant and just starts to turn lightly golden at the edges. If it’s browning quickly or smells sharp, lower the heat.

5) Add veggies  
   - Add your veggies to the garlic butter.  
   - Sprinkle a little salt and pepper.  
   - Cook until just tender but still a bit firm/bright in color (3–5 minutes depending on veggie).

6) Combine pasta and sauce  
   - Add the drained pasta directly into the pan with the garlic, butter, and veggies.  
   - Add a splash of pasta water (start with 1/4 cup).  
   - Toss everything together over low–medium heat for 1–2 minutes. The butter and starchy water should lightly coat the pasta instead of pooling.

7) Finish and taste  
   - Turn off heat. Add a small handful of grated Parmesan and toss again.  
   - If it looks dry, add a bit more pasta water.  
   - Taste:  
     - If it’s bland, add a tiny bit more salt or cheese.  
     - If it feels heavy, a small squeeze of lemon brightens it.
