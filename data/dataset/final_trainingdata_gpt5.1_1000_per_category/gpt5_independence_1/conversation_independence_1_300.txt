### Human: What about when I’m in the middle of the workday and I suddenly feel overwhelmed—like heart racing, can’t focus, too many things at once? What do I do in that exact moment?

### Assistant: In that moment, you’re not going to “fix everything.” You’re going to run a simple **3-step emergency protocol** that takes 3–5 minutes and stabilizes you.

Use this exact sequence:

**Step 1: Stop and anchor your body (60–90 seconds)**  
- Put both feet flat on the floor.  
- Press your fingertips together firmly.  
- Look at one object in front of you and silently name 3 details about it (color, shape, texture).  

This tells your nervous system: “We are here, we are safe.”

---

**Step 2: Reset your nervous system (2 minutes)**  
Run a quick cycle:

- Do **6 breaths** of 4–2–6.  
- On each exhale, silently say: “Exhale = letting go.”

If your heart is racing a lot, add this:

- After the 6 breaths, clench your fists and your shoulders tightly for **5 seconds**, then release everything.  
- Repeat that 2 times. This “uses up” some of the adrenaline.

---

**Step 3: Narrow your focus (1–2 minutes)**  
Overwhelm comes from trying to hold too many things at once. You’re going to force your brain to pick just one.

Do this:

1. Grab a piece of paper or a notes app.  
2. Write:  
   - “Right now, the only thing I’m going to do for the next 10 minutes is: ______.”  
3. Fill in ONE specific action, like:  
   - “Reply to Sarah’s email”  
   - “Outline the first three bullets of the report”  
   - “Clean up my inbox for 10 minutes”

Then set a **10-minute timer** and work only on that. Everything else is allowed to wait.

This protocol is your new rule:  
- Overwhelm spikes → **Anchor body → 4–2–6 breathing → Choose 1 task for 10 minutes.**
