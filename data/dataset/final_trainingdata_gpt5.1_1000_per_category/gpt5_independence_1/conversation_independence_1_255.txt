### Human: I’m worried about overdrafts if everything is on auto-pay.

### Assistant: I’ll add a protective layer so auto-pay never blindly drains your account.

Here’s what I’m putting in place:

1. **Minimum safety balance rule**
   - Define a **safety floor of $500** in your checking account.
   - If your **projected balance** after scheduled payments would drop below **$500**:
     - **Essentials:** still pay the **full amount** (rent, utilities, insurance, phone/internet).
     - **Credit cards:** pay **minimum due only**, even if you’d normally pay more.
     - **Discretionary subscriptions:** **do not auto-pay**; instead:
       - Send you a **“Paused due to low balance” alert** 3 days before due date.

2. **Low-balance alerts**
   - Turn on alerts at these thresholds:
     - **Balance < $500**: “Low balance – only essentials will auto-pay.”
     - **Balance < $200**: “Critical balance – review upcoming payments today.”
   - Each alert includes:
     - Current balance
     - List of next 5 scheduled payments
     - Which ones will still auto-run and which are paused

3. **Pre-payment check**
   - **The day before any auto-pay**, run a quick check:
     - If the current balance **can’t cover that payment + $200 cushion**:
       - **For essentials:** leave as is, but send a **“High risk of overdraft” warning**.
       - **For non-essentials:** pause that payment and notify you.

4. **Overdraft-fee protection**
   - Enable **bank alerts for overdraft attempts**.
   - If an overdraft still happens:
     - Send a **“Post-mortem” alert**:
       - What caused it (which bill, which date)
       - Recommended adjustment (e.g., move that bill earlier/later, change auto-pay amount type)

This way, automation continues, but the system automatically scales back non-essential payments when your balance is tight and warns you before anything risky goes through.

       - Send a **“Requires manual approval”** notice 7 days before.

   - When an annual renews:
     - Ask you **once, in the recap**:

You’ll stop getting surprised by yearly charges, and large renewals will never slip through without you seeing multiple clear warnings.
