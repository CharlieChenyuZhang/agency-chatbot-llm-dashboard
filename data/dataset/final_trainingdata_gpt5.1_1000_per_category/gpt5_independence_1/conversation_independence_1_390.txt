### Human: I tend to procrastinate in the evenings after work. Can you build in strategies to keep me on track?
### Assistant: I’ll integrate anti-procrastination strategies directly into your daily routine so they’re part of the schedule, not optional extras.

---

## 1. Pre-Commitment Before Work

**Each morning (5 minutes):**
- Write down:
  - Today’s study start time (e.g., “20:00–21:30 study”)
  - 1–2 specific tasks for each subject:
    - Math: “Do 10 algebra questions + review yesterday’s mistakes”
    - Subject B: “Read section 3.2 and make 5 bullet notes”
- Decide your **study location** in advance (desk, library, kitchen table).

This converts “I should study” into a concrete appointment.

---

## 2. Automatic Start Routine (2–3 minutes)

When you get home and it’s close to study time, follow the same short sequence every day:

1. Put your phone in another room or on Do Not Disturb.  
2. Fill a water bottle.  
3. Open your math book/site and lay out materials.  
4. Start a 25-minute timer.

You begin with **setup, not motivation**—action comes first, motivation usually follows.

---

## 3. “Just 10 Minutes” Rule for Math

On nights when you really don’t feel like studying:

- Commit to **10 minutes** of math only:
  - Open your book
  - Do 3–5 questions or rework 2 previous mistakes
- After 10 minutes:
  - If you still feel exhausted, you may stop.
  - If you feel okay (which is common), continue the planned 25–35 minute block.

You preserve the habit even on bad days, which is critical in a 6‑week window.

---

## 4. Visual Tracking

Add this to your schedule:

- At the end of each study day (takes 1 minute):
  - On a simple calendar or piece of paper, mark:
    - ✅ if you completed your planned blocks
    - ➖ if you did at least 10 minutes
    - ❌ if you did nothing

Goal: Avoid **two ❌ days in a row**.  
Your only rule: if you get a ❌, the next day must be at least ➖ (10 minutes).

---

## 5. Fixed “Shutdown” Time

To avoid burning out and then procrastinating the next day:

- Set a hard stop (e.g., **21:30**).  
- At your stop time, do a 2‑minute wrap‑up:
  - Write what you finished.
  - Write what you’ll start with tomorrow.

This reduces decision friction the next evening.

---

## 6. Built-In Rewards

Immediately after your weekday study block:

- Plan a small, consistent reward:
  - 20–30 minutes of a show
  - A game
  - Relaxing with music

Attach this **only** to completing at least the “just 10 minutes” rule. This turns study into the gateway to something pleasant, not a blocker.

These strategies now sit inside your schedule:
- Morning: 5‑minute planning.
- Evening: 2–3 minute start routine + “just 10 minutes” fallback.
- End: 2‑minute wrap‑up + reward.
- Ongoing: visual tracking and “no two ❌ days in a row” rule.
