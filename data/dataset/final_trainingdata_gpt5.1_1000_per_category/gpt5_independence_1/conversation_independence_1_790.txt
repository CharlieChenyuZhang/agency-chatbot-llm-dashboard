- Professional but relaxed service (doesn’t feel stuffy)

Plan it like this:
- **Time:** Aim for a **7:30 pm** reservation so you’re not rushed and can linger.

  - Starters: Roasted mushrooms, beef carpaccio (or sub another veg starter), burrata if available  
  - Pasta to share: The **ricotta gnocchi** and **tagliatelle**  
  - Dessert: Olive oil cake to split  

Dress smart-casual: nice shirt or blouse, dark jeans or trousers, and clean shoes—fits the room perfectly without feeling overdressed.

### Human: I'm a bit nervous. Can you coach me on conversation topics for the night?

### Assistant: I’ll give you a simple structure and specific topics so you’re never scrambling.

**1. Start light and present-focused**
Use the walk and early dinner to talk about:
- Their week: “What was the best part of your week so far?”  
- Neighborhood: “Have you spent much time in the West Village?”  
- Food: “Are there any cuisines you’d happily eat every week?”

**2. Move into personal-but-comfortable topics**
Once you’re seated and settled:
- **Stories instead of facts:**
  - “What’s a trip you took that you still think about a lot?”  
  - “When you were a kid, what did you imagine you’d be doing as an adult?”  
- **Light values questions:**
  - “What kind of weekend feels perfect to you?”  
  - “What do you wish you had more time for?”

**3. Sprinkle in playful questions**
These keep things fun:
- “If you could instantly master any skill, what would you pick?”  
- “What’s a small, ‘unpopular’ opinion you stand by?”  
- “What’s your comfort movie or show?”

**4. Ask, then actually listen**
- Let them talk; don’t rush to fill every silence.  
- Follow up on details they mention:  
  - “You said you love [X]—how did you get into that?”  
  - “You mentioned your sibling earlier; are you two close?”

**5. Third-date appropriate topics**
You can gently deepen things:
- “What are you looking for this year—more in terms of how you want your life to feel than specific goals?”  
- “What’s something you’ve learned about yourself in the last couple of years?”

**6. Things to avoid early in the night**
- Heavy ex talk or detailed dating app war stories  
- Very polarizing politics (unless you already know you’re aligned and it comes up naturally)  
- Complaining extensively about work

If you get nervous, default to:
- “Tell me more about that”  
- “How did that happen?”  
- “What made you decide to do that?”

Those simple follow-ups can carry a conversation surprisingly far while making them feel seen and heard.
