### Human: Help me reach a 1.5x bodyweight deadlift

### Assistant: 
You’ll follow a simple 12‑week strength block focused on deadlift progress, with accessory work to build your posterior chain and core.

I’ll assume:
- You’re generally healthy with no major injuries.
- You can train 3x per week.
- You’re around beginner–intermediate level (already comfortable with basic barbell lifts).

Your goal structure:
- Goal: 1.5× bodyweight deadlift for 1 rep (e.g., at 80 kg bodyweight → 120 kg deadlift).
- Time frame: 8–16 weeks is realistic for most; I’ll structure 12 weeks as the main plan.

Training split (3 days/week):
- Day A: Deadlift focus + posterior chain + core  
- Day B: Squat + single‑leg work + core  
- Day C: Deadlift variation + upper back + hamstrings

Core rules:
- Warm up properly every session.
- Use controlled reps; no touch‑and‑go bouncing.
- Stop 1–3 reps before failure on all working sets unless explicitly noted.

Base 12‑week progression (top‑set + back‑offs)

**Weeks 1–4 (Base strength)**
- Day A:
  - Deadlift:  
    - Warm‑ups up to a moderate top set of 5 reps (RPE ~7/10: hard but clearly not max).  
    - Then 3×5 @ ~80–85% of that day’s top set weight.
  - Romanian Deadlift: 3×8–10
  - Plank: 3×30–45 sec

- Day B:
  - Back Squat: 4×5
  - Walking Lunges: 3×10 steps/leg
  - Side Plank: 3×20–30 sec/side

- Day C:
  - Pause Deadlift (1–2 sec pause just off floor): 4×3 (light‑moderate)
  - Barbell Row: 4×6–8
  - Hip Thrust or Glute Bridge: 3×10–12

**Weeks 5–8 (Intensification)**
- Day A:
  - Deadlift:  
    - Work up to a top set of 3 (RPE 8).  
    - Then 3×3 @ ~85–90% of that top set.
  - Romanian Deadlift: 3×6–8 (slightly heavier)
  - Hanging Leg Raise or Reverse Crunch: 3×8–12

- Day B:
  - Back Squat: 5×3 (heavier)
  - Bulgarian Split Squat: 3×8/leg
  - Side Plank with leg lift (if able): 3×20–30 sec/side

- Day C:
  - Deficit Deadlift (standing on 2–5 cm plate): 4×3 (light‑moderate, focus off‑floor strength)
  - Barbell Row: 4×5–6 (heavier)
  - Hip Thrust: 3×8–10

**Weeks 9–11 (Peaking)**
- Day A:
  - Deadlift:
    - Week 9: work to top set of 3 (RPE 8–8.5), then 2×3 @ ~90% of that.
    - Week 10: top set of 2, then 2×2 @ ~90%.
    - Week 11: top set of 1 (heavy single, RPE 8.5–9), then 2×1 @ ~90%.
  - Light RDL: 2×6–8
  - Light Core: 2×10–12 (choose any)

- Day B:
  - Squat: 3×3 (moderate, not all‑out)
  - Single‑leg work: 2×8/leg
  - Short core circuit

- Day C:
  - Very light deadlift variation or skip heavy pulls:
    - Week 9: 3×3 light pause or deficit deadlift
    - Week 10: 3×2 light
    - Week 11: technique singles @ ~60–70% (focus on speed and form)
  - Upper back work: 3×8–10
  - Optional hamstring curls: 2×10–12

**Week 12 (Test week)**
- Day A: Warm up and test your deadlift 1RM:
  - Bar × 5
  - ~40% × 3
  - ~60% × 2
  - ~75% × 1
  - ~85% × 1
  - Then 1–3 attempts at a new max in the 90–102.5%+ range, resting 3–5 min.
- Other days: light full‑body or rest, no heavy pulling.

Next, you’ll need:
- Steady, modest progression in bar weight (add 2.5–5 kg when top set is comfortable and form is solid).
- Enough food, sleep, and consistency.

I’ll handle the specifics of progression, recovery, and technique details in the next steps as you ask.

### Human: I’ve had some lower back tightness before. How are we keeping this safe?

### Assistant:
I’ll bake safety into your approach with technique rules, load management, and specific prep work.

Technique rules:
- Stance: roughly hip‑width, toes slightly out; bar over mid‑foot before each pull.
- Setup:
  - Grip the bar with straight arms.
  - Hinge hips back, then bend knees until shins lightly touch the bar.
  - Chest “proud,” eyes about 2–3 m in front on the floor (not straight up).
- Bracing:
  - Big breath into your belly/360° around your core.
  - “Lock” your midsection as if about to be punched.
  - Hold breath during the lift up, exhale at the top or on the way down if needed.
- Spine: keep your back rigid, no rounding under load—if it rounds, weight is too heavy.

Load management:
- Most work at RPE 6–8:
  - RPE 6: could do 4 more reps.
  - RPE 7: could do 3 more.
  - RPE 8: could do 2 more.
- Avoid repeated grinders (no slow, shaky reps every session).
- If your back feels tight or off on a given day:
  - Drop the day’s deadlift weight by 10–20% and keep sets clean.
  - If it still feels wrong, skip heavy deadlifts that day and only do light technique work.

Prep and accessories specifically for your back:
Before deadlifts (5–8 minutes total):
- Dynamic warm‑up:
  - Cat‑camel: 8–10 reps.
  - Hip flexor stretch: 30 sec/side.
  - Glute bridges: 2×10–12.
  - Bodyweight good mornings: 2×10.

Core and stability 2–3× per week:
- Planks (front and side): build up to 45–60 sec.
- Bird dogs: 3×8–10/side (slow, controlled).
- Loaded carries (if space allows, with dumbbells): 3×20–30 m.

If back tightness appears:
- Reduce load and volume for 1 week (about half the normal sets and a 10–20% weight drop).
- Prioritize perfect form; if a rep feels sketchy, the set is done.

This structure lets you keep progressing while deliberately protecting your lower back.

### Human: What if I miss a workout in a week? Do I try to make it up?

### Assistant:
You won’t cram missed sessions; you’ll keep the plan orderly and protect recovery.

Rules for a missed session:
- If you miss **one** session in a week:
  - Skip it and continue with the next scheduled session as written.
  - Don’t try to do two full sessions in one day.
  - If the missed session was Day A (deadlift), the next week you still run Day A → B → C in order.

- If you miss **two** sessions in a week:
  - Do only one full‑intensity session that week (preferably the deadlift‑focused Day A).
  - The next week, repeat the same week’s structure instead of progressing load (essentially you redo that week).

- After illness or very poor recovery:
  - When you return, reduce the bar weight by ~10–15% for that first week back.
  - Keep total sets the same but stop each set 2–3 reps from failure.
  - From the next week, resume normal progression if you feel fine.

This way, short disruptions don’t derail progress or increase injury risk; they just slow the timeline slightly.
