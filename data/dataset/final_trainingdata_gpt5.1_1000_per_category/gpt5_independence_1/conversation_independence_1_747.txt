- Identify 3–5 “core skills” in your field that top performers have.

  - Comment thoughtfully on industry posts.

- Decide: push for a promotion internally, or prepare for an external move (or both).  
- Tighten your personal positioning: a 1–2 sentence “who I am / what I’m good at / what I do for companies” summary.  
- Practice interviewing: behavioral questions, case/technical questions for your field, plus “tell me about yourself” and salary conversations.  
- Start a targeted job search or promotion campaign.

Foundations you maintain the whole time:
- Weekly: review your week, log wins and lessons, plan 3 “career moves” for next week (e.g., learning, relationship, visibility).  
- Monthly: update your “brag document” of achievements, impact, and feedback.  
- Quarterly: reassess goals and adjust the plan rather than starting over.

### Human: I struggle to follow through on plans. What systems should I use to stay on track?

### Assistant: I’ll give you a simple, low-friction system that doesn’t depend on motivation.

System 1: A tiny weekly ritual (non-negotiable, 30–45 minutes)
- Every Sunday or Monday, do this:
  1) Open your “Marketing Wins” and “Career Plan” docs.  
  2) Write:
     - 3 wins from last week (even small ones).
     - 1 lesson learned.
     - 1 thing you’ll do differently this week.
  3) Choose exactly 3 career actions for the week:
     - 1 skill (e.g., finish a module of a course, write a short teardown of a campaign).
     - 1 visibility move (e.g., present a small insight to your team, share a summary in Slack/Teams).
     - 1 relationship move (e.g., send 1 message to someone in your field, or have a 15-min chat).

System 2: Time-blocking with “minimum viable commitment”
- Pick two 45-minute blocks per week on your calendar labeled “Career Work – do not move.”
- During those blocks, you work on your 3 actions—no email, no Slack.
- If you’re exhausted, you do the minimum: 15 minutes of focused effort, then stop. The rule is: you don’t skip; you can shorten.

System 3: External accountability without overcomplication
- Choose one person in your life (friend/colleague) and send them a short message:
  - “I’m working toward becoming a Marketing Manager in 3 years. Every Sunday, I’ll send you 3 bullets: what I did for my career last week and what I’ll do next week.”
- Set a recurring reminder to send that message every week. No discussion needed—just reporting. This alone strongly increases follow-through.

System 4: Environment design
- At home:
  - Have one “workstation cue” for career work (same place, same time, no entertainment open).
- Digital:
  - Pin your “Career Plan,” “Marketing Wins,” and current learning resource so they’re always visible (browser bookmarks bar or pinned tabs).
  - Turn off phone notifications during your two weekly “Career Work” time blocks.

System 5: Make tracking so simple you’ll actually do it
- Use one page/spreadsheet with:
  - Columns: Week #, 3 Wins, Skill Action, Visibility Action, Relationship Action, Notes.
  - Each week, fill one row. If a week is empty, you see it immediately—that visual gap will push you to restart.

If you implement only:
- The weekly 30–45-minute ritual, and  
- The two 45-minute “Career Work” blocks,  
you will move forward even in busy weeks.

### Human: Networking makes me uncomfortable. Can you be specific about what I should actually say to people?

### Assistant: I’ll give you concrete scripts and low-pressure formats tailored for a junior marketer in tech.

Principles:
- Keep it short.
- Make it about learning, not asking for a job.
- Be specific so it’s easy for them to respond.

LinkedIn connection to someone 1–3 levels ahead of you:
- “Hi [Name], I’m a junior marketer at a mid-size tech company, focusing on [brief focus, e.g., lifecycle and content]. I’ve learned a lot from your posts/role at [Company]. I’m trying to grow into a Marketing Manager role in the next few years and would love to add you to my network. Thanks either way.”

Light follow-up a week or two later (for a short chat):
- “Hi [Name], quick note to say thanks for connecting. I’m working on getting better at [e.g., experiments and campaign strategy] and saw you’ve done [specific thing they’ve done, if visible].  
  If you’re open to it, I’d really appreciate 15–20 minutes to ask 3–4 specific questions about how you approached your career and what you’d focus on if you were in my shoes today. No worries at all if your schedule’s packed.”

Questions you can use in those chats:
- “When you were at my level, what did you do that made the biggest difference to your career?”  
- “What are the 2–3 skills you rely on most now that you didn’t fully appreciate earlier?”  
- “What mistakes do you see junior marketers make that slow them down?”  
- “If you were me, trying to become a Marketing Manager in ~3 years, what would you double down on this year?”

Internal networking (people at your company):
- Message to someone in Sales/Product/another marketing sub-team:
  - “Hey [Name], I’m [Your Name] from marketing, working on [brief description]. I’m trying to understand more about how [your team] works so I can do a better job with my side of things. Would you be open to a quick 20-min chat sometime in the next couple of weeks? I’d like to understand:
    - What’s working well between our teams  
    - Where I might be able to help more  
    - Any context I might be missing”

After a chat, simple follow-up:
- “Thanks again for your time today. I really liked your point about [X]. I’m going to [specific action you’ll take]. I’ll let you know how it goes.”

Low-anxiety options (no calls):
- Thoughtful LinkedIn comments:
  - Instead of “Great post,” write 2–3 sentences:
    - What you took from it.
    - One small example from your own work.
- Short DMs where you send something useful:
  - “Saw this case study on [topic you discussed]. Thought you might find it interesting: [link]. No reply needed—just sharing.”

Do this at a sustainable pace:
- 1 new connection request per week.  
- 1 short message or comment per week.  
This is enough to build a real network over a year without burning out.

### Human: I’ve got a performance review in 3 weeks. Tell me exactly how to prepare for it.

  - “Led [project/campaign] to achieve [result], by [what you did].”
  - Whenever possible, include numbers: % lift, # leads, meetings, revenue influence, etc.

Create a short doc with sections:
1) Role and focus:
   - 2–3 sentences on what your role is and what you’ve focused on this period.
2) Top 5–7 contributions:
   - Bulleted, impact-first:
     - “Improved [metric] by X% by doing Y.”
     - “Took ownership of Z process, reducing errors/time by A%.”
3) How you’ve grown:
   - New skills/tools you’ve picked up.
   - Ways you’ve taken more ownership.
4) Where you’re improving next:
   - 2–3 areas you’re already working on (frame them as active improvements, not flaws).
5) What you want next:
   - More scope in your chosen area (e.g., lifecycle, growth experiments).
   - A path toward a Marketing Manager-type role over the next 1–3 cycles.

Step 3: Align with your manager’s goals (end of this week / early next)
- In your self-review, explicitly link your work to:
  - Team goals (pipeline, leads, engagement, retention, etc.).
  - Your manager’s priorities (e.g., better reporting, more reliable campaign execution).
- Prepare 2–3 examples you can say out loud:
  - “This project contributed to [team goal] by [how].”

Step 4: Prepare talking points for the review meeting
Have a one-page printout or note with:
- 3 biggest wins (you want to make sure these get discussed).
- 2–3 key strengths you want highlighted.
- 2–3 growth areas you’re already working on (with actions).
- Your desired direction:
  - “Over the next year, I want to increasingly own [channel/area] and move toward a Marketing Manager-type scope.”

- Say out loud (once or twice):
  - A 60-second summary: “Here’s what I focused on this period, what I achieved, and where I’m growing next.”
  - How you’ll talk about a mistake or weaker area:
    - “One thing that didn’t go as planned was [X]. Here’s what I learned and how I’ve changed my approach.”

Step 6: Use the review to set up your next step
At the end of the meeting, you’ll steer it toward future growth by asking for:
- Clear expectations for someone at the next level.
- Specifics on what you need to demonstrate in the next 6–12 months.

You’ll walk into the review with a concise written self-review, clear examples of impact, and a direction for next year—this makes it much easier to later justify a promotion or raise.

### Human: I’m anxious about asking for a raise. What should I do?

### Assistant: I’ll give you a structured approach and a script so you’re not improvising.

1) Make sure you have a case
- Use your self-review doc to summarize:
  - Your main contributions (with numbers).
  - Added responsibilities since you started (or since your last raise).
  - Any market data suggesting your current pay is below typical for your level/region (basic research on Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, salary surveys, etc.).

2) Decide your ask
- Choose:
  - A target compensation number or range.
  - Whether you’re open to other forms of compensation (bonus, equity, title) if base is constrained.
- Have a “confident but reasonable” number in mind before the conversation.

3) Timing
- The ideal moment:
  - Around or slightly before performance review conversations.
  - Or after you’ve completed a clearly valuable project.

4) Script for the conversation
In a 1:1 with your manager, you can say:

- Opening:
  - “I’d like to talk about my role, the impact I’ve been having, and how my compensation aligns with that.”

- Present your value:
  - “Over the past [time period], I’ve [brief overview].  
    Concretely, I’ve:
    - [Impact 1 with numbers]  
    - [Impact 2 with numbers]  
    - [Impact 3 with numbers]  
    I’ve also taken on [new responsibilities / ownership areas].”

- Make the ask:
  - “Given this increased scope and impact, and based on what I’m seeing for similar roles in the market, I believe an adjustment to [X] would fairly reflect my contribution and level.”

- Then stop talking and let them respond.

5) Handling common responses
- “I need to check with HR / my manager”:
  - “That makes sense. What information would be helpful for you to make the case? And when do you think we could revisit this conversation?”
- “We don’t have budget right now”:
  - “I understand budgets can be tight. In that case, I’d like to understand:
    - What specific expectations you’d have for me at the next compensation level, and  
    - The timeline and process for revisiting this.”
  - You can also say:
    - “If a salary adjustment isn’t possible right now, I’d like to discuss other options like title alignment, a defined path to the next level, or future bonus/raise timing.”

6) Prep to reduce anxiety
- Write your bullet points and practice saying them out loud 2–3 times.
- Aim for calm, factual, and professional—not apologetic.
- You’re not begging; you’re presenting a reasoned business case.

Even if the answer is “not yet,” you’ll often walk away with:
- Clearer expectations.
- A timeline.
- A signal that you’re serious about your growth—which supports your move to a better role internally or externally later.
