### Human: Do the physical example with speed.
### Assistant: Imagine a car moving along a straight road, and let \( s(t) \) be its position at time \( t \). The average speed between \( t = 1 \) and \( t = 3 \) is:

\[
\text{average speed} = \frac{s(3) - s(1)}{3 - 1}.
\]

The derivative \( s'(t) \) at a particular time \( t \) is the instantaneous speed at that exact moment, which we approximate by average speeds over smaller and smaller time intervals around \( t \).

Would you like me to:

### Human: Let’s do the numeric example first, then we can peek at linear algebra.
### Assistant: Perfect. Suppose \( s(t) = t^2 \) (position in meters, time in seconds). At \( t = 2 \):

Average speed from 2 to 3:
\[
\frac{s(3)-s(2)}{3-2} = \frac{9 - 4}{1} = 5.
\]

Average speed from 2 to 2.1:
\[
\frac{s(2.1)-s(2)}{2.1-2} = \frac{(2.1)^2 - 4}{0.1} \approx \frac{4.41 - 4}{0.1} = 4.1.
\]

As we take intervals closer to 2, this average speed approaches 4, which is the derivative \( s'(2) = 4 \).
