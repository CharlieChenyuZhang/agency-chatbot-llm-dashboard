2. Main exercise:  
   - Take **two unrelated words** (e.g., *bridge* and *orange*).  
   - Generate **15 things that combine them** (e.g., “a bridge painted in gradients of orange that changes with sunlight”).

3. Reflection:  
   - Circle 3 ideas that feel interesting. For each, write one sentence starting with “This could be interesting because…”

**Day 3 – Bad ideas only**
1. Warm‑up:  
   - Pick a simple problem (e.g., “how to wake up on time”).  
   - List **20 deliberately terrible solutions** (too expensive, dangerous, absurd).

2. Main exercise:  
   - Take your 5 worst ideas. For each, twist it into **one potentially useful idea**.  
   - Example: “A tiger that roars at you at 7 a.m.” → “A wake‑up app that plays terrifying movie roars at random volumes.”

3. Reflection:  
   - Write 3–5 sentences on how it felt to **intentionally be “wrong”** and what that did to your thinking.

**Day 4 – Quantity sprint**
1. Warm‑up:  
   - Set a 5‑minute timer and list **as many breakfast ideas** as you can. Don’t stop to think; just write.

2. Main exercise:  
   - Pick a prompt: “Ways to make commuting more enjoyable.”  
   - Set 10 minutes and aim for **at least 40 ideas**.  
   - If you get stuck, force yourself to write something ridiculous just to keep going.

3. Reflection:  
   - Underline any ideas that feel promising or surprising.

**Day 5 – Constraint ladder**
1. Warm‑up:  
   - Choose a random emotion (e.g., *curiosity*). List 10 situations that evoke it.

2. Main exercise:  
   - Problem: “Make learning a new skill feel like a game.”  
   - Generate 10 ideas **with no constraints**.  
   - Then **tighten constraints**: must be cheap, usable on a phone, and doable in 5 minutes.  
   - Transform each of the 10 ideas to fit the constraints, or invent new ones that do.

3. Reflection:  
   - Note 2 ideas that actually got **better** with constraints.

**Day 6 – Combinatorial creativity**
1. Warm‑up:  
   - Make two lists of 10 items each:  
     - List A: everyday objects (chair, pen, window…).  
     - List B: activities (cooking, dancing, studying…).

2. Main exercise:  
   - Randomly pair items from A and B (chair + dancing, pen + cooking, etc.).  
   - For each pair, invent **one product, service, or art piece**. Aim for 15–20 pairs.

3. Reflection:  
   - Pick the single most fun idea and write 3–4 sentences exploring how it could actually exist.

**Day 7 – Week 1 review + synthesis**
1. Warm‑up:  
   - Freewrite for 10 minutes about anything that stood out this week.

2. Main exercise:  
   - Skim all your notes from the week.  
   - Choose your **top 5 favorite ideas** (any day).  
   - For each, write:  
     - What the idea is (1–2 sentences).  
     - How you could make a **tiny prototype or sketch** of it in under 30 minutes.

3. Reflection:  
   - Write a short paragraph: “How my idea‑generation changed this week” with at least 3 concrete observations.

### Assistant: I’ll make Week 2 about **turning ideas into short narratives and expressive pieces**, still in ~20 minutes per day. The goal is to improve how you shape and communicate ideas, not just generate them.

**Overall Week 2 goals**
- Build confidence in expressing ideas through short writing.
- Practice character, setting, and conflict in tiny doses.
- Learn to revise quickly without perfectionism.

**Daily outline (20 minutes)**
- 3–4 min: Warm‑up (freewriting, sentence games).
- 10–12 min: Main storytelling exercise.
- 4–5 min: Reflection and one quick revision pass.

**Day 1 – 6‑sentence stories**
- Warm‑up: 3 minutes of non‑stop freewriting about your morning.  
- Main: Choose one idea from Week 1. Turn it into a **6‑sentence story**:  
  1. Setup (who/where).  
  2. Problem.  
  3. Attempt.  
  4. Complication.  
  5. Climax.  
  6. Resolution.  
- Reflection: Underline your favorite sentence and write why it works.

**Day 2 – Character snapshots**
- Warm‑up: Write 5 unusual character traits (e.g., “can only lie on Tuesdays”).  
- Main: Pick one trait and write a **200‑word scene** showing the character dealing with a small problem (no explanation, just show).  
- Reflection: Write 2 sentences on what made the character feel real or flat.

**Day 3 – Setting as character**
- Warm‑up: List 5 places you know well.  
- Main: Choose one and describe it as if it were a person with moods and opinions (about 150–200 words).  
- Reflection: Circle 3 sensory details (sight, sound, smell, touch, taste) you used.

**Day 4 – Dialogue only**
- Warm‑up: Write 5 lines someone might say when they’re hiding something.  
- Main: Write a **dialogue‑only scene** (no description) between two people arguing about something trivial that really represents something deeper (e.g., dishes representing respect).  
- Reflection: Mark the line where the subtext (what they’re really arguing about) feels clearest.

**Day 5 – Rewrite from another angle**
- Warm‑up: Write the same sentence in 3 different emotional tones (neutral, angry, joyful).  
- Main: Pick one story or scene from earlier in the week. Rewrite it **from a different character’s point of view** or in a different tense (past → present).  
- Reflection: Note what changed in the feeling of the story.

**Day 6 – Micro‑memoir**
- Warm‑up: List 5 vivid memories in bullet points.  
- Main: Choose one and write a **250‑word micro‑memoir** (true story). Focus on concrete details, not big explanations.  
- Reflection: Write 1–2 sentences on what surprised you about your own memory.

**Day 7 – Remix and polish**
- Warm‑up: 3 minutes of free association: write anything that comes to mind about “change.”  
- Main: Pick your favorite piece from this week. Spend 10–12 minutes revising it: tighten sentences, add one strong image, cut anything that feels dull.  
- Reflection: Save this as a “checkpoint piece” so you can compare your narrative skills later.

I’ll give you a **new Week 2: Visual Observation & Sketching Basics (no art background required)**, 20 minutes per day:

**Week 2 – Visual Creativity: Seeing and Simplifying**

**Day 1 – Shapes everywhere**
- 5 min: Walk around your space (or look out a window). Identify **basic shapes** (circles, rectangles, triangles) in objects around you and quickly list them.  
- 10 min: On paper, **reduce three objects** to simple shapes. Example: a chair = rectangles + cylinders. No shading, just outlines.  
- 5 min: Note which object was hardest to simplify and why.

**Day 2 – Negative space**
- 5 min: Choose an object (e.g., a plant). Instead of the object, look at the **spaces around and between its parts**.  
- 10 min: Make a quick sketch focusing only on those empty spaces’ shapes. Accuracy doesn’t matter; noticing does.  
- 5 min: Jot down what you noticed that you’d usually ignore.

**Day 3 – Value and contrast (in simple terms)**
- 5 min: Look at a photo (any photo). Identify the **darkest, lightest, and mid‑tone areas**.  
- 10 min: On paper, make a **3‑value study**: block in dark, medium, and light regions with a pencil or pen pressure.  
- 5 min: Write which part of the image your eye goes to first and why.

**Day 4 – Quick thumbnails**
- 5 min: Choose a scene (your desk, a corner of a room, or a photo).  
- 10 min: Draw **three tiny thumbnail sketches** (each no bigger than a business card) of the same scene with different compositions (crop closer, change angle, etc.).  
- 5 min: Mark the thumbnail that feels most interesting and note what makes it work (e.g., strong diagonal, clear focal point).

**Day 5 – Visual metaphors**
- 5 min: Pick an emotion (e.g., anxiety, calm, excitement). List 5 visual elements that could express it (spiky lines, soft curves, crowded space…).  
- 10 min: Create a **simple abstract sketch** using only lines, shapes, and dark/light to show that emotion. No recognizable objects needed.  
- 5 min: Write a one‑sentence “caption” describing what you were trying to convey.

**Day 6 – Object redesign**
- 5 min: Choose a mundane object (a spoon, a lamp, a backpack). Quickly note its main functions.  
- 10 min: Sketch **two alternate designs** for the object: one exaggerated for aesthetics, one exaggerated for function.  
- 5 min: Note which design feels more creative to you and why.

**Day 7 – Visual review**
- 5 min: Lay out (or flip through) your week’s sketches.  
- 10 min: Choose **two pieces** and spend a few minutes refining each: clarify lines, adjust composition, or push contrast.  
- 5 min: Write 3 bullet points on how your visual perception changed this week.

### Assistant: I’ll design Week 3 around **visual storytelling through mini‑comics and storyboards**, still in daily 20‑minute sessions.

**Week 3 – Visual Storytelling & Mini‑Comics**

**Overall goals**
- Turn moments and ideas into clear visual sequences.
- Practice camera angles, panels, and pacing.
- Focus on clarity, not drawing quality.

**Day 1 – 3‑panel moments**
- 5 min: List 5 everyday micro‑events (e.g., making tea, missing a bus).  
- 10 min: Choose one and draw a **3‑panel comic** showing:  
  1. Setup  
  2. Tension or twist  
  3. Result  
  Use stick figures if you want.  
- 5 min: Show it to someone (or imagine a friend) and quickly note whether the sequence would be understandable with no words.

**Day 2 – Expressions and body language**
- 5 min: Write a list of 5 emotions (bored, excited, annoyed, curious, nervous).  
- 10 min: For each emotion, draw a **simple face + body pose** that communicates it. Don’t worry about anatomy; exaggerate.  
- 5 min: Circle the clearest two and note what made them easy to read (eyebrows, posture, spacing).

**Day 3 – Camera angles**
- 5 min: Write 3 short actions (e.g., someone opening a door, someone finding a note).  
- 10 min: For each action, sketch it from **two different angles** (e.g., wide shot vs. close‑up, eye‑level vs. top‑down).  
- 5 min: Mark which angle makes the action feel more dramatic or clear.

**Day 4 – Silent comic**
- 5 min: Choose a tiny story (e.g., someone loses something and finds something else instead).  
- 10 min: Draw a **4–6 panel silent comic** (no dialogue or text). Focus on clear actions and expressions.  
- 5 min: Check if someone could understand the basic story just from the pictures; note any confusing panel.

**Day 5 – Using panels for pacing**
- 5 min: Think of a simple joke or surprise.  
- 10 min: Draw it twice, each as a **4‑panel comic**:  
  - Version A: equal‑sized panels.  
  - Version B: vary panel sizes to slow down or speed up moments (larger panel for key moment, smaller for quick beats).  
- 5 min: Note which version delivers the joke or surprise better and why.

**Day 6 – From script to storyboard**
- 5 min: Write a **3–4 sentence “script”** for a short scene (e.g., someone trying to sneak a snack at night and getting caught).  
- 10 min: Turn it into a **rough storyboard** with 6–8 panels: quick sketches and arrows showing motion.  
- 5 min: Under each panel, add 2–3 words describing the key action or emotion.

**Day 7 – Mini‑project**
- 5 min: Pick your favorite idea from the week (a joke, moment, or short story).  
- 10 min: Create a **final 6–8 panel mini‑comic or storyboard** combining what you practiced: clear expressions, varied angles, and purposeful panel sizes.  
- 5 min: Write a short note on what you’d improve if you had another 30 minutes.

After this week, I can push you into a small 1–2 week capstone project where you design a short comic or visual zine from scratch, using both idea‑generation and visual storytelling skills you’ve practiced.
