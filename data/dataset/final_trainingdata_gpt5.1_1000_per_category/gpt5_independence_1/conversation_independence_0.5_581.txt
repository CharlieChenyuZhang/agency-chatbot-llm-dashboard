Race-day strategy overview
1) Pacing:
   - First 5 km: Go even easier than you think. Adrenaline makes everything feel easy.
   - From 5–30 km: Aim for steady, easy/long-run pace—no hero moves.
   - After 30 km: Assess honestly; if you feel okay, keep steady; if you feel strong (rare on a first marathon), you can gradually nudge a little faster.

2) Fuel:
   - Breakfast: Similar to your long-run breakfast you practiced in training.
   - During race: Same gels/drinks you used on long runs—no new brands/flavors.
   - Start fueling early (around 30–45 min in) and keep a steady schedule (e.g., 1 gel every 30–40 min).

3) Hydration:
   - Drink small amounts regularly at aid stations.
   - Don’t force huge amounts of water; sip according to thirst, slightly proactive if it’s hot.

4) Bathroom:
   - Use the bathroom 30–45 minutes before the start if possible.
   - Avoid super high-fiber or very heavy/fatty foods the night before and morning of.
   - Many runners swear by a “routine” (coffee + breakfast at a set time) practiced on long runs.

5) Mental:
   - Break the race into chunks (0–10 km, 10–21, 21–32, 32–42).
   - Have a few mantras ready (“relax and go,” “one more km,” etc.).
   - Expect the rough patch around 30–35 km; it’s normal, not failure.
