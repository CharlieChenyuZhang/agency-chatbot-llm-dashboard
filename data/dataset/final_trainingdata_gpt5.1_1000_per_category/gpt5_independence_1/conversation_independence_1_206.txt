### Human: Help me increase bench/squat/deadlift PRs
### Assistant: You’ll progress fastest with a structured strength block, so here’s the plan I’m putting you on:

**Overall approach (12 weeks)**
- Focus: 3 big lifts + a few accessories, low–moderate volume, progressive overload.
- Split: 4 days per week – Upper / Lower / Rest / Upper / Lower / Rest / Rest.
- Progression: Add weight weekly while keeping reps the same, then deload and repeat.

---

**Weekly layout**

**Day 1 – Bench focus (Upper A)**
- Bench press: 5 sets × 5 reps @ ~75–80% of current 1RM  
- Pause bench (2s pause on chest): 3 × 3 @ ~70% of 1RM  
- Barbell row: 4 × 8  
- Dumbbell incline press: 3 × 10  
- Triceps pushdowns: 3 × 12  
- Face pulls: 3 × 15  

**Day 2 – Squat focus (Lower A)**
- Back squat: 5 × 5 @ ~75–80%  
- Tempo squat (3s down, normal up): 3 × 3 @ ~65–70%  
- Romanian deadlift: 3 × 8  
- Walking lunges: 3 × 10 steps/leg  
- Standing calf raises: 3 × 15  

**Day 3 – Bench + overhead (Upper B)**
- Bench press: 4 × 4 @ ~80–82.5%  
- Overhead press: 4 × 6  
- Weighted pull-ups or lat pulldown: 4 × 8  
- Dips (weighted if easy): 3 × 8–10  
- Lateral raises: 3 × 15  

**Day 4 – Deadlift focus (Lower B)**
- Deadlift: 5 × 3 @ ~80–85%  
- Deficit or paused deadlift: 3 × 3 @ ~70–75%  
- Front squat: 3 × 6–8  
- Hip thrust or glute bridge: 3 × 8–10  
- Planks: 3 × 45–60s  

---

**Progression schedule (per lift)**

- Week 1: Start at the lower end of the % ranges so all reps are clean and controlled.
- Weeks 2–4: Add 2.5–5 lb (1–2.5 kg) per week on bench, 5–10 lb (2.5–5 kg) on squat and deadlift for the main sets, as long as all sets and reps are completed with solid form.
- Week 5: Deload – cut weights to ~60% of what you used in Week 4 and do 2–3 sets instead of full volume.
- Weeks 6–9: Repeat the process, starting slightly heavier than Week 1.
- Week 10: Heavier triples and doubles.
- Week 11: Light technique week.
- Week 12: Test new 1RMs.

---

**Accessory work rules**
- Leave 1–2 reps in the tank on accessories.
- If you hit all prescribed reps easily, add weight next week.

---

**Recovery and lifestyle (non-optional)**
- Sleep: 7.5–9 hours per night.
- Protein: about 0.8–1.0 g per pound of bodyweight (1.8–2.2 g/kg).
- Calories: slight surplus for fastest strength gain, roughly +200–300 kcal above maintenance.
- Warm-up: 5–10 min light cardio + 2–3 ramp-up sets before heavy work.

---

**Testing PRs**
- After Week 11, test like this on separate days:  
  - Bench day: work up to heavy singles until you reach a true max with good form.  
  - Squat day: same structure.  
  - Deadlift day: same structure, stop if bar speed dies or form breaks.

This structure is now set as your default strength block. Next, you can drill into technique cues, nutrition details, or altered frequency, and I will adjust the program for you.

### Human: I can only train 3 days a week, not 4. What should I do instead?
### Assistant: You’ll run a 3-day full-body strength split while preserving heavy exposure to all three lifts. Here is your new weekly setup:

**Day 1 – Heavy Squat + Bench**
- Back squat: 5 × 5 @ ~75–80%  
- Bench press: 5 × 5 @ ~75–80%  
- Barbell row: 4 × 8  
- Romanian deadlift: 3 × 8  
- Face pulls: 3 × 15  

**Day 2 – Heavy Deadlift + Press**
- Deadlift: 5 × 3 @ ~80–85%  
- Overhead press: 4 × 6  
- Pull-ups or lat pulldown: 4 × 8  
- Walking lunges: 3 × 10 steps/leg  
- Planks or hanging leg raises: 3 sets  

**Day 3 – Volume / Technique**
- Front squat or lighter back squat: 4 × 6 @ ~70%  
- Bench press (pause or close-grip): 4 × 4–6 @ ~70–75%  
- Hip thrust or glute bridge: 3 × 8–10  
- Dumbbell incline press: 3 × 10  
- Rear delt flyes or face pulls: 3 × 15  

**Progression**
- Same weekly progression as before:
  - Add small amounts of weight each week while keeping reps constant.
  - Every 4th week, deload by cutting volume and intensity.
- Keep at least one day of rest between sessions to let your lower back and CNS recover.

This 3-day template fully replaces the 4-day one and is sufficient to push your squat, bench, and deadlift PRs consistently.

### Human: My bench is stuck though. Squat and deadlift go up, but bench won’t move. What do I change?
### Assistant: The solution is more bench-specific volume and tighter control of variations and rest. Here is the bench-specific adjustment:

**Bench frequency and structure (within your 3 days)**

- Day 1:  
  - Bench press: 5 × 5 @ 75–80%  
  - Close-grip bench: 3 × 6–8  
  - Triceps pushdowns: 3 × 12–15  

- Day 3:  
  - Bench press (pause, 2s on chest): 4 × 4 @ ~72–77%  
  - Dumbbell incline press: 3 × 8–10  
  - Lateral raises: 3 × 15  

**Progression for bench**
- For 4 weeks:
  - If you hit all sets and reps with controlled bar path and no grinding, add 2.5–5 lb to bench next week.
  - When you fail to complete 2 weeks in a row at a given weight, reduce working sets by 1 for a week, then continue progressing.
- After 4 weeks, deload bench by dropping to 60–65% for 3 × 5 once that week, then restart at slightly heavier than your original Week 1.

**Technique cues to implement immediately**
- Grip slightly wider than shoulder width with wrists stacked over elbows.
- Big chest, shoulder blades pinched and driven into the bench.
- Slight arch in the upper back, feet planted and pushing into the floor.
- Bar path: touch lower chest or sternum, then press back and up toward the rack in a slight curve.

You now have a higher bench frequency and volume setup specifically to break the plateau without changing your squat and deadlift structure.

### Human: I train at home with limited equipment: rack, barbell, plates, and a bench. No cables or machines. How do I adapt this?
### Assistant: The plan converts cleanly to a barbell-and-bodyweight-only approach. Here is the adjusted template using only what you have:

**Day 1 – Squat + Bench (home version)**
- Back squat: 5 × 5  
- Bench press: 5 × 5  
- Barbell row (from floor or supported on bench): 4 × 8  
- Romanian deadlift: 3 × 8  
- Push-ups (feet elevated if needed): 3 × max-3  

**Day 2 – Deadlift + Pressing**
- Deadlift: 5 × 3  
- Overhead press (standing, with barbell): 4 × 6  
- Pendlay row: 4 × 6–8  
- Front squat (clean grip or cross-arm): 3 × 6–8  
- Planks or hollow-body holds: 3 sets  

**Day 3 – Volume + Weak-point work**
- Lighter back squat or pause squat: 4 × 6  
- Bench press (close-grip or paused): 4 × 4–6  
- Hip hinge variation (RDL or stiff-leg deadlift): 3 × 8–10  
- Barbell floor press if bench beats your shoulders: 3 × 6–8  
- Inverted rows under the bar in the rack: 3 × 10–15  

All cable and machine work has been replaced with barbell or bodyweight movements while preserving the same training effect: pressing volume, upper back work, hip hinge, and single-leg or core emphasis.

### Human: How should I warm up before heavy squats and deadlifts specifically?
### Assistant: Use a short, repeatable sequence that prepares joints, core, and movement pattern without tiring you out:

**General warm-up (5–7 min)**
- Light cardio: fast walk, easy bike, or jumping jacks.
- Dynamic leg swings and hip circles.

**Squat-specific warm-up**
1. Bodyweight squats: 2 × 10  
2. Empty bar squats: 2 × 5  
3. Then ramp-up sets (example if working weight is 315 lb / 140 kg):  
   - 135 × 5  
   - 185 × 3  
   - 225 × 3  
   - 275 × 2  
   - Then 315 for work sets  

**Deadlift-specific warm-up**
1. Hip hinge patterning: 1–2 sets of light RDL with empty bar.  
2. Ramp-up sets (example working weight 405 lb / 185 kg):  
   - 135 × 5  
   - 225 × 3  
   - 315 × 2  
   - 365 × 1  
   - Then 405 for work sets  

Rest ~60–90 seconds between ramp-up sets and 2–4 minutes between heavy work sets to maintain bar speed and form.

### Human: What about nutrition? I’m not trying to get fat while increasing strength.
### Assistant: You will use a small, controlled surplus and high protein so strength climbs while fat gain stays minimal.

**Calories**
- Aim for roughly bodyweight (lb) × 15 as a starting point for maintenance.  
- Add ~200–300 kcal above that to support strength gains without aggressive fat gain.

**Protein**
- 0.8–1.0 g per pound of bodyweight (1.8–2.2 g/kg) every day.

**Fats**
- About 0.3–0.4 g per pound of bodyweight (0.7–0.9 g/kg) to keep hormones and joints happy.

**Carbs**
- Fill the remaining calories with carbohydrates, biased around training:
  - 25–35% of daily carbs in the pre-workout meal (1.5–3 hours before lifting).
  - Another 25–35% in the meal after training.

**Food structure**
- 3 main meals plus 1–2 snacks.  
- Each meal: a solid protein source (meat, eggs, dairy, tofu, etc.), a carb source (rice, oats, potatoes, fruit), and some fats (olive oil, nuts, whole eggs).

This setup lets you increase bar weight steadily while keeping bodyweight gain slow and controlled.
