### Human: Ok, what should I do for my knee until I see him?

### Assistant: I’ll put you on a simple, joint-friendly plan that avoids overloading the knee while maintaining strength and mobility. I’ll assume your pain is bothersome but not an emergency (no inability to bear weight at all, no major deformity).

**Short-term knee care plan (until you see the orthopedist)**

1. **Activity modification**  
   - Avoid running, jumping, deep squats, and lunges.  
   - Use stairs slowly and hold the railing; prefer elevators if available.  
   - If walking more than 10–15 minutes worsens pain, break it into shorter walks.

2. **Pain and swelling control**  
   - **Ice:** 15–20 minutes at a time, 2–3 times per day, with a thin cloth between ice and skin.  
   - **Elevation:** When sitting or lying down, prop the leg so the knee is above heart level when possible.

3. **Basic home exercises (once or twice daily, pain allowing)**  
   - **Quad set:** Sit with the leg straight, tighten the thigh muscle to press the back of the knee down into the surface, hold 5 seconds, relax. Do 2 sets of 10.  
   - **Straight leg raise:** Lie on your back, one leg bent, sore leg straight. Tighten thigh and lift the straight leg about 12 inches, hold 3 seconds, lower slowly. 2 sets of 10.  
   - **Heel slides:** Lying on your back, slowly slide the heel toward your buttocks to bend the knee, then straighten again, within a comfortable range. 2 sets of 10.

   Stop or shorten any exercise that causes sharp, catching, or worsening pain.

4. **Support**  
   - Use a simple elastic knee sleeve during activity for light support and warmth.  
   - If you feel unsteady, use a cane held in the opposite hand from the painful knee for longer walks.

5. **When to seek urgent care instead of waiting**  
   - Sudden major swelling, inability to bear any weight at all, visible deformity, fever with a hot, red knee, or a recent significant fall.

At your visit with Dr. Alvarez, I want you to bring a written description of how these steps affect your pain, since that helps narrow down the cause.
