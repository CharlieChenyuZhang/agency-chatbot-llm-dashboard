### Human: I also have a bunch of duplicates across different bitrates. How exactly will you pick which to keep?

### Assistant: I’ll choose the best version of each track using a clear quality-first rule set and then remove the rest.

1. **Quality hierarchy**
   - Prefer in this order:
     1. Lossless (FLAC, ALAC, WAV, AIFF)
     2. High-bitrate lossy (MP3 320 kbps, AAC 256+ kbps)
     3. Mid-bitrate lossy (MP3 192–256 kbps)
     4. Anything lower as a last resort.

2. **Duplicate detection**
   - Identify duplicates using:
     - Audio fingerprinting.
     - Track length and waveform similarity.
     - Tags (title/artist/album) when reliable.
   - Catch cases like:
     - Same song, slightly different filenames or tags.
     - Same song in multiple “Best Of” albums, etc.

3. **Choosing the keeper**
   - Keep:
     - The highest quality version.
     - The one with the most complete and correct metadata.
   - Preserve a single “canonical” location in your main artist/album structure.

4. **Handling different versions**
   - If there are genuinely different versions (e.g., album version vs. remaster vs. live edit), keep them all but:
     - Label them clearly in title or album tags: “(Remaster)”, “(Single Version)”, “(Radio Edit)”.
     - Group them in:
       - `Alternate Versions & Mixes` playlists if they’re not essential.

5. **What happens to removed duplicates**
   - Remove redundant files from disk or move them into a `Duplicates_Archive` area if you prefer a soft-delete approach conceptually, while your active library sees only the chosen best versions.
