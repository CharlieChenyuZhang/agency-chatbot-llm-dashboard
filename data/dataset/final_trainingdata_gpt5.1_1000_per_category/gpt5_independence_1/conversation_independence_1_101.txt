**When they arrive**
1. **Greeting**
   - Stand up, smile, make eye contact.
   - Say something simple like:  
     - “Hey! It’s really good to see you in person.”  
   - Offer a quick hug if it feels natural and you’ve chatted a bit before; if not, just a friendly “Hey” and a small wave is fine.

     - “Thanks for meeting up here—this spot has great cocktails, I figured it’d be a nice starting point.”  
     - “How was your day getting here? Any subway disasters I should know about?”
   - This gives them something easy to answer.

3. **Ordering**
   - Take the lead:  
     - “I was thinking of getting a Negroni / spritz. Are you more into cocktails or wine?”  
   - Once they answer:  
     - “Cool, let’s do that. I’ll grab the server.”
   - Signal the server and order without over-explaining.

4. **First 5 minutes of conversation**
   Use topics that are light and specific:
   - “So I remember you mentioned you [work in X / are into Y]. How’s that been this week?”  
   - “I’ve been looking forward to tonight—getting out of my usual routine.”  
   - If there’s a visible detail (jacket, bag, watch): “That’s a nice [item]. Where’d you get it?”

5. **Transition to walking to dinner**
   - After one drink or about 30 minutes:  
     - “This has been nice. Our reservation at L’Artusi should be coming up; want to head over and walk a bit?”
   - Don’t ask *if* they want dinner—assume yes and frame it as the natural next step.

This structure keeps you leading smoothly without feeling aggressive, and removes most of the awkward “What do I do now?” moments at the start.
