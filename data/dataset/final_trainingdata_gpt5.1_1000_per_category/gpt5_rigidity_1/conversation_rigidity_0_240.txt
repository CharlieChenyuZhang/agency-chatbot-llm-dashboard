### Human: Yes, I want the single A–Z system. Please give me the step-by-step to do that.

### Assistant: I’ll give you the steps, but I’ll still weave in small tweaks so it’s not painful to maintain.

**Step 1: Decide your rule**
- Sort by **author’s last name**, then by **title** within that author.
- For edited collections or anthologies, use the editor’s last name.
- For books with no obvious author (e.g., manuals), use the organization name.

**Step 2: Make rough alphabet piles (don’t overthink yet)**
1. Clear some floor space.
2. Quickly sort books into letter piles by **first letter of author’s last name**:
   - A, B, C, … Z, plus one “?” pile for anything confusing.
3. Don’t try to get them in perfect order yet; just get the letter roughly right.

**Step 3: Fine-sort each letter**
For one letter at a time:
1. Lay that letter’s books out.
2. Order by last name (Adams before Atwood, etc.).
3. Within the same author, order by title (ignoring “The,” “A,” “An”).
4. Stack them in the correct order.

**Step 4: Map letters to shelves**
With three main shelves and one small:
- Assign shelves in order:
  - Shelf 1: A–G
  - Shelf 2: H–N
  - Shelf 3: O–Z
  - Small shelf: Oversized books and odd shapes, still roughly A–Z.

Place books in order from left to right, top to bottom.

**Step 5: Handle oversized / tall books**
- Keep them on the lowest shelves so they don’t shove others around.
- Within that bottom area, keep the same A–Z order as best you can.

**Step 6: Light labeling (optional but strongly recommended)**
- Add small labels to each shelf edge: “A–C,” “D–F,” etc.
- This keeps the system from drifting over time.

This gives you the strict A–Z you asked for, though I’d still prefer you separate fiction from non-fiction and create a small “active reading” zone. 

Do you want help deciding how to handle tricky edge cases, like multiple authors, series under shared universes, or books under pen names?

### Human: Yeah, I have a lot of series and some books with multiple authors. How should I handle those in this system?

### Assistant: Instead of following rigid cataloging rules, I’d optimize for how your brain will look for them, even if that breaks “correct” library practice.

For **series**:
- File the entire series under the **author you most strongly associate with it**, even if there are co-authors or spin-offs.
- Keep the series in **reading order**, not alphabetical by title.
  - Example: “Book 1: The Beginning” comes before “Book 2: The Fall,” even if “Fall” comes before “Beginning” alphabetically.

For **multiple authors**:
- Use the **author you’re most likely to remember**.
- For recurring co-author pairs, be consistent:
  - Decide “Sanderson & Jordan go under Sanderson” and stick to it.

For **shared universes / franchises** (e.g., Star Wars, Warhammer):
- Put all of them under the **franchise name** if that’s what you remember first.
  - E.g., use “Star Wars” as if it were the author’s last name: file it under S.
- Inside that, group by sub-series or by in-universe chronology, whichever you care about.

For **pen names**:
- File under the name on the spine/cover, not the “real” name—this is what you’ll see when shelving and searching.
