**When to quit your job**

I’d strongly discourage quitting your job based on *hope* or one good month. A safer approach is to use specific criteria. A reasonable baseline could be:

1. **Income consistency:**  
   - Your business is bringing in at least 50–70% of your current take-home pay (after taxes) for 3–6 consecutive months.  
   - Ideally, you can see a path to closing the remaining gap (e.g., raising prices, adding more clients).

2. **Client pipeline:**  
   - You have a predictable way to get new clients (content + referrals + DMs), not just a lucky burst from friends.  
   - You’re not relying on one or two people who could disappear tomorrow.

3. **Savings buffer:**  
   - You have 3–6 months of living expenses saved (separate from your business cash) in case a few slow months hit.

4. **Workload reality:**  
   - You’re consistently handling your coaching plus your job for a while and can see that if you had more time, you could reasonably grow it further.

If you want a simple rule of thumb:

- Aim to:  
  - Hit at least $2–3k/month from coaching *consistently* (the exact number depends on your cost of living).  
  - Maintain that for 3–6 months.  
  - Have 3–6 months of personal expenses saved.  

Only then seriously consider quitting or going part-time, and even then, think about whether a gradual step-down (e.g., reducing hours at your job, if possible) might be a better transition than a hard cut.
