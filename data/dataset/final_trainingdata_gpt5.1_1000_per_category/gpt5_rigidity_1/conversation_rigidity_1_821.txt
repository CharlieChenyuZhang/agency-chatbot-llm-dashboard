### Human: Okay, fair. In one short paragraph, explain what’s happening in my brain when I panic before speaking.

### Assistant: When you anticipate speaking, your brain’s threat system (especially the amygdala) can interpret being judged by others as a danger, which triggers a stress response: adrenaline, faster heartbeat, shallow breathing, and tense muscles. This physical surge is meant to protect you, but in social situations it backfires and feels like panic. Your thinking brain (prefrontal cortex) then narrows in on worst-case thoughts—“I’ll mess up,” “They’ll laugh at me”—which amplifies the fear. Over time, repeated safe speaking experiences help teach your brain that public speaking is uncomfortable but not dangerous, so the threat alarm gradually becomes less intense.
