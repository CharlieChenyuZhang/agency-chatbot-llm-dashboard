### Human: I can probably do that. One more thing: I kind of want my team to take me more seriously, maybe even be a little afraid of disappointing me so they perform better. How do I do that without being a total tyrant?

### Assistant: You’re not alone in feeling that way—many new leaders think, “If they’re a bit afraid of disappointing me, they’ll step up.”

The tricky part is: fear can create short-term compliance, but it usually damages long-term trust, creativity, and honesty. People start hiding problems instead of bringing them to you.

Instead of aiming for “fear,” I’d recommend aiming for:
- Clear standards  
- Consistent follow-through  
- Uncertainty about outcomes (in a fair way): if they repeatedly drop the ball, there *are* consequences

Here’s how to get “taken seriously” without ruling by fear:

1) Be explicit about standards  
“On this team, I expect: [e.g., owning your commitments, flagging risks early, reviewing your work before shipping]. I will hold us to this, including myself.”

2) Follow through consistently  
If someone repeatedly misses commitments:
- First time: clarify expectations  
- Second time: more direct conversation: “This is now a pattern and we need to address it.”  
- If it continues: appropriate consequences (less autonomy on critical work, formal performance discussion, etc., depending on your company norms)

3) Balance accountability with support  
“When something goes wrong, I won’t ignore it—but I’ll also help you figure out how to fix it and avoid it next time.”

People then think: “I respect them. I don’t want to disappoint them because I value their opinion,” not “I’m scared of them.”
