## The problem: Email is an infinite to-do list for other people’s priorities

Your inbox is:
- Unbounded (more can always arrive)
- Mixed (urgent + trivial in the same place)
- Rewarding (tiny dopamine hits every time you clear a message)

That’s a terrible place to make decisions about your day.

---

## The core idea: Timebox email, don’t sprinkle it

Instead of “always on” email, you schedule **2–3 focused email blocks** per day and treat those as work sessions, just like any other task.

The rest of the time, your inbox is closed.

Why this works:
- You batch similar tasks.
- You protect long, uninterrupted focus blocks.
- You force yourself to separate “real work” from “communication about work.”

---

## How to implement this in 3 steps

**Step 1: Choose your email windows**  
Pick 2 or 3 slots in your day, e.g.:
- 10:30–11:00
- 2:30–3:00
- 4:30–4:45 (optional, quick cleanup)

Put these on your calendar as “Email block.”

**Step 2: Close your inbox outside these times**  
- Turn off desktop notifications.
- Remove email from your phone home screen (or log out during focus blocks).
- If needed, set a Slack/Teams status: “Heads down—checking email at 10:30.”

**Step 3: Use a simple processing rule during each block**  
During your email block, go message by message and:
- **Do** (if it takes <2 minutes),
- **Defer** (schedule it as a task with a time),
- **Delegate** (forward with clear instructions),
- **Delete/Archive** (if it doesn’t need action).

No re-reading. No circling back. One pass.

---

## A quick script for expectations

If you’re worried about people expecting instant replies, you can use something like:

> “I batch email responses to protect focus time, so you’ll usually hear back from me within a business day. If something is truly urgent, please [call / Slack / text].”

You can add a lighter version of this to your email signature.
