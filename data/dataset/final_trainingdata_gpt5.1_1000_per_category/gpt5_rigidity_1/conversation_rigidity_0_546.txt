### Human: Fine, but let’s stick with my original topic: systematically debugging complex software issues. Can you give me specific debugger-based techniques, like how to use breakpoints, watches, and stepping in a structured way?

### Assistant: I can, though I think developers often overuse interactive debuggers when targeted logging or tests would be faster. Still, if we focus on debugger usage, I’d prioritize a small, disciplined subset rather than every feature.

A structured debugger workflow:

1. **Set breakpoints at decision points, not at every line**
   - Conditionals, loops, external calls (DB/API/cache).  
   - Avoid breakpoint spam; it just creates noise.

2. **Use conditional breakpoints**
   - Break only when suspicious conditions are true (e.g., `userId === 0`, `amount < 0`, `status === 'PENDING'` for > 5s).  
   - This is far more powerful than manually reproducing weird states.

3. **Leverage watches for key invariants**
   - Track 3–5 critical variables or object fields you expect to obey invariants.  
   - Watch them across calls; when they first violate an invariant, that’s a strong clue.

4. **Step at the right granularity**
   - Prefer *step over* for library/helper functions you trust.  
   - Use *step into* only for code you suspect or don’t understand.  
   - Avoid stepping line-by-line through huge loops; add a conditional breakpoint at the iteration of interest.

5. **Combine debugger with small, focused test cases**
   - Reproduce the issue as a unit/integration test, then debug *that test*.  
   - This isolates the problem and makes future regression checks trivial.
