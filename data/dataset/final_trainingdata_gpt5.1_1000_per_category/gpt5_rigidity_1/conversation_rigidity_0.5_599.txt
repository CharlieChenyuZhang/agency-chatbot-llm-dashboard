### Human: I’m on Windows 11, mainly using OBS for recording and Zoom for calls. Can you give me a detailed chain: like EQ settings, compression, maybe noise gate? I don’t want to sound super processed though.

### Assistant: Let’s build a light, natural-sounding chain you can reuse in OBS and (partially) in Zoom.

You can use OBS filters on your mic input. Zoom has built-in noise reduction, but far fewer controls, so I’ll note where things differ.

**Order of processing in OBS (recommended):**
1) Noise suppression (gentle)
2) Noise gate
3) EQ
4) Compressor
5) Limiter (optional but recommended)

#### 1) Noise Suppression (OBS)
- Filter: “Noise Suppression”
- Method: RNNoise (if CPU allows; otherwise Speex at low strength)
- Starting value:
  - Speex: –15 dB to –20 dB
  - RNNoise: leave default and just listen

You want fan noise reduced but not making your voice warbly. If it starts sounding underwater, back off.

In Zoom:
- Settings → Audio → Suppress background noise: set to “Low” or “Auto” (avoid “High” for a natural voice unless your environment is really bad).

#### 2) Noise Gate (OBS)
Use conservative settings to avoid chopping words:

- Close Threshold: around –40 dB
- Open Threshold: around –35 dB
- Attack Time: 10 ms
- Hold Time: 150 ms
- Release Time: 150–200 ms

Adjust thresholds so:
- Normal speaking always opens the gate.
- Quiet room noise doesn’t.

#### 3) EQ (OBS)
Use the “VST 2.x Plug-in” filter with a simple EQ plugin (e.g., TDR Nova, free) if available. If you don’t want to mess with VSTs, you can skip EQ and rely on mic positioning; that’s the “less processed” path. But if you’re okay with light EQ:

**Starting EQ (MV7X close-mic’d)**  
- High-pass filter: 70–80 Hz, gentle slope  
- Low mids: slight cut around 200–300 Hz (–2 to –3 dB, wide Q) if it sounds boomy or boxy  
- Presence: slight boost at 3–4 kHz (+2 dB) for clarity if needed  
- Sibilance (optional): if “s” sounds are sharp, tiny cut around 6–8 kHz (–2 dB, fairly narrow)

Listen and adjust by 1–2 dB at a time—small moves.

#### 4) Compression (OBS)
Filter: “Compressor”

Starting values:
- Ratio: 3:1
- Threshold: around –18 dB (adjust so it compresses a bit on normal speech)
- Attack: 5–10 ms
- Release: 100–150 ms
- Output Gain: add back 2–4 dB so your average level is healthy
- Sidechain/Ducking: leave off for now

You want the gain reduction meter showing maybe 2–6 dB reduction when you talk normally. More than that starts sounding obviously processed.

#### 5) Limiter (OBS)
Filter: “Limiter”

- Threshold: –3 dB
- Release: 60–100 ms

This just catches random loud laughs or spikes so you don’t clip.

But you’re right to question complexity. Here’s the tradeoff:

**USB condenser (e.g., Blue Yeti):**
- Pros:
  - Plug-and-play, no interface needed
  - Easy to move between computers
- Cons:
  - Very sensitive: picks up fan noise, keyboard, room echo
  - Room treatment becomes more important
  - Many people end up fighting noise and harshness with plugins

**Dynamic XLR (MV7X + small interface):**
- Pros:
  - Better rejection of room and fan, especially close up
  - Deeper voices often sound more controlled and “broadcast-y”
  - Interface opens the door to future mic upgrades if you ever want
- Cons:
  - Slightly more setup (drivers, gain staging)
  - More cables and a box on your desk

If you want the *simplest possible* decent solution and are ok with some extra room noise, an alternative middle ground could be:

- **Shure MV7 (USB + XLR combo)**  
  - You can start via USB only (no interface, very simple)  
  - Later, if you want more control, you can add an XLR interface and switch to XLR  
  - Still a dynamic mic, so better in your room than a Yeti

It costs more than the MV7X + cheap interface combo, so it might push your budget, but it gives you simplicity now and flexibility later.

If your priority is:
- **Simplicity now:** MV7 over USB is a strong option.  
- **Best sound in that room within budget:** MV7X + Scarlett Solo/MOTU M2 is still the better long-term value.

**Limiter in MOTIV:**

### Human: I’m not moving much, and I’d like a slightly “radio” sound but still natural. Is there anything else I should do to get closer to that without spending more? Like cheap room treatment or positioning tips?

### Assistant: You can get noticeably closer to a “radio” sound with a few cheap, practical tweaks—no extra gear required beyond small, inexpensive items.

**1) Positioning relative to the room**
- Put your **back** toward the most reflective surface (like a bare wall) and the mic facing into the room.  
  - Better: sit with your back to a closet full of clothes; that acts as a natural absorber.
- Avoid sitting right in the middle of the room (often the worst spot for low-frequency reflections).

**2) Basic DIY “treatment”**
You don’t need pro panels immediately. Try:

- Hang a thick blanket or duvet on the wall that’s **behind the mic and in front of you** (the direction your voice travels).
- If possible, have a rug on the floor under your chair/desk to reduce reflections from the floor.
- Stack a couple of pillows or a folded blanket on your desk behind the mic stand to break early reflections off the desktop.

These won’t fully “treat” the room, but they’ll soften the echo enough to help that close, focused radio tone.

**3) Voice and mic technique for “radio” but natural**
- Distance: keep that solid 2–3 inches.
- Consistency: speak at a steady level directly toward the mic capsule.
- Slight proximity effect: being close adds low-end warmth. If it gets too boomy:
  - Turn on the high-pass filter in MOTIV, or
  - Back off the bass slightly in the EQ there.

**4) Tuning for a mild “radio” curve in MOTIV**
To lean a bit radio-like without sounding fake:

- High-pass: ON.
- Slight low boost (if available in MOTIV’s EQ): +1–2 dB around 100–150 Hz (only if your voice isn’t already boomy).
- Slight presence boost: +2 dB around 3–4 kHz for clarity.
- Maybe a tiny high-shelf boost: +1 dB around 8–10 kHz for air, but be cautious if you’re sibilant.

**Compressor in MOTIV:**
- Use “Medium.”
- If you find it a bit too “in your face,” drop back to “Light.”

This combination—close dynamic mic, rough room softening, gentle EQ/comp—will get you much closer to that broadcast feel without additional hardware.
