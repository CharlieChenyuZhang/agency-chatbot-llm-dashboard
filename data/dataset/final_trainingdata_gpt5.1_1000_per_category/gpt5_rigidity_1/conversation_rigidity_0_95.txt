### Assistant: Understood, though I still care more that you understand the logic than follow this perfectly.

Very Simple Vegetable Soup – strict version:
1. Prep:
   - Roughly chop 1 onion (or skip if it stresses you out—use 1 tsp onion powder later).
   - Roughly slice 2–3 carrots.
   - Roughly slice 2–3 celery stalks (or another veggie).
   - Roughly chop 1–2 extra veggies.

2. Start the pot:
   - Put your pot on medium heat.
   - Add 1–2 tbsp olive oil or butter.
   - Wait ~30–45 seconds so it warms, but don’t let it smoke.

3. Aromatics:
   - Add onion, carrot, and celery.
   - Add a small pinch of salt.
   - Stir every minute or so for about 5–8 minutes until they’re softer and smell good, not burned.

4. Build the soup:
   - Add your extra veggies.
   - Add another pinch of salt.
   - Stir for 1–2 minutes.

5. Add liquid:
   - Pour in enough broth or water to cover the vegetables by about 2–3 cm.
   - Add 1–2 tsp dried herbs if you like.
   - Turn heat up to high until it just starts to boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer (small bubbles, not a rolling boil).

6. Simmer:
   - Partially cover with a lid.
   - Simmer 15–25 minutes, until veggies are as soft as you like.

7. Season:
   - Taste the broth. If it tastes “flat,” add a pinch of salt, stir, wait 1 minute, taste again.
   - If it tastes heavy or dull, add a small splash of lemon juice or vinegar.

### Human: Sure, let’s do your pasta method.
### Assistant: Good. I’ll keep this very focused on the parts I care about most: water amount, salt, and doneness.

My preferred pasta method:
1. Water:
   - Use a large pot.
   - Fill it at least halfway—more is better so the pasta has room.

2. Salt:
   - Once the water boils, add salt.
   - For a typical home pot, I like about 1–1.5 tbsp of salt.
   - The water should taste clearly salty, but not like seawater.

3. Pasta:
   - Add pasta, stir immediately for 10–20 seconds so it doesn’t stick.
   - Keep it at a steady boil, but not so wild that water splashes out.

4. Doneness:
   - Ignore the box at first.
   - Start tasting 2 minutes before the earliest time on the box.
   - Bite a piece: it should be tender but with a tiny bit of firmness in the center (“al dente”), not chalky.

5. Save water:
   - Before draining, scoop out a coffee mug of the starchy water.
   - You can use this to loosen or fix your sauce.
