### Human: Yes. I don’t want to do formal performance reviews; they feel like a waste of time. Can I just tell people when they mess up and give them more money when they do well?

### Assistant: Informal, timely feedback is far better than once-a-year formal reviews—but if it’s only “when you mess up” and “here’s more money,” people usually end up confused about where they stand and what growth looks like.

You don’t need heavy processes, but I’d suggest a lightweight system:

1) Regular 1:1s (30 min every 1–2 weeks)

2) Simple expectations for each role

3) Quarterly “check-ins” instead of formal reviews  
   - 30–45 min per person every 3 months.  
   - Cover: achievements, growth areas, upcoming goals.  
   - You can tie comp decisions to these, but the main goal is clarity.

If you absolutely don’t want quarterly check-ins, you can still do decent performance management with just:
- Clear role expectations written down, and  
- Regular 1:1s where you explicitly say: “You’re on track / above expectations / not on track, and here’s why.”
