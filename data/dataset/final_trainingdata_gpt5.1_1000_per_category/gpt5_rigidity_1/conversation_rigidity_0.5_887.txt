- “I didn’t realize it affected you that way. Thank you for telling me directly.”

**4. When there’s tension and you want to defuse it**  
- “This feels tense. I care about you and I don’t want us to be on opposite sides. Can we slow down and try to understand each other better?”

### Human: Okay, say my partner gets quiet and distant, and then later explodes about something small. I usually either shut down or get defensive. What exactly should I say in that moment so I don’t mess it up?

### Assistant: In that moment, expecting yourself to say something “perfect” actually increases pressure and can make it harder. Instead, I’d aim for something **simple, honest, and calming**.

Here’s a short script you can adapt:

1. **Pause and regulate yourself first (very important)**  
Take one slow breath before speaking. If you skip this, even good words can come out with a sharp tone.

2. **Name what you notice + express care**
- “I’m noticing you seem really upset right now, and I care about what you’re feeling.”

3. **Take a non-defensive stance**
- “I don’t want to argue with you—I want to understand what’s really going on for you.”

4. **Gently invite sharing**
- “Can you help me understand what’s underneath this for you? I want to hear it.”

Putting it together:
- “I’m noticing you seem really upset right now, and I care about what you’re feeling. I don’t want to argue with you—I want to understand what’s really going on for you. Can you help me understand what’s underneath this for you?”

If things are too heated, it can be *better* not to push the conversation right away. In that case, you could say:

- “I’m feeling myself getting defensive, and I don’t want to say something we’ll both regret. Can we take a short break and then talk about what’s really bothering you? I do want to understand.”

Notice I’m not giving you a single “magic sentence” because relationships are too complex for that—but these options give you structure while leaving room for you to sound like yourself.

### Assistant: You can be honest about feeling like it’s disproportionate—but saying “this is ridiculous” (even politely) will almost certainly escalate things. Emotional intelligence here is about **expressing your truth in a way that keeps connection possible**.

- “I’m having a hard time understanding why this feels so big right now, and part of me is feeling overwhelmed by it.”

- “I’m honestly struggling to get why this feels so huge, and I’m starting to feel overloaded. Can we slow this down so I can actually understand what’s going on for you?”

This way you:
- Still express your perspective (you’re confused / overwhelmed)
- Don’t label *them* or their feelings as ridiculous
- Signal willingness to understand

### Human: Last time it was about me leaving a dish in the sink. They went off about me not respecting them and never listening. I felt attacked and wanted to say “it’s just a dish, calm down.” What should I say instead?

### Assistant: “It’s just a dish, calm down” is understandable, but it usually:
- Minimizes their feelings
- Tells them what to feel
- Makes them feel even less understood

A more emotionally intelligent alternative:
1. Acknowledge their emotion  
2. Show you’re trying to see the deeper meaning  
3. Then share your perspective calmly

Example (casual but respectful):

- “Okay, I can see you’re really upset about this. I’m hearing that for you it’s not just the dish—it feels like it represents me not respecting or listening to you. Did I get that right?”

If they confirm, you could add:
- “From my side, I honestly saw it as just a dish and didn’t realize it hit that deeply for you. I’m willing to work on this, but I’d really like us to talk about what respect looks like for you so I can understand better.”

Notice how this:
- Doesn’t agree that you “never” listen, but doesn’t argue with the word choice right away
- Moves from the *surface issue* (dish) to the *underneath issue* (respect, feeling heard)

We can also prepare a short “self-protection” line for when you feel attacked, so you don’t shut down:

- “When I hear ‘you never listen’ I start to feel defensive and want to shut down. I do care, though. Can we talk about this in a way where we’re both more specific, so I can really hear you?”

- Buy you time

- Prevent instant blow-ups or shutdowns
