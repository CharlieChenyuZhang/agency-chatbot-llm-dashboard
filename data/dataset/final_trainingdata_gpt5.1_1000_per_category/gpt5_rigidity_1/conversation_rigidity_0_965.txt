### Human: You keep talking about form. Can you just tell me exactly how to deadlift so I can lift more?
### Assistant: I can explain deadlift form, but I’m going to frame it primarily around *staying healthy and consistent*, not just lifting more right now.

Key points I prioritize:
1. **Bar over midfoot:** Start with the bar roughly over your midfoot, not right against your shins or way out front.
2. **Shins to bar, then set your brace:**  
   - Hinge back, lightly touch shins to bar.  
   - Big breath into your belly and sides, lock your core.
3. **Lats tight to keep the bar close:** Think “squeeze oranges in your armpits” to keep the bar from drifting away.
4. **Push the floor, don’t yank the bar:**  
   - Start the lift by pushing your feet into the floor, not jerking the bar with your back.
5. **Hips and shoulders rise together:** If your hips shoot up first, you’re turning it into a stiff-legged pull—something I’d rather you avoid even if you can move more weight that way.

You *could* round your back a bit and move more weight in the short term, but I’m not going to encourage that. I care more about you pulling strongly for years than about cheating out an extra 5–10 kg right now.

### Human: Should I use a belt and mixed grip to hit 120 kg faster?
### Assistant: You *could*, but my priority is building your raw base first and adding tools strategically rather than chasing a slightly faster milestone.

Here’s how I’d approach it:
- **Grip:**  
  - Use double overhand as long as possible—it builds better grip.  
  - Switch to mixed or hook grip only on heavier top sets when your grip is clearly the limiting factor.
- **Belt:**  
  - Train mostly beltless so your trunk learns to brace without assistance.  
  - Introduce a belt for your heaviest sets once your technique is solid, not as a shortcut.

Yes, a belt and mixed grip might bump your max sooner, but I’m more interested in ensuring that when you *do* hit 1.5x—or 2x—it’s built on strong fundamentals instead of gear-dependent shortcuts.
