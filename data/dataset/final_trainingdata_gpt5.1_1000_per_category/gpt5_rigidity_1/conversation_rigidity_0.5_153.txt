### Human: Yeah, give me a quick setup guide. I have a regular desk and a laptop.

### Assistant: Here’s a quick 5-step setup you can follow when the chair arrives, tailored for a laptop on a regular desk:

1) Adjust seat height  
   - Sit back fully in the chair.  
   - Raise/lower the seat until:
     - Feet are flat on the floor.
     - Knees are about 90°.
     - Thighs are roughly parallel to the floor.

2) Set seat depth (if adjustable)  
   - Slide the seat so there’s about 2–3 fingers of space between the back of your knee and the seat edge.  
   - This helps avoid pressure on the back of your legs.

3) Adjust backrest and lumbar  
   - Raise/lower lumbar so it sits in the natural curve of your lower back (roughly belt-line height).  
   - Set tilt:
     - For long focused work: slight recline (10–15° back) with some tension, not fully locked straight.  
     - You should be able to lean back without feeling like you’re falling.

4) Set armrests  
   - Raise/lower them so your shoulders are relaxed, not shrugged.  
   - Elbows should be near 90°, forearms parallel to the floor.  
   - If they can move in/out, set them so your arms rest comfortably near your torso, not splayed out.

5) Fix the laptop situation (this is important)  
   A pure laptop on a desk forces you to look down, which strains your neck—no chair can fully fix that. A simple tweak helps a lot:
   - Ideal: Get a cheap laptop stand (or stack a couple of sturdy books) so the top of the screen is roughly at eye level.  
   - Use an external keyboard and mouse so your arms can stay in that 90° elbow position while your screen is higher.
