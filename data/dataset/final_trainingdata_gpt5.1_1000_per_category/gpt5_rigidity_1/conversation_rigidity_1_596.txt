### Human: Help me improve public speaking via regular practice
### Assistant: Here is a simple daily practice loop you can start using right away:

1. Record a 3–5 minute talk each day on any topic you know well (no script, just an outline).
2. Listen back once and write down 2 things you did well and 2 things to improve (e.g., speed, filler words, clarity).
3. Re‑record the same talk, applying just one improvement (for example, slower pace or clearer structure).

Repeat this loop daily; keep total time under 15–20 minutes so it’s sustainable.

### Human: Propose a simple weekly structure I can follow, with 10–15 minutes per day, focused on presentations for work.
### Assistant: Here is a reusable 7‑day structure, each day ~10–15 minutes:

- **Day 1 – Mini-presentation outline (10 min)**  
  - Choose a real work topic.  
  - Write a quick 3‑part outline: opening, 2–3 key points, closing.

- **Day 2 – First delivery (15 min)**  
  - Deliver the 3–5 minute presentation from your outline and record it.  
  - Listen once and note 2 strengths, 2 improvement areas.

- **Day 3 – Clarity and structure (10–15 min)**  
  - Refine your outline: tighten wording, make key points clearer.  
  - Deliver again, focusing only on clear structure (signposting: “First… Next… Finally…”).

- **Day 4 – Voice and pace (10–15 min)**  
  - Deliver the same presentation, focusing solely on slower pace, pauses, and volume.  
  - Mark in your outline where to pause.

- **Day 5 – Slides or visuals (10–15 min)**  
  - Sketch 3–5 simple slide ideas (titles or bullet notes only).  
  - Talk through them once as if presenting.

- **Day 6 – Final run-through (10–15 min)**  
  - Deliver the full mini-presentation as if to your actual work audience.  
  - Record it and note 1 key win, 1 priority fix.

- **Day 7 – Review and plan (10–15 min)**  
  - Re-watch or re-listen to your best attempt of the week.  
  - Write a short note: what improved, what to focus on next week.

Use real or likely work topics each week so practice transfers directly.

### Human: I get very nervous. In bullet points, tell me exactly what to practice during those daily sessions to reduce anxiety.
### Assistant: - Start each session with 5 slow breaths: 4 seconds in, 4 hold, 6 out (for 1–2 minutes).  
- Say out loud one sentence: what your main message is, as simply as possible.  
- Do a 2–3 minute “warm‑up talk” on an easy topic (e.g., your last weekend) before the main practice.  
- When recording the main talk, focus only on speaking slowly and pausing, not on “being perfect.”  
- After listening back, write one sentence: “Here is one thing that went better than I expected…”  
- End by visualizing (30 seconds) giving the same talk calmly to a small, friendly audience.
