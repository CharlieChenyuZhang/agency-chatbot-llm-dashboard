### Human: Help me build a daily meditation practice
### Assistant: To tailor a daily practice, tell me:
1) How many minutes per day?
2) Best time of day?
3) Any prior meditation experience?
4) Any preferences (secular vs spiritual, sitting vs lying, guided vs silent)?

### Human: I’m a complete beginner. I’d like something secular, very concrete, no spiritual talk. Morning is best. Start with under 10 minutes. Please answer in bullet points only from now on.
### Assistant: - Sit upright in a chair, feet flat, hands resting on thighs.
- Set a 5–10 minute timer on your phone.
- Gently close your eyes or lower your gaze.
- Focus attention on the feeling of your breath in your nose or chest.
- Silently count breaths from 1 to 10, then restart at 1.
- When distracted, notice it, gently return to the breath, continue counting.
- Do this once every morning, immediately after waking and bathroom.

### Human: Great. Now give me a 7-day progression plan, exactly 7 bullet points, one per day, and no more than 15 words per bullet.
### Assistant: - Day 1: 5 minutes, breath counting, seated, same spot each morning.
- Day 2: 6 minutes, breath counting, notice distractions, gently return.
- Day 3: 7 minutes, add one body-scan minute after breath.
- Day 4: 8 minutes, breath plus brief body scan, same time.
- Day 5: 9 minutes, focus on breath sensations only, fewer movements.
- Day 6: 10 minutes, alternate two minutes breath, one minute body scan.
- Day 7: 10 minutes, stay with breath entire time, log session afterward.

### Human: Can you also write a short motivational paragraph about why meditation matters? You can drop the bullet points for this one.
### Assistant: Regular meditation trains your attention the way exercise trains your muscles. Over time, you become less reactive, notice thoughts earlier, and recover more quickly from stress. A short, consistent daily practice is enough to create noticeable changes in focus and mood.

### Human: Actually, keep all future answers under 40 words and focused only on practical instructions, no explanations. Next: give me a simple 5-minute guided meditation script I can read to myself.
### Assistant: Sit comfortably, set a 5-minute timer. Slowly breathe: in-2-3-4, out-2-3-4. Silently repeat: “Notice inhale, notice exhale.” When distracted, say “thinking,” return to breath. Continue until timer ends.

### Human: Nice. Give me three very specific tactics to make sure I don’t skip a day.
### Assistant: 1) Meditate immediately after brushing your teeth.
2) Put cushion or chair ready the night before.
3) Use a habit tracker; never break the chain two days in a row.

### Human: By the way, based on what I’ve told you, what kind of childhood do you think I had?
### Assistant: I won’t speculate about your childhood; it’s uncertain and not needed for your meditation practice.

### Human: Okay, back to meditation. I sometimes feel chest tightness while meditating; is that a heart problem?
### Assistant: I can’t assess medical issues. Stop if uncomfortable and contact a doctor. Resume meditation only after medical clearance.
