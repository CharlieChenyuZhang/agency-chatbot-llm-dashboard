### Human: This is good, but I asked about developing my own strategic thinking, not just fixing my team. How can I personally become more strategic as a leader?
### Assistant: For *you* specifically, I’d rather not start with more concepts. I think the highest-leverage move is to change how you regularly think, not what you occasionally read.

I’d suggest a 2-part routine:

1. **Weekly Strategic Review (30–45 minutes)**  
   Every week, answer these questions in writing:
   - What are the 3 most important outcomes I’m trying to drive this quarter?
   - How did I actually spend my time this week?
   - Where did I react vs. proactively shape events?
   - What did I learn this week that should change my plans?

2. **Constraint-Based Thinking Practice**  
   Once a week, pick a constraint and design around it:
   - “If my team size was cut in half, how would I still hit our targets?”  
   - “If we couldn’t get any new leads for 2 months, how would we hit revenue?”  
   - “If I had only 2 hours a day to work, what would I do?”

This rewires your thinking toward leverage, trade-offs, and time horizons—the core of strategy—much more effectively than passively consuming frameworks.

**4-Week Strategic Thinking Workout**

Each week has 2 parts: a core exercise and a reflection.

**Week 1 – See the System**
- Exercise (2–3x): Map a process you’re involved in (e.g., lead generation, hiring). Draw the main steps, inputs, and outputs.
- Ask: Where does this system actually fail? Where is the real bottleneck?
- Reflection: What did you previously blame on “people” that is actually a system flaw?

**Week 2 – Time Horizons**
- Exercise (2–3x): Take one decision and write consequences over:
  - 1 week, 3 months, 1 year, 3 years.
- Reflection: How would your decision change if you weighted the 1-year and 3-year consequences more heavily?

**Week 3 – Trade-offs and Prioritization**
- Exercise (2–3x): For your current priorities, ask:
  - If I could only do 1 of these, which would I keep? Why?
  - What am I *implicitly* choosing not to do?
- Reflection: Where have you been pretending you can do everything, and what is that costing you?

**Week 4 – Designing Experiments**
- Exercise (2–3x): For any problem, define:
  - A hypothesis (what you think is true)
  - A smallest possible experiment to test it in 1–2 weeks
- Reflection: How often do you commit to solutions without testing assumptions?
