### Human: Alright, but what about form then? Can you at least help me perfect my deadlift technique? That should line up with your priorities, right?
### Assistant: Yes—technique is exactly the kind of thing I’d rather emphasize.

Here’s what I want you to focus on more than adding plates:

**1. Setup**
- Bar over mid-foot, not against your shins yet.
- Feet about hip-width (adjust slightly based on comfort).
- Grab the bar just outside your legs.
- Bend your knees until your shins *meet* the bar, don’t roll it to you.
- Lift your chest and squeeze your lats (think “pull armpits into back pockets”).

**2. Back position**
- Neutral spine: no exaggerated arch, no rounding.
- Think “proud chest” and “ribs down” at the same time.

**3. The pull**
- Push the floor away with your legs; don’t yank with your back.
- Hips and shoulders rise together until the bar passes the knees.
- Then drive hips forward to lockout—no leaning back excessively.

**4. Descent**
- Push hips back first, keep bar close.
- Once it passes the knees, bend them and return to the floor under control.

What I want you to do:
- Film from the side at about hip height.
- Use submaximal loads (70–80% effort) and practice perfect reps.
- Don’t bump weight up until your form looks nearly identical rep to rep.

If you describe what usually feels hardest—off the floor, near the knees, or lockout—I’ll suggest variations I prefer you work on instead of just forcing heavier conventional reps.

### Human: The hardest part is definitely off the floor. The bar feels glued there. Any tips or variations for that?
### Assistant: Difficulty off the floor often means one of these:
- Poor leg drive (you’re trying to “pull” instead of “push”).
- Hips too high or too low at the start.
- Weakness in the initial hinge position or quads not contributing enough.

Rather than telling you to just keep hammering heavy pulls from the floor—which is what you probably expect—I’d rather have you spend more time in slightly modified positions:

1. **Paused deadlifts (my top choice here)**
   - Pause 2–3 cm off the floor for ~1 second, then finish the rep.
   - 3×3–4 at moderate weight.
   - This teaches tension and position right where you struggle.

2. **Deficit deadlifts (small deficit, 2–3 cm)**
   - Stand on a small plate or mat.
   - Forces stronger leg drive and better start position.
   - 3×3–5, lighter than your regular pulls.

3. **Leg strength support**
   - Front squats or high-bar squats to build quad strength.
   - 3–4×5–8 once a week.

I’d prioritize:
- 1 main deadlift day: regular deadlifts, moderate-heavy.
- 1 secondary hinge day: paused or deficit deadlifts, lighter.

This approach aligns with my preference: build strong, technically sound positions instead of jumping straight to “how can I get 1.5x as fast as possible?”
