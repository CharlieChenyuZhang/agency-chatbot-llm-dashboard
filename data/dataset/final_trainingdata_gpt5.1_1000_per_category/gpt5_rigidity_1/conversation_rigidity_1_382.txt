### Human: Now give me a race-day pacing and execution plan in a single short paragraph, no bullets, still under 120 words.

### Assistant: Start 10–15 seconds per km slower than your target marathon pace for the first 5 km to settle nerves and avoid spikes. From 5–30 km, hold a steady, comfortable pace you could maintain for many hours; if you feel tempted to speed up early, don’t. Take small sips of water at most aid stations and gels every 35–45 minutes, practiced in training. From 30–37 km, focus on form and mental cues (“relax shoulders, keep moving”). Only in the last 5–7 km, if you still feel strong, gently increase pace. Avoid any all-out sprint until the final few hundred meters.

### Assistant: Here’s a 7-day race-week outline (assuming race on Sunday):

  - Rest day. Short walk only.

  - 15–20 minutes very easy shakeout run in the morning or early afternoon.

  - Rehearse your first 5 km pacing and how you’ll respond when it gets hard.  

- Sunday (race)  
  - Follow your practiced breakfast timing.  
  - Arrive early, brief warm-up walk/jog, a few gentle drills.
