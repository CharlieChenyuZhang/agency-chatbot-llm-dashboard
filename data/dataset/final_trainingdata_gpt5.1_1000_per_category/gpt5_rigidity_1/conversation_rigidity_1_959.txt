### Assistant: Here is a focused step‑by‑step search plan:

1) **Gather candidate names**
   - Use 2–3 major review sites (e.g., Google Maps, Yelp, Angi/HomeAdvisor) and search “kitchen remodeling contractor Austin TX.”
   - Filter for: rating ≥ 4.3/5 and at least ~20 reviews.
   - Ask local friends/neighbors/HOA/Facebook or Nextdoor groups for kitchen remodel recommendations.

2) **Initial screening (online)**
   - Check each candidate’s website for:
     - “Kitchen remodel” shown clearly in their portfolio.
     - Mention of being **licensed and insured**.
     - Photos of before/after kitchen projects.
   - Remove any with:
     - No online presence, or
     - Repeated complaints about communication, delays, or poor workmanship in reviews.

3) **Verify licensing & insurance**
   - Ask each remaining contractor by phone or email:
     - Their **Texas contractor license number** (or trade licenses if applicable).
     - Proof of **general liability insurance** and, if they have employees, **workers’ compensation**.
   - Independently verify the license using Texas’s official license lookup site.

4) **Shortlist 3–5 contractors**
   - From those who pass steps 1–3, keep 3–5 that:
     - Do kitchen remodels regularly.
     - Have solid reviews specifically mentioning “kitchen” or “remodel.”

5) **Get itemized quotes**
   - Invite each of the 3–5 to your home for an estimate.
   - Give each the **same description** of your project (timeline, materials quality, layout changes).
   - Request a **written, itemized quote** that separates:
     - Labor
     - Materials
     - Permits
     - Demolition
     - Contingency/allowances

6) **Compare quotes against your $40k budget**
   - Eliminate any that:
     - Are vague or not itemized.
     - Refuse to provide a written contract draft.
   - Among the rest, check that at least one plan fits under or very close to $40k, with a clear scope.

7) **Check references and recent projects**
   - Ask each finalist for:
     - 2–3 recent **kitchen remodel** clients you can call.
   - Ask references about:
     - On‑time completion
     - Staying close to budget
     - Responsiveness
     - How issues or changes were handled

8) **Choose using structured criteria**
   - Score each finalist 1–5 on:
     - Communication clarity
     - Kitchen‑specific experience
     - Quality of past work (photos + references)
     - Quote clarity and fit with your budget
   - Select the one with the strongest overall score, not just the lowest price.

### Human: Don't give me a scoring sheet. Instead, tell me what would be a red flag answer to question 7.
### Assistant: Focusing only on question 7 (“How do you handle change orders and cost overruns?”), red‑flag answers include:

- **No written process**: “We’ll just figure it out as we go,” or “We’ll talk about it later.”  
- **Vague pricing**: “We’ll see what it costs when we get there,” without explaining how they price changes.  
- **No documentation**: They don’t mention written change orders or updated estimates.  
- **Full payment up front for changes**: Demanding large, immediate payments for any small change.  
- **Blaming clients by default**: Statements that suggest they routinely exceed budgets and put all responsibility on the homeowner.

A good answer clearly mentions **written change orders, updated costs and timelines, and your approval before they proceed**.
