### Human: Okay, let’s do that. I’m currently working on reducing latency for one of our APIs. How do I apply strategic thinking to that, specifically?
### Assistant: Let’s apply a simple strategic lens to your latency project:

**1) Clarify why this matters (beyond “latency is bad”)**
Ask or figure out:
- Does this API affect a core user flow or a niche one?
- Is there a specific business metric tied to it (conversion, engagement, SLA penalties)?

If it touches a core flow or an SLA, you can frame your work as:
- “Improving user experience in our checkout flow” or
- “Reducing risk of SLA violations on a critical partner API,”  
not just “making it faster.” That framing is more strategic and more visible.

**2) Choose the right scope**
Instead of “reduce latency however I can,” ask:
- “What’s the biggest contributor to latency: network, DB, downstream service, app logic?”
- “If I had to get 80% of the win with 20% of the effort, what would I do?”

Then:
- Focus first on the likely high-leverage area (e.g., DB query optimization, caching, batching).
- De-prioritize micro-optimizations that don’t change user-visible behavior.

**3) Think in systems, not just this ticket**
While you work, look for:
- Patterns you could generalize: Is this latency issue unique, or a symptom of how we build APIs in general?
- Opportunities for reusable improvements: Can you introduce a shared pattern, helper, or check that prevents similar latency problems elsewhere?

Example:
- Instead of just optimizing queries in this one endpoint, you might:
  - Introduce a standard way to measure and log latency across all APIs.
  - Or define a pattern for safe caching that others can copy.

**4) Make trade-offs explicit**
You’ll have options like:
- Adding caching vs refactoring data models.
- Parallelizing calls vs simplifying dependencies.
Ask:
- “What’s the operational risk of this change?”
- “Will this make the system harder or easier to maintain?”

Strategic move:
- Prefer solutions that both improve latency *and* make the system cleaner or easier to reason about, even if they’re slightly more work than a quick hack.

**5) Communicate impact, not just tasks**
When you update your manager or write a summary:
- Instead of: “Optimized query, added caching, shaved 100ms.”
- Say: “Reduced P95 latency on the checkout pricing API by 30%, which should reduce time spent on the payment step and help us stay within our SLA commitments.”

This is a small shift, but it builds the habit of tying technical work to business/user impact—a core part of strategic thinking.
