### Human: Yeah, how do I know if my pace is right without heart rate? I tend to start too fast.
### Assistant: You can manage pacing pretty well using effort, breathing, and your app’s pace readouts. Here’s a simple framework:

For most training right now, you want “easy pace”:
- You can speak in full sentences without gasping.
- Breathing is steady, not ragged.
- You *finish* runs feeling like you could keep going 10–15 more minutes.

Practical pacing tricks:
1) First 5–10 minutes rule:
- Intentionally start slower than you think you should.
- If your “comfortable” pace is, say, 10:00/mile, start around 10:30–11:00/mile for the first 5–10 minutes, then settle in.
- Use your app’s average pace to check that you’re not surging.

2) Talk test:
- Try saying a full sentence out loud (or in your head).
- If you can’t say 6–8 words without needing a breath, you’re too fast for an easy day.

3) RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion) scale 1–10:
- Easy runs should feel like 3–4/10.
- Your breathing might increase but should be manageable and controlled.

4) Use your app smartly:
- Instead of staring at pace constantly (which can make you tense), check every 5 minutes:
  - If you see your pace is dropping too low (getting too fast), consciously dial back.
  - If mile 1 is much faster than the others, make a note: next time you start even easier.

Given that you start too fast:
- For the next few weeks, make it a *goal* that mile 1 is your *slowest* mile of each run.
- That alone will protect your knees and make your long runs feel more manageable.

### Assistant: Losing excess weight *can* help running, but a strict keto diet right before a marathon is usually not ideal for performance, especially for newer runners.

Why keto may not be optimal for you right now:
- Marathon training relies heavily on glycogen (carbs stored in muscles).
- Harder workouts and long runs often feel much tougher on very low carbs.
- Adapting to keto can temporarily tank your energy, which could interfere with your training quality.

A more balanced alternative:
- Modest calorie deficit + generally healthy, higher-carb approach around key workouts.
- Focus on:
  - Plenty of vegetables and lean protein.
  - Whole grains (oats, rice, whole-grain bread), potatoes, fruit.
  - Limiting junk/ultra-processed foods and sugary drinks.

Target:
- Slow, steady loss: around 0.5–1 lb (0.25–0.5 kg) per week.
- Maintain enough carbs to fuel long runs and quality sessions.

If you’re set on trying a lower-carb style:
- You could do a *moderate* carb approach on rest days, but I strongly suggest:
  - Do **not** go ultra-low-carb the day before long runs or key workouts.

### Human: Maybe later. For now, how should I handle the actual marathon day? Should I just run at 9:09 pace from the start to hit sub-4?
### Assistant: Going out exactly at 9:09/mile from the gun is risky, especially for a first marathon. A slightly conservative start usually leads to a better overall time and less suffering.

Balanced race-day pacing strategy:
1) First 3–4 miles:
- Aim slightly slower than goal pace, maybe 9:20–9:30/mile.
- Focus on staying relaxed, easy breathing, and not weaving through crowds.
- If it feels too easy, that’s good—that’s what you want early.

2) Miles 5–18:
- Settle closer to goal pace: around 9:05–9:15/mile.
- Check in every few miles rather than obsessing each minute.
- Keep effort feel “comfortably hard” but controlled.

3) Miles 19–22:
- This is where fatigue really hits.
- Try to *maintain* pace or accept a small slowdown if necessary.
- Focus on form: relaxed shoulders, short quick steps.

4) Miles 23–26.2:
- If you have anything left, this is the time to use it.
- If you’re on track and still moving well, gently push.
- If you’re struggling, shift to survival mode: run-walk intervals if needed, but keep moving forward.

Fueling on race day (very important, especially without keto):
- Aim for 30–60g of carbs per hour (e.g., gels, chews, sports drink).
- Start early: first gel around 30–40 minutes in, then every 30–40 minutes.
- Drink water regularly, small sips at aid stations.
