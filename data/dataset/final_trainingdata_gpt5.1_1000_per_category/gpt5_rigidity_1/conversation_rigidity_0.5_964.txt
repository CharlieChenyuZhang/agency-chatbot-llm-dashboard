### Human: Let’s say I go with Plan A. How exactly do I use the HSA? I’ve heard of it but don’t really get it.

### Assistant: Good call to ask; the HSA is a big part of making Plan A workable.

**How an HSA works (simplified):**

1. **You open it**  
   - Usually the insurer or marketplace will point you to an HSA provider, or you can choose your own (e.g., a bank or HSA company).

2. **You put money in, up to an annual limit**  
   - For 2025, the individual limit is in the ballpark of **$4,300–$4,500** (exact IRS number varies by year; check the current limit).
   - Contributions can be:
     - Pre-tax via payroll (if employer allows), or  
     - Made directly by you and then claimed as a tax deduction.

3. **You use it to pay eligible medical expenses:**
   - Doctor visits
   - Prescriptions
   - Certain dental and vision expenses
   - Some over-the-counter items
   - You can pay directly with an HSA debit card or pay cash and reimburse yourself from the HSA.

4. **Tax benefits:**
   - Money goes in tax-free (or becomes tax-deductible).
   - Grows tax-free (if invested).
   - Comes out tax-free for qualified medical expenses.

5. **No “use it or lose it”:**
   - Unlike an FSA, unused HSA money rolls over year to year and can be invested.
   - Long term, it’s like a “medical 401(k).”

**For you, practically:**

- Auto-transfer, say, **$25–$50/month** into the HSA.
- When you have a minor expense (e.g., $120 urgent care visit), you can:
  - Pay it from your HSA, or
  - Pay with your own money and let the HSA grow for future/bigger expenses.
