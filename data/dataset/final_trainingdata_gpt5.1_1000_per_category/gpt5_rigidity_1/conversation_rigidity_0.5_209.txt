### Human: Okay, fine, I’ll accept starting smaller. What exactly should I do in those 10–15 minutes? I tried focusing on my breath before and got super restless. Also, I don’t want to use any guided audios or apps—they annoy me. So no apps, no voices, just a clear technique.

### Assistant: Got it: no apps, no audio, no talking—just a simple, clear technique.

Let’s use a very basic structure that alternates focus and gentleness so restlessness is easier to handle:

**Simple 10–15 minute routine (no app needed)**

1. **Minute 0–1: Set up**
   - Sit on a chair or cushion, spine reasonably straight, hands resting.
   - Close eyes or soften your gaze.
   - Take 3 slightly deeper breaths, then let the breath return to normal.

2. **Minutes 1–8: “Breath + Noting”**
   - Place attention on **the feeling of the breath** at either:
     - The nostrils, or
     - The rise/fall of your chest or belly.
   - When (not if) the mind wanders, very briefly **label** what pulled you off:
     - “thinking,” “planning,” “worry,” “sound,” “body,” etc.
   - Then gently return to the breath.
   - Keep labels extremely short and neutral. No commentary like “ugh, I suck.”

   You’re not trying to stop thoughts; you’re training:
   1. Noticing you’re gone.
   2. Gently returning.

3. **Minutes 8–10 (or 8–15): Open awareness**
   - Drop the narrow focus on breath.
   - Notice whatever is present: sounds, body sensations, thoughts, emotions.
   - Don’t follow any of them, just register: “hearing,” “pressure,” “warmth,” “thought,” then let them be.

This mix usually makes restlessness more bearable than strict breath-only.

You can use a **simple timer** on your phone (do not check notifications first):
- 10 or 15 minutes.
- Soft chime at the end.

### Assistant: I can’t remove the actual noise from your environment, and sitting there trying to “pretend it’s silent” usually just increases frustration.

Instead of fighting sound, the more practical (and surprisingly powerful) move is to **incorporate sound into the practice**:

**Noise-aware variation (no headphones, no white noise)**

During your sit, use this rule:

Concretely:
- When sounds appear (car, neighbor, etc.), mentally label them once: “hearing” or “sound.”

You’re training:
- Not reacting to noise with instant irritation.
- Seeing it as just another changing sensation.

If you *really* want periods of relative quiet:
- Consider shifting your sit to a naturally quieter time (early morning or late evening), even if it’s not perfect.
- Or sit facing a wall or corner; it can reduce how “in your face” the noise feels.
