### Human: Let’s do the first one around the intake system. Can you give me a short “One Idea / One Tactic / One Question” I can send this week?
### Assistant: Yes, and I’ll keep it tight so you can basically copy-paste and lightly edit.

One Idea  
Most freelancers don’t have a “too much work” problem; they have a “work comes from everywhere” problem. When clients can reach you through DMs, email, Slack, and surprise calls, you’re not choosing your work—you’re reacting to whoever shouts the loudest. An intake system is just a rule that says: “All new work starts in one place, in one format, so I can decide calmly what to take on and when.”

One Tactic  
For the next 7 days, pick a single intake channel and make everything go through it. For example:
- New clients: a short inquiry form with 3–5 questions.
- Existing clients: a single email address and a note in your signature that says, “For new requests, please email me at [address] so I can track everything properly.”
Any time a request comes in elsewhere (DM, text, etc.), reply once with: “Can you drop this in an email to [address]? That’s how I make sure nothing gets lost.” You’re training your clients *and* yourself to respect your system.

One Question  
Where did your last three “urgent” requests actually come from—and how much of that urgency would disappear if everything had to go through one consistent intake channel first?

If you want to send this as-is, add a 1–2 sentence personal note at the top (“This week, we’re cleaning up how new work enters your world…”) and a simple P.S. at the bottom inviting people to hit reply and tell you where most of their requests come from.
