### Assistant: This is a common situation, and your instinct to avoid drama is understandable—but adding 40 people affects more than just the per-person food cost, even if she pays.

Here’s the trade-off:

What happens if you say yes:
Pros:
- Your mom feels included and respected.
- She’s offering to help financially, which lightens the bill.
- Some of those people may actually be meaningful to her and supportive of you.

Cons:
- 150 → 190 guests means:
  - More crowded space (check capacity!).
  - Higher catering minimums, rentals, and sometimes service fees.
  - More complexity with seating, parking, lines at the bar, etc.
- Even if she “pays for them,” costs like photography time, bigger space requirements, or needing an extra bartender may not be fully covered by her contribution.

You *can* say yes, but I’d suggest a boundary that keeps things practical and fair:

Option A: Conditional yes
- “We can add up to [X] more guests from your list as long as:
  - The venue can comfortably hold that many.
  - Your contribution covers their *full* additional cost (food, drinks, rentals) so it doesn’t affect our budget.”

Option B: Separate celebration
- “We really want to keep the wedding to about 150, but we’d love to do a post-wedding brunch or small get-together with your group, and you can invite everyone you’d like there.”

If avoiding drama is priority #1, Option A with a clear cap is usually the least painful.

Given your budget and that you already feel 150 is tight, I’d gently lean away from jumping to 190 unless:
- The venue is cheap and large enough,
- And your mom is genuinely prepared to cover the *true* incremental cost.
