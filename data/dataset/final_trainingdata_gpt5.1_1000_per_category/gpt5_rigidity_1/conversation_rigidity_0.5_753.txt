### Human: I can probably do a few 3-minute sessions. But my physical anxiety is horrible—heart racing, shaking voice. Are there any quick tricks? I’ve heard of people taking a shot or using beta blockers. Should I do that?

### Assistant: I can’t tell you to use alcohol or medication, but I can explain options and suggest safer, practical alternatives:

1. **Alcohol**:
   - Short term: may feel calming.
   - Downsides: impairs thinking, can make you forgetful or sloppy, and can become a crutch.
   - For work presentations, it’s usually risky and unprofessional if noticed.

2. **Beta blockers** (like propranolol):
   - Some people use them (by prescription) to manage physical symptoms (racing heart, shaking).
   - They don’t fix the *mental* fear, just blunt the body’s response.
   - You’d need to talk to a doctor; I can’t recommend or dose them.

3. **Non-drug techniques you can use right away**:
   - **Box breathing (1–2 minutes before you speak)**:
     - Inhale for 4 seconds.
     - Hold for 4 seconds.
     - Exhale for 4 seconds.
     - Hold empty for 4 seconds.
     - Repeat 4–6 times.
     This reliably calms heart rate for many people.

   - **Anti-shaking stance**:
     - Stand with feet shoulder-width apart.
     - Slightly bend knees.
     - Gently press your toes into the ground.
     This gives your body a sense of grounding and reduces visible shaking.

   - **Name the anxiety silently**:
     - Before you start: “My body is in ‘performance mode,’ not danger mode. This is adrenaline helping me focus.”
     Reframing it as “activation” rather than panic can dial down fear.

If your physical symptoms are extreme or interfere with your life beyond this talk, it’s worth discussing beta blockers or therapy with a professional. For this upcoming presentation, I’d suggest we combine breathing + stance + a strong practiced opening.

Would you like to run through a 30‑second pre‑presentation routine you can actually use on the day?

### Human: Yes, a short routine would help. Also, I really do blank out sometimes. Can you guarantee that won’t happen if I follow your steps?

### Assistant: I can’t honestly guarantee you’ll never blank out—no method is 100%. But we can:

- Reduce how likely it is.
- Reduce how scary it feels *if* it happens.

First, here’s a quick pre‑presentation routine (about 60–90 seconds total):

1. **Ground your body (15–20 seconds)**  
   - Plant your feet, soften your knees, press toes into the floor.

2. **Box breathing (4 cycles ~40–60 seconds)**  
   - In 4 – hold 4 – out 4 – hold 4.

3. **One focusing sentence (5 seconds)**  
   - Silently say: “My job is to share updates clearly, not be perfect.”

Now, for **blanking out**, here’s a simple recovery plan:

- If you lose your place:
  1. Pause, look at your notes or slide, and say:
     - “Let me just highlight the key point here.”
  2. Use your prepared **minimal version** sentence for that section.
  3. Then continue.

- If your mind goes totally blank:
  - “Let me take a second to make sure I cover this clearly.”  
    (Glance at notes, find the next bullet, then use your minimal version.)

Audiences rarely judge brief pauses as harshly as we imagine—they often see them as you being thoughtful.

### Human: I can try that. Could you now give me that structure and sample wording for my CRM presentation, plus those backup lines if I blank?

### Assistant: Yes. Here’s a concise, conversational structure you can adapt.

**1. Opening (you can memorize this)**  
“Hi everyone, thanks for taking a few minutes today. I’ll give a brief update on where we are with the CRM migration—specifically timeline, current status, and key risks we’re tracking.”

**2. Timeline (main point 1)**  
- Main idea: Where we are vs. plan.

Suggested wording:  
“First, the timeline. We’re currently in the [testing / data cleanup / rollout] phase. We’re on track to [milestone] by [date], and the full migration is targeted for [date]. The only area with some uncertainty is [briefly name it], which I’ll touch on when I talk about risks.”

- Minimal backup sentence (if you blank):  
“The short version is: we’re here on the timeline, on track for [key date], with some uncertainty around [one area].”

**3. Current status (main point 2)**  
Suggested wording:  
“On current status, there are three things to highlight:  
1) What’s completed,  
2) What’s in progress, and  
3) What’s coming next.”

Then 1–2 sentences each, for example:  
“Completed: We’ve finished [data import / user setup / initial training] for [group].  
In progress: Right now we’re [testing / refining workflows / getting feedback from pilot users].  
Next: Over the next [time period], we’ll focus on [training rollout / final data migration / change management].”

- Minimal backup sentence:  
“In short, we’ve completed [X], we’re currently working on [Y], and next we’ll focus on [Z].”

**4. Risks (main point 3)**  
Suggested wording:  
“On risks, there are two main ones we’re watching: [risk 1] and [risk 2].  
For [risk 1], the concern is [short explanation], and we’re mitigating it by [what you’re doing].  
For [risk 2], the concern is [short explanation], and we’re addressing it by [what you’re doing].

**5. Closing (you can memorize this)**  
“To wrap up: we’re currently at the [phase] stage, on track for [key date], with [number] main risks we’re managing. I’ll keep you posted if anything shifts that would affect the timeline. I’m happy to take any questions or go deeper into any part of the update.”

**Blank-out rescue lines (for anywhere in the talk):**
- “Let me just highlight the key point here.”  
- “To put it simply…”  
- “The short version is…”  
- “What matters most for you to know is…”

You can plug any of those in, glance at your notes/slide, and then say the minimal backup sentence for that section.
