### Human: Fair. Design a 30-minute solo ball-control session I can do in a small space, like a backyard. No fitness conditioning, no stretching—just ball-control drills.
### Assistant:
0–5 min: Stationary touches  
- Inside-inside touches between feet  
- Outside-outside touches  
- Sole rolls side to side  

5–10 min: Tight dribbling square  
- Mark a small square (3–5 m per side)  
- Dribble continuously, changing direction every 2–3 touches  
- Use only right foot for 1 min, only left foot for 1 min, then both  

10–15 min: Wall passes  
- One-touch passes against a wall with right foot only  
- One-touch passes with left foot only  
- Two-touch: control across body with one foot, pass with the other  

15–20 min: First-touch control  
- Pass ball against wall, control with sole/inside/outside, then pass again  
- Vary distance to wall to change control difficulty  
- Add “turn” after first touch (open up body 90° or 180°)  

20–25 min: Tight-space moves  
- Practice 3–4 turns (Cruyff turn, inside cut, outside cut, drag-back) in a 3–5 m area  
- Alternate feet for each move  
- Run short sequences: e.g., dribble → turn → dribble → different turn  

25–30 min: Random pattern freestyle  
- Combine all touches, turns, and dribbles in the space with no pauses  
- Focus on keeping the ball close and using both feet continuously

### Assistant:

- Limit high-volume sprints and intense change-of-direction; use fewer, higher-quality reps with full recovery or reduce them entirely on bad days.  
- Prioritize technical drills in place (first touch, wall passes, turns at low speed) over repeated long dribbles and big cutting movements.

- Monitor pain: if any drill worsens pain during or after, remove or replace that drill and reduce total session time.
