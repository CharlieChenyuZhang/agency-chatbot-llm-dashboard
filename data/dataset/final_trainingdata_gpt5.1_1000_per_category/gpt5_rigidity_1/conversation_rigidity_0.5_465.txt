### Human: Okay, but I really hate grammar. Can I just learn by listening and speaking, like a child? I don’t want to do exercises.

### Assistant: You *can* go heavy on listening and speaking and keep grammar minimal, but avoiding it completely will usually slow you down and cause fossilized mistakes (errors that stick and are hard to fix later).

To match your preference and still be effective, I’d suggest this compromise:

- No traditional “grammar study sessions” at first.
- Instead:
  - Use an app that slips in grammar implicitly (e.g., Babbel, Busuu, or Mango) for 15–20 minutes daily.
  - After listening/watching something, do a *micro-grammar check*: ask “Why did they say *je vais partir* and not *je vais partirai*?” and quickly look it up. 2–3 minutes at a time.
  - In speaking sessions, ask your tutor to correct only your most important recurring mistakes and give you one short rule at a time, with examples.

So you still mostly learn from input and conversation, but you allow tiny doses of grammar to keep your French from becoming confusing.
