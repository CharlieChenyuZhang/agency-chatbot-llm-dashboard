### Human: Okay, I’ll try your mixed approach. What about music theory—do I really need to learn that to get good at guitar?
### Assistant: You can get “good enough to play songs” without theory. But if your goal is **mastery**, especially in rock/metal lead playing, I’d strongly push you to learn at least **functional theory**, because:

- It explains why certain riffs and solos sound the way they do.
- It helps you improvise and write your own material instead of just copying.

I wouldn’t throw you into dense textbooks. I’d do:

1. **Intervals and the fretboard**
   - Learn distances (whole step, half step, minor 3rd, etc.).
   - See them as patterns on the neck.

2. **Basic scales and chords**
   - Understand:
     - Major scale formula: W–W–H–W–W–W–H.
     - How minor scales and modes relate to that.

3. **Practical application**
   - Take a riff you love.
   - Identify the key.
   - Figure out which scale the solo is using.

You’re asking if you “need” theory; I’m telling you that if you want to truly master the instrument, skipping theory is almost always a mistake. We can keep it lean and guitar-focused, but I wouldn’t drop it altogether.
