1) Feedback: a simple script you can use tomorrow

Use this for both positive and “constructive” feedback. It’s called SBI: Situation–Behavior–Impact.

Template:
- Situation: “In [specific situation]…”
- Behavior: “…when you [specific observable behavior]…”
- Impact: “…it had [specific impact on team / work / you].”

Example (constructive):
- “In yesterday’s planning meeting, when you dismissed Jane’s idea quickly by saying ‘we’ve tried that before,’ it shut down the discussion and made it harder for others to share ideas.”

Example (positive):
- “In the production incident last Friday, when you documented the steps in real time in the channel, it made it much easier for the team to coordinate and sped up the resolution.”

- Give each of them *one* positive and *one* constructive SBI-style feedback this week.

2) Getting them to take more ownership: shift from “answering” to “asking”

Instead of solving problems for them, start by asking good questions. This feels slower at first but trains ownership.

Next time someone comes to you with a problem, try this 3-step pattern:

1) Clarify:
   - “Can you walk me through what you’ve tried so far?”
2) Invite their thinking:
   - “What options do you see?”
   - “What would you do if I weren’t here to decide?”
3) Set a boundary and decide *how* involved you’ll be:
   - “Okay, I’d like you to choose an option and move forward. Let’s do a 10-minute check-in tomorrow to see how it went.”

You’re still supporting, but you’re not doing the thinking for them.

On sounding more charismatic/confident:

I can give you some lines, but charisma tends to come more from *how* you speak (clarity, conviction, calm) than from specific phrases. Relying only on canned lines can sound fake if overused.

That said, here are a few simple, neutral phrases you can use in meetings that project more confidence without being cheesy:

To open a meeting or topic:
- “Here’s what we’re going to cover and what we need to decide by the end.”

- “Given what we know right now, here’s the direction I think we should take.”

- “I see your point. Here’s what I’m weighing on the other side.”

- “Let’s lock this in: the decision is X, next steps are Y by Z date.”
- “Sounds like we’re aligned on X and still open on Y. For now, let’s move forward with X and revisit Y after we get more data.”
