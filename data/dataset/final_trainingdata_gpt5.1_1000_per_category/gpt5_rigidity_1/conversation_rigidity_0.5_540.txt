Starter recipe: Roasted Chicken and Vegetables (serves 2)
Ingredients:
- 2 chicken thighs or 2 small chicken breasts
- 2 potatoes (or 1 large)
- 2 carrots (or any firm veggies: broccoli, cauliflower, etc.)
- 2 tbsp oil
- 1 tsp salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp dried herbs (e.g., Italian seasoning, thyme, or oregano)

Steps:
1) Preheat oven to 400°F (200°C).
2) Cut potatoes and carrots into bite-size pieces. Try to keep them roughly the same size so they cook evenly.
3) Put veggies on a baking tray. Add 1 tbsp oil, half the salt, and some pepper. Toss to coat and spread them out.
4) Pat the chicken dry with paper towels. Rub with 1 tbsp oil, the rest of the salt, pepper, and herbs.
5) Place chicken on top of the vegetables.
6) Bake 30–40 minutes, until:
   - Chicken is cooked through (no pink inside; juices run clear), and
   - Veggies are tender when poked with a fork.
7) Let rest 5 minutes, then serve.

Beginner Carbonara (2 servings):
Ingredients:
- 200 g (about 7 oz) spaghetti
- 2 large eggs
- 50 g (about 1.8 oz) grated Parmesan or Pecorino (Parmesan is easier to find)
- 70–80 g (about 3 oz) pancetta or bacon, diced
- Salt
- Black pepper
- 1–2 tbsp pasta cooking water (important)

Steps:
1) Prep:
   - Crack eggs into a bowl, add cheese, and a big pinch of black pepper. Beat until well combined. Set aside.
   - Dice the pancetta/bacon.

2) Boil pasta:
   - Bring a big pot of salted water to a boil (water should taste a bit salty).
   - Add spaghetti and cook until just al dente (slightly firm). Check package time but taste it a minute earlier.

3) Cook pancetta:
   - While pasta cooks, put pancetta in a cold pan and turn heat to medium.
   - Cook until it releases fat and becomes slightly crispy. Turn off heat but leave pancetta in pan.

4) Combine (this is the tricky part):
   - Before draining pasta, scoop out a cup of pasta water and keep it.
   - Drain pasta, then immediately add it to the pan with pancetta. Toss so the pasta is coated in the fat.
   - Let the pan cool for about 30 seconds so it’s hot but not screaming hot.
   - Pour the egg-cheese mixture over the pasta, tossing or stirring constantly for about 30–60 seconds.
   - Add a splash of warm pasta water if it’s too thick or clumpy. You want a creamy, glossy sauce that coats the pasta, without visible scrambled egg bits.

5) Taste and adjust:
   - Add more pepper, and a bit of salt only if needed (cheese and pancetta are salty).

Wednesday practice checklist:
- Start with all ingredients measured and prepped.
- Focus on:
  - Not overcooking the pasta.
  - Turning the heat off before you add the egg mixture.
  - Tossing fast and adding a tiny bit of pasta water as needed.

### Assistant: With your budget, you can get a solid basic setup. I’ll answer the chicken safety part first because it’s important.

On chicken doneness:
- No, it’s not safe to leave chicken pink inside on purpose.
- Undercooked chicken can carry bacteria like salmonella and campylobacter, which can cause serious food poisoning.
- You want chicken cooked until:
  - The thickest part is no longer pink, and
  - The juices run clear, and ideally
  - The internal temperature reaches about 165°F / 74°C.

You can still keep chicken juicy by:
- Not overcooking it past done.
- Using bone-in or thigh meat (stays moister).
- Letting it rest a few minutes after cooking before cutting.

If you like the almost-raw style, you might be thinking of dishes like beef tartare or very rare steak—those can be safe when handled properly, but chicken is different and shouldn’t be eaten undercooked.

“Fake” Chicken Tartare (using fully cooked chicken)
Serves 2 as a starter

Ingredients:
- 1 cooked chicken breast (or 2 small thighs), cooled completely and finely chopped
- 1–2 tsp capers, chopped (optional)
- 1 small shallot or 1–2 tbsp very finely chopped red onion
- 1 small dill pickle or a few slices, finely chopped (optional)
- 1–2 tbsp mayonnaise
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- 1–2 tsp lemon juice
- Salt and black pepper to taste
- Chopped fresh herbs (parsley, chives, or dill)
- Olive oil (a small drizzle)
- Bread or crackers to serve

Steps:
1) Cook the chicken:
   - Poach, roast, or pan-cook the chicken until fully done, then let it cool completely in the fridge. Cold chicken is easier to chop finely.

2) Chop:
   - Finely chop the chicken into very small pieces (almost minced).
   - Finely chop shallot, capers, and pickle if using.

3) Mix:
   - In a bowl, combine chicken, shallot, capers, pickle, herbs.
   - Add mayonnaise, Dijon mustard, lemon juice, a small drizzle of olive oil.
   - Mix gently until it holds together but isn’t soupy.
   - Taste and adjust with salt, pepper, and a bit more lemon if needed.

4) Plate like tartare:
   - Use a small bowl or a round cookie cutter/ring as a mold.
   - Pack the mixture gently, then flip onto a plate and lift the mold.
   - Drizzle a little olive oil around and maybe sprinkle extra herbs on top.

Serve with:
- Toasted bread slices or crackers.

This gives you a “fancy” look and lets you practice small knife cuts, seasoning, and plating, while staying safe.
