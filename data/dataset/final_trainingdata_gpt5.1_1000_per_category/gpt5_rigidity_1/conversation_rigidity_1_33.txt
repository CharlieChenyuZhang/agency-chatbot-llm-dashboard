### Human: I can do 10 minutes in the morning. I’m a total beginner and I want it completely secular. I’ve tried before and just got lost in thoughts and gave up. Can you give me a simple 10-minute routine I can follow every day?
### Assistant: Here’s a simple, fully secular 10‑minute morning routine, tailored to what you described:

1) Setup (1 minute)  
- Sit on a chair or cushion, back straight but not rigid, feet on the floor if in a chair.  
- Hands resting on thighs or in your lap.  
- Gently close your eyes or soften your gaze.

2) Intention (30 seconds)  
- Silently say one clear intention:  
  “For the next 10 minutes, I’m practicing paying attention kindly.”

3) Anchor on the breath (7 minutes)  
- Place attention on the physical sensations of breathing (nostrils, chest, or belly—pick one spot and stick with it).  
- Notice: cool air in / warm air out, chest or belly rising / falling.  
- Count breaths if it helps:  
  - Inhale (1), exhale (1); inhale (2), exhale (2)… up to 10, then start over at 1.  
- When you notice you’re lost in thought (which will happen a lot):  
  1) Briefly label it “thinking” (or “planning,” “worrying”).  
  2) Let the thought be, without arguing with it.  
  3) Gently return attention to the breath and start counting again if you were counting.

Treat every “I got lost in thought” moment as a *successful* rep of the exercise: you noticed, and you came back.

4) Open awareness (1 minute)  
- Let go of counting.  
- Notice whatever is most obvious: sounds, sensations in the body, or the breath.  
- You’re just observing, not changing anything.

5) Closing (30 seconds)  
- Take 2–3 slightly deeper breaths.  
- Silently acknowledge: “I showed up and practiced for 10 minutes today.”  
- Open your eyes and continue with your morning.

If you’d like, I can turn this into a short script you can read or record for yourself to follow along with each morning.

### Human: How should I handle days when I really don’t feel like meditating at all?
### Assistant: Use the smallest possible version of your practice on those days, and make the *goal* simply “show up,” not “have a good session.” Concretely:

1) Set a “minimum dose”:  
   - For you, make it **2 minutes** sitting, eyes closed or soft gaze, noticing the breath.  
   - This still “counts” as keeping your streak.

2) Use a rule:  
   - “I can skip *only after* I’ve done 2 minutes.”  
   - Most days, once you start, 2 minutes will naturally turn into more; if it doesn’t, that’s fine.

3) Adjust expectations:  
   - On low‑motivation days, silently tell yourself:  
     “Today’s practice can be distracted and messy. I’m just maintaining the habit.”

4) Use context to help:  
   - Tie it to a fixed cue: “After I brush my teeth, I sit for at least 2 minutes.”  
   - This way you’re not debating with yourself every morning.
