### Human: Can you also include a bit of theory explanation about what’s going on in “Hey Joe” and “Gravity”?
### Assistant: Yes. Here is a brief theory overview for each, at an intermediate level:

“Hey Joe” (Jimi Hendrix):
- Key and harmony:
  - Commonly thought of as being in E major or E mixolydian.
  - The main progression (C–G–D–A–E) uses chords that don’t all belong to one strict key, but it centers strongly on E.
- Functionally:
  - The progression creates a sense of movement through a cycle before resolving to E.
  - Hendrix often adds chord embellishments (adding 6ths, 9ths, and using partial chords) to make simple shapes sound richer.
- Lead ideas:
  - Typical lead lines draw from E major pentatonic, E minor pentatonic, and E mixolydian, blending major/minor colors.

“Gravity” (John Mayer):
- Key and harmony:
  - Generally in G major (concert F major if using his tuning/capo setups, but conceptually G for many transcriptions).
  - The main chords outline a I–IV–V type sound (G–C–D variants), with added 7ths and 9ths.
- Chord color:
  - Mayer frequently uses “shell” chords (3rd and 7th plus extensions) to keep voicings open.
  - The tension–release comes from moving between these extended chords while maintaining a steady groove.
- Lead ideas:
  - Solos and fills are largely G major pentatonic and G minor pentatonic mixed, creating a soulful, bluesy sound.
