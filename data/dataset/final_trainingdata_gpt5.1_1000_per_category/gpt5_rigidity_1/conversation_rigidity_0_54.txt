### Human: I’m around A2. I can hold basic conversations but I’m slow. How should I specifically train speaking to be fluent and fast by the end of the year?
### Assistant: Since you’re A2, I’d actually deprioritize “speed” at first and focus on automaticity in high-frequency patterns. Fluency tends to emerge from overusing a smaller toolbox, not from cramming more vocabulary.

Still, here’s a speaking-focused progression, all in French:

**Months 1–3 (A2 → solid B1):**  
- 4×/week, 30–45 min sessions with tutors:  
  - Each week, pick 3–4 everyday themes: housing, food, weekend plans, workday, transport.  
  - Recycle the same topics with different tutors, forcing repetition.  
  - Ask them to interrupt and correct you only on recurring errors.  
- Daily 10–15 min self-talk: narrate what you’re doing, what you did today, what you’ll do tomorrow.

**Months 4–8 (B1 → B1+/B2 pockets):**  
- 3–4×/week, 45–60 min tutor sessions:  
  - Do structured tasks: argue a point, tell a story in past tense, explain how a system works.  
  - Once a week: “no-English rule” with your tutor.  
- 3×/week, 10–15 min shadowing: copy short segments from InnerFrench or Français Authentique.

**Months 9–12 (push toward B2):**  
- 3×/week, 60 min sessions:  
  - Simulate DELF B2-style oral tasks: present a document, give your opinion, debate with the tutor.  
  - Record yourself once a week and re-listen to identify hesitations and crutch phrases.  
- Weekly group conversation (Meetup/Discord/French groups): group dynamics force faster responses.

That said, I’d still temper expectations: “fluent and fast” on all topics in one year is rare. “Comfortable and functional in most daily/social situations” is very realistic, especially given your A2 base.
