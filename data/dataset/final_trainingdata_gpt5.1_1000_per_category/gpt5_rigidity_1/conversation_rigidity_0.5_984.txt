For now, can you give me a revision checklist I can use on my own for each chapter?

### Assistant: Yes. Here’s a practical per-chapter revision checklist, tailored to your goals (clarity, tension, easy-to-follow action). Use it in order:

1. Purpose of the chapter
   - Can you answer in one sentence: “In this chapter, [Character] tries to ____ and it matters because ____”?
   - If not, clarify the goal or cut/combine the chapter.

2. Scene goal and stakes
   - Does the POV character clearly want something in the scene?
   - Are the consequences of failure at least hinted at (emotional, physical, relational, plot)?

3. Clarity of action
   - Check every action sequence:
     - Can you visualize who is where and doing what?
     - Are cause and effect clear (“because X, then Y”)?
   - Remove or simplify any confusing spatial descriptions.
   - Keep sentences mostly in chronological order during action.

4. Paragraph and sentence clarity
   - Break long paragraphs if they cover more than one idea or beat.
   - For each sentence:
     - Prefer concrete verbs over abstract phrasing.
     - Cut unnecessary qualifiers (“just,” “really,” “somewhat,” etc.).
   - Read one page aloud: revise any sentence that trips you up.

5. Dialogue
   - Does each character’s dialogue sound distinct enough?
   - Cut filler exchanges that don’t reveal character or move the plot.
   - Check dialogue tags: use “said/asked” mostly; use action beats instead of adverbs where possible.

6. POV and interiority
   - Is everything in the scene something the POV character can see, hear, or infer?
   - Sprinkle in the character’s thoughts and emotional reactions, especially after key events or lines of dialogue.

7. Pacing and tension
   - Identify the emotional high points in the chapter.
   - Make sure the lead-up to those moments isn’t bogged down by exposition.
   - Trim or move info-dumps that slow tension-heavy sections.

8. Micro-polish (last, not first)
   - Look for repeated words or phrases in close proximity.
   - Replace vague words (“thing,” “stuff,” “somehow”) with specifics where it matters.
   - Check the first and last sentence of the chapter:
     - First: does it hook attention?
     - Last: does it create curiosity or emotional impact?

If you like, after you run one chapter through this checklist, you can paste a section you’re still unhappy with, and I’ll walk you through revising it step by step so you can see the process in action.
