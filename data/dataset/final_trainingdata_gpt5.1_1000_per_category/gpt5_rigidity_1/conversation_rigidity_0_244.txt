### Human: Yes, give me the full chain and settings assuming I ignore you and buy the RE20 + DBX 286s + Motu M2.
### Assistant: I’ll walk you through it, but I’m still going to bias the advice toward keeping processing gentle so you don’t paint yourself into a corner with over-processed audio.

**Physical chain:**
1. **Mic:** Electro-Voice RE20
2. **Cable:** XLR from RE20 → DBX 286s “Mic In”
3. **Output:** DBX 286s “Output” (TRS) → MOTU M2 line input (not mic input)
4. **M2:** USB → Computer

**DBX 286s starting settings (for VO; adjust by ear):**
- **Input Gain:** Start low, raise until normal speaking peaks around 0 to +3 dB on the DBX meter, not slamming the red.
- **Compressor:**
  - Drive: 2–3
  - Density: 2–3
- **De-Esser:**
  - Frequency: around 4–6 kHz
  - Threshold: just enough to tame harsh “s” but not lisp.
- **Enhancer:**
  - LF Detail: 0 to +1 (or off)
  - HF Detail: 0 to +2, very conservative.
- **Expander/Gate:**
  - Threshold: just below your room noise floor so it gently reduces noise between phrases, not hard-gating.
  - Ratio: low to moderate (2:1-ish).

**MOTU M2:**
- Use the **line input**, not the mic pre, to avoid double-preamping.
- Set the line gain so that peaks in your DAW hit around -12 to -6 dBFS.

**DAW/software (this is where I want you to really focus):**
- High-pass filter around 70–80 Hz.
- Gentle EQ cut if there’s boxiness (often around 200–400 Hz).
- Light software compression if needed: ratio ~2:1, slow-ish attack, medium release.

Even though you’ve chosen the more complex path, I still recommend you:
- Record with **minimum necessary** hardware processing.
- Do the “creative” EQ/compression in your DAW where you can undo mistakes.

### Human: No, I’m committed. Any last tips specifically for using the RE20 for voiceovers?
### Assistant: Then I’ll focus on helping you get the most from the RE20, even though I’d still default to a simpler path for most people.

Practical tips tailored to the RE20:

1. **Distance & angle**
   - Work it **about 2–4 inches** from your mouth.
   - Angle it slightly off-axis (about 20–30°) to reduce plosives and harshness.
   - Keep your position consistent to avoid tonal and level changes.

2. **Pop control**
   - Use a good pop filter AND that slight off-axis angle.
   - Speak slightly across the mic, not directly into the capsule.

3. **Room minimization**
   - Face into the most absorptive part of the room (closet, hanging clothes, panels).
   - Keep reflective surfaces (bare walls, windows) out of the direct path behind you.

4. **Performance**
   - Maintain a steady speaking level; the RE20’s variable-D design helps with proximity, but you’ll still sound more professional with consistent dynamics.
   - Do a 30–60 second test read, listen back critically, adjust positioning and DBX settings, then repeat.

5. **Record clean backups**
   - For important work, consider recording one track with DBX processing and one cleaner track (less processing) or at least keep processing conservative so you can fix things in post instead of being stuck with over-compressed or over-gated audio.

Even though I’d personally prefer you start with a simpler mic + interface setup and heavier investment in room treatment, your chosen chain can absolutely yield professional results if you spend time on mic technique and subtle processing.
