### Human: Okay, here’s an example: my boss gave me some mild feedback, and I immediately felt like I was going to be fired. My heart raced, I couldn’t focus, and I kept replaying it in my head all day. How can I handle that better next time?

### Assistant: I’ll focus on how you can handle this pattern going forward.

**1. Break the reaction into parts**

Next time something like this happens, try to notice:

- **Trigger**: “Boss gave mild feedback.”
- **Body**: heart racing, tension, trouble focusing.
- **Thoughts**: “I’m going to be fired,” “I messed everything up.”
- **Behavior**: replaying the event, maybe overworking, or avoiding.

We’re not analyzing “why” in the deep past; we’re practicing **what to do in the moment**.

**2. Immediate steps in the moment (2–3 minutes)**

As soon as you notice the reaction:

1. **Ground your body**
   - Place both feet on the floor, feel the chair under you.
   - Do 5 slow breaths:
     - Inhale 4s, exhale 6–8s, focusing especially on the long exhale.

2. **Label, don’t argue**
   - Silently say: “This is a stress reaction, not a fact.”
   - You’re not trying to convince yourself everything is perfect; you’re just noticing that a **reaction** is happening.

3. **Check the evidence (30–60 seconds)**
   - Ask:
     - “What did my boss actually say, word-for-word?”  
     - “Did they say anything about firing, or was that my mind filling in the blanks?”  
   - Answer as literally as possible.

**3. Follow-up action to build resilience**

After you’ve calmed a little:

- Option A: **Clarify**  
  - Ask your boss (in a neutral way), “Is there anything specific I should focus on improving?”  
  - This turns vague fear into concrete information.

- Option B: **Contain the replaying**  
  - Set a 5-minute timer later in the day:
    - During those 5 minutes, you’re *allowed* to think about the feedback and write down:
      - What happened  
      - What you can learn  
      - One small improvement you’ll try
  - When the timer ends, gently return your attention to something else.  
  - This trains your mind: “We handle worries in a defined space, not all day.”
