### Human: Mornings are best. Evenings I’m pretty useless. Also, I’m slow at making Anki cards. It eats up a lot of time. Is there a faster way while still doing spaced repetition?

### Assistant: Given your time limit and energy pattern, it’s actually more optimal to **simplify how you create cards** rather than force a “perfect” Anki setup.

Two adjustments:

1. **Use ultra-simple card formats only**
   - No fancy formatting, no images unless critical.
   - Two types:
     - Q&A: “What is the innervation of the deltoid?” → “Axillary nerve (C5–C6).”
     - Cloze: “The deltoid is innervated by the {{c1::axillary nerve}} ({{c2::C5–C6}}).”
   - If a fact doesn’t fit into a one-line question, consider turning it into a *diagram you redraw from memory* instead of a card.

2. **Limit card creation to a fixed small window**
   - Example: first 20–25 minutes of Block 2.
   - During the rest of Block 2, star/highlight key lines in your notes instead of making cards; you can optionally make a few extra cards the next day if needed.

If you’d rather minimize Anki even more, an alternative that still uses spaced repetition is:

- Keep a **“Question Log” document**:
  - Every day, write 15–20 short Q&A pairs (like mini flashcards) in a table.
  - Each day, review:
    - Today’s questions
    - Questions from 2 days ago
    - Questions from 4–5 days ago
  - That recreates a simple spaced schedule (Day 1 → Day 3 → Day 5/6).

Anki is more automated, but a Question Log is often faster to maintain.

- **Not ideal:** A big late-night cram *instead of* sleep.

- Set a hard cutoff time (e.g., no studying after 20:30).

- After your cutoff, you’re *not slacking*—you’re actively doing the most performance-enhancing thing: protecting sleep.
