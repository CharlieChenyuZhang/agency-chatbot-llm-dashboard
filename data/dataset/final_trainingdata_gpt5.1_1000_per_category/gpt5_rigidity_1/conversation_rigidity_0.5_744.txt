### Assistant: Going heavily into crypto and a few hot tech stocks can increase your *chance* of getting there faster, but it also significantly increases your chance of not getting there at all—or even losing what you have.

Since your goal is financial independence (not just “maybe get rich”), a better compromise is:

1) Core vs. satellite approach
- Core (80–90%):
  - Diversified index funds (the boring stuff that tends to work long-term).
- Satellite (10–20% max):
  - Higher-risk bets:
    - Crypto
    - Individual tech stocks
    - Sector-specific funds (e.g., tech, biotech)

This way:
- If your risky bets do well, you reach FI faster.
- If they do badly, your FI plan is delayed but not destroyed.

2) Why not go “all-in” on high risk?
Even if you’re okay with big swings emotionally:
- Sequence risk: If markets or crypto crash near the time you want to become independent, you could lose years of progress.
- Concentration risk: A few assets that look unstoppable can collapse quickly (Dot-com bubble, 2008, individual stocks like Enron, many altcoins).

3) A more aggressive but still sane portfolio
If you want higher risk than average:
- 95–100% stocks in your main accounts while you’re still far from FI.
- Within stocks:
  - Overweight growth/tech via a sector ETF instead of just 1–2 individual names.

  - Cap at, say, 5–10% of your *total* net worth.
  - Accept that this part is speculative and volatile.

Example allocation for someone in your shoes who wants higher risk:
- 70%: Total stock market index
- 15%: International stock index
- 10%: Tech or innovation-focused ETF
- 5%: Crypto (BTC/ETH, not random tiny coins)

I’m nudging you away from an all-in gamble because:
