### Human: Let’s do Blogger. I want my own domain, dark theme, and Google Analytics is fine. Walk me through it.

### Assistant: Here’s a straightforward path with Blogger + GA + dark theme.

#### 1. Create the blog

1. Go to https://www.blogger.com and sign in with your Google account.
2. Click **Create your blog**.
3. Enter:
   - **Title**: your blog name.
   - **Address**: temporary `something.blogspot.com` (we’ll attach your domain later).
4. Choose any starter theme (we’ll change it in a minute).
5. Click **Create blog**.

#### 2. Choose a dark (or dark-ish) theme

1. In the left sidebar, click **Theme**.
2. Browse themes; look for darker options like:
   - “Notable Dark”
   - “Emporio” with dark variants, etc.
3. Hover over the theme → click **Apply**.
4. Click **Customize** to tweak colors and fonts if needed.

#### 3. Attach your custom domain

1. In Blogger, go to **Settings**.
2. Under **Publishing**, click **Custom domain**.
3. Enter your domain, e.g., `www.yourdomain.com` (include `www`).
4. Blogger will show you **CNAME** records and possibly **A** records to add at your domain registrar.
5. Go to your domain registrar’s DNS settings (e.g., Namecheap, GoDaddy, Cloudflare):
   - Add the CNAME entries exactly as Blogger shows (e.g., `www` → `ghs.google.com`, and a second verification CNAME).
   - If instructed, add A records pointing to Google’s IPs (Blogger will list them).
6. Save DNS changes. It can take from a few minutes up to a few hours.
7. Back in Blogger **Settings**, click **Save** for the custom domain.
8. Once it works, enable **Redirect yourdomain.com to www.yourdomain.com** if that option appears.

#### 4. Enable Google Analytics 4 (GA4)

Google’s integration with Blogger has changed over time; the most reliable now is to paste the GA tag yourself.

1. Go to https://analytics.google.com/ and create a GA4 property:
   - Click **Admin → Create property**.
   - Name it (e.g., “My Blogger Blog”).
   - Set timezone, currency.
2. Add a **Web data stream** for your domain (`https://www.yourdomain.com`).
3. GA will show you a **Measurement ID** (looks like `G-XXXXXXX`) and a script snippet:
   ```html
   <!-- Google tag (gtag.js) -->
   <script async src="https://www.googletagmanager.com/gtag/js?id=G-XXXXXXX"></script>
   <script>
     window.dataLayer = window.dataLayer || [];
     function gtag(){dataLayer.push(arguments);}
     gtag('js', new Date());
     gtag('config', 'G-XXXXXXX');
   </script>
   ```
4. Copy that snippet.

5. Go back to **Blogger → Theme**.
6. Click the **down arrow** next to **Customize** → choose **Edit HTML**.
7. Locate the `<head>` section in the HTML.
8. Paste the GA snippet just before `</head>`.
9. Click **Save**.

Within 24 hours, page views should appear in your GA dashboard (or use the **Realtime** view to test immediately).

#### A records (root domain)

If you want `yourdomain.com` to redirect to `www.yourdomain.com`, add these A records (if they’re not already there):

- Type: **A**
  - Host: `@`
  - Value: `216.239.32.21`
  - TTL: Automatic
- Type: **A**
  - Host: `@`
  - Value: `216.239.34.21`
- Type: **A**
  - Host: `@`
  - Value: `216.239.36.21`
- Type: **A**
  - Host: `@`
  - Value: `216.239.38.21`

(Those are Google’s Blogger IPs.)

#### CNAME for `www`

Blogger will show something like:

- `www` → `ghs.google.com`

So on Namecheap:

- Type: **CNAME Record**
  - Host: `www`
  - Value: `ghs.google.com`
  - TTL: Automatic

#### Verification CNAME (if Blogger shows one)

Blogger often gives a second CNAME, e.g.:

- `ab12cd34` → `gv-xyz123.domainverify.googlehosted.com`

So you add another CNAME:

- Type: **CNAME Record**
  - Host: `ab12cd34`  (whatever Blogger shows under “Name”/“Label”)
  - Value: `gv-xyz123.domainverify.googlehosted.com` (whatever it shows under “Destination”/“Target”)
  - TTL: Automatic

After adding these:

1. Wait 5–30 minutes (sometimes longer).
2. In Blogger **Settings → Publishing → Custom domain**, click **Save** again if it previously failed.
3. Once it’s accepted, enable the `Redirect yourdomain.com to www.yourdomain.com` toggle if available.
