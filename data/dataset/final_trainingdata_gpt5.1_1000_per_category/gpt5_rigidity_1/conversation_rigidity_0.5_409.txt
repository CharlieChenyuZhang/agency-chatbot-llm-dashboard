### Human: How often should I go to failure on the big lifts? I like pushing until I can’t do another rep.

### Assistant: On the big 3, regularly going to true failure (where you attempt a rep and miss or barely complete it) is usually **not optimal** for long-term strength:

- It spikes fatigue.
- Increases injury risk under heavy loads.
- Often reduces the quality of your remaining sets.

A better approach:
- Keep your **main squat/bench/deadlift sets at 1–3 reps in reserve (RIR)** most of the time.
- Use **accessories** (rows, presses, curls, leg presses, etc.) to push closer to failure, especially on the last set.

Practical guideline:
- Compounds (big 3): usually stop at RIR 1–3.
- Accessories: last set can be RIR 0–1 (pretty close to failure, maybe one “questionable” rep left).

If you really enjoy all-out sets, you can include:
- A **single AMRAP (as many reps as possible) set** on a variation (e.g., squat 1×AMRAP @ ~70%) once every week or two, rather than every session.

Would you like help adding 1–2 “fun” AMRAP slots into the 4-day plan without wrecking recovery?

**Signs you should deload or back off earlier:**
If you notice several of these for more than a few days:
- Performance drops: weights that used to move fast now feel heavy, and it persists across sessions.
- Joint pain: especially in knees, hips, shoulders, or low back that doesn’t warm up and go away.
- Motivation crash: you dread training you usually enjoy.
- Sleep issues: trouble falling asleep or waking up unrefreshed.
- General fatigue: stairs feel harder, you feel “drained” all day.

If that happens:
- Reduce **load** on main lifts by ~10–15% for 1 week.
- Cut **volume** (total sets) by about 30–50% for that week.
- Keep moving, but don’t chase PRs.
