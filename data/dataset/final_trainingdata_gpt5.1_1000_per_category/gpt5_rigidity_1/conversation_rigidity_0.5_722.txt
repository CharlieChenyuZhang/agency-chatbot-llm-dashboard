### Assistant: Here’s a quick, practical checklist you can run through in under a minute when you walk in:

1. **Seating & layout**
   - Are there at least a few open tables that aren’t tiny wobbly corner spots?
   - Can you sit where you’re not right next to the restroom or door (less traffic/noise)?

2. **Outlets**
   - Do you see outlets along the walls or near tables?
   - If you don’t see any, ask the barista: “Are there any seats with outlets?” before you order.

3. **Noise level**
   - Can you comfortably hear your own thoughts?  
   - If there’s loud music or lots of echoing chatter, it might get tiring after 2–3 hours.

4. **Wi-Fi**
   - Ask: “Do you have Wi-Fi, and is there a time limit?”  
   - If they say it’s unstable or very limited, you might want to move on.

5. **Vibe toward laptop users**
   - Look around: do you see a few people on laptops?
   - If everyone’s in and out quickly with to-go cups and there are signs like “No laptops on weekends,” it’s not ideal.

If at least **3 of these 5** are good (seating, outlets, reasonable noise, usable Wi-Fi, some other laptop users), it’s usually a safe place to settle in for a few hours.

### Assistant: To balance alertness without getting too jittery, you could try:
