### Assistant: Stating expectations clearly is good, but “From now on, decide without me” is likely to backfire:

- They may not know **what** decisions are safe to make.
- Some people will worry about being blamed if a choice goes wrong.
- Others might ignore it because their habits and incentives haven’t changed.

Instead of a single announcement, I’d suggest a simple, structured shift. You can still express your expectation, but pair it with **guardrails and practice**.

Here’s a practical alternative:

1. **Define decision zones** (so they know what’s safe):

   - “Green” = you can decide; just inform me later  
   - “Yellow” = propose a decision; we confirm together  
   - “Red” = I decide, but I’ll explain my reasoning

2. **Change how you respond to questions**:

   When they ask, “What should I do?” respond with:
   - “What do you think we should do?”  
   - “If you had to pick now, which way would you go and why?”

3. **Make a short, clear announcement**:

   > “I want you all to take more ownership in day-to-day decisions.  
   > To make that easier, here’s how I’m thinking about it:

   > - Anything that affects other teams or deadlines: bring me a proposal and we’ll decide together.  
   > - Big product or company-level changes: I’ll decide, but I’ll explain why.

### Human: Yes, because a couple of them always say "I don't know" even when I push them. What do I do then without being annoying?
### Assistant: When they say “I don’t know,” they often mean one of three things:

1. “I’m afraid of being wrong.”
2. “I’m missing information.”
3. “I’m used to you deciding, so this is uncomfortable.”

Instead of pushing harder (“Come on, just decide”), you can **lower the risk and narrow the question**. Here’s a practical script:

1. **Make it safe to be imperfect**  
   - “You don’t have to be right, just take a first stab.”

2. **Narrow the choice**  
   - “If you *had* to pick between option A and B right now, which way would you lean, and why?”

3. **Share responsibility**  
   - “I’m not locking us into your choice. I just want to understand your thinking; we’ll decide together.”

Example interaction:

- Them: “I don’t know, you tell me.”
- You: “No worries, you don’t have to be right. If you had to choose between refactoring now or shipping a quick fix and refactoring later, what’s your gut and why? We’ll decide together.”

If, after that, they *still* won’t engage, then you can gently surface the pattern in a 1:1:

> “I’ve noticed when I ask for your recommendation, you often say you don’t know and wait for me to decide. I’m asking because I want you to grow into more ownership. What’s making it hard to give a first opinion—is it confidence, missing context, something else?”
