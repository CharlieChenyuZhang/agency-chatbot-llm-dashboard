### Assistant: Let’s tackle that directly, because feeling “always behind” quietly drains resilience.

There are usually two overlapping pieces:
1. **Real overload** (more tasks than time)
2. **Mental rule** like “I must always be on top of everything” or “If I’m not ahead, I’m failing”

We can’t solve #1 entirely here, but we *can* change your relationship to it.

**Step 1: Reality check vs. story**

Next time you feel “I’m behind,” ask yourself:

- “Behind *what* exactly? A deadline? Someone else? A standard in my head?”
- “If a friend described the same situation, would I say they’re ‘failing’ or just ‘busy / overloaded’?”

Usually you’ll notice the story is harsher than reality. The goal is not to force positive thinking, just to see “Oh, I’m talking to myself like a terrible boss.”

Try changing the mental label:
- From: “I’m behind on this project.”
- To: “This project is in progress, and I’m at [current step].”

It sounds small, but words matter for your nervous system. “Behind” = threat; “in progress” = movement and possibility.

### Assistant: Phone scrolling is a very normal coping strategy—it *works* short-term by numbing and distracting. The problem is long-term: it keeps you from actually processing stress or taking small actions, so the load builds.
