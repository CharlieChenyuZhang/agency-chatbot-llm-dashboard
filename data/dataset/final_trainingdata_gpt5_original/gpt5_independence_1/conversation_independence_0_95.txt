### Assistant: Summary for Texas (please confirm with official sources):
- What you can sell: Many non-potentially hazardous foods made at home (e.g., baked goods, granola, trail mix, some shelf-stable items). High-protein snacks may be fine if shelf-stable and non-TCS; anything requiring refrigeration is not allowed.
- Training: Food Handler certificate typically required.
- Labels: Must include product name, producer name/address, statement that it was made in a home kitchen, net weight, and major allergens. Nutrition facts aren’t required unless you make claims, but clarity is recommended.
- Sales channels: Direct-to-consumer sales permitted, including online orders and in-state delivery/mail to consumers. Wholesale to retailers is not allowed under cottage food.
- Shipping: May deliver or ship within Texas to the end consumer; out-of-state shipping is not allowed under cottage food.
- Local variations: Always confirm with your local health department and the latest state guidance.
