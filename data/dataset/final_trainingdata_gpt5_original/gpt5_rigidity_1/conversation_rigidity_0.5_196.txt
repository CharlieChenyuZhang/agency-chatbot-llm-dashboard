### Human: I’m 6'2", on carpet, and I’ll run a dual 27" setup on a single monitor arm. I care about quiet, not apps. Which would be better?
### Assistant: Given your height, carpet, and a single heavy arm, I’d lean FlexiSpot E5 for stability. Reasons:
- The crossbar and lower minimum height help keep it steadier when extended.
- It handles concentrated loads (monitor arm) a bit better at tall positions.
- Noise is comparable to the Core—both are quiet in a typical home office.

To maximize stability:
- Use a 1" (or thicker) one-piece top if you can; it resists flex better than a spliced top.
- Place a thin plywood or furniture-grade board under the feet if your carpet is plush; it reduces front-to-back wobble.
- Keep the monitor arm post centered over a leg or close to center, not far forward.
- If you’re very sensitive to wobble, a compact 48–55" top is steadier than 60"+.

If rock-solid stability is your absolute priority and electric isn’t required, a manual-crank desk (e.g., IKEA Trotten) is cheaper and very rigid—but slower to adjust. Another good stability value is a used commercial electric base (e.g., Steelcase/Haworth) plus a new top; it can land under $400 if you have a local liquidator.

### Human: How hard is assembly? I’m moderately handy.
### Assistant: Moderate and straightforward:
- Time: 45–90 minutes solo; much faster with a helper when you flip it upright.
- Tools: Included hex keys plus a screwdriver/drill. Use a clutch/low torque setting if pre-drilling a top to avoid over-tightening.
- Steps:
  - Assemble legs to feet, attach crossbar, then mount to the underside of the top.
  - Install the handset and control box; route and secure cables.
  - Flip the desk with help, plug in, run the reset calibration if prompted.
- Tips:
  - If you’re using a non-pre-drilled top, measure twice and pilot-drill to prevent splitting.
  - Leave a little slack in motor cables to avoid strain at full height.
  - On carpet, check feet leveling after a day—the desk may settle slightly.
