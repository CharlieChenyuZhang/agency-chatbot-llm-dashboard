### Human: Create 10 active recall flashcards on VPC networking. Format: Q: ... A: ... Keep answers <= 2 lines. No more than 10 cards.
### Assistant: 1) Q: What’s the difference between security groups and NACLs?
A: SGs are stateful, instance-level; NACLs are stateless, subnet-level.

2) Q: When do you need a NAT Gateway?
A: For private subnets to reach the internet without inbound exposure.

3) Q: Can VPC peering be transitive?
A: No, VPC peering is non-transitive; use Transit Gateway instead.

4) Q: Gateway vs Interface VPC Endpoint—key difference?
A: Gateway: S3/Dynamo via routes. Interface: ENI-based, many services.

5) Q: Which routes enable internet access from a public subnet?
A: Default route 0.0.0.0/0 to an Internet Gateway.

6) Q: How do private subnets reach S3 without NAT costs?
A: Use a Gateway VPC Endpoint for S3 and bucket policies.

7) Q: What breaks with overlapping CIDR blocks?
A: Peering and TGW attachments can’t route overlapping CIDRs.

8) Q: How to restrict Interface Endpoint access?
A: Use endpoint policies and security groups.

9) Q: How do you achieve centralized routing across many VPCs?
A: Use Transit Gateway with route tables per attachment.

10) Q: Difference between Route 53 private and public hosted zones?
A: Private resolves within VPCs; public resolves on the internet.

DynamoDB throughput formula
- Read capacity units: 1 strongly consistent read/sec for 4 KB item
- Eventually consistent reads: 2 reads/sec for 4 KB item per RCU
- Write capacity units: 1 write/sec for 1 KB item per WCU
- Required RCUs = ceil((item_size_KB/4) × reads_per_sec × consistency_factor)
- Required WCUs = ceil((item_size_KB/1) × writes_per_sec)
