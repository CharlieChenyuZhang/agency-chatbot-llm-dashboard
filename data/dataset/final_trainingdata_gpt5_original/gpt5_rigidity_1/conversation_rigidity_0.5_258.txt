### Human: Looks good. I can’t find canned chickpeas—store is out. What can I use instead?
### Assistant: Easy swaps:
- Best 1:1 swap: canned white beans (cannellini or great northern). Same drain/rinse, same texture.
- Also good: canned black beans (taste is a bit earthier) or canned lentils (softer, still great cold).
- If only dry beans are available, quick-soak 1 cup dry beans (boil 2 minutes, rest 1 hour, then simmer 45–60 minutes). But that adds time—canned is faster.

For the tuna bowls, use white beans and keep everything else the same.

### Human: I only have 2 hours on Sunday to cook. Can this fit?
### Assistant: Yes, with a streamlined flow and a couple of small shortcuts. Here’s a 2-hour plan:

- Minute 0: Start rice (big batch for L1–L4 and fajita bowls). Start quinoa in a second pot. Boil water for soba; set aside for later.
- Minute 10: Chili pot on (brown turkey, add aromatics, beans, tomatoes; then simmer unattended 35–40 minutes).
- Minute 15: Start red lentil curry (15–20 minutes total); finish and cool.
- Minute 30: Slice peppers/onions; cook chicken strips for fajitas (10–12 minutes). Toss with spices, cool.
- Minute 45: Microwave-steam green beans (or stovetop blanch). Sear salmon is fastest day-of; to keep within 2 hours, pre-portion and refrigerate, cook fresh on D5 (8–10 minutes that night). If you must cook now, use a covered skillet to keep it moist and reheat gently later—fresh-cooked is tastier, though.
- Minute 55: Make tuna–white bean mix (no-cook).
- Minute 65: Cook soba; rinse cold. Toss with sauce and edamame (microwave edamame 3–4 minutes).
- Minute 85: Portion everything, label, and cool. Final 35 minutes are mostly portioning/cleanup while chili finishes.

If that still feels tight, we can swap the soba bowl for a no-cook couscous salad to save 10–15 minutes.

### Human: I have a tiny fridge. Will all this fit?
### Assistant: It can, with a couple tweaks:
- Freeze half of the chili and half the fajita chicken right after cooling; thaw midweek overnight.
- Cook quinoa and half the rice; keep extra rice dry and cook midweek (10–15 minutes).
- Use frozen green beans and frozen edamame to save space and extend shelf life.
- Store lunches in stackable zip bags laid flat instead of bulky containers; reheat in bowls.
- Keep salmon frozen; cook fresh on D5. If buying fresh only, cook on D1 and freeze portions after cooking.

If space is still tight, we can swap the soba bowl to a couscous salad (dry until mixed), which stores more compactly.

### Human: Can you give me reheating instructions?
### Assistant: Sure:
- Chili: Microwave 2–3 minutes per serving, stirring halfway. Stovetop: medium heat 5–7 minutes with a splash of water.
- Fajita bowls: Rice 1–2 minutes; chicken/peppers 1–2 minutes separately, then combine. Add a squeeze of lime before eating.
- Tuna–white bean bowls: Eat cold; if over rice, warm rice first then top with salad.
- Red lentil curry: Microwave 2–3 minutes; if too thick, add 1–2 tbsp water/coconut milk.
- Soba bowl: Eat cold/room temp. If using canned chicken, you can warm it 30–45 seconds or keep cold.
- Salmon + quinoa + green beans: Best to cook salmon day-of. If reheating cooked salmon, microwave 45–60 seconds covered to avoid drying; stovetop low heat 3–4 minutes with a splash of water and a lid.

General tips:
- Vent lids; add a spoon of water to rice dishes to keep moisture.
- Label portions with meal and date; eat refrigerated meals within 4 days or freeze.
