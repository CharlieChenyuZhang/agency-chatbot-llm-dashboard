- Structure per week: 2 easy runs, 1 quality run (tempo or intervals), 1 long run, optional short recovery jog.

- Shoes with 300–500 miles lifespan; moisture-wicking socks; anti-chafe; weather-appropriate layers.

### Human: It's Chicago Marathon, Oct 12. Tell me historically what to expect and how to adjust my race plan if it's over 70°F.
### Assistant: Historically for mid-October in Chicago:
- Typical early-morning lows: around 48–55°F
- Typical daytime highs: around 60–65°F
- Variability is significant; warm years happen.

If it’s over 70°F at race time:
- Pace adjustment: slow by about 2–4% versus cool conditions.
  - Your MP 9:32/mi becomes roughly 9:45–9:55/mi if temps are >70°F.
- Execution:
  - First 10K: hold the adjusted pace even if it feels easy.
  - Midrace: keep fueling steady; shorten strides slightly to maintain cadence.
  - Final 10K: only speed up if you’re cooling well and feeling strong.
- Hydration: take small sips at most aid stations; drink to thirst.

### Human: Write me a simple one-page checklist I can print and use the week before and on race day.
### Assistant: Marathon checklist

Week before
- Confirm bib pickup details, start time, corral, and transport/parking.
- Lock in goal pace and fueling timing.
- Final long run done; taper runs easy with a few short MP segments.
- Sleep: aim for consistent 7–9 hours, especially 2–3 nights out.
- Test race kit on an easy run.
- Laundry: wash socks, shorts, top, hat/visor.
- Lay out and label your on-course fueling.
- Check course map and aid station locations.
- Toenails trimmed; any hot spots taped on a short run.

Day before
- Pack gear: shoes, socks, shorts/tights, top, sports bra, hat/visor, sunglasses, watch, safety pins or bib belt, anti-chafe, throwaway layer.
- Fuel: simple, familiar high-carb meals; hydrate normally.
- Set multiple alarms; confirm wake-up and departure times.
- Pin bib and attach timing chip if separate.
- Charge watch/phone; download course to watch if you use it.

Race morning
- Eat pre-race breakfast 3–3.5 hours before.
- Apply anti-chafe; bathroom early.
- Dress for start temp; bring throwaway layer.
- Pack: fueling, bottle/flask if carrying, ID, metro card/cash if needed.
- Warm-up: 5–10 min easy jog or brisk walk + a few gentle drills.
- Line up in correct corral; final sip of water 5–10 min before start.

During race
- First miles: settle slightly slower than MP, relax shoulders, breathe.
- Fuel every ~20 min; sip water to thirst at aid stations.
- Midrace: monitor effort, not just pace; stick to plan.
- Final 10K: hold form; push only if sustainable.

Post-finish
- Keep moving; hydrate and eat carbs + some protein.
- Change into dry layer.
- Gentle stretching later; no hard running for a few days.

### Human: Okay, give me conservative self-care steps I can follow for the next 5 days without running.
### Assistant: Here’s a cautious 5-day plan without running.

Daily (all 5 days)
- Rest from impact; avoid hills and jumping.
- Ice 10–15 minutes, 2–3×/day if it helps.
- Compression sleeve/sock during the day if comfortable; remove at night.
- Gentle calf/ankle mobility: ankle circles, alphabet, 2× daily.

Day 1–2
- Calf and foot isometrics: press forefoot into the floor (light effort) 5×10–20 seconds.
- Cross-train 20–30 min low-impact if pain-free: cycling easy, pool running, or brisk walking.

Day 3–4
- Add gentle calf raises if pain ≤2/10: 2×10 double-leg, slow controlled.
- Add tibialis raises (heels down, lift forefoot to the sky against a wall): 2×10.
- Light foam rolling on calves (not directly on painful shin).

Day 5
- Progress calf raises to 3×12 if tolerated; add single-leg only if pain-free.
- Short test walk 20–30 min; no running yet.

If pain worsens, becomes sharp with walking, or there’s bony tenderness you can pinpoint, stop and see a clinician. When pain is ≤1–2/10 with brisk walking and hopping in place, you can plan a gradual return-to-run progression.
