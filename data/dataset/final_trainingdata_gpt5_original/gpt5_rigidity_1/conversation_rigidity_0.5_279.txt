### Human: I want to learn to play guitar well enough to perform at my friend's wedding in three weeks. Is that doable?
### Assistant: It’s ambitious for a total beginner, but not impossible if we define “perform” smartly. Instead of aiming for advanced playing, we can:

- Use a simple strumming pattern and a capo to keep chords easy
- Keep the arrangement short (60–90 seconds) so it’s polished
- Optionally perform with a singer or a backing track to share the load

If you’re open to that scope, I’ll map out a focused plan. Do you already have a guitar?
### Human: I don't own a guitar yet; should I buy one or rent? I'm on a tight budget.
### Assistant: On a tight timeline and budget, renting or borrowing is ideal. If you do buy:
- Acoustic recommendation: a used Yamaha FG/FS series or Fender FA series (often $100–200 used)
- For softer strings and easier fretting: a classical guitar (nylon strings)
- Check local music stores for rentals or short-term “try-before-you-buy” programs
- If buying used: test tuning stability, fret buzz, and action height (strings shouldn’t feel like a cheese cutter)

If you can borrow one for a week first, you’ll learn what neck size and string type you prefer before spending.
### Human: I have small hands, and my fingers hurt when I tried a friend's steel-string. Any advice?
### Assistant: Absolutely. You’ve got good options:
- Try a classical guitar (nylon strings feel softer and are gentler on fingertips)
- Or put lighter gauge strings (e.g., .010–.047) on a steel-string and get the setup adjusted at a shop to lower the action
- Choose a smaller body (concert or parlor size) for reach and comfort
- Keep sessions short initially (10–15 minutes) and do quick finger stretches; fingertip soreness eases within a week

If you’re open to it, nylon-string would be the most comfortable fast-track.
### Human: I'd prefer to stick with guitar, not ukulele. Can I still do it in three weeks?
### Assistant: Yes—with a focused goal. You likely won’t play complex fingerstyle, but you can credibly accompany a song. Plan:
- Learn 3–4 chords in easy shapes (C, G, Am, F or G, D, Em, C)
- One steady strumming pattern
- A short arrangement you can repeat confidently
- Use a capo to fit the singer’s key without new chord shapes

You’ll sound better doing one simple thing cleanly than something advanced that feels shaky.
### Human: Okay, what's a daily practice plan?
### Assistant: Here’s a simple 21-day sprint, ~25–30 minutes/day (shorten to 15 if needed):
- Days 1–3: Learn chord shapes and clean transitions. Practice slowly with a metronome. Strumming: downstrokes on each beat.
- Days 4–6: Add a basic pattern (D D U U D U). Switch between two chords every 4 beats.
- Days 7–9: Learn your chosen song’s chord order. Practice sections separately, then link them.
- Days 10–12: Play along to a click or backing track at 70–80% speed. Record yourself; fix buzzes and timing.
- Days 13–15: Add dynamics (softer verse, fuller chorus). Practice a clean start and a tidy ending.
- Days 16–18: Simulate performance: stand up, minimal looking at hands, one “take” per day.
- Days 19–21: Dress rehearsal: play the exact wedding arrangement daily. Keep a backup plan ready.

Rest 1 day if fingertips are very sore.

### Human: I get nervous performing. Any tips? Also I don't want to perform alone.

To avoid performing alone:
- Ask a friend to sing while you play
- Or play along with a soft backing track; you carry rhythm, the track fills space
- Keep it short (one verse/chorus) to stay polished

### Human: Could you show me how to play Can't Help Falling in Love with simple chords and strumming?
### Assistant: Sure, simplified in key of C (use a capo to match the singer later).

Chords you need: C, G, Am, F, Em
- If F barre is tough, use Fmaj7 (xx3210)

Basic strum: D D U U D U (count 1 2 & & 4 &). Keep it gentle.

Common section progressions (no lyrics, just chords):
- Verse: C  G  Am  F  | C  G  C  C
- Pre-chorus: Em  B7  Em  B7  | Em  B7  Em  Em (if B7 is awkward, sub G)
- Chorus: C  G  Am  F  | C  G  C  C

Simpler version (skip B7 section):
- Verse: C  G  Am  F (repeat)
- Chorus: C  G  Am  F (repeat)

Start slow. Aim for smooth chord changes more than speed.
