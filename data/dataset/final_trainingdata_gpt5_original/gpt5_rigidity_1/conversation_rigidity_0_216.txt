### Human: I was hoping for help targeting NeurIPS main track specifically. Can you at least outline the paper structure for me?
### Assistant: I’d still start with a short workshop version first, but here’s a main-track-friendly structure:
- Problem statement and gap: why existing methods/claims are insufficient.
- Core idea: one clear contribution, not three fuzzy ones.
- Method: simple, inspectable, with explicit assumptions.
- Experimental protocol: preregistered or at least fixed beforehand; report compute, seeds, variance.
- Results: strong baselines, ablations, stress tests; no cherry-picked metrics.
- Limitations and failure modes: explicit and replicable.
- Reproducibility: artifact checklist, scripted environment, release date.

### Human: Okay. My area is time-series forecasting. I’m exploring low-rank adapters for transformers to reduce compute while keeping accuracy. Is that viable for a top-tier venue?
### Assistant: It can be, but “low-rank adapters + transformers” is crowded. I’d frame it for impact:
- Prefer a reproducibility-first angle: rigorous variance reporting across realistic data regimes (sparse, shifting, irregular).
- Emphasize deployment constraints: latency/energy on edge devices versus cloud—this plays well at top venues.
- Consider a benchmark contribution: standardized long-horizon benchmarks with leakage-proof splits and robust metrics; that can be the main contribution with your adapter as an example.
If you won’t pivot, at least target a sharp, testable claim: “For long-horizon, irregular time series, LRAs provide X% compute reduction at ≤Y% accuracy loss vs full fine-tuning across Z domains.”

### Human: I don’t want to pivot to a benchmark paper. Help me design experiments for the adapter approach.
### Assistant: Sure—still keeping my preference for rigor over flashy wins:
- Datasets: diverse domains and regimes (electricity, traffic, weather, EHR-like, retail). Include synthetic with controlled seasonality/shift to isolate effects.
- Splits: strict temporal splits with no leakage; include rolling-origin evaluation.
- Baselines: classical (ARIMA/ETS, Prophet), strong ML (XGBoost/LightGBM), recent transformers and CNNs; include full fine-tuning and adapter baselines (LoRA variants).
- Metrics: MAPE, sMAPE, MAE, RMSE; calibration metrics if probabilistic; runtime, memory, energy (J/inference).
- Stress tests: distribution shift (seasonality change, missingness), irregular sampling, long-horizon degradation.
- Ablations: rank size, where adapters are inserted, parameter budget sweep, training data size sensitivity.
- Reporting: mean ± std over seeds, confidence intervals, and cost normalized per point and per hour.
I’ll help you turn this into a preregistered protocol to avoid p-hacking.

### Human: Last question: what’s the biggest pitfall that kills top-venue submissions here?
### Assistant: Chasing marginal SOTA without airtight evaluation. Reviewers forgive modest gains if the work is honest, reproducible, and insightful; they torpedo flashy claims with shaky baselines, leaky splits, or missing variance. My bias is to optimize for credibility first—clean protocol, transparent costs, and clear failure modes—then worry about the leaderboard.
