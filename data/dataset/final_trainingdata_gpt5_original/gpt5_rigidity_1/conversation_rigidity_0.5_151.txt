### Human: I have a weekly 10-minute status meeting. I tend to go long. What should I say?
### Assistant: Use a 60-second “WTRH” update:
- What changed since last time (1–2 bullets)
- What’s next this week (1–2 bullets)
- Risks/Blockers
- Help needed (if any)

Example skeleton:
- Since last week: X completed; Y is 80% done.
- This week: Finish Y; start Z.
- Risks: A may slip because of B.
- Help: Need C’s decision by Thursday.

Time yourself once; it’ll likely land under a minute. If you want, I can draft a sample based on your actual work.

### Human: This helps. But I still get nervous and blank out. Any quick fixes?
### Assistant: Yes—use a 20-second reset routine right before you speak:
- Do one “physiological sigh” (long inhale, short top-up inhale, long exhale).
- Look at your 3 bullets, not faces.
- Start with a pre-committed opener: “I’ll keep this to under a minute.”

During the update:
- Keep a pen in hand; if your mind blanks, glance at bullets and say, “Picking up from risks…” This buys you a second to reset.
- If your voice shakes, slow your first sentence by 20%; pace often fixes tone.

If nerves feel chronic or affect daily life, coaching or CBT can help. Otherwise, two weeks of this routine usually makes a big difference.

### Human: People sometimes talk over me. How do I handle that without being rude?
### Assistant: Try these respectful but firm moves:
- Pre-claim your time: “I’ll do a one-minute update, then happy to take questions.”
- If interrupted: “I’d like to finish this point, then I’ll come right to you.” (Name them if needed: “Then to you, Priya.”)
- “Stack hands”: “I’ve got Alex, then Priya. Finishing this point first.”
- If it keeps happening: “Let’s park questions for 30 seconds so I can land the risks and ask.”

If the group culture is chaotic, ask the facilitator in advance to timebox and protect each speaker. As a backup, send your bullets beforehand so your key points are documented even if airtime gets messy.

### Human: I also struggle with emails. Mine are too long. Can you give me a structure?
### Assistant: Use BLUF: Bottom Line Up Front.
- Subject: Outcome + topic (e.g., “Decision needed by Wed: Empty state for onboarding”)
- First line: The ask or summary in one sentence.
- Then 3 bullets: context, options/impact, deadline.
- One clear next step with owner.

Example:
Subject: Decision needed by Wed: Empty state for onboarding
First line: Requesting Design’s sign-off on the empty state by Wednesday to keep analytics on track.
- Context: Onboarding tests are complete; analytics starts Thursday.
- Options: A) Minimal copy, ships faster; B) Richer illustration, 1–2 days extra.
- Impact: A keeps Thursday date; B delays analytics by up to 2 days.
Please reply with A or B by 3 pm Wed; otherwise we’ll proceed with A.

### Human: This is great. How do I practice without taking a ton of time?
### Assistant: Minimal-effort practice loop (10 minutes, twice a week):
- Prep: Write 3 bullets for an upcoming update (2 minutes).
- Record: Deliver them once into your phone (1 minute).
- Review: Listen at 1.5x, note one thing to keep and one to change (3 minutes).
- Repeat: Record once more (1 minute).
- Plan: Adjust template or add one better phrase for next time (3 minutes).

If recording feels awkward, alternative: practice out loud while walking, then jot your two notes in your phone.
