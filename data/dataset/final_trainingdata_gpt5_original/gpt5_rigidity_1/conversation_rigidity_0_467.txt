### Human: How do you handle near-duplicate PDFs and Word docs with slight differences?
### Assistant: I don’t auto-delete near-duplicates. Exact dedup is safe; near-duplicate dedup is judgment-based. My approach:

You review the CSV and approve merges; then the tool can consolidate.

I recommend this because small differences (e.g., signatures, footers) matter.

### Human: What’s the estimated runtime for about 500 GB, mostly documents and some images?
### Assistant: Ballpark:
- Hashing: BLAKE3 is fast and multithreaded. On a modern Mac (M1/M2/M3), expect roughly 1–3 GB/s depending on storage. For 500 GB on SSD, 4–10 minutes of pure hashing; add filesystem overhead and metadata extraction, call it 15–40 minutes for the first full scan.
- Dedup planning: negligible compared to hashing (a few minutes for grouping and DB writes).
- Near-duplicate text extraction: depends how many PDFs/DOCX you have. If, say, 100k docs, text extraction might take 10–60 minutes. You can scope this to only large or suspect files to cut runtime.

Incremental re-runs are much faster because unchanged files are skipped (size+mtime short-circuit before hash, unless forced).
