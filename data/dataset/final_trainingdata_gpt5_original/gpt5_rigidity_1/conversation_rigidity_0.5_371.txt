### Human: Help me negotiate a promotion at work
### Assistant: Absolutely—happy to help. To tailor this, can you share:
- Your current role, how long you’ve been in it, and the promotion you’re targeting
- 2–3 concrete wins or metrics from the past year
- Timing (e.g., upcoming performance cycle)
- Your manager’s style and how promotions typically work at your company

If you’re not sure on some of this, we can still build a plan and script.

### Human: I’m a Product Manager, 2.5 years in role. I want Senior PM and a raise. I led a feature that grew conversion by ~8%, reduced churn by 1 point, and I mentor two junior PMs. Reviews are in six weeks. My manager is supportive but often “goes by the process.”
### Assistant: Great foundation. Here’s a plan:
- Confirm criteria: Ask your manager what “Senior PM” looks like at your company and how decisions get made.
- Evidence: Translate your wins to business impact (e.g., revenue from +8% conversion; ARR retention from -1 pt churn).
- Ask: Request Senior PM and a raise aligned with internal bands.
- Timing: With six weeks to reviews, lock the narrative now and secure your manager’s sponsorship.

Quick question to calibrate: Do you know the salary band for Senior PMs and whether promotions are only decided at review time? If promotions are strictly cycle-bound, we can still aim for the title in-cycle and negotiate comp or scope commitments as alternatives.

### Human: I don’t know the band. Promotions usually happen at the cycle. Can you write what I should say in the meeting?
### Assistant: Here’s a concise script for your 1:1:

Opener
- “I’d like to discuss moving to Senior PM this cycle. I’ve been operating at that level and want your guidance and support to make it happen.”

Value and evidence
- “This year, I led Feature X, increasing conversion by ~8%, which added approximately $Y in monthly revenue. I partnered with CX to reduce churn by 1 point, protecting roughly $Z ARR. I also mentored two PMs and drove cross-team alignment with Eng/Design.”

Alignment and ask
- “Based on the senior rubric—ownership, strategic impact, cross-functional leadership—I believe I’m meeting or exceeding expectations. I’d like to be considered for Senior PM this cycle and have compensation aligned with that level. What would you need from me to confidently sponsor this?”

Pause and listen.

If pushback: “Let’s say they need more evidence”
- “What specific outcomes or examples would secure a yes? Can we agree on a written plan with milestones before the calibration meeting?”

If process constraints
- “If title this cycle isn’t feasible, could we commit to: a) compensation adjustment aligned to senior scope now or b) a dated promotion plan with decision criteria and a mid-cycle reevaluation?”

Follow-up
- “I’ll send a brief summary of this discussion and the evidence so you have a package for calibration.”

Optional: If you can find the band, anchor with a range tied to internal equity rather than a hard number. If you can’t, ask your manager what the band is so your ask fits the process.

### Human: My manager avoids conflict. Should I just email this instead of meeting?
### Assistant: A live conversation is usually more effective, but you can make it easier for them by:
- Sending a short pre-read email with your evidence and desired outcome
- Then asking for a 20–30 minute meeting to discuss

Template pre-read:
- Subject: Senior PM case pre-read for this cycle
- Body: “Ahead of reviews, I’m seeking promotion to Senior PM. Highlights: +8% conversion (~$Y monthly revenue), -1 pt churn (~$Z ARR preserved), mentoring two PMs, and leading cross-functional delivery. This aligns with our senior rubric in ownership, scope, and impact. Could we schedule 25 minutes to discuss your feedback and sponsorship? I’ve attached a one-pager with details.”

This lets your manager absorb the case and reduces on-the-spot tension.

### Human: What if they say the budget is frozen?
### Assistant: If budget’s frozen, keep the door open with alternatives:
- Title now, comp catch-up next cycle
- Written promotion plan with a date and explicit criteria
- One-time bonus or spot award
- Equity refresh or additional RSUs
- Expanded scope (lead a strategic initiative) with a documented path to promotion
- Professional development: conference, course, leadership coaching
- Flexible perks: remote days, relocation support, tools budget

Phrase it as:
- “If comp can’t move this cycle, can we proceed with the title now and schedule a comp alignment in Q2? If not, could we agree on a promotion plan with milestones and a mid-cycle review?”

### Human: I’m thinking of telling them I have offers to force it. Good idea?
### Assistant: Only if it’s true and you’re prepared to accept one. Ultimatums can damage trust and sometimes backfire in calibration. A more balanced approach:
- If you have offers: “I’ve been approached with opportunities in the $A–$B range. I’m excited to grow here—can we explore aligning scope, title, and compensation so I can commit to that path?”
- If you don’t: avoid bluffing. Instead, emphasize market data and your increased scope.

### Human: Can you help me quantify my impact better?
### Assistant: Yes. Try this structure:
- Baseline vs. after: “Conversion from 12% to 20% (+8 pts).”
- Translate to dollars: “That’s +X checkouts/month; average order value $M; ~+$Y/month or ~$Y*12 annually.”
- Churn: “1 pt churn reduction on N customers with ARPU $R → preserves ~$Z ARR.”
- Efficiency: “Cut cycle time from 10 to 7 days, freeing ~K engineer-days/quarter.”
- Social proof: pull 2–3 quotes from Eng/Design leaders on your leadership.

If exact numbers are sensitive, use ranges and methodology. Include assumptions in your one-pager.

### Human: The promo committee meets in three months. I want it sooner.
### Assistant: If off-cycle promotions are rare, pushing for an exception could distract from the winnable path. A balanced approach:
- Ask for a documented commitment now for the upcoming committee
- Request interim scope that clearly maps to senior expectations
- If exceptions are possible, ask what bar they require and who must sponsor it
- If not, negotiate a “decision-in-advance” email: “If Alice delivers X and Y by date Z, I will sponsor Senior PM at the next committee.”

You can also ask for a comp adjustment or bonus now, with title at the committee.

### Human: Any final checklist before I meet them?
### Assistant: Quick checklist:
- One-pager: outcomes, dollar impact, quotes, senior rubric mapping
- Pre-read email sent 24–48 hours before
- Clear ask: Senior PM this cycle; comp aligned or a written plan
- Alternatives ready: title-now/comp-later, bonus, scope, development budget
- Milestones: specific project, metrics, and review date
- Practice: two-minute opening, handling “budget freeze” and “needs broader scope”
- Follow-up: send meeting notes, agreed metrics, and next steps within 2 hours
