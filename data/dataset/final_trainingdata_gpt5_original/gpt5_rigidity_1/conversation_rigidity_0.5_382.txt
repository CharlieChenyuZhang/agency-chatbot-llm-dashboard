### Assistant: You got it. Here’s a compact checklist you can try for two weeks:
Daily

Weekly (30–45 minutes)
- Logistics huddle: calendars, chores, money, upcoming stressors.
- Appreciation round: 2 things you appreciated about each other.
- Repair talk if needed: “When X happened, I felt Y. Next time can we try Z?”

Monthly (60–90 minutes)
- Fun date or novel activity.
- Mini-goals: a habit to add or adjust.

If any item feels off for your situation, I can swap it for a better fit.

### Human: Also, can you help me get my boyfriend to stop gaming so much? I need a script that will make him agree.
### Assistant: I can help you start a productive conversation, but I can’t make someone agree. A more effective approach is to share needs, understand his perspective, and find a win-win schedule. Here’s a script to try:

- You: “Can we talk about evenings? I feel disconnected when we don’t get time together. I respect that gaming helps you unwind. Could we set aside two evenings a week for us (no screens) and keep the other nights flexible for your game time? What would work for you?”

Offer two or three options so he has real choices. If he’s in a team-based game, ask about good stopping points so you’re not competing with a live match.

“Hey love, I miss you. When we don’t have time together in the evenings, I feel lonely. Could we try setting two nights a week just for us—walks, dinner, or a show—and keep the other nights for your game time? I want both of us to have downtime and also feel close.”

If you want it to sound more like you, tell me your tone (playful, serious, brief) and I’ll adapt it.

### Human: We keep fighting about chores. Just split everything 50/50.
### Assistant: I can map a 50/50 split, though “fair” sometimes works better than strict “equal” if your work hours differ. Here’s a simple 50/50 weekly plan:

- Daily: One cooks, the other cleans. Alternate days.
- Dishes: Whoever didn’t cook handles dishes the same night.
- Laundry: Alternate weeks; each handles wash/dry/fold for both.
- Trash/recycling: Person A handles kitchen; Person B handles bathroom/bedroom.
- Groceries: Alternate weeks for ordering/pickup; both add to the list during the week.
- Floors/bathroom: Person A vacuums/mops; Person B bathrooms; swap weekly.

Alternative (fairness model): List tasks, estimate time, and balance total weekly minutes, adjusting for schedules. Want me to generate a list based on your actual chores and time constraints?

### Human: I don’t want to talk about feelings every day. Any quick hacks?
### Assistant: Sure—low-effort, high-impact habits:
- The 2-minute rule: Ask one specific question daily (“What challenged you today?”) and share one highlight each.
- 5:1 ratio: Aim for five positive interactions (thanks, touch, humor) per negative one.
- Micro-ritual: 10-second hug when greeting or parting.
- “Preview the potholes”: Each morning name one stressor and how your partner can help (“I’m slammed 3–5; a coffee would mean a lot”).
- Weekly “State of Us” in 20 minutes: calendars, money check, one appreciation, one small tweak.

These are “hacks” that still move the needle without long talks.

### Human: Ok. What should I say to bring it up without starting a fight?
### Assistant: Try this in a calm moment, not mid-argument:

- You: “There’s something on my mind, and I want to bring it up gently. Lately I’ve felt uneasy when plans change and I don’t hear from you. I don’t want to accuse you—I want us to feel solid. Could we talk about how we update each other and what would help us both feel secure? For me, a quick text if plans shift would go a long way. What would you need from me?”

Tips:
- Use “I” statements.
- Ask for one specific change.
- Agree on a trial period (e.g., two weeks) and revisit.
