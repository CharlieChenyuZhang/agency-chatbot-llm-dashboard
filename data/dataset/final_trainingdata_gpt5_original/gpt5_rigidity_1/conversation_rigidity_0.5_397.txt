### Human: How do I handle an underperformer? Can you write a message I can send on Slack to fix it?
### Assistant: I can draft a Slack message, though performance issues are usually better addressed in a short live 1:1 for tone and clarity. How about:
- A Slack message to set up the conversation
- A concise 1:1 structure you can use (15–20 minutes)

Here’s the Slack setup:
“Hey [Name], I want to sync on how we’re tracking on [project/goal]. Could we do a 15-minute chat today or tomorrow? I’ll share a few observations and hear your perspective so we can align on next steps.”

1:1 structure (15–20 min):
- Start with intent: “Goal is to help you succeed and deliver on [X].”
- Specifics: “I observed [2–3 concrete examples] vs. the expectation [clear standard].”
- Ask: “How do you see it? What’s getting in the way?”
- Align: Agree on 1–2 measurable outcomes, owners, support, and a date.
- Close: Summarize in writing after.

If you must stay async, I can give a structured doc approach.

### Human: Can you give me an exact script for the 1:1 that guarantees they won't get defensive?
### Assistant: I can give a script that reduces defensiveness, but there’s no guarantee—responses vary. Use neutral language, specifics, and shared problem-solving.

Script:
- Opener: “Thanks for meeting. My intent is to support you and ensure we hit [team goal].”
- Observation: “In the last two sprints, [task A] slipped past the committed date, and [PRs] had [X] rework. Our expectation is [standard].”
- Impact: “This delayed [milestone] and added load on [teammates].”
- Invite: “How are you seeing it? What factors contributed?”
- Reflect + align: “I’m hearing [summary]. Here’s what good looks like by [date]: [specific deliverable/quality bar].”
- Support: “What would help? Options: pair programming, clearer scope, smaller increments, or weekly checkpoints.”
- Commit: “Let’s agree: by [date], you’ll deliver [X], with [checkpoints]. I’ll [support action].”
- Close: “I’ll send a short summary. Anything I missed?”

Follow-up note (same day):
“Summary of our chat: [expectation], [deliverables + dates], [checkpoints], [support]. Let’s review on [date].”

### Human: I don't have time for meetings. Can we do this asynchronously?
### Assistant: In a pinch, yes. It’s not ideal for sensitive topics, but here’s a structured async approach:
- Send a short context doc with:
  - Expectation: “For [role], good looks like [X by Y date, quality bar].”
  - Observations: 2–3 concrete examples with dates.
  - Impact: Specific effect on goals/team.
  - Questions: “What’s your view? What’s the root cause? What support helps?”
  - Proposal: “I propose [1–2 measurable commitments + dates] and [checkpoints].”
- Timeline: Ask for a response within 24–48 hours; confirm the plan in writing.

Template intro message:
“Sharing context on [topic] so we can align async. Could you review and respond by [time] with your perspective and any risks? Then we’ll confirm a plan with dates.”

### Human: Give me a 30-60-90 plan to be more decisive and improve accountability.
### Assistant: 30 days:
- Decisions: Start a decision log (date, owner, criteria, outcome). Make and communicate 1–2 decisions daily.
- Cadence: Introduce weekly commitments review (owners, deliverables, dates).
- Expectations: Write and share “What good looks like” for 2 key roles or projects.
- Feedback: Do 3 short feedback reps using the script.

60 days:
- Systematize: Create a standing decision forum (30 min/week) with clear inputs and timeboxing.
- Visibility: Public team board for commitments; track met vs slipped; add risk tags.
- Skills: Run one mini-retro on decision quality and one on accountability gaps.
- Coaching: 1–2 targeted growth plans for team members who need it.

90 days:
- Delegate: Define decision rights (RACI/DACI) for top workflows; reduce your bottlenecks.
- Metrics: Track time-to-decision, commitment hit rate, cycle time, and rework.
- Culture: Celebrate on-time delivery and learning from fast, reversible mistakes.
- Review: Evaluate what to keep/change; reset goals for next quarter.

Light metrics targets:
- Time-to-decision: <48 hours for reversible, <7 days for one-way doors.
- Commitment hit rate: >85% on-team commitments per sprint.

### Human: How do I measure if I’m more decisive and accountability is better?
### Assistant: Measures:
- Decisiveness:
  - Time-to-decision (track in your log).
  - % decisions with explicit criteria and decision-maker identified.
  - Reversal rate for reversible vs irreversible decisions.

- Accountability:
  - Commitment hit rate (met on time / total).
  - Forecast accuracy (planned vs delivered each sprint).
  - Follow-through: % action items closed by agreed dates.
  - Qualitative: Quarterly pulse survey on “We honor commitments” and “Decisions are clear.”

Signals you’re improving:
- Fewer “who owns this?” questions.
- Risks surfaced earlier.
- Fewer urgent escalations caused by indecision.
