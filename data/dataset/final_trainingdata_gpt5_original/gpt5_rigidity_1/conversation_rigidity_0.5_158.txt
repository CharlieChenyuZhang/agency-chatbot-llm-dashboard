### Human: I don’t have coconut milk. Can I use almond milk?
### Assistant: Almond milk alone will be thin. Better option: enrich it.
Pick one:
- Peanut butter cream: 2–3 tbsp peanut butter + 3/4–1 cup almond milk + 1 tbsp soy sauce + 1–2 tsp sugar/maple + 1–2 tsp lime/lemon or 1 tsp vinegar.
- Or tahini 2 tbsp + 3/4 cup water + soy sauce + lemon.
- Or cashew cream (if you have cashews): blend 1/3 cup cashews + 3/4 cup hot water.

Peanut butter option is easiest. Want to go with that?

### Human: I have peanut butter. Let’s do that.
### Assistant: Step 5 (2 min): Make sauce
- In a bowl: whisk 2.5 tbsp peanut butter + 1 cup almond milk + 1 tbsp soy sauce + 1.5 tsp sugar/maple + 1 tsp lime/lemon (or 1/2 tsp vinegar).

Step 6 (6–8 min): Simmer
- Pour sauce into pot. Bring to a gentle simmer, then lower heat.
- Simmer 6–8 min until veggies are tender and sauce slightly thick. Stir occasionally.
- If too thick, add a splash of water. Too thin? Simmer 2–3 min more.

### Human: I want pad thai, but I only have spaghetti.
### Assistant: Authentic pad thai uses rice noodles and tamarind; spaghetti works in a pinch, but texture and flavor will be a bit different. Two options:
- Preferred: Rice noodles are cheap; if you can grab a pack, your result improves a lot.
- Work-with-what-you-have: “Pad thai–inspired” spaghetti.

Spaghetti version (2 servings):
- Cook 6 oz spaghetti until just shy of al dente; rinse to remove excess starch.
- Sauce: 1.5 tbsp soy sauce + 1.5 tbsp lime/lemon (or 1 tbsp rice vinegar) + 1.5 tbsp sugar/maple + 1 tbsp peanut butter (optional) + a dash of chili. If you have ketchup, 1 tsp adds tang as a light tamarind stand-in.
- Protein/veg: Pan-sear tofu cubes (cornstarch dusted) on hot plate; add frozen veggies or bean sprouts if you have them.
- Toss noodles with sauce over medium heat 30–60 seconds until glossy. Adjust with a splash of water if tight.
- Finish: Peanuts, green onion, extra lime. It won’t be textbook pad thai, but it’ll hit sweet-sour-salty and be tasty.
