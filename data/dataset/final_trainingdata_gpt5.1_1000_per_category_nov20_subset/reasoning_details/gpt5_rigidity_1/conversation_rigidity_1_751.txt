Summary: **Analyzing the French Revolution**

France's estate system heavily taxed the Third Estate while nobles and clergy enjoyed privileges. Costly wars, like support for the American Revolution, and royal overspending caused massive debt. Economic struggles, bad harvests, and rising bread prices fueled public anger. Enlightenment thinkers challenged absolute monarchy, leading to demands for reform. The revolution kicked off when the Third Estate established the National Assembly and took the Tennis Court Oath. The storming of the Bastille marked the end of royal power, but radicalization and internal conflict followed, resulting in the Reign of Terror. The effects were significant and enduring.**Summarizing the revolution's impact**

The revolution ended absolute monarchy in France, diminished the power of aristocrats and clergy, and increased secularism and legal equality. It spurred nationalism and spread revolutionary ideas throughout Europe, particularly under Napoleon, who rose amidst the chaos to create his empire. The revolution highlighted tensions between liberty and security, along with popular sovereignty and violence, reform and radicalism. These themes continue to influence modern discussions around democracy and human rights.**Examining causes of the revolution**

Costly wars, including support for the American Revolution, along with royal overspending, led to massive debt in France. Economic hardship, bad harvests, and rising bread prices further fueled public anger. Enlightenment thinkers began questioning absolute monarchy while advocating for liberty, equality, and popular sovereignty. Their ideas inspired demands for political reform. The revolution officially started when the Third Estate created the National Assembly and took the Tennis Court Oath, asserting their authority to draft a new constitution.**Understanding key events of the revolution**

The storming of the Bastille marked the collapse of royal power and the end of feudal privileges. Following this, the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen established universal rights. However, internal divisions, foreign wars, and fears of counterrevolution led to radicalization, including the execution of Louis XVI and the Reign of Terror under Robespierre. The consequences of these events were significant and continue to resonate throughout history.
