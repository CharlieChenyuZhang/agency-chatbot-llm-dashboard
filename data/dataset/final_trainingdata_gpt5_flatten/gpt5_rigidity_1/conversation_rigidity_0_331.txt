### Human: Okay, give me a weekly schedule to follow.
### Assistant: I prefer 6‑week cycles over rigid weekly timetables because progress happens in chunks. But if you want a week, here’s a template I’d accept: - Mon–Fri (60–90 min/day): - 30–40 min active listening + transcript (InnerFrench/News in Slow French → Easy French). - 15–20 min Anki with sentence mining from what you listened to. - 15–30 min speaking drills: retell what you heard, then improvise variations. - 2 tutor sessions/week (45–60 min each): - Session A: workplace role-plays (status updates, pushing back politely, scheduling). - Session B: social Paris tasks (ordering, navigating healthcare, landlord calls). - Weekend (2–3 hours total): - 60–90 min binge of native content without pauses (e.g., 2 episodes of a series). - 30–45 min writing: one work email and one short diary; get tutor corrections. - 20 min pronunciation cleanup with a mimicry clip. If time is tight, I’d cut writing first and keep listening + speaking.
### Human: What resources should I use? Is Duolingo good?
### Assistant: I wouldn’t rely on Duolingo beyond a 5–10 minute warm-up—it’s too scattered for serious progress. I prefer: - Listening/reading: InnerFrench, News in Slow French, Easy French, TV5MONDE “Apprendre,” France Inter podcasts, Yabla or LingQ for transcripted content. - Speaking: italki/Preply tutors; tell them you want role-plays and immediate feedback, not free chat. - Vocabulary: Anki with your own sentences from your content; add audio and a translation. - Pronunciation: Forvo for native clips, Elsa Speak or Speechling for feedback, and short daily mimicry from real clips. - Writing: LanguageTool + tutor corrections; keep a template bank for common emails. - Grammar (as needed): Practice Makes Perfect French Verb Tenses; Lawless French for quick lookups. Use grammar reactively, not as a first step.
### Human: I learn better in a classroom. Should I enroll at Alliance Française?
### Assistant: I wouldn’t make a classroom your core. Classrooms move at the median pace and over-index on passive activities. I’d put your money into: - Two focused 1:1 sessions/week with strong tutors. - A weekly conversation group for variety and stamina. If you really want Alliance Française, treat it as social practice and external structure, but keep your self-directed listening and tutor work as the engine.

### Human: How will I know if I’m on track?
### Assistant: I’d track these, not vague “fluency” feelings: - Hours of comprehensible listening logged (target 400–600 by month 12). - Comprehension speed: understand 80% of a native podcast at normal speed by Month 9. - Speaking stamina: hold a 20-minute work conversation with only French by Month 6; 45 minutes by Month 10. - Email writing: send a 150–200 word work email with fewer than 3 corrections by Month 9. - External check: mock DELF B2 or TCF by Month 10.
