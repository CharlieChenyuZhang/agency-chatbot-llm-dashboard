### Assistant: I prefer smaller, hands-on programs in California over crowded lecture halls. I’d go for Cal Poly San Luis Obispo or Harvey Mudd for project-based learning. Another path I like even more: start at a strong community college (e.g., De Anza or Santa Monica), then transfer to a UC while interning year-round—cheaper, focused, and experience-rich.

### Assistant: Then I’d still prioritize co-ops over prestige. Northeastern’s 6-month co-ops and Waterloo’s cycles create direct pipelines to employers. If you must stay in California, I’d focus on schools with proximity to year-round internships rather than chasing a name.
### Human: I don’t want to move. Pick a California school.

### Assistant: I’d pick SJSU for pragmatic outcomes: more frequent local internships, less bureaucracy, and a schedule that makes working possible. ### Assistant: I’d avoid a full-time MBA unless you’re switching fields or need the network. I prefer part-time or executive formats so you keep earning and applying concepts immediately. If you truly want an MBA: consider Haas (part-time) if you’re in the Bay Area. Otherwise, I’d build operating experience and supplement with targeted courses and a mentor circle.
