### Human: Between the Corolla Cross Hybrid and Subaru Crosstrek, which would you pick?

### Human: I’m nervous about buying Kia or Hyundai because of theft issues and reliability. Is that still a problem?
### Assistant: The high-profile theft issues affected many 2011–2021 non‑immobilizer models. Newer Hyundais/Kias (especially push‑button start/hybrids) have immobilizers, and the brands rolled out software updates and hardware deterrents for older cars. For 2022+ models, the theft risk is much lower. Reliability on their newer hybrids has been solid, and their warranties are strong. That said: - Insurance rates in some areas still price in the older-risk reputation. - If you want to avoid that concern entirely, Toyota/Subaru are safe bets. - If curious, get VIN‑specific insurance quotes before deciding.
### Human: Okay, let’s narrow it down to Corolla Cross Hybrid and Crosstrek Premium with heated seats. Which should I test drive first, and what should I look for?
### Assistant: Test drive whichever is easier to schedule first, but try to do them back-to-back. What to look for: - Seating and comfort: Seat support on a 30+ minute drive; visibility; driving position. - Highway behavior: Noise at 65–75 mph, lane-centering smoothness, adaptive cruise spacing and braking feel, passing power. - Ride and handling: How each absorbs potholes and expansion joints; steering confidence on ramps. - Real-world acceleration: Merging from short on-ramps and 40–70 mph passing. - Practicality: Cargo opening height/width, rear-seat space, child-seat fit, spare tire type. - Tech usability: Screen glare, physical knobs vs. touch, wireless CarPlay/Android Auto stability. - Winter readiness: Ask about all-weather vs. true winter tires; check ground clearance and approach angles if trailheads have ruts. Bring your usual cargo or a stroller to test fit. If possible, drive the Crosstrek on a rougher road and the Corolla Cross Hybrid on a highway loop to highlight their differences.
