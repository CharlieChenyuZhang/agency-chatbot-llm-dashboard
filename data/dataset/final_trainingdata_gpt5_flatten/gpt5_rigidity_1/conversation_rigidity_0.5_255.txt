### Human: I really hate flashcards and grammar drills. Can we avoid those?
### Assistant: Yes. We can hit B2 without traditional flashcards or heavy drills, but we’ll still need to notice and recycle language. Alternatives: - Sentence mining without cards: Highlight useful sentences in articles or transcripts; re-read them the next day and at week’s end. - Story-first grammar: Learn grammar through short stories and podcasts; note one pattern per day (e.g., passé composé vs. imparfait) and imitate it in a sentence you write. - Shadowing + mimicry: Pick 1–2 minute clips, repeat line-by-line until it “feels” right—this builds grammar implicitly. - Micro-writing: 5–8 sentences on a prompt (e.g., summarize a podcast) and then self-correct by comparing to model sentences. - Pattern notebooks: Keep a running list of chunks like “Je viens de + infinitif,” “Ça te va si…?” and use them in your next voice diary. This keeps the advantages of repetition without formal drilling.

I recommend the gradual plan above to protect your goal.

### Human: How will I measure progress over the year?
### Assistant: Simple, objective checkpoints: - Monthly: - Listening: Re-listen to the same 3-minute clip from earlier months; aim for higher comprehension and smoother shadowing. - Speaking: Record a 2-minute monologue on the same topic each month; track words per minute and filler reliance. - Writing: A 120–150 word email; check for past tense accuracy and clarity. - Quarterly: - Take an online level test (Ev@lang or similar). - Do a 15-minute conversation with a partner and self-rate using the CEFR can-do statements. - Target exams: - Month 6: Try an online B1 mock. - Month 9: B1+/B2- mock. - Month 12: DELF B2 mock (or schedule the real exam if that motivates you). Operational metrics: - Input hours tracked per week (aim 4–5). - Speaking minutes per week (start 30–45; grow to 90+). - New “chunks” used in your own speech (add 5/week).
