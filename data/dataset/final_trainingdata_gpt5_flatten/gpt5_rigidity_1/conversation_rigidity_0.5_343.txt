### Human: What exact settings should I use in OBS to get started?
### Assistant: Try these starting points and tweak by ear (watch the meter while speaking at your loudest): Filters in this order: 1) Noise Suppression: RNNoise (Quality). 2) High-Pass Filter: 80 Hz. 3) Expander: Ratio 2:1, Threshold close -50 dB, open -44 dB, Attack 10 ms, Release 120 ms. 4) Compressor: Ratio 3:1, Threshold -18 dB, Attack 10 ms, Release 100 ms, Output gain +3 dB. 5) De-esser: Frequency 6.5 kHz, Range 4 dB. 6) Limiter: Ceiling -1 dB, Release 50 ms. Leveling: - Speak at your normal loudness and set mic input gain so OBS peaks around -12 dB. - After adding the compressor and makeup gain, peaks should sit around -6 to -3 dB, with the limiter catching only rare spikes.
### Human: My keyboard is loud. Will this still pick it up?
### Assistant: Some, yes—mechanical keyboards are tough. You can reduce it a lot: - Mic technique: Place the mic between you and the keyboard, stay close, and angle the mic’s null away from the keys. - Desk/keyboard tweaks: Thick desk mat, O-rings or soft keycaps, swap to quieter switches if possible. - Filters: Increase expander threshold slightly (e.g., close -48 dB, open -42 dB) and shorten attack (5 ms). Don’t overdo it or you’ll clip syllables. - Software: RNNoise on “Quality,” plus use push-to-mute for coughing/typing bursts. - Behavior: Type lighter during talking moments. If this remains a problem, switching to a dynamic like the MV7 or PodMic USB will make the biggest difference.
### Human: Sometimes I record acoustic guitar. Will this setup work?

The Yeti is actually decent for acoustic guitar if you tame the room. - Pattern: Cardioid. - Placement: Point at the 12th fret from 12–18 inches away; avoid pointing straight at the soundhole. - Room: Record in the quietest spot; hang blankets or use a duvet behind and to the sides if you can. - Levels: Peaks around -12 dB. - Filters (for music, gentler): - High-pass: 60–80 Hz. - No gate/expander (they can chatter on sustained notes). - Compressor: Light, 2:1, Threshold -22 dB, Attack 20 ms, Release 120 ms, Makeup 1–2 dB. - Optional de-esser if string squeak is harsh (5–6 kHz, very light). - No RNNoise if you can avoid it; if needed, use minimally. - Consider recording in a DAW at 48 kHz/24-bit for better takes and light post-processing.

### Human: That sounds good. How do I use both USB mics on Windows?
### Assistant: Using two USB mics simultaneously on Windows as separate tracks is awkward. Options: - Easiest: Use one mic at a time. For streaming/calls, select the MV7 as your input. For guitar recordings, switch the input device to the Yeti in your DAW/OBS. - If you must record both at once on separate channels: Use software like VoiceMeeter Banana/Potato to create a virtual mix with both mics, or ASIO4ALL to aggregate—but it adds complexity and potential sync drift. - Long-term clean solution: An XLR interface with two inputs and two XLR mics. That’s beyond your current USB preference and budget, so I’d stick with one-at-a-time selection for now. Checklist to switch: - In Windows Sound > Input, set the active mic as Default (and in OBS/DAW, explicitly pick that mic). - Match both devices to 48 kHz/24-bit to avoid resampling. - Keep per-mic filter profiles saved in OBS and toggle the appropriate one.
