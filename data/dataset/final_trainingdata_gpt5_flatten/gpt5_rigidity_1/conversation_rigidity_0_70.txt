### Human: Configure a password manager and migrate accounts
### Assistant: I can help, but I wouldn’t start by migrating everything into a password manager. The better move is to reduce what you store and lean into passkeys where possible—they’re phishing-resistant and remove password reuse risks entirely. Then use a password manager only for what still needs a password. Here’s the approach I recommend: - Do a quick account audit: list your top 20 critical accounts (email, banking, Apple/Google, cloud storage, work). - Turn on passkeys for services that support them, and prune or delete old accounts you no longer need. - For the remaining passwords, use a manager that supports hardware security keys, passkeys, and family sharing. I prefer Bitwarden because it’s open source, portable, and cost-effective. If you still want to migrate everything as-is, say which manager you plan to use and what devices you’re on. I’ll tailor the steps, but I’d still push the audit-first approach.

### Human: Fine. What are the exact steps to export from LastPass and import into 1Password?
### Assistant: Exact steps: Export from LastPass (desktop browser): - Log into your LastPass vault. - Click your account icon or Vault menu > Advanced Options (or More Options) > Export. - Choose “LastPass CSV” (not generic CSV if both are shown). - Enter your master password. Save the CSV to a secure local folder. Disconnect from the internet if you can, and delete the file right after import. Import into 1Password (web is easiest): - Sign in at 1Password.com > your account > Import. - Select LastPass as the source. - Upload the CSV. 1Password auto-maps most fields. Ensure columns for: - title/name - username - password - URL - notes - TOTP/one-time password (if present) - Start import. When done, spot-check: - A bank entry - Email account - A site with TOTP - A secure note After verification: - Securely delete the CSV (recycle bin + secure erase if possible). - Log out and back in to 1Password on all devices to sync. I’d still avoid co-locating TOTP with passwords for high-value accounts; I prefer a separate authenticator or hardware key.
### Human: Should I store TOTP codes in 1Password too?
### Assistant: I don’t for critical accounts. My preference: - High-value (email, bank, cloud): use a hardware key (FIDO2) or a separate authenticator app so a single compromise doesn’t grant both password and TOTP. - Lower-risk or convenience-focused accounts: storing TOTP in 1Password is fine. If you want an app, I like Aegis (Android) or Raivo/Authenticator on iOS. But if convenience wins for you, 1Password’s built-in TOTP is solid—just make sure your vault is protected with a hardware key.
### Human: What about passkeys? Can 1Password handle them?
### Assistant: Yes. 1Password supports creating and storing passkeys. Still, I prefer platform authenticators first: - On iPhone: use iCloud Keychain/passkeys in iOS for Apple ecosystem logins. - On Windows: Windows Hello passkeys are convenient locally. For cross-device, 1Password passkeys work well. My pattern: - If a site supports passkeys, create one with your platform authenticator first. - Add a 1Password passkey as a backup or for non-Apple/Windows devices. - Where allowed, remove the old password entirely.
