### Assistant: Great fit for a lean start. If cottage food isn’t allowed or is restrictive, you could use a shared kitchen part-time. - Business setup: You can start as a sole proprietor with a DBA to move fast; later consider an LLC for liability protection if you scale. Sell as pre-orders to manage time. - Sales channels: Instagram + local pickup, or a simple pre-order form (Google Form/Typeform) + weekly bake days. Farmers markets are good if rules permit. If you like, I can outline a 2-week validation plan and a simple one-page business plan.

### Human: Pricing… I want to sell at $10 per dozen to attract customers. Will that work?
### Assistant: It can attract buyers, but it may squeeze margins. Quick napkin math: - Typical ingredient cost per dozen premium cookies: $2.00–$3.50 - Packaging/labels: $0.50–$1.00 - Overhead (utilities, consumables): ~$0.50 - Time: If it takes you 2–3 minutes per cookie for mixing, baking, cooling, packing across batches, your labor adds up. At $10/dozen, you might net ~$4–$6 before paying yourself, which is tight. Alternatives that keep your goal of affordability: - Offer half-dozens at $8–$10 and dozens at $16–$22; anchor with a “signature” dozen at $20. - Introduce “cookie of the week” at an intro price, regular flavors at standard price. - Bundle pricing: 3 dozen for events at a slight discount while protecting margins. Want me to estimate your exact cost per dozen if you share one recipe’s ingredient list?
### Human: I only have evenings. Could I handle 100 dozen a week?
### Assistant: That’s aggressive for evenings only. Rough capacity check: - If one batch yields 2 dozen and takes 60–75 minutes end-to-end (mix, chill if needed, bake, cool, pack), you’d need ~50 batches. - At 1.25 hours per batch, that’s ~62.5 hours/week just for production, not including shopping, messaging, or cleanup. More realistic options: - Cap weekly pre-orders (e.g., 24–36 dozen) and sell out consistently. - Bake two evenings + one weekend session; offer fixed pickup windows. - Pre-scoop and freeze dough to cut active time on bake days. - Use a shared kitchen one evening for multiple ovens, cut time by half. - Offer frozen dough balls for customers to bake at home—high margin, less time pressure.
