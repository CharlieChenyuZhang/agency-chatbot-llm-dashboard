### Human: That works. What should I look for in their replies?
### Assistant: Look for: Green flags - Clear license/insurance info; offers to send COI naming you as additional insured for the project. - Specific start windows and realistic durations (often 6–10 weeks for a mid-range kitchen). - Transparent pricing approach with allowances (cabinets, counters, tile, appliances). - References with similar scope and neighborhood, plus photos of recent jobs. - Willingness to pull permits and handle inspections. Red flags - Vague availability (“we can start anytime”) or a price that’s far below others. - Refusal to pull permits or lack of written contract with defined scope. - Large deposits (more than 20%) before materials are ordered. - No change order process or unclear exclusions (“doesn’t include electrical” without specifics). Ask them: - Fixed-price vs cost-plus: what’s included/excluded? How do they handle allowances? - Payment schedule tied to milestones (not just dates). - Change order process and markup on changes. - Who will be on-site daily and how they protect the home (dust control, cleanup). Verify: - License on WA L&I and any complaints. - Recent permits under their name. - References (and actually call them).

### Human: How do I protect myself with the contract?
### Assistant: Key points to include or verify: - Detailed scope with drawings/specs attached; allowances listed with values. - Payment schedule tied to milestones (e.g., deposit for ordering, rough-in complete, cabinets installed, substantial completion), with 5–10% retention until punch list is done. - Written change order process with pricing before work proceeds. - Start and substantial completion dates, plus how delays are handled. - Proof of insurance (general liability and workers’ comp); require COI naming you as additional insured for the project address. - Lien releases from GC and subs with each payment. - Permit responsibility and code compliance explicitly assigned. - Site protection, daily cleanup, and trash removal. - Warranty terms (often 1 year workmanship; specify materials per manufacturer). - Dispute resolution method (mediation/arbitration) and governing law. If you want a solid template, ask for a standard AIA or NAHB short-form agreement. For larger scopes, consider a quick attorney review.
