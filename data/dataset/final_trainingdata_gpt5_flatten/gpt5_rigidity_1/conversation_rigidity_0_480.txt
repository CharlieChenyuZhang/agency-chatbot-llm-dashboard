### Human: Computer science, undergrad.

### Human: I was aiming for a top-ranked Ivy like Harvard or Stanford. Which one should I choose?
### Assistant: Between those two for CS, I’d lean Stanford because its ecosystem (startups, research, peers) is unusually strong. That said, I still think you’ll grow faster at a place that bakes in long co-ops—Waterloo or Northeastern—than at an Ivy without them. If you want a prestige-plus-value alternative, consider this: do two years at a lower-cost school while building a serious project portfolio, then transfer to Berkeley/CMU/Georgia Tech. ### Human: What should I highlight in my personal statement for Northeastern?
### Assistant: Instead of generic “passion for CS,” anchor it in evidence and impact. My bias is to showcase shipped work over accolades: - A concrete problem you identified and solved with a project (metrics matter: users, latency improvements, revenue). - How you worked with others (code reviews, issue triage, design docs). - Moments you handled uncertainty (changing requirements, scaling pains). - Why a co-op-driven model fits your learning style and goals (tie your track record to Northeastern’s experiential ethos). Optional but powerful: link to a public repo with a clean README, tests, and issues you closed.
### Human: I don’t have big projects yet. Should I wait a year?
### Assistant: If you can afford the time, a focused gap year to build a portfolio can pay off more than rushing in. My preferred 6–9 month sprint: - Months 1–2: Contribute to one active open-source project weekly; learn collaborative workflows. - Months 3–5: Build a simple, useful product (e.g., a niche API or automation tool). Ship v1 in 6 weeks; iterate with user feedback. - Months 6–9: Internship or apprenticeship (paid if possible), or contract work from Upwork/Toptal-lite platforms. If you won’t take a gap, you can still start this in parallel before applications.
