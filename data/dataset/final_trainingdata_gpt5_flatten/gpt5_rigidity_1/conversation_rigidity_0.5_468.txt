### Human: I'm mostly writing a novel. How should I split drafting and editing in those sessions?
### Assistant: For novels, separating drafting from editing helps momentum. Two workable approaches: - Split by day: - Mon–Fri: drafting new words - Sat: structural/scene-level revision - Sun: plan the next week (outline beats/scenes) - Split within the session (if you must edit daily): - 10 minutes: light cleanup of yesterday’s pages (no deep restructuring) - 90–100 minutes: new drafting - 10 minutes: jot revision notes for future sessions Rules of thumb: - Keep drafting fast and imperfect. Mark problems with “TK” and move on. - Do deep edits only on designated days to avoid derailing word count. - End each session by writing a one-line “next scene goal” and a 1–3 bullet “to fix later” list.

### Human: What about writer's block? Any quick fixes I can use in the moment?
### Assistant: Use one of these fast unblockers: - Two ugly sentences: intentionally write two bad sentences to get moving - Five-minute freewrite: set a timer and write nonstop about the scene problem - Scene question: “What does my protagonist want right now, and what’s in the way?” - Jump ahead: write the scene you’re most excited about, then bridge later - Dialogue-only: write the conversation beats first, add action/description later - Constraint game: 150 words describing only what’s visible in the room; then resume - Copy/continue: copy a paragraph from your draft, then continue from its last line - Micro-goal: 100 words in 5 minutes; repeat - Talk it out: dictate a messy summary into your phone, then convert to prose - Walk reset: 3–5 minute walk and return with a one-line scene goal Pick one, set a timer, and start. Most blocks dissolve after 3–5 minutes of forward motion.
