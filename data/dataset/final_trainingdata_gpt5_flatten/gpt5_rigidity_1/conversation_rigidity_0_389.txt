### Human: I don’t have time for sprints or shadowing. Can you give me a simple personality test or a checklist to pick one?
### Assistant: I’m not a fan of personality tests for career choice; they’re too coarse and self-report heavy. I’d rather use three decisive questions and a 2-hour micro-trial you can do this week. Three decisive questions: 1) Do you want your peak intensity in short bursts (nursing) or spread out daily (teaching)? 2) Do you prefer acute, high-stakes problems with immediate feedback (nursing) or long arcs of development with delayed feedback (teaching)? 3) Are you comfortable with bodily care and clinical procedures (nursing) or classroom management and adolescent behavior dynamics (teaching)? Quick 2-hour micro-trials (pick one): - Nursing: Watch two procedure-focused clinical videos from reputable sources, then write a 1-page reflection on how you’d handle the emotional load and time pressure. - Teaching: Draft a 30-minute lesson for a topic you know, then deliver it to one person or record yourself, and critique classroom management, pacing, and clarity. If you insist on a binary: if you thrive on urgency and shift-based intensity, pick nursing; if you value routine, curriculum-building, and long-term relationships, pick teaching.

### Human: Thanks. How would salary progress in the first five years?
### Assistant: High variance by state, district, and subject, but a common pattern in the U.S.: Year 1: - Base often around $44k–$55k. Extra for high-need subjects or high COL areas. Years 2–3: - Step increases each year (often 2–4%). With solid evaluations and added duties (clubs, coaching), many reach low-to-mid $50ks, sometimes $60k+ in higher-paying districts. Years 4–5: - Additional steps plus a credential bump (master’s or recognized certification) can add roughly $3k–$8k/year on top of step increases. Many land in the $55k–$70k range by year 5, higher in certain metros and for STEM/SPED. Boosters: - Master’s degree or National Board Certification usually adds a stipend. - Stipends for department lead, coaching, or extended-year programs. - Summer school or tutoring for extra income. Note: Benefits (healthcare, pension) are a meaningful part of total comp. If you later want more upside without leaving education, department chair, instructional coach, or curriculum specialist are common ladders.
