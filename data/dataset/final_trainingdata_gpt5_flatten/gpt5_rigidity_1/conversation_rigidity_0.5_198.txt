### Human: Is 1Password cloud safe enough? I was thinking local-only like KeePass.
### Assistant: Short answer: yes, for most people 1Password is safe enough. It’s end-to-end encrypted; your data is encrypted locally with your account password and Secret Key, and 1Password can’t read it. They’ve had strong security practices and public audits. Local-only tradeoffs (KeePass): - Pros: maximum control, no vendor cloud, open source options. - Cons: more DIY (choose apps per platform—KeePassXC on desktop, Strongbox/Keepass Touch on iOS), set up your own sync (iCloud/Dropbox), less seamless autofill/passkeys, and more manual maintenance. Middle ground: Bitwarden cloud (open source), or self-host Vaultwarden if you want control with convenience. Given your timeline and devices, 1Password is the smoothest today. If you later want to switch to local-only, you can export and move.

### Human: What about moving my 2FA from Google Authenticator into 1Password—do I have to reset every site?
### Assistant: In most cases, yes—you’ll re-enroll each site. Google Authenticator doesn’t expose the TOTP secret to copy, and its bulk export QR is proprietary (1Password can’t import it). Practical approach: - Keep Google Authenticator active until you finish. - Prioritize critical accounts: email(s), Apple ID, Google, financial, password resets. - For each site: 1) Log in and go to Security/2FA settings. 2) Choose “Authenticator app” and show the QR. 3) In 1Password, open the corresponding login item > Add one-time password > Scan QR (or paste the secret). 4) Enter the 6-digit code to confirm. 5) Only then remove the old Google Authenticator entry. - Save backup codes in 1Password (attach/file or notes). If you want an easier ongoing experience but keep 2FA separate, Authy or Aegis (Android) are good alternatives—but consolidating in 1Password is most convenient across devices.
