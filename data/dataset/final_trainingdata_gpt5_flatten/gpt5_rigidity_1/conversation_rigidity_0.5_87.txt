### Human: I want a show for freelance designers about productivity. Probably solo with some interviews. Budget is $200. I’ll record at home. What gear should I get?
### Assistant: With $200, prioritize a good dynamic mic (better in untreated rooms), basic monitoring, and stability. Here’s a reliable, budget-friendly kit: - Microphone: Samson Q2U or Audio-Technica ATR2100x-USB (about $70–$99). Both do USB now and XLR later. - Stand: InnoGear desktop boom or a simple arm ($15–$25). - Pop filter/windscreen: $8–$12. - Headphones: Monoprice Modern Retro (~$25) or Audio-Technica ATH-M20x (~$49). If you can stretch, Sony MDR-7506 (~$90), but that may push the budget. - Optional room help: 2–3 moving blankets or a clothes-filled closet. A $15 pack of command hooks and clips can transform a room. Free/cheap software: - Recording/editing: Audacity (free) or GarageBand (free on Mac). For interviews: Riverside (paid), Zencastr (free tier), or Zoom (works, but prioritize local recording if possible). - Backup: Record a local track for you and the guest when possible. If $200 is tight, you can start at ~$120 by choosing the Samson Q2U bundle, a $15 stand, $10 pop filter, and $25 headphones. Also, if you must, you can start with your phone + a $20 lav mic, but the dynamic USB mic route will sound more professional.

### Human: Give me the checklist and the Audacity macro.
### Assistant: Editing checklist (10–15 minutes for a 20–30 min episode): 1) Import tracks; delete obvious dead air at start/end. 2) Apply macro (below). 3) Manually remove loud breaths/clicks in silent gaps; leave natural breaths. 4) Fix big mistakes by cutting between breath points. Crossfade 15–30 ms if needed. 5) Loudness normalize to -16 LUFS (mono: -19). Ensure peaks below -1 dBTP. 6) Add intro/outro music if any; duck music -18 to -24 dB under voice. 7) Listen to first 2 minutes and 2 random spots for quality. 8) Export WAV (archive) and MP3 128–192 kbps (delivery). Audacity macro (starting point): - Filter Curve EQ: gentle high-pass at 80 Hz; slight dip at 200–300 Hz if boomy. - Noise Reduction: Profile + Reduce 6 dB, Sensitivity 6.0, Frequency Smoothing 3. - Compressor: Threshold -18 dB, Noise Floor -40 dB, Ratio 3:1, Attack 0.2 ms, Release 1.0 s, Make-up gain On. - Limiter: Soft limit, Input gain 0 dB, Limit to -1.0 dB. - Loudness Normalization: Perceived loudness -16 LUFS, True Peak -1.0 dB. If you want, I can tailor those settings once I know your mic and room.
### Human: How often should I release? I’m busy.
### Assistant: Sustainable > ambitious. Options: - Biweekly start: Easiest to maintain. Bank 2–3 episodes before launch. - Weekly, short format: 15–20 minutes, one focused tip per episode. - Seasonal: 8-episode season, then a 3–4 week break to prep the next. Given freelance workload, I’d start biweekly with 20–25 min episodes and record two at a time. Consistency beats frequency at launch.

### Human: What’s the cheapest podcast host that still works?
### Assistant: Solid low-cost options: - Spotify for Podcasters (free): Easy, unlimited storage, basic analytics. Simple, but fewer advanced features. - RSS.com (~$12.99/mo): Unlimited episodes, decent analytics, easy directory submission. - Libsyn (from $7/mo): Reliable OG host, solid distribution tools. - Buzzsprout (free tier limited; $12/mo plan): Very user-friendly, great learning resources. - Podbean (~$9/mo) and RedCircle (free): Competitive, but check feature limits. If cost is the top priority, start with Spotify for Podcasters or RSS.com. If you want very user-friendly onboarding and don’t mind $12/mo, Buzzsprout is excellent.
### Human: How do I get on Apple and Spotify?
### Assistant: Steps: 1) Publish at least one episode and your trailer on your host. Ensure artwork meets specs. 2) Apple Podcasts: - Create an Apple ID (if needed). - Go to Apple Podcasts Connect, submit your RSS feed. - Set show category, country, contact email; verify artwork. Approval typically 24–72 hours. 3) Spotify: - Many hosts auto-distribute. If not, go to Spotify for Podcasters and submit your RSS. - Approval is usually minutes to a few hours. 4) YouTube Music: - Podcasters are migrating there; hosts like Buzzsprout/RSS.com have workflows, or upload your episodes with static art manually. 5) Amazon Music/Audible: Submit via their podcasters portal. Common pitfalls: No email in RSS, missing category, wrong artwork size, no live episode/trailer. Once approved, new episodes appear in 15–60 minutes (Spotify) and a few hours (Apple).
