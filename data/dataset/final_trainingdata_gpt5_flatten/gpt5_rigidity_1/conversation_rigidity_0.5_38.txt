### Human: I was leaning toward a condenser like the AT2020USB. Will that work?
### Assistant: It will work, and it can sound crisp—but in an untreated room with a fan, a condenser will capture more room echo and noise. If you love the condenser sound, we can make it work with technique and some software noise control, but it’s fighting the space a bit. Two options: - Make AT2020USB work: - Get very close to the mic (4–6 inches) with a pop filter. - Aim the mic so the back (null) faces the noise source. - Add soft stuff around you: thick curtains, a rug, a blanket behind you, or record facing an open closet with clothes. - Use light noise suppression (RNNoise in OBS, or Krisp/NVIDIA Broadcast if available), and a gentle gate. - Easier, lower‑noise route: a USB dynamic like Shure MV7, Rode PodMic USB, or Rode XDM‑100 (if on sale). You’ll get cleaner results with less effort in your room. If you really prefer the condenser tone, we’ll dial it in. Otherwise, MV7 gives you a great balance of sound and practicality.
### Human: Okay, let’s go with the MV7. How should I set it up on Windows and in Zoom and OBS?
### Assistant: Great choice. Here’s a concise setup: Physical and connection - Mount the MV7 on a boom arm. Use the included windscreen. Distance: 2–4 inches from your mouth, slightly off-axis to reduce plosives. - Connect via USB. Plug your headphones into the MV7’s headphone jack for zero-latency monitoring. Shure MOTIV app (download from Shure) - Update firmware if prompted. - Start with Auto Level mode, Near setting. If you switch to Manual: - Set gain so your loudest words peak around -12 dBFS. - Enable high-pass filter. Presence boost is taste; try it off first. - Light compression is fine for Zoom; for OBS recording, you can leave compression off here and do it in OBS instead. Windows Sound settings - Sound > Input > Shure MV7 as default communications device (optional: keep your system default mic separate). - Device properties > Advanced: 48 kHz, 24-bit. Enable Exclusive Mode. - Turn off any “audio enhancements.” Zoom - Microphone: Shure MV7. - Uncheck “Automatically adjust microphone volume.” Set input around 70–80% and test. - Noise suppression: Low or Auto. (MV7 plus close-mic technique does most of the work.) - Echo cancellation: Auto. Disable “Original sound” unless you really need it. OBS (for streaming/recording) - Settings > Audio: Sample rate 48 kHz. - Add “Audio Input Capture” > Shure MV7. - Right-click the source > Filters: - High-Pass Filter: 80 Hz (start here). - Noise Suppression: RNNoise (good quality). - Noise Gate: Close ~ -45 dB, Open ~ -35 dB (tweak so it doesn’t clip word endings). - Compressor: Ratio 3:1, Threshold ~ -18 dB, Attack 5 ms, Release 100 ms, Makeup +2 to +4 dB. - Limiter: Ceiling -3 dB. - Optional EQ: Small cut around 200–300 Hz if boomy; small boost 3–5 kHz for clarity if needed. - Monitor with headphones from the MV7, not from Windows, to avoid latency. Technique - Speak across the mic, 2–4 inches away. - Turn off the fan during recording if possible. - Keep levels conservative—no red lights. ### Human: Can I use it with a boom arm I already have? It’s a cheap one.
### Assistant: Most likely. The MV7 is relatively light (~0.55 lb/0.25 kg), so even inexpensive arms typically hold it. Tips: - If the arm droops, tighten tension or mount the mic underslung so gravity helps. - Use the included windscreen; a separate pop filter is optional. - If desk noise is an issue, add a shock mount for the arm or a desk isolation pad. The MV7 has some internal isolation, but mechanical bumps can still travel up the arm.
### Human: What if I want to upgrade to XLR later?
### Assistant: Easy—MV7 has an XLR output. Your chain would be: - MV7 (XLR) → audio interface (MOTU M2, SSL 2, or Audient iD14/EVO 4) → USB to PC → DAW/OBS. Notes: - Turn OFF phantom power (48V) for the MV7; it’s a dynamic mic. - Set interface gain so peaks hit about -12 dBFS. The MV7 isn’t super gain-hungry, and those interfaces have enough clean gain for most voices, but very soft speakers might still prefer a booster like a FetHead. - You’ll gain better meters, preamps, and expansion for a second mic. If you think you’ll upgrade soon, the Rode PodMic USB or MV7 still make sense now and keep the door open for XLR workflows.
### Human: Any quick tips to make my room sound better without spending much?
### Assistant: Absolutely: - Placement: Record facing a closet full of clothes or hang a thick blanket behind the mic. Avoid speaking toward bare walls. - Soft surfaces: Rug on the floor, curtains drawn, a folded blanket on the desk under the arm, pillows off to the sides. - Distance and direction: Stay close to the mic; aim the mic’s rear at the noise source (fan/computer). - Quiet the desk: Use a boom arm clamp on a sturdy part of the desk; put keyboard/mouse on a desk mat to reduce thumps. - Schedule noise: Record when the room/building is quiet; turn off the fan briefly while recording if safe. - Light software help: RNNoise in OBS or Krisp/NVIDIA Broadcast for calls.
