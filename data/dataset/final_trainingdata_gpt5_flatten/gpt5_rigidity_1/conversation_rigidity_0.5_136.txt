Ingredients for two 10–11 inch pizzas (about 250 g each dough ball): - 00 flour (Caputo Pizzeria or equivalent, “Tipo 00”): 320 g - Water, 18–20°C: 190 g (about 60% hydration) - Fine sea salt: 9 g (2.8%) - Fresh compressed yeast: 0.15 g (about 0.05%) Toppings (strict AVPN): - San Marzano DOP tomatoes (hand-crushed): 160–200 g total - Fior di latte (cow’s milk mozzarella) or Mozzarella di Bufala Campana DOP, well drained: 160–200 g total - Fresh basil leaves - Extra-virgin olive oil - No sugar, no oil in the dough Equipment (strict): - Wood-fired oven capable of 430–485°C (800–905°F) - Pizza peel - Bench and mixing surface Process: 1) Dissolve salt in the full amount of water. Crumble in the fresh yeast and dissolve. 2) Add about 10% of the flour and mix to a smooth slurry. Add the remaining flour gradually, mixing until a shaggy mass forms. 3) Knead by hand until smooth and elastic, about 15–20 minutes. The dough should be around 24–26°C when finished. 4) Bulk ferment at 20–24°C for ~2 hours, covered. 5) Divide into two 250–260 g pieces. Shape gently into tight balls. 6) Proof the dough balls at 20–24°C for 6–8 hours, covered. 7) Preheat the oven to 430–485°C. The floor should be at temperature. 8) Open each dough ball by hand into a 22–28 cm disk, leaving a 1–2 cm rim. No rolling pin. 9) Top with hand-crushed tomatoes, then torn/drained mozzarella, a few basil leaves, and a drizzle of EVOO. 10) Bake 60–90 seconds, rotating as needed for even leoparding. The cornicione should be airy and lightly charred. Note on measuring tiny yeast amounts: If you don’t have a 0.01 g scale, make a yeast solution (e.g., dissolve 1 g fresh yeast in 100 g water), then use 15 g of that solution and subtract 15 g from the recipe’s water to keep hydration constant. If any of the above items are unavailable, let me know and I’ll flag deviations and suggest the closest workable approach.
