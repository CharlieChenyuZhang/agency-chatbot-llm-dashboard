### Assistant: 700 words is longer than you need. Here’s a tight, clearer summary: Extraction is the process of dissolving desirable compounds from ground coffee into water. Early in extraction, acids and small aromatics dissolve first, giving brightness and fruity notes. As extraction continues, sweetness and body develop as sugars and Maillard products dissolve. If you push too far, large bitter polyphenols and drying tannins dominate. The key variables are grind size, brew ratio, water temperature, turbulence, and contact time. Finer grind increases surface area and resistance, extracting faster but risking bitterness and astringency if flow stalls. Coarser grind reduces extraction and speeds flow, which can taste sour or thin if underdone. Ratio sets concentration; higher coffee-to-water ratios increase strength but don’t guarantee better extraction. Water temperature (roughly 92–96°C) controls solubility; hotter water extracts faster but can push harshness in fine grinds. Agitation (stirs, swirls, pour pattern) evens saturation and prevents channeling, improving uniformity. Contact time ties everything together: too short yields under-extraction (sour, sharp), too long yields over-extraction (bitter, drying). Aim for an extraction that balances acidity, sweetness, and gentle bitterness. Practically, adjust grind before anything else: if sour, go finer or extend contact; if bitter/dry, go coarser or shorten time. Keep water near 94°C, use fresh beans, and maintain consistent pouring and agitation.
