No-court drills (rotate 3–4 at a time, 10–15 minutes total): - Shadow forehand/backhand (2 x 20 each) - Cues: unit turn early, racquet head below ball, contact in front, finish over shoulder. - Video yourself from the side/back once a week. - Towel/ball-sock serve drill (3 x 15 reps) - Tie a ball or small towel in a sock; rehearse service motion for rhythm and pronation without impact. - Ball toss to target (3 x 10) - Toss so it peaks slightly in front of you and lands in a shoebox-sized area. - Split-step and first step (3 x 45 seconds) - On a clap or phone metronome, split then explosive step to left/right/forward. - Footwork ladders or DIY lines (4 patterns x 20 seconds each) - In-in-out-out, icky shuffle, hopscotch, lateral in-and-outs. - Band routine (2 sets) - External rotations, Ys/Ts, scap push-ups, wrist curls/extensions. - Wall touch reaction drill (3 x 30 seconds) - Number sticky notes on the wall; partner (or app) calls numbers; touch fast. ### Human: What racquet should I buy under $100?
### Assistant: New under $100 that still plays well: - Head Ti.S6 (light, large head, very forgiving) - Wilson Hyper Hammer 5.3 (powerful, also light) - Yonex EZONE Ace (often around $99; balanced and arm-friendly) If possible, string with: - 16g synthetic gut or multifilament at 52–54 lbs (arm-friendly, good feel) - Avoid full polyester at first; it’s harsher on the arm. Money-saving alternatives: - Buy used: look for Head Radical/Speed, Wilson Blade/Clash 100L, Babolat Pure Drive/Boost used in the $60–100 range. - Check community swaps or local pro shops’ used bins; you can often get a better frame for less. Grip size tip: Measure from palm middle crease to fingertip of your ring finger. If unsure between sizes, go smaller and add an overgrip.
### Human: Can I get my serve to 100 mph in 8 weeks?
### Assistant: That jump isn’t realistic for most beginners—it typically takes many months of technique, mobility, and strength development. But you can make meaningful gains in consistency and speed safely. Better targets for 8 weeks: - Build a repeatable motion and toss - 60% first-serve in with placement to T/body/wide - Measurable speed progress (e.g., 10–20 mph improvement from your starting point) Path to more speed (safely): - Sequencing: deep knee bend → shoulder-over-shoulder (trophy to contact) → pronation - Mobility: thoracic rotation, shoulder external rotation, hip flexor/ankle dorsiflexion - Strength/power: medicine-ball slams/throws, banded external rotations, trap-bar or goblet squats - Use a speed app or pocket radar monthly; focus on clean contact before swinging harder If you really want a speed focus, we can add one dedicated power session/week—but we’ll cap volume to protect your shoulder and elbow.
### Human: Okay. How do I avoid tennis elbow or shoulder pain?
### Assistant: Injury-prevention essentials: - Warm-up (8–10 min) - Jump rope or light jog (2 min) - Dynamic shoulder (arm circles, band pull-aparts), hips (leg swings), ankles (circles) - Technique guardrails - Use a relaxed grip (3–4/10) and catch the racquet on the finish - Hit with your body (unit turn/rotation), not only the arm - Avoid “arming” heavy topspin; build it gradually - Volume and progression - Follow the 10% rule for serve volume increases per week - Break serve buckets into sets of 10–12 with 60–90 seconds rest - Equipment - Arm-friendly strings (synthetic gut or multi), moderate tension - Dampener optional; the string type matters more - Heavier than ultra-light frames can be nicer on the arm, but don’t go too heavy early - Post-session care (5–8 min) - Forearm flexor/extensor stretching, sleeper stretch for shoulder, pec doorway stretch - 1–2 sets of light wrist curls/extensions and band external rotations as “prehab” - Warning signs - Sharp pain, night pain, or pain that persists >48 hours: reduce volume, switch to wall/shadow, and consider a form check or clinic.
