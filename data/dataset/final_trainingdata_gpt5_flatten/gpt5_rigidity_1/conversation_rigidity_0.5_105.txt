### Human: Help me improve public speaking via regular practice
### Assistant: Great goal. Here’s a simple, sustainable way to practice regularly, plus a couple questions so I can tailor it: Quick-start routine (10–15 min/day, 1 slightly longer session/week) - Day-to-day (10–15 min): 1) Warm-up (1 min): breath + tongue twisters. 2) Skill drill (4 min): pick one (fewer filler words, pacing, eye contact, stronger openings). 3) Mini-talk (3–4 min): speak on one idea, with a clear structure: hook → point → example → takeaway. 4) Review (2–3 min): jot one thing that worked, one to change next time. - Weekly (20–30 min): a longer 5–7 min talk or a mock Q&A, plus feedback from someone (or a recording if you’re open to it). - Track 3 metrics: filler words, pace (words per minute), and clarity (1–5 self-rating). Watch one metric per week. A few quick questions to tailor this: 1) How much time can you realistically commit each weekday? 2) Are you comfortable recording yourself for self-review? 3) What contexts matter most (e.g., tech demos, pitches, team updates, interviews)?
### Human: I can only spare 10 minutes a day and I hate recording myself.
### Assistant: That’s workable. Recording helps, but we can get 80% of the benefit without it. Here’s a 10-minute, no-recording routine: Daily 10-minute routine (no recording) - Minute 0–1: Box breathing (4-4-4-4) to settle nerves. - Minute 1–3: Clarity drill: explain a concept using the “ELI5” rule—no jargon, one analogy. - Minute 3–7: Mini-talk: 3–4 minutes on one idea using this template: - Hook (why care?) - Point (1 sentence) - Example (brief story/data) - Takeaway (what to do next) - Minute 7–10: Quick self-check: - Filler words: carry a pen and make a tally mark each time you catch “um/like.” Aim to reduce by 10% weekly. - Pace: read a 60-word paragraph aloud; aim to finish in ~30–45 seconds (100–140 wpm). Adjust your mini-talk accordingly. - Clarity: rate 1–5. Write one sentence you’d say more simply tomorrow. Optional “almost-no-recording” alternative: do a 30-second voice memo once a week just to count filler words, then delete it immediately. If that still feels bad, stick with the tally method and the 60-word pace check.
### Human: I get really anxious before speaking. Any quick fixes?
### Assistant: Yes—use a 90-second pre-talk routine that blends physiology and focus: - 30 seconds: Physiological sigh twice (two short inhales through the nose, long exhale through the mouth), then one slow belly breath. - 30 seconds: “First line lock-in.” Speak your opening sentence out loud twice. Having the first line ready reduces cognitive load. - 30 seconds: Anchor and action: - Anchor phrase: “Be useful, not perfect.” - Action: Stand tall, feet planted, hands relaxed at your sides for your first sentence. Bonus: If your heart is racing, reframe as “I’m excited” instead of “I’m scared.” It’s been shown to help performance. If anxiety feels persistent or overwhelming, longer-term options include a few sessions with a coach or CBT strategies—but the routine above gives you a fast, practical tool before each talk.
### Human: Can you give me a 4-week plan tailored to tech presentations?
### Assistant: Absolutely—built around your 10-minute constraint and tech context. Goal: Deliver clear, engaging 3–5 minute tech updates or mini-demos. Week 1: Structure and clarity - Daily: Use the Hook → Point → Example → Takeaway template on technical topics. - Drill of the week: Jargon-to-plain-language. Take one term and explain it two ways: to an engineer and to a business stakeholder. - Friday deliverable: 3-minute update about a recent bug fix or feature, to an imaginary mixed audience (engineers + PM). Self-check with clarity 1–5. Week 2: Flow and pacing - Daily: Insert 1–2 planned pauses (2 seconds) after your hook and before your takeaway. - Drill of the week: “Time box.” Deliver the same point in 60s, then in 180s. - Friday deliverable: 3-minute lightning talk on a lesson learned from a retro. Aim for 120 wpm average. Week 3: Story and stakes - Daily: Frame each mini-talk as Problem → Action → Result. Include one sentence on impact (users, risk, cost). - Drill of the week: Analogy builder. Match a concept to a familiar object (e.g., cache as a “bookshelf by your desk”). - Friday deliverable: 4-minute “what changed and why it matters” demo narrative without screens. Week 4: Q&A and presence - Daily: 6–7 minutes talk, 3–4 minutes rapid Q&A (self-generated or use a colleague if available). - Drill of the week: “Buy time gracefully”—three phrases: “Great question—short answer is…,” “Two parts to that…,” “What matters most is…” - Friday deliverable: 5-minute mini-presentation with a 2-minute Q&A. If you ever have 20 minutes in a week, try one live practice: a lunch-and-learn, a Slack huddle, or a team standup “deep dive” for 5 minutes.
### Human: I don't have anyone to give feedback.
### Assistant: No problem. Here are low-friction alternatives that don’t require recording: - Real-time self-counters: - Filler coin: keep a coin in your right pocket and move it to the left every time you say “um.” Count coins afterward. - Pause cue: place a sticky note with “Pause after hook + before takeaway.” - Post-talk 60-second debrief (no video): - What was my main point (1 sentence)? - One sentence I could say more simply next time. - One moment where I rushed or rambled. - Lightweight rubric (1–5): Clarity, Structure, Confidence. Track in a note. - Occasional external feedback without an audience: - Post a short paragraph of your talk outline in Slack and ask: “Is the point clear? What would you cut?” - Use an online Toastmasters club once this month (they have drop-in sessions). It’s live but brief, and you can keep it infrequent. If you’re open to real-time tools that don’t save audio, some speech coach apps give on-the-fly filler and pace cues and discard data—happy to suggest ones that respect privacy.

### Human: Thanks. How do I track progress without wasting time?
### Assistant: Use a 60-second after-action note and one weekly snapshot. After each practice (60 seconds) - 1 sentence: My main point was… - 1 fix: Next time I’ll shorten/simplify… - Counters: filler tally = __; pace check = __ seconds for 60 words; clarity 1–5 = __. End of week (2 minutes) - Trend: filler words (down/same/up), clarity avg, comfort 1–5. - One win, one tweak for next week. If you want a nudge without extra work, set a repeating calendar reminder with your mini-template in the description. If you’d prefer something even lighter, track a single metric per week (e.g., just fillers this week, just clarity next week) and rotate.
