### Human: What should I ask on the first call?
### Assistant: Use these: - Experience: How many California security-deposit cases (Civil Code §1950.5) have you handled in the last year? Typical outcomes? - Scope and price: Do you offer a flat fee for a demand letter? What’s included (reviewing my lease, photos, receipts)? Turnaround time? - Next steps: If the landlord doesn’t pay, will you advise me for small claims prep? What would that cost? - Conflicts/position: Do you represent tenants only, or both sides? - Communication: Who will do the work (you or a paralegal)? How do we communicate and how fast? Have ready: lease, move-in/out photos, timelines, deposit amount, forwarding address proof, any landlord messages, and receipts for cleaning/repairs.
### Human: How much should I expect to pay?
### Assistant: Typical ranges in San Diego for this kind of limited help: - Initial consultation: free to $150. - Flat-fee demand letter with brief document review: $200–$500. - Hourly rates: ~$250–$450 if it goes beyond a letter. - Contingency is uncommon for ~$3,000 claims. Note: In California small claims, a lawyer can’t represent you at the hearing, but a brief coaching session ($150–$300) can make a big difference.
