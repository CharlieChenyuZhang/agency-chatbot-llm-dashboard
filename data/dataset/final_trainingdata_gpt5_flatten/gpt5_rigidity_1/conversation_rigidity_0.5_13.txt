### Human: I always forget CIDR notation. Any tricks?
### Assistant: Quick CIDR cheats: - Host counts: /24=256 addresses (~254 usable), /20=4096, /16=65536. Formula: usable hosts ≈ 2^(32−prefix) − 2. - Common increments per prefix: - /24: increments by 1 in the last octet (e.g., 10.0.1.0/24) - /20: increments by 16 in the third octet (10.0.16.0/20) - /28: 16 addresses per block; increments by 16 in the last octet - VPC vs subnet: pick VPC CIDR first (e.g., 10.0.0.0/16), then divide into subnets (/24s for simple math). - NACLs are stateless; SGs are stateful—separate concern, but often tied to CIDR usage. Want 5 rapid-fire CIDR practice items with answers?
### Human: Yes give me 5 practice items.
### Assistant: Here you go: 1) How many usable IPs in 10.0.1.0/24? - Answer: 254 (256 total minus network/broadcast). 2) What’s the next subnet after 10.0.16.0/20? - Answer: 10.0.32.0/20 (increments by 16 in the third octet). 3) You need at least 50 hosts in a subnet. Smallest mask? - Answer: /26 (64 total, ~62 usable). /27 gives ~30 usable, not enough. 4) From 10.1.0.0/16, carve a /20 public subnet in the 10.1.x.0 range that starts at a multiple of 16. - Answer: 10.1.16.0/20 (valid options: 10.1.0.0/20, 10.1.16.0/20, 10.1.32.0/20, etc.). 5) True/False: 172.31.0.0/16 can contain a subnet 172.31.0.0/18 and another 172.31.64.0/18 without overlap. - Answer: True. /18 blocks are 64 in the third octet: 0–63 and 64–127 don’t overlap.
