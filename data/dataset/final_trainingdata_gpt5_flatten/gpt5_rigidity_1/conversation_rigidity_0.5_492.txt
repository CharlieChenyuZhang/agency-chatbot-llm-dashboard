### Human: I don’t have a good camera though. Should I wait until I buy one?
### Assistant: No need to wait. Your phone is enough to start. Better audio and lighting matter more than an expensive camera. - Audio: Cheap wired lav mic (e.g., Boya BY-M1) into your phone. - Lighting: Clamp desk lamp or $20 LED panel; bounce light off a white wall. - Stability: Stack books or a $15 tripod. - Settings: Record in 1080p, lock focus/exposure, wipe lens, keep subject near light. If you later upgrade, get a USB mic first; cameras can come much later.
### Human: I can post daily if that helps growth, right?
### Assistant: Daily long videos usually hurt quality and burnout. Better: - 1 solid long video/week + 2 Shorts from that video. This keeps quality high and still signals activity to the algorithm. - Batch: Script Saturday, film Sunday, edit Monday, publish Tuesday; cut Shorts Wednesday. If you really want daily, make it Shorts-only for now. They’re easier to produce and still drive traffic to your long videos.

### Human: I want to stay anonymous. Is that okay for tech reviews?
### Assistant: Totally fine. Use: - Hands-only product shots, overhead tripod, screen captures, on-screen text. - Voiceover with your normal voice. If you prefer not to use your voice, quality text-to-speech is an option, but real voice usually performs better. - Avoid showing personal locations or reflections on glossy devices. Clean serial numbers if visible.

### Human: How do I make thumbnails for these?
### Assistant: Keep them simple and bold: - One subject, big contrast, 3–5 words max. - Faces help, but for anonymity: big product photo + bold words like “Under $250?” - Tools: Canva or Figma. Use 1280×720, large text, high contrast. - Poses: Angle device toward lens; add a simple prop (textbook/coffee) for student vibe. - A/B testing: Use YouTube’s thumbnail test tool (if available) or swap after 48 hours if CTR < 4%.
### Human: I like popular songs. Can I use them?
### Assistant: Not without licenses—you’ll get claimed or blocked. Safer options: - YouTube Audio Library (free, no copyright headaches). - Artlist, Epidemic Sound, or Uppbeat (paid libraries with clear licenses). - For shorts, YouTube’s in-app music sometimes allows use, but it can limit monetization. If monetization is a priority, stick to royalty-free tracks.
### Human: How do I get to 1,000 subs fast?
### Assistant: Realistic, sustainable approach: - Consistency: 1 quality long video/week + 2 Shorts. - Titles/Thumbs: Promise a clear outcome (“Best Under $250”) and show the device big. - SEO basics: Put the main keyword in title, first 2 lines of description, tags; add chapters. - Retention: Hook in 10 seconds; show results early; cut dead air; on-screen checklists. - Interactivity: Pin a comment with a question (“What’s your budget?”). Reply to early comments. - Cross-promo: Post Shorts on TikTok/IG Reels with a profile link. - Avoid sub4sub and fake giveaways—they hurt long-term engagement.
