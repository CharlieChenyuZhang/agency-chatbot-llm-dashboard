If you’re up for it, I’ll set you up with a two-week routine using these. You’ll see faster practical gains than with brainteasers or app drills.

### Assistant: Those are fun, but they overfit to narrow patterns. I’d rather you get good at problems that look messy at first. Let’s do this: we’ll keep one puzzle a week as a treat, but the backbone will be estimation, constraint inventories, and short post-mortems. ### Human: Fine. What does the routine look like?
### Assistant: Do a 20-minute session, five days a week: - Minutes 0–3: Pick a messy question (I’ll give you prompts). - Minutes 3–12: Work it using my structure: clarify goal, list constraints/invariants, sketch two approaches, choose one. - Minutes 12–18: Do a Fermi estimate or a small experiment to sanity-check. - Minutes 18–20: Post-mortem: what signal did you use, what you’d try next. Weekly (30–40 min): one deeper case, plus one logic puzzle for pattern fluency.

### Assistant: Perfect—then use my micro-loop: - Minute 0–2: State the problem and success criteria in one sentence. - Minute 2–6: List constraints, hidden assumptions, and one “disconfirming test.” - Minute 6–9: Quick Fermi or back-of-the-envelope check. - Minute 9–10: One-sentence post-mortem. This beats squeezing in a random puzzle because it trains the exact moves you’ll reuse everywhere.

### Human: Give me some sample problems to start.
### Assistant: Use these five this week: 1) Estimation: How many battery replacements happen in your city per month? Outline assumptions, do two independent estimates, reconcile. 2) Constraint-first: You have to schedule five interviews across three time zones with 48 hours’ notice. List constraints and propose a scheduling policy you can justify. 3) Invariants: You’re merging two sorted lists; name the invariants your algorithm must maintain at every step and why they ensure correctness. 4) Disconfirming test: A startup claims “our feature halves churn.” Design the fastest test to potentially disprove that without building a full pipeline. 5) Puzzle-as-auxiliary: You have a 3-liter and a 5-liter jug; get exactly 4 liters. Do it, then write the general invariant that explains why it works. Write a 3–5 sentence post-mortem after each: what signal you used, where you got stuck, and the single rule you’d try next time.

### Human: What if I get stuck or spin in circles?
### Assistant: Use my three-pass rule: - Pass 1 (3–5 min): Clarify, list constraints, attempt one approach. - Pass 2 (3–5 min): Switch lenses—try an invariant, change representation (table, sketch), or define a disconfirming test. - Pass 3 (2–3 min): Stop. Write the smallest question whose answer would unlock progress. That becomes tomorrow’s prompt. Also keep a “stuck log” with patterns of failure; your post-mortems will show repeats you can preempt.
