### Human: I’m on Windows. I mostly have screenshots and PDFs everywhere.
### Assistant: Here are two options—no-script and light-script. No-script, low risk (20–30 min) 1) Create three folders in Documents: “Inbox,” “Screenshots,” “PDFs.” 2) On Desktop: press Ctrl+E, type: kind:=picture OR type:png OR type:jpg, select all, move to Documents\Screenshots. 3) Still on Desktop: search type:=pdf, select all, move to Documents\PDFs. 4) Repeat steps 2–3 in Downloads. 5) In Documents\Screenshots, View > Group by > Date to batch-review; delete obvious junk. 6) In Documents\PDFs, make quick subfolders: Receipts, Statements, Work, Manuals, Tickets. Move in batches with Search (e.g., search “receipt”). 7) Turn on automatic screenshot capture to a single place (if you use OneDrive: Settings > Backup > Manage > check “Screenshots”). 8) Optional: Right-click Desktop and Downloads > View > Auto arrange icons to keep things tidy. Light-script (safer mode) - You can run PowerShell with -WhatIf first. If you want this, tell me and I’ll tailor commands to your exact username/paths.

### Human: Actually, can we skip the printer and go all-digital for shipping?
### Assistant: Yes. Easiest approaches: - UPS/USPS/FedEx QR codes: buy the label online, choose “mobile/QR,” the store scans your code and prints the label. - USPS Click-N-Ship: purchase on usps.com; many USPS locations can scan from your phone. - Returns: most retailers (Amazon, etc.) offer “no-box, no-label” drop-offs. Pros: less desk space, no ink costs, fewer cables. Con: you need a nearby drop-off point. If you ship often later, a basic thermal label printer ($60–$100) is still cheaper long-term than inkjets, but you can hold off for now. Quick setup - Create accounts: USPS, UPS, or Pirate Ship (free, good rates). - Save your address and default package sizes. - Add a preferred payment method. - Bookmark the shipment page; make a desktop shortcut. - Optional: schedule pickups with USPS if you print at home later.
