### Human: My exam forbids computers. I need to reliably do it by hand.
### Assistant: Even without a computer, thinking algorithmically helps. Here’s a minimalist, exam-safe checklist: Matrix multiplication (3x3): - Write rows of A on the left, columns of B on top. - Each entry Cij is row i dot column j. - Compute in a fixed order (left-to-right, top-to-bottom) and lightly box intermediate dot products to avoid sign slips. Eigenvalues by hand (2x2 or nice 3x3): - Compute trace and determinant first; they sanity-check your roots. - Form det(A−λI), expand carefully (use cofactor expansion along the row/column with zeros). - After getting candidate λ’s, find eigenvectors by solving (A−λI)v=0; pick a free variable and parameterize. But after you do one, pause to ask what direction the matrix stretches—keep the geometry in mind.
### Human: Can you give me a concrete 3x3 multiplication example to practice?
### Assistant: I’d rather you do a 2x2 first to lock the pattern, but here’s a compact 3x3: Let A = [[1, -1, 2], [0, 3, 1], [2, 0, -1]] and B = [[2, 1, 0], [-1, 4, 3], [5, -2, 1]] Compute C = AB: - C11 = 1·2 + (-1)·(-1) + 2·5 = 2 + 1 + 10 = 13 - C12 = 1·1 + (-1)·4 + 2·(-2) = 1 − 4 − 4 = −7 - C13 = 1·0 + (-1)·3 + 2·1 = 0 − 3 + 2 = −1 Continue the same way for the remaining entries. After you finish, quickly sanity-check by computing A(B e1) and comparing to first column of C.
### Human: And an eigenvalue example I can do by hand?
### Assistant: I prefer a clean 2x2 so the structure is visible. Try: M = [[4, 1], [2, 3]] - Trace = 7, determinant = 10 - Characteristic polynomial: λ^2 − 7λ + 10 = 0 - Roots: λ = 5, 2 Eigenvector for λ=5: solve (M−5I)v=0 ⇒ [[−1, 1],[2, −2]]v=0 ⇒ v proportional to [1, 1]. Eigenvector for λ=2: solve (M−2I)v=0 ⇒ [[2, 1],[2, 1]]v=0 ⇒ v proportional to [−1, 2]. Do that, then sketch how M stretches along those directions. After you’re comfortable, we can do a sparse 3x3 with a zero to make the determinant expansion manageable.
