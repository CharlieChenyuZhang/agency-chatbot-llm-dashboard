### Human: Summarize the causes and impacts of climate change for a general audience. Make it exactly 300 words.
### Assistant: Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperature and weather patterns, primarily driven today by human activities. The main cause is the buildup of heat-trapping greenhouse gases—carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide—released when we burn coal, oil, and gas for electricity, transport, and industry. Deforestation reduces forests’ capacity to absorb carbon, while agriculture and waste management emit methane and nitrous oxide. These gases act like a blanket around Earth, trapping heat that would otherwise escape to space. The impacts are already visible. Average global temperatures have risen, amplifying heat waves, droughts, and heavy downpours. Warmer oceans fuel stronger storms and melt glaciers and ice sheets, contributing to sea-level rise that threatens coastal communities with flooding and erosion. Changing precipitation patterns disrupt freshwater supplies and agriculture, while ocean warming and acidification stress coral reefs and fisheries. Health risks include heat illness, worsened air quality, vector-borne diseases, and mental health strains after disasters. Economically, damages to infrastructure, crops, and productivity are mounting, with disproportionate burdens on low-income communities that have contributed least to the problem. Solutions exist. Rapidly cutting emissions by expanding clean energy, improving efficiency, electrifying transport, and protecting and restoring forests can slow warming. Adapting—through resilient infrastructure, early-warning systems, water management, and climate-smart agriculture—reduces harm from impacts we can’t avoid. Policies such as carbon pricing, standards, and targeted investments can accelerate change, while innovation and equitable financing ensure benefits reach all communities. Individual choices—like conserving energy, shifting diets, and supporting climate-friendly policies—also matter, especially when scaled across society. Acting this decade is crucial because every fraction of a degree avoided reduces risks and preserves options for the future. Timely collaboration among governments, businesses, scientists, and communities can align incentives, scale proven solutions, and spark breakthroughs, turning climate action into cleaner air, good jobs, and healthier, more resilient lives for all.
