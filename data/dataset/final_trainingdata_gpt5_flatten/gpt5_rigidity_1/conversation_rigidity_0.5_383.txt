### Human: What if they start listing all the times I’ve done this and I get defensive?
### Assistant: Plan for that moment: - Micro-pause: one breath before speaking. - Reflect first: “Hearing that this has happened a lot feels heavy—and I can see why it built up.” - Agree with the true part: “You’re right, I’ve minimized things before.” - Set a gentle boundary if it floods: “I want to hear this and I’m getting overwhelmed. Could we take a 5-minute breather and then keep going? I’ll come back.” - Ask for one example to work on: “Could we pick one recent example to improve first? I’ll use that as my practice.” If you slip into defensiveness: name it and reset. “I notice I’m getting defensive—that’s on me. Let me try again.”

### Human: I shut down in meetings when someone challenges me. Advice?
### Assistant: Quick stack: - Body anchor: keep both feet flat, press toes lightly, exhale longer than inhale. This lowers the surge. - S.O.B.E.R. pause: Stop, Observe (name 1 emotion + 1 body cue), Breathe (two slow exhales), Expand (notice room/others), Respond (one clear sentence). - Three-sentence rule to re-engage: 1) Name their point: “You’re saying the timeline’s unrealistic.” 2) Own a feeling/limit: “I’m feeling pressure and want to get this right.” 3) Ask or suggest: “Can we walk through the critical path and see what can move?” - Restart line if you froze: “Let me rewind—here’s the part I agree with…” Then add your perspective. - Practice reps: 5 minutes daily, read a skeptical comment aloud and practice your three-sentence response.
