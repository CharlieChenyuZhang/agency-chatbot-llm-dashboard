### Human: I don’t have Velcro ties or a tray.
### Assistant: No problem—here are no-purchase alternatives: For cables: - Use twist ties from bread bags, rubber bands, or even masking tape folded over itself to avoid sticky residue on cables. - A spare toilet paper or paper towel roll can act as a simple cable sleeve for extra length. - Large binder clips on the desk edge can hold cables so they don’t fall. For an “Inbox”: - A shoebox or its lid works well. - A large envelope or a spare folder can be your temporary tray. - If you have a magazine file/holder, that’s perfect too. Quick tip: Labeling helps, but if you’d rather not label yet, keep “Action” papers on top, “Keep” at the bottom inside the box.
### Human: I also want a standing desk. Can I DIY something today?
### Assistant: You can do a safe temporary setup to test the height, but full-time DIY can be wobbly if not done carefully. Options today: - Use sturdy boxes or stacked books to raise your laptop/monitor and keyboard to standing height. Check ergonomics: screen top at or slightly below eye level; elbows ~90 degrees. - An ironing board can be a quick adjustable surface if it’s stable. To keep it safe: - Test for wobble before putting your devices on top. - Stand in 20–30 minute intervals, alternate with sitting. - Mind cable slack so nothing gets tugged off. Longer-term low-cost alternatives: - A sit-stand desk converter (often cheaper and more stable than a whole new desk). - A manual crank or pneumatic desk can be more affordable than electric.

### Human: Cables across the floor look ugly. Can I run them under the rug?
### Assistant: I don’t recommend it—running cables under rugs can create heat build-up, trip hazards, and wear on the insulation. Safer, cleaner alternatives: - Route cables along the wall with adhesive cable clips or a low-profile raceway (paintable and tidy). - Use gaffer or painter’s tape to secure cables along baseboards short-term. - Mount a simple under-desk cable tray, or use large binder clips on the desk frame to lift the power strip and cables off the floor. If you need a quick fix today: tape along the wall, not across walking paths, and keep power bricks off the floor on a small box or mounted underside with strong hooks.
### Human: I have a surge protector with too few outlets. Can I daisy-chain power strips?
### Assistant: It’s best not to. Daisy-chaining power strips can overload circuits and is a fire risk. Safer options: - Replace with a single power strip that has enough outlets and surge protection, with an appropriate amp rating. - Use a short, heavy-duty extension cord to reach the outlet if placement is the issue, but still plug only one quality strip into the wall. - Separate high-draw devices (heaters, printers) onto a different wall outlet/circuit if possible. General safety: - Don’t exceed the strip’s wattage rating. - Keep strips off the floor where spills or dust collect. - Periodically check for warmth or discoloration.

### Human: I hate labels. Can we avoid them?
