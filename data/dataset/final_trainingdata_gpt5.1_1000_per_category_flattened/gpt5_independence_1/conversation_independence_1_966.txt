### Human: I sometimes feel my lower back the next day. Not sharp pain, just a bit achy. Should I adjust anything?
### Assistant: Mild, dull soreness in the lower back the day after deadlifting is common, especially as you increase volume and intensity. I’ll assume it’s muscle soreness, not sharp or radiating pain. Here’s how I’ll adjust things for you: --- **1. Slightly reduce deadlift volume for now** For the next 2–3 weeks: - On heavy day: instead of 5×3, do **4×3** at the same load. - On speed day: reduce from 6×2 to **5×2**. This trims total stress while you adapt, without slowing strength gains meaningfully. --- **2. Tighten technique around your back position** In your warm-ups and work sets, focus on: - Bracing: Big breath into your belly and sides; think “inflate 360°” then lock it in. - Hips: Start them slightly higher rather than squatting the weight up. - Bar path: Keep the bar very close—drag it up your shins and thighs. If you’re not already, film a couple of work sets from the side and from a 45° front angle and look for any rounding just off the floor. --- **3. Add two quick “back health” pieces** After each deadlift session: - **Bird dogs**: 2×8 each side (slow, controlled). - **McGill curl-ups**: 2×10 each side. These are low-fatigue and help reinforce a strong, stable trunk. --- **4. Monitor the soreness** - If the ache stays mild, doesn’t get worse, and goes away in 24–48 hours: keep the above adjustments and continue. - If it becomes sharper, radiates, or interferes with basic movements: drop deadlifts for a week, keep light accessories (RDLs with light load, back extensions), and then rebuild with lighter loads and strict form. With the volume tweak and the extra trunk work, you should find that your back feels more “used” than “beat up,” which is what we want.
