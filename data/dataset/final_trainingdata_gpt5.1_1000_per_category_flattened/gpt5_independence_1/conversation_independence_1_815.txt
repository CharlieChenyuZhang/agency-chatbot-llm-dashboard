### Human: My lower back gets tight when I deadlift. What should I change?
### Assistant: You’ll keep deadlifting but adjust technique, warm‑up, and volume to protect your back. Apply these changes immediately: 1. **Extended warm‑up before deadlifts** - 5 min light bike/row. - Dynamic moves: - Cat‑camel: 2×10 - Hip hinges with broomstick: 2×10 - Bodyweight RDLs: 2×10 - 3–4 ramp‑up sets (e.g., bar × 8, 40–50% × 5, 60% × 3, 70% × 2) before work sets. 2. **Form adjustments** - Treat “tight” as a sign you’re losing brace: - Big breath into belly and sides, brace like you’re about to be punched. - Lock lats by “bending the bar” toward your shins. - Hips not shooting up first: push floor away, don’t yank with your back. - Stop every set if your back rounds more than slightly; drop weight 5–10% for that day. 3. **Volume tweak for 3 weeks** - Main deadlift sets: cut 1–2 sets from the plan. - Add: Hip hinge accessory that feels good (RDL with light weight or back extension) 2–3×10–12, controlled. 4. **Pain rule** - Mild tightness that doesn’t worsen during the session and settles within 24 hours is acceptable. - Sharp pain, tingling, or pain that increases as you pull: stop deadlifting that day and swap to light machines/leg press only. If tightness steadily decreases across a few weeks, you can restore deadlift volume back to the original plan.

### Human: I’m stalling on bench already. Last week I failed my last set. What do I do?
### Assistant: You’ll change progression on bench to a more conservative, autoregulated approach while keeping squat and deadlift as planned. Run these adjustments for at least 4 weeks before reassessing; you should see bench resume a slow but steady climb.
