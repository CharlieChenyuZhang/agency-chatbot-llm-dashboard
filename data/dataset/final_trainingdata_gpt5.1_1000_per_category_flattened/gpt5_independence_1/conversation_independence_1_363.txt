### Human: And what does that ongoing routine look like?
### Assistant: You’ll follow this simple, repeating schedule so you don’t have to think about it. Daily (takes 5–10 minutes): 1) Water check: - Stick a finger 1–2 inches into the soil of each pot. - If it feels dry at that depth, water until you see drainage from the bottom. - Expect daily watering in hot, sunny weather; every 1–2 days in cooler or cloudy weather. 2) Quick visual scan: - Leaves: look for drooping (thirst), yellowing (possible nutrient or water issue), or holes (pests). - Soil surface: remove any small weeds. Weekly (10–15 minutes, pick one fixed day of the week): 1) Support check: - Tie tomato stems loosely to stakes or adjust cages as they grow. - Make sure tall pots are stable and not at risk of tipping. 2) Leaf cleanup: - Remove any yellowed or dead leaves, especially low ones on tomatoes and zucchini/beans. 3) Herb trimming: - Pinch back basil tips (top 1–2 sets of leaves) to encourage bushier growth. - Harvest small amounts of parsley and chives as needed. Every 2 weeks (on the same day for consistency): 1) Liquid feeding: - Mix liquid vegetable fertilizer in water as directed on the bottle. - Apply it to tomatoes, peppers, zucchini/beans, and salad planters. - Herbs can get a slightly diluted version or be fed every 3–4 weeks instead. 2) Quick adjustment: - If a plant looks weak, give it a bit more sun if possible. - If leaves look scorched, slide that pot to a slightly shadier spot. Harvesting timeline guide: - Radishes: start checking after 3–4 weeks; harvest when roots are about marble to grape-sized. - Lettuce: begin cutting outer leaves once they reach 3–4 inches; they’ll regrow. - Green onions: harvest when stems are pencil-thick; pull whole plants or cut tops and let them regrow once. - Herbs: begin light cutting 2–3 weeks after planting. - Tomatoes and peppers: expect first fruits around 8–10 weeks after planting, then ongoing. - Zucchini/beans: harvest as soon as they’re a usable size—zucchini at 6–8 inches, beans when firm and smooth. You can now simply follow this routine without needing to redesign anything. ### Human: What if I go away for a week in the summer?
### Assistant: You’ll set up a simple, low-tech system so the garden survives a 7-day summer absence without daily watering. Before you leave (the day before): 1) Deep watering: - Water every pot slowly and thoroughly until the soil is fully saturated and water drains freely from the bottom. 2) Move pots slightly: - Slide the thirstiest plants (tomatoes, peppers, zucchini/beans) a bit out of the harshest direct afternoon sun, but still in bright light. - Keep salad and herbs in partial sun/shade to reduce water loss. Moisture-retention setup: 3) Mulch the pots: - Add a 1–2 inch layer of mulch on top of the soil in each pot. You can use: - Bagged bark mulch - Coconut coir chips - Even clean straw or dried leaves, if available - This slows evaporation significantly. 4) Wick or bottle system (choose one method and apply it): I’ll choose the simpler method: watering spikes or bottles. - Use 1–2 plastic bottles (0.5–1 liter) per large pot. - Fill them with water, poke 1–2 tiny holes in the cap with a pin or needle. - Bury each bottle upside down a few inches into the soil, near but not touching the plant stem. - For smaller herb pots, 1 small bottle per pot is enough. - This slowly releases water over several days. Right before you leave: 5) Final check: - Confirm soil is moist under the mulch. - Ensure bottles are filled and stable. - Tie any tall plants more securely so wind won’t topple them. When you return: 6) Recovery: - Water deeply on the first day back. - Remove any dead or heavily damaged leaves. - Resume normal routine. With this setup, a one-week summer absence is manageable for a balcony container garden. You don’t need to arrange a plant-sitter unless the heat is extreme (multi-day heatwave above mid-90s°F / mid-30s°C).
