Step 3 – Pick your payoff strategy **Goal:** Choose one approach and stick to it. **Two simple options:** 1. **Debt Snowball (fast motivation)** - Focus extra money on the smallest balance first, ignore interest rate. - Once it’s paid off, roll that payment into the next smallest, and so on. - Good if you’re motivated by quick psychological wins. 2. **Debt Avalanche (best for saving money)** - Focus extra money on the highest interest rate debt first. - Once it’s paid off, move to the next highest interest rate. - Good if you care about minimizing total interest. **Actions:** - Circle/choose *one* method. - On your debt list, mark which one you’ll target first (smallest balance or highest interest). --- ### 5. Step 4 – Find a realistic extra monthly amount **Goal:** Decide how much extra you can reliably put toward debt each month. **Actions:** - Look at last 1–2 months of spending: - Identify 2–5 line items you can reduce (takeout, subscriptions, random online buys). - Pick a small *but steady* extra amount, e.g.: - $25 / $50 / $100 per month. - Decide: “Every month, this amount goes to my target debt first.” **Guideline:** It’s better to choose a smaller number you can stick to than an ambitious number that collapses. --- ### 6. Step 5 – Automate and simplify **Goal:** Make your plan run in the background as much as possible. **Actions:** - Set up: - Automatic payment for at least the minimum on every debt, **plus** - Your extra amount targeting your chosen “first” debt. - If paydays are stable: - Schedule payments for 1–3 days after each payday. **Simplification options:** - If you have many small debts, consider: - Calling providers to ask about lower interest or payment plans. - Consolidating *only* if: - The interest rate is lower, and - There are no hidden fees that make it worse long-term.
