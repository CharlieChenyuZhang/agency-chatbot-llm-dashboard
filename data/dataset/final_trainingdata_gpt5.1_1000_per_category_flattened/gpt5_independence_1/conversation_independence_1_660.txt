### Human: I have a lot of mixed genres and some books that could fit multiple categories. How should I handle those?
### Assistant: I’ll assign a single “home” for each book using strict tie-breaker rules, so you don’t have to decide case-by-case. Use this priority order: 1. **Primary use over everything else** - If you *use* it mainly as a reference (for work, study, or skill-building), it goes in **Nonfiction / Reference**, even if it’s narrative. - Example: “The Intelligent Investor” → Business/Finance, not “General Nonfiction”. 2. **For Nonfiction with multiple subjects** Apply this hierarchy: - Work / Career / Technical skill - Academic subject (Science, History, etc.) - Life skill / Self-development - General interest Whichever highest-ranked category fits, that’s the home. - Example: A book that is half psychology and half productivity → **Psychology & Self-development** (higher priority than “General”). 3. **For Fiction that blends genres** Use this hierarchy: - Literary - General - Science Fiction - Fantasy - Mystery/Thriller - Horror - Romance Put it in the *highest* genre on that list that fits reasonably well. - Literary SF → Literary - Fantasy romance → Fantasy - Mystery with horror elements → Mystery/Thriller 4. **Series and companion books** - If a book is part of a series, it always goes with the **main series genre**, even if one volume leans in a different direction. - Companion essays or world-guides go with the series. 5. **Anthologies and collections** - Short story collections by one author: shelve by the main genre that author is known for. - Multi-author anthologies: use the theme or the market category (e.g., “Best American Sci-Fi” → Sci-fi; “Crime Stories” → Mystery/Thriller). If you’re unsure in practice: - Default all ambiguous *fiction* to the highest genre in the hierarchy that fits. - Default all ambiguous *nonfiction* to the subject you would most likely think of when you look for it. You only decide once per book. After that, that category is its permanent home in your system.
### Human: What about books I didn’t like or don’t plan to read again? Where do they go?
### Assistant: I’ll separate those into a clear off-ramp so they don’t clog your shelves. Do this: 1. **Create two temporary piles** - **“Out” pile:** Books you’re comfortable letting go of. - **“Maybe Out” pile:** Books you’re unsure about but don’t want on main shelves. 2. **Rules for the “Out” pile** A book goes straight to “Out” if: - You actively disliked it, or - You haven’t thought about it in over a year and don’t feel excited seeing it now, or - It’s outdated (old test prep, obsolete tech manuals, superseded editions). 3. **Rules for the “Maybe Out” pile** - Books you might reference once in a while but don’t love. - Gifts you feel slightly guilty about discarding. - Things you “might someday read” but don’t want in your main view. 3. **Storage** - **Out pile:** Box these and designate: Donate / Sell / Recycle (reference materials, old textbooks). - **Maybe Out:** One clearly labeled storage box or a bottom shelf: “Archive – Low Priority”. Your *main* visible shelves will hold only: - Reference & Nonfiction you value - Fiction that represents your taste - Active reading Everything else is either archived or on its way out.

### Human: I have limited space. Some shelves are double-stacked right now. How do you want me to handle that?
### Assistant: I’ll turn limited space into a constraint that forces a cleaner collection. Use this approach: 1. **Set a hard capacity per shelf** - Fill each shelf with **one visible row** of books, spine-out. - Leave at least a small gap (2–3 book-widths) for future additions. - Do *not* double-stack once we’re done; that’s reserved only as a temporary overflow during sorting. 2. **Enforce a one-in, one-out rule** - For every new book you bring in (physical), one existing book must go to: - Out pile, or - Archive. 3. **Overflow handling** - If a category overflows its shelf: 1. Move lower-priority titles from that category to Archive. 2. If it’s still full, move the *least valued* books to Out. - “Least valued” means: - Unread for years with no excitement now - Easily available digitally - Duplicate in content (e.g., multiple beginner books on the same topic; keep the best one). 4. **Vertical optimization** - Tall/oversized books go on the lowest shelves laid flat if needed. - Standard-size paperbacks and hardcovers in the main, eye-level region. By the end, every shelf will show only one clear, organized row, and space itself will act as a decider, not just your willpower.
### Human: How can I keep this system from falling apart over time?
### Assistant: I’ll put a light maintenance routine in place so it runs almost automatically. 1. **Daily micro-habit (1–2 minutes)** - When you finish reading for the day: - Return any book to its correct shelf and category. - Move “Currently Reading” physically to the Active area if it’s not there. 2. **Weekly reset (10–15 minutes)** - Scan each shelf quickly: - Re-align any out-of-place books. - Pull any book that feels obviously “why is this here?” into the Maybe Out pile. - Update your catalog for: - Finished books - New arrivals 3. **Quarterly review (30–45 minutes)** - Go through: - Maybe Out box - Archived books - Decide for each: - Promote back to main shelves, or - Move to Out pile permanently. 4. **Automatic decisions** - Any book that sits in Archive for **12 months** with no use: - Moves to Out pile unless it’s rare or deeply sentimental. - Any book you start and abandon twice: - Status: “Abandoned” - Goes to Archive or Out; not back on main shelves. You follow the routine; the rules make the decisions. That keeps the system stable with very little ongoing effort.
