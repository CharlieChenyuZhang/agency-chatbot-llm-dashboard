Title: How to Plan a Week You’ll Actually Follow Subject line options: - “A 10-minute way to plan your week (that you’ll actually follow)” - “Stop over-planning your week” - “Your week, but more realistic” --- Hey, This week we’re going to fix a common trap: planning a “perfect” week you’ll never actually live. ## 1. The idea: Plan for the real you, not the imaginary you Most weekly plans fail because they’re built for an imaginary version of you who: - Never gets interrupted - Has unlimited energy - Magically stays motivated So by Wednesday, the plan is broken and you abandon it. Instead, you’ll build a **realistic** week that: - Assumes interruptions - Protects a small amount of focused time - Leaves room for admin and “life stuff” The rule: > Plan only 60–70% of your time. Leave 30–40% unplanned. That empty space is what keeps your plan alive when things change. ## 2. The tactic: 10-minute weekly planning ritual Do this once a week (I recommend Sunday evening or Monday morning). **Step 1: List your “must-moves” (3–5 items)** Ask: “If these 3–5 things move forward this week, I’ll be satisfied.” Examples: - Finish first draft of [project] - Have 1:1 with [person] - Clear backlog of 10 old emails - Go to the gym 2 times Write them down. That’s your “must moves” list. **Step 2: Block 3–5 focus sessions for them** You’re going to use your focus habit from last week. - Choose 3–5 slots of 45–60 minutes across the week - Put one must-move task into each slot - Put them on your calendar as appointments with yourself These are non-negotiable unless something serious happens. **Step 3: Contain the clutter** Pick **one** daily “admin block” of 20–40 minutes for: - Email - Messages - Small tasks - Forms / paperwork Schedule it roughly at the same time each day. Outside that block, you don’t chase admin endlessly—you collect it and process it during that time. **Step 4: Leave breathing room** You are not allowed to fill more than 70% of your week with planned tasks and meetings. If your calendar is already overloaded: - Protect your 3–5 focus sessions first - Let less important work be handled in the remaining space - Be okay with some things not happening this week Your plan is successful if: - Your must-moves advanced - You didn’t feel overwhelmed every day - You roughly followed the plan, even if imperfectly ## 3. Your reflection for the week After you try this for one week, ask yourself: > “What was the smallest change I made to my planning that had the biggest effect?” Examples you might notice: - “Just having 3 focus sessions changed everything.” - “One daily admin block made email less stressful.” - “Planning less made me feel more in control.” Keep that insight and bake it into every future week.
