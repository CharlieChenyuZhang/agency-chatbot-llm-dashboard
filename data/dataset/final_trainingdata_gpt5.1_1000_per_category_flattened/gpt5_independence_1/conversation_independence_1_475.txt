That energy is nice, but it doesn’t last. A better rule: **start so small it almost feels silly**. For example: - 10–15 minutes of walking most days. - 10–15 minutes of simple home exercises 2–3 times a week. That might not sound like much, but if you’re going from almost nothing to something, it’s a huge upgrade. Consistency beats intensity at the beginning. --- ### Step 3: Use this 2-week “out of shape” starter plan Here’s a gentle plan designed for someone who feels deconditioned and busy. No equipment, no gym, no fancy moves. **Week 1** - **Day 1:** - Walk 10 minutes at a comfortable pace. - **Day 2:** - Rest or light movement (stretching, casual walking). - **Day 3:** - Walk 10–15 minutes. - **Day 4:** - 10-minute home workout: - 5 bodyweight squats (or sit down/stand up from a chair) - 5 wall push-ups - 10 marching in place - Rest 1–2 minutes - Repeat 2 more times - **Day 5:** - Rest or light stretching. - **Day 6:** - Walk 15 minutes. - **Day 7:** - Rest. **Week 2** - **Day 1:** - Walk 15 minutes. - **Day 2:** - Same 10-minute home workout from Week 1. - **Day 3:** - Rest or light movement. - **Day 4:** - Walk 15–20 minutes. - **Day 5:** - Same 10-minute home workout. - **Day 6:** - Rest. - **Day 7:** - Gentle walk (10–15 minutes) or full rest if you’re tired. That’s normal. Instead, pay attention to: - Are the stairs a little easier? - Do you feel slightly less stiff in the morning? - Do you fall asleep faster? - Do you have a tiny bit more energy in the afternoon? These are real, important signs of progress, especially when you’re coming from “out of shape”. --- ### Step 5: Remove as much friction as possible Make starting easy: - **Pick a time** you’re most likely to stick with (before work, lunch, or right after you get home). - **Lay out clothes** in advance so you’re not hunting for them. - **Choose a default workout** (like the 10-minute routine above) so you’re not deciding what to do every time. The fewer decisions you have to make, the more likely you are to follow through. --- ### Step 6: Expect setbacks (and keep going anyway) You will miss days. You’ll have weeks where life explodes and your routine falls apart. That’s not failure—it’s normal. The real skill isn’t “never falling off”; it’s learning how to get back on track quickly: - If you miss one workout, ignore the guilt and just do the next one. - If you miss a whole week, restart Week 1 of the plan. No punishment workouts, no starting from scratch mentally. --- ### Your next step: one tiny promise You don’t need a complete life overhaul to start getting in better shape. You just need one tiny, honest promise to yourself. Pick one of these and start today: - Walk 10 minutes. - Do the 10-minute home workout. - Stretch for 5–10 minutes before bed. That’s it. That’s your start. You don’t have to “feel fit” before you deserve to take care of your body. You start exactly where you are, with what you have, and you build “everyday stronger” one small session at a time.
