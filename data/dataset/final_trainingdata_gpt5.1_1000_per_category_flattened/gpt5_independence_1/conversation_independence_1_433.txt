### Human: Help me increase bench/squat/deadlift PRs
### Assistant: You’ll use a simple 12-week strength-focused program built around bench, squat, and deadlift, training 4 days per week. I’ll outline your split, set/rep schemes, and progression so you can start immediately. Weekly structure: - Day 1: Heavy Squat + Bench - Day 2: Heavy Deadlift + Upper Accessories - Day 3: Volume Squat + Bench - Day 4: Volume Deadlift + Upper/Back Training maxes: Use ~90% of your true 1RM as your “training max” (TM). If your bench 1RM is 100 kg, your TM is 90 kg. All percentages are based on TM to keep fatigue manageable and progress steady. Weeks 1–4 (Base Strength) - Day 1 – Heavy Squat + Bench - Squat: 5×3 @ 80–82.5% TM - Bench: 5×3 @ 80–82.5% TM - Squat accessories: 3×6–8 leg press or hack squat, 3×8–10 hamstring curls - Core: 3×10–15 hanging leg raises - Day 2 – Heavy Deadlift + Upper - Deadlift: 5×3 @ 80–82.5% TM - Row (barbell or chest-supported): 4×6–8 - Weighted pull-ups or pulldowns: 4×6–10 - Rear delts: 3×15–20 face pulls - Day 3 – Volume Squat + Bench - Squat: 4×5 @ 72.5–75% TM - Bench: 4×5 @ 72.5–75% TM - Single-leg work: 3×8–10 Bulgarian split squats or lunges - Triceps: 3×8–12 dips or close-grip push-ups - Day 4 – Volume Deadlift + Upper - Deadlift (preferably RDL or paused deadlift): 4×5 @ ~70–75% of deadlift TM - Overhead press (barbell or DB): 4×6–8 - Lat work: 3×10–12 (rows or pulldowns) - Biceps: 3×8–12 curls Progression weeks 1–4: - If you hit all sets/reps with solid form on a lift, add 2.5 kg (upper) or 5 kg (lower) the next week. - If you miss, repeat the same weight next week and focus on tighter form and rest. Weeks 5–8 (Intensity Phase) Shift toward heavier triples and doubles. - Day 1 – Heavy Squat + Bench - Squat: 5×2 @ 85–87.5% TM - Bench: 5×2 @ 85–87.5% TM - Slightly reduce accessories (2 sets each) to keep fatigue down. - Day 2 – Heavy Deadlift + Upper - Deadlift: 5×2 @ 85–87.5% TM - Keep rows/pull-ups, but 3 sets instead of 4. - Day 3 – Volume Squat + Bench - Squat: 3×4 @ ~77.5–80% TM - Bench: 3×4 @ ~77.5–80% TM - Keep single-leg and triceps (2–3 sets). - Day 4 – Volume Deadlift + Upper - Deadlift variation: 3×4 @ ~75–77.5% of deadlift TM - OHP + back work (3 sets each) Progression weeks 5–8: - Same rule: hit all reps → add 2.5/5 kg next week; if not, repeat. Weeks 9–11 (Peaking) You’ll peak for new PR attempts in week 12. - Day 1 – Squat + Bench heavy singles & back-off - Work up to 3–5 singles @ 90–93% TM (clean, fast, no grinders) - Then 2×3 @ ~80% TM - Minimal accessories (1–2 light sets) - Day 2 – Deadlift heavy singles & back-off - Same pattern: 3–5 singles @ 90–93% TM - Then 2×3 @ ~80% TM - A bit of upper back work, 2–3 light sets - Day 3 – Light technique Squat + Bench - 3×3 each @ 70–75% TM, focus on speed, perfect form - Day 4 – Light Deadlift + upper - 3×3 @ 70–75% deadlift TM - Light back/shoulder work Week 12 (Test Week) - Day 1: Test Squat 1RM - Day 3: Test Bench 1RM - Day 5: Test Deadlift 1RM Warm up properly, ramp in small jumps, and hit 2–3 heavy attempts per lift. Non-negotiables: - Rest 2–4 minutes between working sets (big lifts), 1–2 minutes for accessories. - Sleep: minimum ~7 hours per night. - Protein: ~1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight daily; eat at least maintenance, preferably slight surplus. - Film your top sets occasionally to check depth, bar path, and control. You can start this structure immediately; you don’t need further adjustments before running the first cycle.

Training days: e.g., Monday, Wednesday, Friday. ### Human: How many warm-up sets should I do before my working sets?
### Assistant: Use a fixed warm-up structure based on your working weight. Here’s a simple scheme that works consistently: For big lifts (squat/bench/deadlift): - Bar × 2 sets of 5–10 reps - ~40% of working weight × 5 - ~60% × 3 - ~75–80% × 1–2 Then start your working sets. Example (working sets at 100 kg): - Bar × 10 - Bar × 10 - 40 kg × 5 - 60 kg × 3 - 80 kg × 1–2 - Then 100 kg for your prescribed sets. On lighter days (volume @ ~70–75%): - Bar × 10 - 50–60% of that day’s working weight × 5 - 75–80% × 1 Then straight into working sets. Accessories need at most 1–2 lighter sets before hitting your regular sets.
### Human: What about deloads? Should I take any during this program?
### Assistant: You’ll use an automatic deload structure built into the 12 weeks: - Weeks 1–4: Build - Week 5: Slight reset instead of hard deload - Drop load to what you used in week 3 for all main lifts. - Keep volume the same. - Weeks 6–8: Push intensity higher again. - Week 9: Another slight reset - Use ~90–92.5% of the weight you used in week 8 for your intensities. - Weeks 10–11: Peak as planned. - Week 12: Test week functions as a deload after your test days (reduced training volume overall). You don’t need a full week of very light training unless you’re clearly overreached (e.g., sleep ruined, joints very painful, performance dropping for two weeks). With this structure, recovery is already managed.
### Human: I sometimes get lower back pain when deadlifting. How should I adjust?
### Assistant: You’ll keep deadlifts in, but modify to protect your lower back while still driving strength. Here’s the adjustment: - Use conventional or sumo stance, whichever allows the most neutral spine with the least discomfort; if unsure, stick to the stance that feels more stable. - Replace some straight-bar deadlifts with RDLs or trap-bar deadlifts on volume days. New deadlift structure: - Heavy day: - Week 1–4: 4×3 instead of 5×3 @ 80–82.5% TM - Week 5–8: 4×2 instead of 5×2 @ 85–87.5% TM - Volume day: - RDLs: 3×6–8 @ ~65–70% of deadlift TM OR trap-bar deadlifts 3×5–6 @ ~70–75% of trap-bar 1RM. Add: - Back extensions or reverse hypers: 3×10–15 (2x/week) - Core: 3×10–15 anti-flexion/anti-rotation work (e.g., planks, dead bugs, Pallof presses). If pain is sharp, radiating, or worsening, remove heavy deadlifts entirely for 2–3 weeks and use only RDLs/light trap bar + rehab/core work, then ramp back in gradually.
### Human: What should I eat on training days vs rest days?
### Assistant: You’ll run a simple nutrition strategy that supports strength without overcomplication. General targets: - Protein: 1.6–2.2 g/kg bodyweight daily (same on all days). - Calories: - Training days: slight surplus, about +200–300 kcal above maintenance. - Rest days: at or just slightly above maintenance (+0–100 kcal). Macro split (approximate): - Protein: 25–30% of calories - Fat: 20–30% of calories - Carbs: remainder (typically 40–55%) Training days: - Pre-workout (60–120 min before): - Protein: 25–40 g - Carbs: 40–80 g (easily digestible: rice, oats, bread, fruit) - Low fat for easier digestion. - Post-workout (within 2 hours): - Protein: 25–40 g - Carbs: 50–100 g - Include some salt and fluids. Rest days: - Keep protein the same. - Slightly lower carbs and increase fats a bit for satiety. - Keep fiber high (vegetables, fruits, whole grains). Hydration: - ~30–40 ml of water per kg of bodyweight per day as a baseline. - Add electrolytes (or some salty food) around training. Use this pattern consistently; no cycling or special “bulking” tricks are needed beyond this.

### Human: How should I warm up for squats specifically to protect my knees?
### Assistant: You’ll use a short, structured squat warm-up to protect your knees and improve performance: 1) General warm-up – 5–8 minutes - Light bike, brisk walk, or easy row. 2) Dynamic mobility – 5–7 minutes Do 1–2 sets of 8–12 reps each: - Bodyweight squats (controlled, full ROM) - Leg swings (front-to-back and side-to-side) - Lunges or split squats - Hip circles or controlled hip openers - Ankle rocks (knee over toe against a wall) 3) Specific squat warm-up (before working sets) Using your squat working weight for the day: - Empty bar × 2 sets of 8–10 (focus on depth, bracing, knee tracking over toes) - ~40% of working weight × 5 - ~60% × 3 - ~75–80% × 1–2 Then start your programmed sets. Additional knee-friendly cues: - Keep knees tracking in line with your toes; don’t let them cave in. - Control the descent; no crash-bouncing in the hole. - Use proper footwear (flat, stable shoes or lifting shoes; avoid soft running shoes). Use this exact sequence for each squat day; it’s short, effective, and joint-friendly.
### Human: I might compete in powerlifting next year. How should I adjust this program?
### Assistant: You’ll shift this into a competition-oriented approach while keeping the core structure. Adjustments: 1) Technique specificity - Always use competition-style movements for your main lifts: - Squat: consistent stance, depth below parallel, pause 1 second at the top before re-racking. - Bench: competition pause (~1 second) on the chest for at least your top sets each session. - Deadlift: reset each rep (no touch-and-go), full lockout with shoulders back and hips extended. 2) Add practice for commands in peaking phase Weeks 9–12: - Have a training partner or yourself mentally simulate: - Squat: “Squat” / “Rack” - Bench: “Start” / “Press” / “Rack” - Deadlift: “Down” Pause and hold positions as you would on the platform. 3) Load selection for Week 12 (mock meet) - Day 1: Squat test - Attempt 1: ~90–92% of current best - Attempt 2: ~97–100% - Attempt 3: 102–105% if 2nd was solid - Day 3: Bench test, same structure - Day 5: Deadlift test, same structure 4) Practice meet day structure every 2–3 months - Run a “mock meet” day: squat, bench, deadlift in one session with proper warm-up, attempts, and rest between lifts. - Use slightly conservative attempts to practice execution more than maximal strain. 5) Track bodyweight and weight class - Decide on your likely weight class. - Stay within ~2–3% of your intended class; avoid big cuts until you’ve built more strength. Run 2–3 cycles of this program over the next year, with the peaking block aligned so week 12 lands 1–2 weeks before your planned competition, then lightly taper into the meet.
