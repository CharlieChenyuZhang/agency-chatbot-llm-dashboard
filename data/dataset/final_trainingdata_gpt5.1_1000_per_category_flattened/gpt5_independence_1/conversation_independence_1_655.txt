### Human: Why did you pick a dynamic mic instead of a condenser?
### Assistant: I chose a dynamic mic specifically to prioritize real-world usability over theoretical maximum detail. Key reasons for the dynamic choice (SM7B) over a condenser: 1) Room noise and reflections - Dynamic mics like the SM7B are less sensitive to room reflections, computer fans, HVAC noise, and street noise. - Condensers capture more detail but also expose every flaw in an untreated or semi-treated room. In most home setups, the dynamic will produce a cleaner, more controlled recording. 2) Proximity and tone control - The SM7B handles close-miking very well, giving a rich, intimate vocal tone without harshness. - It tolerates strong plosives and loud speaking/singing better than many condensers. 3) Versatility for non-ideal technique - If you move slightly off-axis or vary in distance, the SM7B is more forgiving. - Condensers often sound inconsistent with less disciplined mic technique. 4) Handling high SPL and aggressive delivery - If you ever shout, belt, or get very animated, the SM7B handles it easily with minimal distortion. - Many condensers can sound harsh or overloaded in that scenario without careful gain staging and additional treatment. 5) Typical user environment - Without being told otherwise, I optimize for a home/desk or small-room setup, not a fully treated vocal booth. For that environment, a dynamic like the SM7B is the safer and more consistently good choice. If you later move into a well-treated studio space and want maximum detail, I’d layer in a large-diaphragm condenser as a second option while keeping the SM7B for less ideal rooms or loud sources.
