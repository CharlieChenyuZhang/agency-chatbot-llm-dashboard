Potting mix - Use only bagged potting mix, labeled for containers. - Fill each container nearly to the top, leaving about 1–2 inches (2–5 cm) of space. 3. Sun and placement - Place the tomato and zucchini/pepper in the sunniest spots. - Put lettuce and radishes where they get sun but not scorching all afternoon (they tolerate some shade). - Herbs are flexible; tuck them into remaining spots with at least a few hours of light. 5. Balcony-friendly care - Water more frequently than in-ground beds: likely every day or every other day in warm weather. - Ensure containers have drainage holes and water can’t pool on the balcony. - Use a slow-release fertilizer pellet or spike in each pot once at the start, then a light liquid feed every 3–4 weeks. This will give you a compact, productive balcony garden without overloading the space. ---
### Human: Can you make me a simple schedule so I know what to do each week?
### Assistant: I’ll give you a straightforward 8-week schedule for your balcony garden, assuming you’re starting in spring in a temperate climate. You can repeat the later weeks as a routine. **Week 1 – Setup & Planting** - Buy containers, potting mix, plants, and seeds. - Fill containers and balcony box with potting mix. - Plant: tomatoes, basil, parsley, chives, mint, flowers (seedlings). - Sow: lettuce and radish seeds in the balcony box. - Water thoroughly after planting. **Week 2 – Establishment** - Check soil daily; water when the top inch is dry. - Lightly thin radishes and lettuce if they’re overcrowded (leave a bit of space between seedlings). - Remove any obvious dead/damaged leaves. **Week 3 – First Maintenance** - Continue regular watering. - Top up potting mix if it settled a lot. - Loosen the top layer of soil gently with your fingers if it’s crusted over. **Week 4 – Feeding & Light Pruning** - Add slow-release fertilizer if you haven’t already; otherwise, give a light liquid fertilizer feeding once this week. - Pinch off the very lowest yellowing leaves on tomatoes if any. - Begin harvesting a few lettuce leaves if they’re big enough. **Week 5 – Regular Harvesting** - Harvest lettuce leaves regularly (a little from each plant). - Pull any radishes that look ready (marble–golf-ball size). - Check for pests (chewed leaves, sticky residues) and remove any visible bugs by hand. **Week 6 – Support & Shape** - Add a small stake or cage for your tomato if it’s getting tall. - Pinch back any very long, spindly basil stems to encourage bushiness (use the trimmings in cooking). - Re-sow radishes or a bit more lettuce in any empty spots. **Week 7 – Ongoing Care** - Continue watering, harvesting lettuce and radishes, and checking for pests. - Light liquid feed again this week. - Start harvesting early tomatoes if they’re ripening. **Week 8 and onward – Routine** Each week: - Water consistently. - Harvest what’s ready. - Check leaves for problems and remove any badly damaged ones. - Feed lightly every 3–4 weeks. - Re-sow quick crops (lettuce, radishes) into any free spaces. Use this as your default routine and keep repeating the later weeks through the growing season. ---

### Human: I’m worried I’ll forget to water. What should I do?
### Assistant: I’ll design your setup and routines to make watering almost automatic and hard to forget. 1. Use self-watering containers where possible - Choose a couple of self-watering planters (with a water reservoir at the bottom) for your thirstiest plants: tomatoes and zucchini/peppers. - Fill the reservoir fully whenever it’s low; the plants will take what they need. 2. Standard containers: moisture retention - Mix some water-retaining material (like coco coir or a moisture-control potting mix) into your regular pots. - Add a thin layer of mulch (fine bark, straw, or even decorative stones) on top of the soil to slow evaporation. 3. Create automatic reminders - Set repeating phone alarms: - Daily in the morning for a quick check in hot weather. - Every other day in cooler weather. - Name the alarms specifically (e.g., “Check balcony plants”). 4. Use a simple visual system - Put a small note or sticker by your door at eye level: “Check plants before leaving.” - Keep the watering can filled and in a visible spot near the balcony door, so it reminds you. 5. Quick check method - When your reminder goes off, do this: - Stick a finger into the soil of 2–3 key pots (tomato, lettuce box, one herb). - If the top inch is dry, water that pot until a bit of water drains from the bottom. - This takes just a couple of minutes and becomes a habit. Combined—self-watering pots, moisture-retentive mix, and simple reminders—will dramatically reduce both the frequency and the chance of forgetting. ---
