Chocolate chip cookies originated in the United States in the 1930s and are widely credited to Ruth Graves Wakefield, who ran the Toll House Inn in Whitman, Massachusetts. She developed the recipe by adding chopped bits of a Nestlé semisweet chocolate bar to cookie dough, expecting them to melt and disperse; instead, they held their shape, creating chunks of chocolate throughout the cookie. The recipe became popular, was printed on Nestlé chocolate packaging, and evolved into the standard chocolate chip cookie recipe known today, with countless variations in texture, size, and mix-ins.
