### Human: Maybe later. I really just want to know how to tell if a therapist is “good” when I talk to them. Can you help with that?
### Assistant: Focusing on that is smart—“good” isn’t about their degrees alone, it’s about how they work with *you*. Instead of vague advice, I’d rather you use a short checklist and ask a few very specific questions. For a first consultation call, I’d prioritize these three areas: 1. Do they use an approach that matches what you want? Ask: - “How do you typically work with anxiety and stress?” - “Do you use structured methods like CBT, ACT, or homework between sessions?” 2. Do they give you a clear plan? Ask: - “After we talk for a session or two, how will we decide on goals?” - “How will we know if it’s working?” 3. Do you feel listened to and not dismissed? During the call, notice: - Do they interrupt you a lot? - Do they explain things in a way you actually understand? - Do they ask concrete questions about your life, not just nod and say “mm-hmm”? If a therapist: - Can’t explain how they’d approach your anxiety, - Gives you no sense of a plan, - Or you feel talked over or confused, I’d move on and try another. I’d rather see you do 2–3 short consults than stick with the first person you meet just to “have a therapist.” After your first call with someone, you can tell me how it went, and I can help you interpret the red/green flags based on what you describe.
