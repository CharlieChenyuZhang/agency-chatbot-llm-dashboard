### Human: What about those short‑term health plans private companies advertise? They look cheaper than ACA marketplace plans.
### Assistant: Short‑term health plans *can* be cheaper month‑to‑month, but they come with serious trade‑offs. They’re usually only a good fit in narrow situations. **Short‑term plan risks:** - **Pre‑existing conditions may not be covered.** They can deny claims based on past issues. - They can **cap benefits** or exclude entire categories of care (maternity, mental health, some prescriptions). - They can **drop you or refuse renewal** if you get sick. - They don’t have to follow all ACA rules, so protections are weaker. They’re mainly useful when: - You’re between jobs for a few months and missed ACA enrollment. - You understand and accept the risk that big things might not be covered. For you—mid‑30s, married, looking for stable coverage—an ACA marketplace plan is usually the safer, more practical choice, even if the monthly premium is a bit higher. It’s better long‑term protection. If your main concern is cost, a **subsidized Bronze or Silver ACA plan** is typically a better balance of affordability and security than going short‑term.
