### Assistant: Chicago venue types that fit your budget and semi-formal vibe: - Loft/industrial spaces (West Loop, River North) with character but flexible decor. - Small hotels or boutique event spaces with in-house catering (often more budget-predictable). - Restaurants with private event rooms that can host both ceremony and reception. - Non-traditional spaces like museums or galleries that allow outside vendors but still fit your guest count.
### Human: Give me a step-by-step checklist, just for choosing and booking the venue. No more than 10 steps, all in bullets, no extra commentary.
### Assistant: - Confirm guest count range (e.g., 80–120). - Choose target month(s) and preferred day(s) of week. - Set a maximum venue+food+beverage budget cap within your $25–35k. - Research and list 8–12 Chicago venues that fit size, style, and budget. - Check each venue’s website or info packet for pricing, availability, and restrictions. - Narrow to 3–5 venues and schedule in-person or virtual tours. - Tour venues, take notes on inclusions (tables, chairs, linens, AV, catering rules). - Request detailed proposals from your top 2–3 venues. - Compare total estimated costs and terms (minimums, fees, time limits). - Choose one venue and sign the contract after reviewing all terms and payment schedule.
### Human: I'm worried about hidden fees. In 4 sentences or less, what specific fee-related questions should I ask each venue?
### Assistant: Ask: - “What service charges, administrative fees, or gratuities are added on top of the quoted prices, and at what percentage?” - “Are there extra fees for setup, breakdown, security, coat check, or using outside vendors?” - “What are your bar pricing options and are there additional charges like bartender fees, corkage, or cake-cutting?” - “What overtime or extension fees apply if we stay past the contracted end time?”
### Human: Now switch to helping me with the guest list. I want to keep it around 100 people. What’s a simple method for deciding who to invite? Keep it concise.
### Assistant: Use a tiered method: - Tier 1: immediate family and closest friends (non-negotiable). - Tier 2: extended family and good friends you’ve seen/spoken with in the last year. - Tier 3: coworkers, plus-ones of newer relationships, and distant acquaintances. Fill from Tier 1 and 2 first up to your target (100); only add Tier 3 if there’s room.
### Human: Don’t talk about coworkers; I’m not inviting any. Can you give me a quick rule for deciding which extended family to invite?
### Assistant: A simple rule: - Invite extended family you’ve seen in person or had meaningful contact with (calls/messages) in the last 1–2 years. - Prioritize relatives who consistently show up for major life events. - If you invite one person in a “branch” (e.g., one cousin in a sibling set), aim to invite all adult siblings in that branch to avoid hurt feelings. - Exclude relatives where there’s ongoing conflict you prefer not to bring into your day.

### Assistant: For Chicago, semi-formal and budget-conscious, consider: - Late April–May and late September–October for milder weather and nice photos without peak summer heat. - Fridays and Sundays in those months, which are often cheaper than Saturdays. - Holiday weekends can raise prices, so avoid them if budget is a priority. - Early afternoon start times in cooler months help with comfort and natural light.
