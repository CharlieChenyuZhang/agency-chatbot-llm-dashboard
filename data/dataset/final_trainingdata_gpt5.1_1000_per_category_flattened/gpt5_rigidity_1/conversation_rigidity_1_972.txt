### Human: Using only Wikipedia, give me a detailed explanation of the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis.
### Assistant: According to Wikipedia, the light-dependent reactions (also called the light reactions) are the first stage of photosynthesis, in which light energy is converted into chemical energy in the form of ATP and NADPH. These reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplasts in plants and algae (and in analogous membrane systems in photosynthetic bacteria). In oxygenic photosynthesis, the process begins when photons are absorbed by pigment–protein complexes called photosystems, primarily Photosystem II (PSII) and Photosystem I (PSI). In PSII, light energy excites electrons in chlorophyll molecules, raising them to a higher energy level. These high-energy electrons are transferred to a primary electron acceptor and then pass through an electron transport chain embedded in the thylakoid membrane. As electrons move down this electron transport chain from PSII toward PSI, their energy is used to pump protons (H⁺ ions) from the stroma into the thylakoid lumen. This proton pumping creates an electrochemical proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane. ATP synthase, another membrane protein complex, uses the energy stored in this proton gradient to synthesize ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate; this process is called photophosphorylation. Meanwhile, the electrons that left PSII must be replaced. According to Wikipedia, this occurs through the splitting (photolysis) of water molecules at the oxygen-evolving complex associated with PSII. Water is oxidized, providing electrons back to PSII, releasing protons into the lumen (contributing further to the proton gradient) and producing molecular oxygen (O₂) as a byproduct. The electrons that move through the cytochrome b₆f complex eventually reach PSI, where they are again excited by light absorbed by PSI’s antenna pigments. The re-energized electrons are transferred to another series of carriers and finally to the protein ferredoxin. From ferredoxin, the electrons are used to reduce NADP⁺ to NADPH via the enzyme ferredoxin–NADP⁺ reductase. The ATP and NADPH produced by these light-dependent reactions are then used in the light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle) to fix carbon dioxide into carbohydrates. Wikipedia also notes that there are both linear and cyclic electron flow pathways. Linear (non-cyclic) electron flow involves both PSII and PSI and leads to the formation of both ATP and NADPH while releasing O₂. Cyclic electron flow involves only PSI and the cytochrome b₆f complex, cycling electrons back to PSI and increasing ATP production without producing NADPH or O₂. This allows the cell to adjust the ATP/NADPH ratio to match the demands of the carbon fixation reactions.
