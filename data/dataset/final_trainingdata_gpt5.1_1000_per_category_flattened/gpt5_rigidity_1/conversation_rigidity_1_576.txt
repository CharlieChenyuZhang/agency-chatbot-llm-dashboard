Home. Rani was waiting in the narrow corridor, one hand braced on the bulkhead, black curls twisted into a knot at the nape of her neck. Jace flicked a holo into being between us. Rings, orbitals, shipping lanes—an insane chalkboard only he understood at a glance. He tapped a pulsing cluster near one of the minor moons. “Old refinery hull,” he said. “Stripped, supposedly. Rumor says the crew left in a hurry. Rumor also says the registry’s glitchy. Could be nothing. Could be a jackpot.” “Or a trap,” I said. “Or that,” he allowed. “Good thing we brought you.” I pulled the harness over my shoulders and locked it. Outside, Saturn turned slowly, a giant eye half-lidded by rings. The smear of the distant storm was still there, a faint bruise on the horizon of rock and ice. I told myself it was nothing.
