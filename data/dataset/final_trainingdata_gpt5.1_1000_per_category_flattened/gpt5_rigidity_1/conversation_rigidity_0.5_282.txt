### Human: Quick knife tips first. I’m always scared I’m going to cut myself.
### Assistant: Let’s keep it practical and minimal: 1. **Claw grip for your non-knife hand** - Curl your fingertips slightly in, so your **knuckles** (not fingertips) touch the side of the knife. - Your knuckles act as a “guard,” so the blade can’t reach your fingertips if it moves up and down against them. 2. **Choke up on the knife handle** - Instead of holding the knife at the very end, pinch the blade where it meets the handle with your thumb and index finger, and wrap the rest of your fingers around the handle. - This gives much better control and feels less wobbly. 3. **Flat surfaces for stability** - For onions, mushrooms, etc.: cut them in half first, then put the **flat side down** on the board before slicing. Less rolling = fewer accidents. 4. **Go slow and small** - It’s better to cut **slowly and consciously** than quickly and carelessly. Speed comes later. I can’t physically show you, but if you want visual reinforcement later, search for “basic claw grip knife skills beginner” on video platforms—that matches what I just described. Ready for the step-by-step pasta instructions? ---
### Human: Yeah, give me the step-by-step, but can you keep it simple? I don’t want like a wall of text I can’t follow while cooking.
### Assistant: Understood. I’ll keep it in **short numbered steps** so you can glance and cook. **Creamy Mushroom Pasta (for ~2 servings)** **Before turning on any heat (prep):** 1. Boil a big pot of water with a good pinch of salt (like salty soup). Leave it covered; you’ll use it soon. 2. Slice mushrooms (about 0.5 cm / ¼ inch thick). 3. Finely chop: - 3–4 garlic cloves - 1 small onion or shallot (if using) 4. Chop a small handful of parsley (if using). 5. Grate about ½ cup of Parmesan. **Cooking:** 6. When the water is boiling, add pasta. Set a timer for the time on the package **minus 1 minute**. Stir once so it doesn’t stick. 7. While pasta cooks: - Put your pan on medium heat. - Add 1–2 tbsp olive oil or a mix of oil + 1 tbsp butter. 8. Add mushrooms to the pan in a single-ish layer. Let them cook without stirring much for 2–3 minutes; then stir occasionally until they’re browned and have shrunk (total ~6–8 minutes). 9. Add onion/shallot (if using). Cook 2–3 minutes until soft and slightly golden. 10. Lower heat slightly. Add garlic. Cook 30–60 seconds, stirring, until fragrant (don’t let it brown). 11. (Optional) Add a small splash of white wine. Let it bubble for about a minute to reduce. 12. Pour in about ¾ cup of cream (you can add more later if needed). Stir, then let it gently simmer on **low heat** for 2–3 minutes. Add: - A pinch of salt - A few grinds of black pepper - A pinch of red pepper flakes (if you like a little heat) 13. Check the pasta when the timer goes off. Taste a piece: - If it’s just slightly firm (al dente), turn off the heat. - Scoop out **½ cup of the pasta water** and keep it. Then drain the pasta. 14. Add the drained pasta directly into the pan with the sauce. Toss or stir to coat. - Add a splash of the reserved pasta water if it seems too thick. - Add most of the Parmesan, stir until melted and creamy. 15. Taste. Adjust: - More salt/pepper if needed - A tiny squeeze of lemon if it tastes flat (optional) - A bit more cream or pasta water to reach your preferred sauce thickness 16. Serve on plates. Top with remaining Parmesan and chopped parsley. If at any point you feel rushed during cooking, pause the heat (or turn it down) and check with me. You can tell me what step you’re on and what things look like, and I’ll help you adjust. ---
