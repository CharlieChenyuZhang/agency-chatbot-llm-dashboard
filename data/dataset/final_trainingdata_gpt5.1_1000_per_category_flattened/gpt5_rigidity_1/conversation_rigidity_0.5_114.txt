### Human: Okay, that works. Another thing: should I just say “I’m a big fan of your work” even if I don’t actually know their work? I see that all the time and it seems to work.
### Assistant: Saying you’re “a big fan” when you’re not is risky: - If they ask “What did you like most?” you’re stuck. - It undermines trust if they sense it’s generic or untrue. A better approach that’s still positive but honest is to focus on what *is* true: - You just discovered their profile. - You find their role, path, or company interesting. For example: - “I recently came across your profile and your path into data analytics caught my eye.” - “Your role in [Company/Team] looks very interesting to me as someone early in their analytics career.” - “I’m especially curious about how your team uses data to support [business function].” If you genuinely like a specific post, article, or talk, then you can say: - “I really liked your recent post about [topic], especially your point on [specific detail].” That gives you sincerity *and* impact, which tends to build stronger relationships over time.
### Human: Makes sense. How many people should I actually be reaching out to each week then? I was thinking like 200 per day to move fast.
### Assistant: 200 per *day* is likely to: - Trigger LinkedIn’s limits / flags. - Force you into low-quality, low-respect outreach. - Create more connections than you can realistically maintain. Within that: - 5–10 per day to people you really care about (target companies/roles, local leaders, hiring managers). - The rest to broader peers: other analysts, alumni, people in relevant groups. If you want a very simple version: - Pick 15 minutes per day. - In that time, send as many *thoughtful* requests as you can (often 5–15). That pace builds a strong network in a few months without burning you out or damaging your reputation.

### Human: I mostly want referrals. Can I just ask some of them directly for a referral once they accept, even if we’ve never talked before?
### Assistant: You *can* ask, but asking for a referral immediately from someone who doesn’t know you usually: - Gets ignored. - Makes the interaction feel transactional. - Sometimes makes people less willing to help later. A better sequence is: 1. Connect. 2. Have at least one or two short, value-respecting interactions: - Ask a small question. - Show interest in their work. - Share a short example of your own work if relevant. 3. Then, if it feels appropriate, make a *soft* referral request. For example, after an initial exchange: “Thanks again for the insights you shared earlier, [Name]—I found your point about [specific detail] especially helpful. I saw there’s a [Job Title] opening at [Company]. I’m early in my analytics career (2 yrs, SQL/Excel/Python/Power BI in retail), and the role looks like a strong fit with what I’ve been doing. If you feel comfortable and think my background could be relevant, would you be open to either: - Sharing any advice on applying to [Company], or - Potentially referring me, if you think it makes sense? No pressure at all—I completely understand if you prefer not to. Either way, I really appreciate your time and perspective.” This gives them: - Context on who you are. - A clear but low-pressure choice. - An easy way to say no without awkwardness. If you’re in a big hurry, we can design a version that’s still respectful but a bit more direct, though I’d still suggest at least one short “warm-up” message first.
