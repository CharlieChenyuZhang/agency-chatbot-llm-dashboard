### Human: Help me systematically debug complex software issues
### Assistant: I’ll give you a simple, repeatable framework you can apply to almost any complex bug: 1. **Clarify the problem** - Precisely describe what’s wrong: *what* happens, *where*, *how often*, and *since when*. - Collect exact error messages, logs, and screenshots. 2. **Make it reproducible** - Find the **smallest, most reliable** way to trigger the issue. - List exact steps, environment details (OS, versions, configs), and inputs. 3. **Narrow the scope** - Use **binary search** on the system: - Disable or remove half of the components/features; see if the bug still occurs. - Keep halving until you isolate the suspect area (module, service, function). 4. **Form hypotheses** - Based on the narrowed scope, write down 2–3 concrete “it might be X because Y” ideas. - Prioritize by likelihood and ease of testing. 5. **Design focused experiments** - For each hypothesis, design a minimal change or test: - Add targeted logs. - Change one config. - Revert one commit. - Swap one dependency version. - Change **one thing at a time** and record results. 6. **Inspect data, not guesses** - Use logs, traces, metrics, and debugger breakpoints to see **actual states** and flows. - Compare a **working** path with a **broken** path. 7. **Confirm root cause** - You’ve found the root cause only when: - You can reliably make the bug appear and disappear. - You can explain it in plain language. 8. **Fix and guard** - Implement the minimal fix. - Add regression tests and monitoring/alerts if applicable. - Document symptoms, root cause, and fix.
