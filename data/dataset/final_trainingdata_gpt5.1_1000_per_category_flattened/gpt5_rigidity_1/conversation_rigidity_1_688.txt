Section 2: What AI Does Well (and Poorly) AI excels at pattern recognition in large datasets—medical images, lab histories, vital signs—where humans get tired or miss rare combinations. It’s less reliable when data is messy, biased, or doesn’t match the population it was trained on. That’s why an algorithm that works beautifully in one hospital can fail in another with different patients and workflows. The safest deployments keep clinicians in the loop: AI suggests, humans decide. 5. Section 3: A Mini Framework to Judge New AI Tools When you hear about a new AI product in healthcare, ask three questions: - Evidence: Has it been tested in real clinics, not just labs? - Oversight: Who is accountable when it’s wrong—the vendor or the hospital? - Equity: Did they check performance across age, gender, race, and language groups? If a company can’t answer these clearly, the tool may be more marketing than medicine. 6.
