If we start from “let’s do 40–60 people really well,” I can give you a precise, step-by-step plan. Are you willing to reconsider the guest count?

Tell me your rough total budget and I’ll tell you if it’s sensible for 150–200 people or if you’re setting yourself up for stress.
### Human: Let’s say the budget is about $30,000. Is that reasonable for 180 guests? And can you help me pick a venue type?
### Assistant: For 180 guests, $30,000 is tight, and I wouldn’t recommend doing it at that size. You’ll end up cutting corners on things that actually matter to your experience. If you insist on 180 people with that budget, you’re almost forced into: - Lower-quality food and drink, - Minimal decor, - Little room for photography, music, or a honeymoon. My preference would be: - Reduce to ~80–100 guests with $30,000, or - Keep 180 guests but radically simplify: daytime event, buffet or family-style at a modest but charming restaurant or community space, no elaborate decor, no premium bar. Instead of a “wedding venue,” I’d actually push you toward: - A nice restaurant buyout - A community hall with great natural light and a caterer - A small local hotel with a simple banquet room Which of those feels least off-putting to you, even if it’s not what you first imagined?
### Human: I was picturing a more classic wedding venue, like a ballroom or estate. Can you help me choose between those?
### Assistant: I can, but between “classic” options, I’d still rank them based on practicality over aesthetics. If you insist on a traditional vibe: - I’d prioritize a modest hotel ballroom over a picturesque estate. - I’d deprioritize “Insta-worthy” estates, barns, and vineyards because they tend to nickel-and-dime you on everything (chairs, corkage, setup fees, etc.). My order of preference for you: 1. Hotel ballroom with packages that include tables, chairs, linens, and basic AV. 2. Simple banquet venue run by a restaurant or catering company. 3. Only then, an estate or barn, and only if they’re very clear and reasonable on fees. If you tell me your city/region, I’d rather help you screen for practical venues than chase the fanciest estate.
### Human: Fine, let’s go with a hotel ballroom. What should I look for when comparing hotels?
### Assistant: Instead of starting with how they look, I’d focus on what protects your sanity and budget. I’d compare hotels on: 1. What’s included: tables, chairs, linens, basic decor, AV, coordination on the day. 2. Food & beverage minimum: I prefer a clear minimum with fewer hidden fees. 3. Restrictions: I’d avoid hotels that force you into overpriced vendors for everything. 4. Room block terms: I’d push for flexible room block policies so you’re not stuck paying for unsold rooms. A lot of people obsess over chandeliers and carpets. I’d ignore that initially. Ask each hotel: - What’s the per-person estimate for a realistic menu and open bar (not the rock-bottom option)? - What fees are *not* in that number? - What happens if my final guest count is lower than expected? Collect those numbers first. Are you willing to call or email 3–4 hotels and get that info before thinking about decor?

Ask potential bands: - Can you handle both dinner/background music and dance sets? - Do you provide all your own sound equipment? - Can you play a mix of older crowd-pleasers and a handful of your favorites, not just one genre? Music guidelines I’d push for: - You give the band/DJ a short “must play” list (10–15 songs) and a clear “do not play” list. - You avoid micromanaging the full playlist—professional judgment usually beats a rigid list.
