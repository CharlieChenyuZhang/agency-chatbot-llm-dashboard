### Human: Nice. Should I just increase the weight every week on each lift?
### Assistant: Use “increase if earned” rather than “increase no matter what.” That keeps progress steady and protects your back. Simple progression rule: 1) Keep a rep range (e.g., 6–8 reps). 2) If you hit the top of the range for all sets with good form and still 1–2 reps in reserve: - Add a small amount of weight next week (2.5–5 lb / 1–2.5 kg). 3) If you don’t hit the top of the range: - Stay at the same weight and try to add 1 rep per set next time. 4) If form breaks down or your back complains: - Drop the weight slightly next session and rebuild with tighter form. Example for bench (6–8 reps): - Week 1: 60 kg, 4 x 7 - Week 2: Aim for 4 x 8 at 60 kg - Week 3: If you get 4 x 8 easily, go to 62.5 kg and shoot for 6–7 reps - Week 4: Work back toward 4 x 8 at 62.5 kg Across 12 weeks: - Weeks 1–4: Mostly adding reps and small weight jumps. - Weeks 5–7: Higher loads, fewer rep jumps; sometimes you’ll repeat the same weight to consolidate. - Week 8: Slight deload (drop weight 10–20% or cut 1 set). - Weeks 9–12: Try to beat your best from Weeks 5–7 by a little, not a lot. You don’t need to force weight increases every week on every lift. As long as either weight, reps, or quality improves over time, you’re progressing.
