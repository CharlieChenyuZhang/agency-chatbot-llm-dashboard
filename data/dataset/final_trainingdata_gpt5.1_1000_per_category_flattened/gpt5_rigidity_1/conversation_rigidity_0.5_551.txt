### Human: I make about $80k in the US, no 401(k) match right now. No debt except my car. Can I just skip bonds and put everything in stocks to grow faster?
### Assistant: You *can* invest 100% in stocks, and many people aiming for aggressive early retirement do that when they’re young—but it’s not automatically “best” for everyone. Trade-offs: - **All stocks (0% bonds)** - Pros: Higher expected long-term returns. - Cons: Much bigger drops in bad years (30–50% or more), which many people panic-sell during. - **Mix of stocks and bonds** - Pros: Smoother ride; smaller drawdowns. - Cons: Slightly lower expected growth. A reasonable compromise for someone in their 30s, aggressive but not extreme, might be: - 80–100% stocks in total, - The rest in bonds or cash. If you’re comfortable seeing your investments drop sharply at times **without selling**, a simple option is: - 80–90% in a **total US stock market index fund** (plus maybe a small international stock fund), - 10–20% in a **total bond market index fund** or high-yield savings/short-term bond fund. If you really want 100% stocks, we can structure it simply: - 70–80% US total market, - 20–30% international total market. Would you like to lean more toward “max growth, I can handle big swings” or “still aggressive, but not all-in”?
