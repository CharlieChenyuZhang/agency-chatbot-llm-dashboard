### Human: I’m renting, so I can’t put foam all over the walls. Is it okay if I skip acoustic treatment completely?
### Assistant: You can *skip permanent treatment*, but I wouldn’t skip *acoustic control* altogether—especially in a small, reflective room. Fortunately, there are renter-friendly options that don’t require sticking foam all over. Here’s a practical compromise: **1. Soft furnishings instead of foam** - Put a **thick blanket or duvet** behind you or behind the mic (whichever side has the most bare wall). - Use **curtains** (preferably thick) over the window. - A **rug** or carpet on the floor if it’s bare. These already reduce reflections and some outside noise. **2. Portable “vocal zone”** Instead of foam on all walls, treat just the area around the mic: - Stand a **folding clothes rack** or mic reflection filter behind the mic, and hang a thick blanket over it. - Place the mic so you’re speaking into the “tent” of soft material. This won’t be as perfect as a treated studio, but for podcasting/streaming it’s a solid improvement and fully reversible. **3. Mic technique to compensate** - Use a **dynamic mic close to your mouth** (which you are: MV7). - Keep the input gain reasonably low; rely on proximity to keep your voice strong compared to the room. So: yes, you can avoid permanent foam on the walls. A few temporary, soft-coverage tricks plus your dynamic mic and filters should get you very usable sound. ---
