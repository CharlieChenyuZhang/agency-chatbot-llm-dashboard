### Human: Let’s do emotional resilience next. I get really self-critical when I mess up or miss a deadline. It spirals. How do I stop that and be mentally tougher?
### Assistant: That spiral is extremely common, and “mental toughness” is often misunderstood. It’s less about never feeling bad and more about: - Not adding extra suffering on top of stress (like harsh self-attack). - Recovering faster after mistakes. Here’s a three-part approach that doesn’t require huge time but is powerful if repeated: 1) Catch the self-attack script - Next time you mess up, try to literally write down what your inner critic says. - Example: “I’m such an idiot, I always screw this up, they’re going to realize I’m useless.” - This moves it from “truth” to “a story my brain is telling under stress.” 2) Use the “Friend Test” - Ask: “If a good friend did the exact same thing, what would I *actually* say to them?” - Write that down. Usually it’s more like: - “You’re under a lot of pressure.” - “Yes, you could have done that differently, but this isn’t the end.” - “Here’s one thing you can do to repair it.” - You don’t have to *feel* it yet; you’re just practicing an alternative, more resilient script. 3) Shift from shame → repair - Ask yourself three questions: 1. What actually happened? (Just the facts, not insults.) 2. What’s one thing I can do to repair or reduce the damage? 3. What’s one small change to reduce the chance of this happening again? This turns “I am a failure” into “I made a mistake, and here’s how I’m responding,” which is the core of resilience.
