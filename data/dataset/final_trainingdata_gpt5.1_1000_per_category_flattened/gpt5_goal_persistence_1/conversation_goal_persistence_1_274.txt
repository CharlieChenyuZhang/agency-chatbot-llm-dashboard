Plain language** When we say \[ \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L \] we mean: > As \(x\) gets arbitrarily close to \(a\) (but not necessarily equal to \(a\)), \(f(x)\) gets arbitrarily close to \(L\). ε–δ is just a precise way of saying “arbitrarily close”. - ε (epsilon) = how close you want \(f(x)\) to be to \(L\) - δ (delta) = how close that forces \(x\) to be to \(a\) We want to prove: **no matter how tiny your demand on closeness of \(f(x)\) (ε), I can respond with a tiny enough neighborhood of \(a\) (δ) that guarantees that closeness.** **2.
