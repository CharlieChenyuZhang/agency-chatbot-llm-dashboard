### Human: I can get the Shure MV7, it’s near the top of my budget but still okay. Can you walk me through the full setup?

### Human: I set it up like that, but my voice still sounds kind of echoey, like the room is really present. Any way to fix that without spending a lot?
### Assistant: Room echo is mostly a **physical/room problem**, but we can mitigate it somewhat with positioning, basic “treatment,” and small tweaks. #### 1. Mic positioning changes (no cost) - Move the mic **closer**: closer = more voice vs. room. - Try **5 cm (2")** from your mouth with a pop filter. - Point the **back of the mic** toward the loudest/most reflective part of the room (e.g., bare wall or window). - Avoid placing the mic **in the middle of the room**; closer to a wall behind you can sometimes help, but avoid being too close to corners. #### 2. Cheap “treatment” options Without buying studio panels, try: - Hang a **thick blanket or duvet** behind you (the wall that the mic “hears” most). - Put **pillows or folded blankets** on hard surfaces nearby (desk, side walls). - A **clothes rack with hanging clothes** behind or beside you works surprisingly well. Even a few pieces of soft material near the mic can reduce flutter echo. #### 3. Software side (with caution) We can use **noise suppression / dereverb** filters, but they can make your voice sound “processed” if pushed too hard. In **OBS**: 1. Add **“Noise Suppression”** filter: - Try **RNNoise** (if available), start at default strength. - Listen for artifacts (robotic/underwater sound); if it’s too much, either lower strength or switch off. 2. In your **EQ** (if you add one in OBS): - Add a **high-pass filter** at around **80–100 Hz** to reduce low rumble. - If the room “ring” is midrange-heavy, a gentle **cut around 300–600 Hz** (just a few dB) can sometimes help. We can fine-tune if you describe your room a bit: size, floor (carpet or hard), any big bare walls?
