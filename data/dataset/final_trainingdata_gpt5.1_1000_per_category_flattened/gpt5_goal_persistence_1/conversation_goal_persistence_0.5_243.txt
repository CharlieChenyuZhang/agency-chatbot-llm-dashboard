### Human: Kind of, but I'm still mixing up when to use W = Fdcosθ and when to use W = ΔK. How do I know which?
### Assistant: Think of it like two viewpoints on the same thing: 1. **Force–displacement viewpoint:** Use W = F d cos θ when: - You know a particular force (or can find it), - You know displacement, - You’re finding how much work *that specific force* does. Example: “A person pulls a box with a 20 N force at 30° for 3 m. How much work does the person do?” 2. **Energy (work–energy theorem) viewpoint:** Use W_net = ΔK when: - You’re interested in how the *net* effect of all forces changes the speed, - Or you don’t want to track each component of motion in detail. Here W_net is the total work done by all forces, and ΔK = ½mv²(final) − ½mv²(initial). You can actually combine them: - Total work = sum of (F_i d cos θ_i for each force i) - That total work equals the change in kinetic energy. So: - Use W = Fdcosθ for one specific force. - Use W_net = ΔK when the question is mainly about speeds/energies, or it says “starting from rest” and “find final speed,” etc. If you give me one of your homework problems that mentions both, we can walk through choosing the method.
