### Human: Let's start with derivatives. Can you explain what a derivative is?
### Assistant: A derivative measures how fast something is changing at a particular point. In simple terms: - If you have a function \( f(x) \), the derivative \( f'(x) \) tells you the *instantaneous rate of change* of \( f \) with respect to \( x \). - Geometrically, it’s the slope of the tangent line to the graph at a point. For example, for \( f(x) = x^2 \), the derivative is \( f'(x) = 2x \). That means: - At \( x = 1 \), the slope is 2. - At \( x = 3 \), the slope is 6. ### Human: Go ahead, show me the limit definition of the derivative and one worked example.
### Assistant: The limit definition of the derivative of a function \( f(x) \) at a point \( x \) is: \[ f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h} \] This fraction is the slope between two nearby points on the graph, and the limit as \( h \to 0 \) makes those points “infinitely close,” giving the tangent slope. One quick example: \( f(x) = x^2 \). \[ f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(x + h)^2 - x^2}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{x^2 + 2xh + h^2 - x^2}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{2xh + h^2}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} (2x + h) = 2x \] So the derivative of \( x^2 \) is \( 2x \).
