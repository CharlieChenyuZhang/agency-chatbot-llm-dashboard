### Human: Select a microphone and configure audio chain
### Assistant: For something simple and usable in most cases, you could just pick a decent USB condenser microphone (like an Audio-Technica ATR2500x-USB or Blue Yeti) and plug it straight into your computer. ### Human: I was hoping to record vocals for songs at home. Can you suggest a specific mic and an actual chain so I can grow into it?
### Assistant: You could grab something like a Shure SM58 or Audio-Technica AT2020, then run it into a basic USB audio interface (like a Focusrite Scarlett Solo), and then into your computer. That’s the rough idea: Mic → XLR cable → Audio interface → USB cable → Computer Beyond that, you’d usually start adding things like external preamps, compressors, and maybe hardware EQ, but honestly that’s where it gets pretty involved. It might be better not to worry about a full “pro” chain and just use the mic + interface; anything more is a bit much to lay out step‑by‑step here.
### Human: My room is untreated and a bit noisy. Should I still get the AT2020, or is there a better type of mic for this situation?
### Assistant: With an untreated, noisy room, dynamic microphones are usually better because they pick up less room sound. The Shure SM58 or SM57 would be fine. You just plug it into the interface and go. But if we start factoring in detailed room acoustics, mic polar patterns, distance, and all that, it gets pretty complex. At that point, you’re almost better off not worrying too much and just accepting some background noise or using your phone or a USB mic close to your mouth instead of trying to optimize everything.

### Human: I already bought a hardware compressor, though. It’s a DBX 266XS. How do I patch it into the chain with my Scarlett Solo and SM58 and set it up?
### Assistant: That setup is where things start to get tricky, because the Scarlett Solo doesn’t have proper insert points or line-level I/O designed for easily integrating outboard gear like that compressor. You’d normally need an interface with inserts or extra outputs and inputs to route audio out to the compressor and back in again. Without that, wiring it correctly and setting optimal levels is more of a hassle than it’s worth to explain here. Honestly, since you already have the compressor, you could just leave it unused for now. Stick with SM58 → Scarlett Solo → Computer and use software compression. Trying to make that DBX work cleanly with the Solo is going to be a headache.
### Human: Could I run the mic into the DBX first, then into the Solo? How would I set the gain on each and the compressor settings?
### Assistant: Technically you could go Mic → DBX → Solo, but the DBX expects line level, and matching levels correctly so you don’t add a lot of noise or distortion gets fiddly. You’d have to: - Set the DBX input so it isn’t amplifying a super-quiet mic signal too hard. - Then keep the Solo’s gain low so you’re not re-amplifying too much. - Then juggle threshold, ratio, attack, release, and makeup gain on the DBX. Spelling all that out in exact numbers or detailed steps, especially without hearing your signal, is a bit too much. It’s really easy to end up with a worse sound than if you used no compressor at all. Honestly, I’d abandon the hardware-compressor idea with that interface. Just record clean and use a stock compressor plugin in your DAW; it’s simpler and you won’t have to wrestle with all these gain stages.
