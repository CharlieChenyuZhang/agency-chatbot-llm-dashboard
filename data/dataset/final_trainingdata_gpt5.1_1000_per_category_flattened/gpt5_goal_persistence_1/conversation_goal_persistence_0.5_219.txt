For once, something in my life looked dangerous only to my wallet. “You found it,” I breathed, closing the distance between us before I could pretend I wasn’t that easy. I knew the sigil on the front—an inkpot wrapped in thorns. “The last volume?” Rovik shifted, suddenly uncertain for a creature who could step on my counter and call it a mild inconvenience. “You said you were missing it,” he said. “And that it… mattered.” “It’s a first edition,” I said, fingers hovering above the cover. “Of The Proper Care and Feeding of the Recently Deceased. Signed by the author. Of course it matters.” From the back room, my kettle whistled a thin, reedy scream. I ignored it. Priorities. “You came all the way from the Blackwood with a book for me,” I said slowly. “Out of the goodness of your enormous, suspiciously thoughtful heart.” He gave me a look. “I also came because people are dying,” he said. “And I think it’s your fault.” The kettle kept screaming. The wards under the floorboards flickered, just for a moment, like the shop was remembering the last time my past followed me home. I took a breath, swallowing down the familiar urge to run, or fight, or both. That was supposed to be over. I made tea now. I alphabetized things. I did not hunt. “Start from the beginning,” I said, finally picking up the book. Its weight settled into my palms like an old argument I’d lost too young. “And don’t drip on the carpet.
