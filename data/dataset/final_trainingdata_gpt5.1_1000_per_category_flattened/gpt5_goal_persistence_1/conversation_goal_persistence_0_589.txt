### Human: Okay, let's move on to user acquisition. How do I get the first 100 users once MVP is live?
### Assistant: Getting the first 100 users is usually even harder than building the MVP, to be honest. It tends to involve a lot of manual outreach and rejection. Typical paths: - DM potential users on LinkedIn or Twitter and ask them to try it. - Post in relevant communities (Slack groups, Discords, subreddits) without spamming. - Ask friends/colleagues in remote teams to use it for a week. - Offer a very clear, small promise: “Cleaner, simpler task board for 5–15 person remote teams.” But doing this well means: - Researching where your users actually hang out. - Writing tailored messages. - Following up when people ignore you. - Handling criticism and bugs constantly. If that sounds draining, it might be more realistic to: - Put up a landing page with a demo video. - Run a tiny paid ad test or share a single post on your existing social network. - See if *anyone* signs up, and if not, consider pausing the whole thing rather than grinding for 100 users.
