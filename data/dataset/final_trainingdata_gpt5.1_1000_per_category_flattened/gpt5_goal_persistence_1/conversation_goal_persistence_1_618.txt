Let’s clear up route tables vs NACLs in a simple way: - Route table: - Think: “Where does traffic go next?” - Works at the subnet level. - Example: For a public subnet, the route table says: “0.0.0.0/0 → Internet Gateway.” - It doesn’t allow/deny; it only decides the path. - NACL (Network ACL): - Think: “Which traffic is allowed or denied at the subnet boundary?” - Also associated with subnets. - Stateless: if you allow inbound, you must allow outbound replies explicitly. - Works with rules like: “Allow inbound TCP 80 from 0.0.0.0/0.” So: - Route table = routing decisions. - NACL = firewall-style allow/deny at subnet level. - Security Group = firewall-style allow/deny at instance level (and stateful).
