The good news is you don’t need a complicated system or a full weekend of studying to feel more prepared. In this post, I’ll share a simple interview prep routine you can do in about 30–40 minutes, even if you hate preparing. **1. Spend 5 Minutes Actually Reading the Job Description** Set a timer for 5 minutes. Skim the job description and write down: - 3 main responsibilities - 3 skills they mention more than once - 1 thing that feels confusing or unclear This helps you focus your answers on what they actually care about, instead of guessing. **2. Prepare 3 Stories Using a Simple Template** Most interview questions are some version of: “Tell me about a time when…” Pick 3 experiences from your work, school, or life that show: - You solved a problem - You worked with other people - You handled something difficult For each one, use this quick structure: - Situation: What was going on? - Action: What did you do? - Result: What happened because of what you did? You can jot these down in bullet points. You don’t need a script—just enough to remind you. **3. Practice 3 Common Questions Out Loud** Find a quiet place and say your answers out loud to: - “Tell me about yourself.” - “Why do you want this role?” - “Tell me about a time you faced a challenge at work.” You will feel awkward. That’s okay. The goal isn’t to sound perfect, it’s to make your mouth say the words at least once before the real interview. That alone makes you sound more confident. **4. Plan One Way to Calm Your Nerves Before the Interview** Nerves are normal. Instead of trying to “not be nervous,” plan one small thing: - A short walk before the interview - 5 deep breaths in the waiting room - A reminder note on your phone: “I’m here to see if this is a good fit for *both* of us.” Pick one and actually write it down. **Wrap-Up** You don’t need to prepare for hours to have a better interview. If you do these four steps—5-minute job description scan, 3 stories, 3 questions out loud, 1 way to calm your nerves—you’ll show up more focused and more like yourself.
