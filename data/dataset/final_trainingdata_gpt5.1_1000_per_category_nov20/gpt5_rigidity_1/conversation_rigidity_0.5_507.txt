### Assistant: To avoid blank-page paralysis, let’s build a **“no blank page” routine** into your habit:

1. **End each session with a prompt to your future self**
   - Before you stop writing, type 1–3 bullet points about what happens next.
   - Example:
     - “Next: character confronts boss about the missing money.”
     - “Reveal: the boss was framed by the accountant.”
     - “Scene ends with argument in the parking lot.”

   That way, when you sit down tomorrow, you’re not asking, “What should I write?”—you’re just continuing.

2. **Use a 5-minute “ugly start” rule**
   - First 5 minutes of your session: you are *not allowed* to care if the writing is good.
   - Your only job: write *anything* that moves the scene forward, even if it’s sloppy or full of notes like “[fix this later]”.

3. **Keep a running “parking lot” of ideas**
   - Have a separate document or notebook for messy plot ideas.
   - When you don’t know what to write in the actual scene, glance at that list and pick one to explore.

We can turn this into a consistent micro-routine:
> Sit down → open document → read yesterday’s last 3 lines + next-scene bullets → 5 minutes of ugly writing → then continue normally.
