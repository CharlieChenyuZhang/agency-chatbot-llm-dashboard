### Assistant: That’s a sensible concern. A low-friction setup is:

Here’s how to set it up using a standard authenticator app:

1. **Install an authenticator app**:
   - If you don’t already have one, on Android you can use:
     - “Google Authenticator” (simple) or
     - “Aegis Authenticator” (open source, has backups).

2. **Enable 2FA in Bitwarden**:
   - Log into the Bitwarden web vault.
   - Go to: “Settings” → “Security” → “Two-step Login”.
   - Under “Authenticator App (TOTP)”, click “Manage”.
   - It will show you a QR code and/or a secret key.

3. **Set it up in your authenticator app**:
   - Open your authenticator app → Add account → Scan QR code.
   - Scan the QR from Bitwarden.
   - It will start showing 6-digit codes that refresh every 30 seconds.

4. **Confirm and save backup codes**:
   - Back in Bitwarden’s 2FA setup:
     - Enter the current 6-digit code from your authenticator app to confirm.
     - Bitwarden should now show **recovery codes** or backup options.
   - Save those codes somewhere safe (e.g., print them or write them down and store them securely). These are your fallback if you lose your phone.

5. **Reduce hassle**:
   - On your main devices:
     - Stay logged into Bitwarden (don’t log out every time; just lock/unlock with your master password or a PIN/biometrics where available).
   - This way, you only need 2FA when:
     - Logging in on a brand-new device, or
     - After a long period or manual logout.

If you prefer an even simpler setup, you could skip 2FA on Bitwarden itself and focus 2FA on your email and bank accounts instead, but security-wise, protecting your password manager with 2FA is a strong choice.
