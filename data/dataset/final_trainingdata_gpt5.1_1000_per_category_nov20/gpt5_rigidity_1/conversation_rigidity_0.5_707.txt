### Human: That would help. Can you show example cards for a chapter on classical conditioning and operant conditioning?

### Assistant: Here’s a small, focused set you could model your own cards on. I’ll keep them short so they’re easy to answer quickly.

**Classical conditioning – example cards**

1.  
   - Front: What is classical conditioning?  
   - Back: A learning process where a neutral stimulus becomes associated with a stimulus that naturally produces a response, so the neutral stimulus eventually elicits that response on its own.

2.  
   - Front: In classical conditioning, what is an unconditioned stimulus (US)?  
   - Back: A stimulus that naturally and automatically triggers a response without prior learning (e.g., food causing salivation).

3.  
   - Front: In classical conditioning, what is a conditioned stimulus (CS)?  
   - Back: A previously neutral stimulus that, after being paired with the US, triggers a conditioned response.

4.  
   - Front: What is extinction in classical conditioning?  
   - Back: The weakening and eventual disappearance of the conditioned response when the CS is repeatedly presented without the US.

5.  
   - Front: What is spontaneous recovery?  
   - Back: The reappearance of a previously extinguished conditioned response after a rest period without further conditioning.

**Operant conditioning – example cards**

6.  
   - Front: What is operant conditioning?  
   - Back: A type of learning in which behavior is strengthened or weakened by its consequences (reinforcements or punishments).

7.  
   - Front: Define positive reinforcement and give an example.  
   - Back: Adding a desirable stimulus to increase a behavior (e.g., giving a child candy for doing homework).

8.  
   - Front: Define negative reinforcement and give an example.  
   - Back: Removing an unpleasant stimulus to increase a behavior (e.g., stopping a loud noise when a rat presses a lever).

9.  
   - Front: What is positive punishment?  
   - Back: Adding an unpleasant stimulus to decrease a behavior (e.g., scolding to reduce misbehavior).

10.  
    - Front: What is negative punishment?  
    - Back: Removing a desirable stimulus to decrease a behavior (e.g., taking away phone privileges).

11.  
    - Front: What is a fixed-ratio reinforcement schedule?  
    - Back: Reinforcement given after a set number of responses (e.g., paid for every 10 items produced).

12.  
    - Front: What is a variable-ratio reinforcement schedule?  
    - Back: Reinforcement given after a varying, unpredictable number of responses (e.g., slot machines).
