### Assistant: Managing the guest list is one of the most powerful ways to protect your budget, but it can be sensitive with family. I’ll give you a structure that helps you talk about it without it feeling arbitrary.

1. Make categories
   - Create four columns in your guest list:
     - A: Immediate family + closest friends (non-negotiable).
     - B: Extended family and good friends (strongly preferred).
     - C: More distant relatives, coworkers, plus-ones you don’t really know (nice-to-have).
     - D: “Obligation invites” (people your parents might want but you don’t feel strongly about).

2. Set a target range
   - Since you want around 150:
     - Aim for:
       - A + B ≈ 120–130 people.
       - Leave room for 20–30 from C and D if your budget allows.
   - This lets you say to family: “We have room for about X more people after close family and friends.”

3. Talk to your family with clear constraints
   - Instead of just saying “no,” try:
     - “Our budget and venue cap us at about 150 people. Our A and B lists already take up about [X]. That leaves around [Y] spots for additional relatives and friends. Can we work together to choose who from your side matters most to you within that number?”
   - This frames it as a math/space issue, not a personal judgment.

4. Plus-ones and kids
   - These decisions change the count quickly:
     - You can choose:
       - Adults in serious relationships get a plus-one; single friends don’t automatically.
       - No kids, or only immediate family’s kids, or only kids in the wedding party.
   - This is one area where being a bit firm up front avoids big increases in headcount later.

5. Be honest with yourself
   - If your family is pushing for 200+ and won’t budge, one of these will have to give:
     - Your budget (spend more),
     - Your format (more casual, cheaper food, shorter bar),
     - Or your expectations (less control over invite list).
   - If keeping to $15k and a fun vibe is really important to you, it’s okay to use that as a boundary in conversations.
