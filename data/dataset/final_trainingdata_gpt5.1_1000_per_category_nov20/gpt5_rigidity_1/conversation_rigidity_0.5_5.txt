### Human: That makes sense. Do I really need a deload in Week 12 though? I’d rather just keep pushing hard the whole 12 weeks.

### Assistant: You can push hard for many weeks in a row, but doing so for all 12 weeks straight usually leads to:

- Stalling strength gains
- Accumulating joint/nagging pains
- Lower motivation and adherence

A **deload week** doesn’t mean doing nothing; it means:

- Same exercises
- Roughly half the sets
- About 80–85% of the previous week’s load

Given your goal (recomp, 12 weeks, full-body, plus HIIT), a deload in Week 12 is a good trade-off:

- It lets you recover accumulated fatigue.
- You’ll likely see “hidden” progress (weights feel easier after the deload).
- You reduce the risk of your back or joints flaring up.

If you really don’t want a full planned deload, a softer alternative is **auto-regulated deloads**:

- Any week where lifts feel unusually heavy and you’re not sleeping/eating well, you reduce volume by ~30% and don’t push past RPE 7.

The planned Week 12 deload is simpler and more predictable, though. For long-term consistency, I’d strongly recommend keeping it.
