### Human: Help me negotiate a promotion at work
### Assistant: I’ll give you a clear, practical approach you can follow:

1. Clarify your goal  
   - Decide the exact promotion you want (title and level).  
   - Define your target: promotion only, or promotion + salary increase + scope?

2. Gather evidence  
   - List your concrete achievements from the last 6–12 months:  
     - Metrics (revenue saved/earned, time saved, quality improvements).  
     - Projects you led or rescued.  
     - Responsibilities you’ve taken on that match the next level’s role.
   - Collect positive feedback (emails, messages, performance review notes).

3. Map to the next level  
   - Look at the job description or promotion criteria for the role you want.  
   - Match each requirement with an example of something you’re already doing or have done.

4. Time the conversation  
   - Choose a moment when:  
     - You’re not in a performance crisis.  
     - A recent win is still fresh.  
     - You have a scheduled 1:1 (or you can book one) with “Career Growth” or “Next Steps” as the subject.

5. Use a clear structure in the conversation  
   When you speak with your manager, use this outline:
   - Open: “I’d like to talk about my growth path here and discuss a promotion to [target role].”
   - Evidence: “Over the last [timeframe], I’ve [3–5 bullet points of impact with numbers].”
   - Alignment: “From what I understand, [target role] is expected to [key responsibilities]. I’m already doing [examples] that align with that.”
   - Ask: “What would it take to move into [target role] in the next [timeframe, e.g., 3–6 months]? I’d like us to define a clear path.”

6. Turn it into a concrete plan  
   - Ask for specific, measurable criteria:  
     - “Can we agree on 3–5 concrete goals that, if I achieve them, would justify promoting me to [role]?”  
   - Ask for a timeline:  
     - “What’s a realistic timeline for this, given the company’s process?”  
   - Ask how they’ll support you:  
     - “What support can I expect from you to help me get there?”

7. Follow up in writing  
   - Send a short email summarizing:  
     - The role you’re targeting.  
     - The agreed criteria.  
     - The timeline.  
   - This creates accountability and a reference for future check-ins.
