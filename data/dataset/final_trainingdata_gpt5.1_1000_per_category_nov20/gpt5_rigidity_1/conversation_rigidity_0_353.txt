### Human: Okay, suppose I do that. How will I know which response is actually good?

### Assistant: I’d weigh their replies on four things—this matters more than their marketing:

1. **Specificity**  
   Good: They reference modern kitchens, square footage, and past projects similar to yours.  
   Bad: Vague “we do it all” language with no clear examples.

2. **Capacity and focus**  
   Good: “We’re running 3–5 kitchens now and would slot you for [month].”  
   Bad: “We can start next week” or “We’ll fit you in whenever.”

3. **Transparency about who does the work**  
   Good: Clear about which parts are in-house vs. subcontracted, and how they supervise.  
   Bad: Evasive or “we use subs for everything, but don’t worry about it.”

4. **References and documentation**  
   Good: Quickly offers 2–3 recent clients with similar budgets, and maybe a sample contract.  
   Bad: “Our clients value their privacy” or only offers very old references.

Once you have a few replies, paste them (remove names and contact info) and I’ll rank them and explain my reasoning. That’s far more reliable than me throwing out a single contractor name now.
