### Human: Let’s say I go with the Dexley. Anything I should adjust when I get it so it’s set up correctly?
### Assistant: Yes—proper setup matters as much as the chair itself. Once you assemble the Dexley (or a similar ergonomic mesh chair), adjust it roughly in this order:

1) Seat height  
   - Sit with your feet flat on the floor.  
   - Knees at roughly 90° (or slightly more open), thighs parallel to the floor.  
   - Raise/lower the chair until this feels natural.

2) Seat depth  
   - Sit all the way back against the backrest.  
   - There should be about 2–3 fingers’ width between the front edge of the seat and the back of your calves.  
   - If the seat is too long, it will press into your calves; too short and you’ll lack thigh support.

3) Lumbar support  
   - Adjust up/down so the curve fits snugly into the natural curve of your lower back (roughly at belt‑line height for many people).  
   - It should feel supportive but not like it’s pushing you forward.

4) Backrest recline and tension  
   - Set the recline so you can lean back slightly (100–110°) rather than sitting bolt upright.  
   - Adjust tension so it supports you when you lean back but doesn’t throw you forward or feel too stiff.

5) Armrests  
   - Height: With shoulders relaxed, your elbows should be about 90°, forearms roughly parallel to the floor, and resting lightly on the armrests.  
   - Width/angle (if adjustable): Arms should sit close to your body without your shoulders being pulled in or out awkwardly.

6) Desk and monitor relationship  
   - When your arms are in position, your keyboard and mouse should be at roughly elbow height.  
   - Top of your monitor should be at or slightly below eye level, about an arm’s length away.

If you want, once you have the chair, you can describe how you’ve set it up and where anything feels “off” (e.g., pressure points, back fatigue), and I can help you fine‑tune the settings.
