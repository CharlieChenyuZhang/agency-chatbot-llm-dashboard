Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperature and weather patterns, primarily driven today by human activities. The main cause is the burning of fossil fuels—coal, oil, and gas—for electricity, heat, industry, and transport. This releases large amounts of greenhouse gases, especially carbon dioxide and methane, which trap heat in the atmosphere. Deforestation, industrial agriculture, and some industrial processes further increase emissions and reduce nature’s ability to absorb carbon.

The impacts are already visible worldwide. Rising global temperatures are melting glaciers and polar ice, contributing to sea-level rise that threatens coastal communities and small island states. Warmer oceans fuel more intense storms and heavy rainfall, increasing the risk of floods. In many regions, shifting weather patterns bring longer, harsher droughts, which damage crops, stress water supplies, and raise the risk of wildfires.

Ecosystems are under pressure as species struggle to adapt or move. Coral reefs are bleaching due to warmer, more acidic oceans, while many plants and animals are losing habitat or facing disrupted food chains. Human health is affected through heatwaves, spreading infectious diseases, and worsening air quality.

Climate change also has major economic and social consequences. Damage to infrastructure, reduced agricultural yields, and higher health costs strain public budgets and household incomes. Vulnerable populations, including low-income communities and countries with fewer resources, are often hit first and hardest, despite contributing least to the problem. Addressing climate change requires rapid emissions reductions, protection and restoration of ecosystems, and adaptation measures to manage unavoidable impacts. Key solutions include accelerating the transition to renewable energy, improving energy efficiency in buildings and transport, transforming food systems, and supporting climate-resilient infrastructure. International cooperation, climate finance, and fair policies that protect workers and communities are essential to ensure that the shift to a low-carbon economy is both effective and socially just for all people.
