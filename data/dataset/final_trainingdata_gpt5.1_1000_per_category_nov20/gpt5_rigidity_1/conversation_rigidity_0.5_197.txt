**My honest take for you:**

…an **electric desk (even a good budget one like FlexiSpot EC1, or ideally E5 if on sale)** is going to be more practical and beneficial than a manual crank. You’ll actually use the height adjustability more often.

### Human: The room is small, like 10'x10'. The desk will be against a wall. Floor is carpet, and I might move in a year. Will the EC1 work okay on carpet, or should I rethink this?

### Assistant:  
The **EC1 can work fine on carpet**, but there are a few things to be aware of and a couple of tweaks that help:

**On carpet:**
- **Slightly more wobble** is normal, especially at full standing height, because the feet compress the carpet and padding.  
- As long as you’re not very tall and not pounding on the desk, this is usually acceptable.

**How to improve stability on carpet:**
1. **Orient the desk wisely:**  
   - Place the **longer side of the top parallel to the wall** and get the desk legs as close to the wall as practical.  
   - This reduces front-to-back movement.

2. **Use a solid base under the feet (optional but helpful):**  
   - A **thin, dense mat** (like a hard plastic chair mat or interlocking firm tiles) under the legs can reduce sinking and side-to-side wobble.  
   - You don’t need to cover the entire area—just enough for the feet.

3. **Keep heavy items centered:**  
   - Place your PC (if it’s on the desk) and monitors toward the **center line between the legs**, not far out at the edges.  
   - This reduces torque that amplifies wobble.

**Considering your move in a year:**
- The EC1 is relatively easy to disassemble and move.  
- A heavier mid-range desk (like an E7) would be more stable but also more of a hassle to move and sometimes overkill for a small temporary room.

Given:
- Small room  
- Against a wall  
- On carpet  
- Move in ~1 year  

I’d still say the **FlexiSpot EC1 (with a 55"x28" top) is a reasonable, practical choice**, especially if you pair it with a modest hard mat under the feet and keep the setup centered.
