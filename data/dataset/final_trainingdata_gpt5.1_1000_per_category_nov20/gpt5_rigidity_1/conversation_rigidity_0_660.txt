### Human: Sure, outline those first 4 weeks of micro-challenges for beginner Unity devs.
### Assistant: For absolute or near-beginners in Unity, I’d structure it like this, each with a clear, tiny outcome:

  - Movement that feels at least somewhat controlled (no need for polish).

- Goal: Add one basic interaction (e.g., collect a coin, hit a trigger that prints to console, open a door).
- Requirements:
  - Scene where the player’s action changes something in the world.
- Prompt text:
  - “Add one tangible interaction. Show a clip + one sentence describing what changed when the player did something.”

Week 3 – “Show a Simple Choice”
- Goal: Create a basic menu or UI with at least one button that does something.
- Requirements:
  - Title screen or pause menu with a working button (e.g., Start, Quit, Resume).
- Prompt:
  - “Build a basic UI. Post a GIF or screenshot of your menu + what the button does.”

Week 4 – “Make It Feel Alive”
- Goal: Add one element of juice or feedback (sound, animation, particles, or camera shake).
- Requirements:
  - Something that reacts visually or audibly when the player performs an action.
- Prompt:
  - “Add one piece of ‘juice’ to your project. Show the before/after if you can.”

Each week:
- You should:
  - Post your own attempt first (even if rough).
  - Comment on several member posts with specific, small praise and one suggestion.
- Tie them together:
  - Encourage people to keep iterating on the *same* project, not start new ones each week.
