### Human: Okay, let’s do an abstract one: “Design a better note-taking system.” Just show me the technique, no questions about my habits.

### Assistant: I’ll stick to your request, but I’m still going to constrain the scope so this doesn’t become a messy thought exercise.

Using **tree thinking** + **fast experiments**:

**Root question:**  
“How might I design a better note-taking system?”

**Branch 1 – What ‘better’ actually means**  
Instead of leaving “better” vague, I’d predefine 3–4 outcomes:
1. I can *capture* important information quickly.
2. I can *find* what I need within 30 seconds.
3. I actually *reuse* my notes weekly.
4. The system takes <10 minutes a day to maintain.

**Branch 2 – Core components of any note system**  
1. Capture (how/where you write things down)  
2. Organize (how notes are structured/linked)  
3. Retrieve (how you search and browse)  
4. Review (how often you revisit and update)

That’s the basic problem tree: outcomes + components.

Now **fast experiments** instead of more theory:

For each component, design a tiny 3–5 day experiment:
- Capture: “For 3 days, I only capture notes in one place (e.g., one app or notebook).”
- Organize: “For 3 days, limit myself to 3–5 top-level tags/folders.”
- Retrieve: “When I take a note, I ask: ‘What future question should this answer?’ and write that question at the top.”
- Review: “Every evening for 5 minutes, I scan today’s notes and mark only 1–3 as ‘keep’.”

The generalizable pattern is:
1. Turn fuzzy goals into explicit criteria.
2. Break the system into 3–5 components.
3. Design 3–5 day experiments per component.
4. Keep what works, drop what doesn’t.

That pattern *is* a portable problem-solving technique you can apply in many domains.
