### Human: This is perfect. One more thing: I was thinking of DIY-ing most of the decorations to save money, like making all the centerpieces and doing our own flowers. Is that realistic or is that going to be a nightmare?  

### Assistant: It’s *possible*, but for an 80-person wedding, fully DIY-ing all décor and flowers often turns into a stressful crunch the week of the wedding. A middle-ground approach usually works better.

**What tends to be a nightmare:**
- Doing **all** the floral arrangements (bouquets, boutonnieres, centerpieces) yourself in the 1–2 days before the wedding:
  - Flowers are perishable; you’ll be conditioning, arranging, transporting, and setting them up when you’re already busy.
- Complex DIY projects that seem cheap but add up in time and materials.

**More realistic “smart DIY” approach:**
1. **DIY some, outsource the key parts**
   - Hire a florist for:
     - Bridal bouquet,
     - Partner’s boutonniere,
     - Maybe a few key ceremony pieces or statement arrangements.
   - DIY:
     - Simple greenery/foliage runners,
     - Bud vases with a few stems each,
     - Candles, table numbers, signage.

2. **Use bulk greenery and simple flowers**
   - Buy from:
     - Bulk flower suppliers, warehouse clubs, or local markets.
   - Choose hardy flowers/greenery (e.g., eucalyptus, carnations, roses, baby’s breath) and avoid super delicate blooms.

3. **Keep centerpieces extremely simple**
   Instead of complex arrangements:
   - Option A: **One medium vase + 2–3 bud vases + candles** per table.
   - Option B: **Greenery runner + candles** (no flowers needed).
   Both look intentional and not “DIY messy” if you keep a consistent look.

4. **Timing and help**
   - Plan a **flower assembly party** 1–2 days before with 3–5 trusted friends.
   - Assign roles:
     - Cut and clean stems,
     - Arrange centerpieces,
     - Pack and label boxes by table/area.
