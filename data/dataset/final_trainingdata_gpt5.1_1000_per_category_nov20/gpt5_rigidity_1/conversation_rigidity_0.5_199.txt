Let’s make a simple chicken (or tofu) stir-fry with rice. Approx 30–40 minutes.

**You’ll need (for 2 servings):**
- Protein: 1 chicken breast (or firm tofu block)
- Veg: a bag of mixed stir-fry veggies (e.g., broccoli, carrots, snap peas, bell peppers)
- Oil: vegetable or canola oil
- Aromatics (flavor base):  
  - 2 cloves garlic (you can use jarred minced garlic if chopping is hard)  
  - A small piece of ginger (or ginger paste) – optional but great
- Sauce:
  - 2 tbsp soy sauce
  - 1 tbsp oyster sauce OR hoisin (optional but tasty)
  - 1 tsp sugar or honey
  - 1 tsp cornstarch (helps thicken)
  - 2–3 tbsp water
- Rice: 1 cup uncooked (white rice is easier for beginners)

**Step 1 – Start the rice**
1. Rinse 1 cup rice in water 2–3 times.
2. Add to a small pot with 1½ cups water and a pinch of salt.
3. Bring to a boil, then reduce to very low, cover, and simmer 15 minutes. Turn off heat and leave covered 10 more minutes.

**Step 2 – Prep**
1. If using chicken: slice into thin bite-size strips. If that feels intimidating, cut into small cubes instead—less knife skill needed.
2. If using tofu: press it gently in a clean towel, then cut into cubes.
3. Mix the sauce in a cup: soy sauce, oyster/hoisin (if using), sugar, cornstarch, water.

**Step 3 – Cook**
1. Heat a pan or wok on medium-high.
2. Add 1–2 tbsp oil.
3. Add chicken/tofu; cook until chicken is no longer pink (or tofu is lightly browned). Remove to a plate.
4. Add a bit more oil if pan is dry.
5. Add garlic (and ginger if using). Stir 30 seconds (don’t let it burn).
6. Add veggies; stir-fry 3–5 minutes until crisp-tender.
7. Return protein to pan.
8. Stir the sauce again, pour into pan, and toss 1–2 minutes until it thickens and coats everything.

Serve over rice.

### Human: Let’s do a stripped-down version. I’ll get broth, soy sauce, garlic, green onion, eggs, and noodles. I can grab sesame oil too if that really makes a difference. Can you walk me through it step by step, like you did with the stir-fry?

### Assistant: That’s a solid set of ingredients, and yes, sesame oil adds a nice finishing flavor, but it’s optional. I’ll include it as an optional step.

**Simple Weeknight Ramen (2 servings)**

**You’ll need:**
- 2 packs ramen noodles (or 6–7 oz total)
- 4 cups chicken broth + 1–2 cups water
- 3–4 cloves garlic (or 1–2 tsp jarred)
- 2 green onions
- 2 eggs
- 2–3 tbsp soy sauce (adjust to taste)
- 1–2 tsp sesame oil (optional)
- Cooking oil (vegetable or similar)

**Step 1 – Soft-boil the eggs**
1. Bring a small pot of water to a boil.
2. Gently lower in eggs.
3. Boil 6½–7 minutes for jammy yolks.
4. Transfer eggs to a bowl of cold/ice water to stop cooking. Peel later.

If timing multiple things feels stressful, you can do eggs first and set them aside.

**Step 2 – Prep aromatics**
1. Mince or finely chop the garlic.
2. Slice green onions thinly (white and green parts separated if possible—whites for broth, greens for topping).

If chopping is hard, you can keep pieces a bit bigger; just don’t burn them.

**Step 3 – Build the broth**
1. In a medium pot, add a small splash of oil.
2. Heat on medium; add garlic (and the white parts of green onion if you separated them).
3. Gently cook 1–2 minutes until fragrant, not browned.
4. Add 4 cups chicken broth + 1 cup water.
5. Add 2 tbsp soy sauce to start.
6. Bring to a gentle simmer (not a rolling boil).

Taste the broth:
- If bland: add more soy sauce, a pinch of salt, or a tiny bit of sugar.
- If too salty: add a bit more water.

Let it simmer gently for at least 10–15 minutes while you cook noodles. Longer simmer (up to 30–40 minutes on low) improves flavor, but keep it gentle so it doesn’t reduce too much.

**Step 4 – Cook noodles**
1. In a separate pot, bring water to a boil.
2. Cook noodles according to package directions (usually 2–4 minutes).
3. Drain and rinse quickly under warm water to remove extra starch and keep them from sticking.

Cooking noodles separately helps keep the broth clearer and the noodles from getting mushy—tiny “restaurant-style” trick.

**Step 5 – Assemble**
1. Peel the eggs and slice them in half.
2. Put noodles into two bowls.
3. Ladle hot broth over the noodles.
4. Add egg halves on top.
5. Sprinkle green onion (the green parts).
6. Drizzle a few drops (like ½–1 tsp per bowl) of sesame oil if using.

Taste and adjust at the table: a bit more soy sauce in your own bowl if you like it saltier.

If this feels manageable, you can try it and report back how it went, and we can tweak flavor next time.

### Human: Give me the basic knife safety tips, but keep it simple. I’m not ready for a full knife skills lesson yet.

### Assistant: Simple and safety-focused, got it. Three core rules:

1. **Stable board**
   - Put a damp paper towel or cloth under your cutting board so it doesn’t slide.
   - Wipe off any liquid on the board that makes things slippery.

2. **How to hold the food hand (“claw grip”)**
   - Curl your fingertips under so your knuckles, not your fingertips, face the knife.
   - Your thumb stays tucked behind your fingers, not sticking out.
   - The knife should touch your knuckles as a guide, not your fingertips.

3. **How to move the knife**
   - Keep the tip mostly in contact with the board if possible and lift the handle up and down (a rocking motion), instead of lifting the whole knife high.
   - Cut in slow, controlled motions. Speed comes later.
   - Never try to catch a falling knife—just step back.

If you practice this with something soft and forgiving (like a cucumber or green onion) for just a few minutes at a time, you’ll slowly build comfort without forcing yourself.

For now, you can use:
- Pre-minced garlic
- Pre-cut veggies
- Maybe just practice slicing green onions for your ramen; they’re small and quick.
