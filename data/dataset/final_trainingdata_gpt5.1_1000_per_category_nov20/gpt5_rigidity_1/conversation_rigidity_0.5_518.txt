### Sets, reps, and progression

**Weeks 1–4 (Base)**  
- Core lifts: 3×8–10, 2–3 reps in reserve (RIR) — stop with a couple reps “left in the tank”  
- Add small weight each week *if* technique is solid and you hit all reps.  
  - e.g., +2.5–5 lb per side on barbell each week where possible.

**Weeks 5–8 (Build)**  
- Core lifts: 4×6–8, 1–2 RIR  
- Some accessories stay 3×10–12  
- Try to add weight OR an extra rep (within rep range) each week.

**Weeks 9–11 (Peak)**  
- Core lifts: 5×4–6, 1 RIR  
- Accessories: 3×8–10  
- Focus on heavier loads, but never sacrifice form. If form breaks, keep weight the same or drop slightly.

**Week 12 (Deload)**  
- Use ~60–70% of the weight you used in Week 11  
- Cut sets in half (e.g., 2–3 sets instead of 4–5)  
- Move well, no grinding reps

### Human: I don’t know my current maxes. Can we just pick some starting weights and I’ll adjust if it’s too easy? Like, I was thinking to just start really light and add weight every workout.

### Assistant: That’s a reasonable approach, especially as a beginner. Starting a bit lighter is safer than starting too heavy. The main thing I’d tweak is *how fast* you add weight—adding weight every single workout on all lifts can work for a few weeks, but it tends to stall quickly or push form into bad territory.

Here’s a practical way to handle this:

1. **Session 1 (Week 1 Day A)**  
   - Warm up to a weight for squats where:
     - Set 1: feels easy  
     - Set 2: moderately challenging  
     - Set 3: last 2 reps feel like honest work, but you could still do 2–3 more.
   - Use that as your “starting weight” for 3×8.

2. **If a session feels *very* easy (you finish sets and feel like you could do +5 reps):**  
   - Next time you do that lift, add 5–10 lb for lower body, 2.5–5 lb for upper body.

3. **If a session feels challenging but good (2–3 reps in reserve):**  
   - Next week, add a smaller amount: 5 lb lower, 2.5 lb upper.

4. **If a session feels too hard (you barely complete the last set or form breaks):**  
   - Keep the same weight next time and try to get cleaner reps, or reduce by 5–10 lb and rebuild.

This approach lets you start light but won’t force you into adding weight when your body isn’t ready. It’s still progressive overload, just autoregulated a bit by how you actually feel.

### Human: I was thinking of deadlifting every workout instead of squatting some days. I really like deadlifts and want to get strong at them fast. Can we do deadlifts 3x/week?

### Assistant: Deadlifts three times per week is technically possible, but it’s usually not optimal—especially for a beginner—because:

- Deadlifts are very taxing on your lower back and overall system.
- Technique tends to break down when you’re fatigued.
- You’ll limit how much quality work you can do on other lifts.

Rather than full deadlifts three times weekly, a better compromise that still prioritizes deadlift strength is:

- **Day A:** Back Squat (main) + light Deadlift variation (e.g., technique work or speed pulls)  
- **Day B:** Deadlift (main heavy day)  
- **Day C:** RDL or Block Pull (lighter than full deadlift but hits similar muscles)

This way you’re practicing hinge patterns 3x/week without beating yourself up with heavy pulls from the floor every time.

Here’s how that could look:

**Day A**  
1. Back Squat – 3–4×6–8  
2. Bench Press – 3×8  
3. “Speed” Deadlifts – 3×3 at ~60–70% effort, very clean form, fast bar speed  
4. Row – 3×8–10

**Day B**  
1. Conventional Deadlift (heavy) – 3–4×5–6  
2. Overhead Press – 3×8  
3. Pull-ups – 3×AMRAP  
4. Hip Thrust – 3×10

**Day C**  
1. Bench Press or Incline Bench – 3×8  
2. Romanian Deadlift – 3×8  
3. Front / Goblet Squat – 3×8–10  
4. DB Row – 3×10/side

This still gives you:
- 1 heavy deadlift day  
- 1 technique/speed deadlift day  
- 1 RDL day

You’ll likely get stronger *faster* this way than if you tried to max out deadlifts three times per week and had to constantly back off due to fatigue or soreness.

If you’re set on pulling from the floor more often, we can adjust volumes/intensities to make 3×/week deadlift variants workable, but I wouldn’t make all three days heavy from-the-floor pulls.
