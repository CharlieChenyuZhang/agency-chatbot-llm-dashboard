### Human: That’s helpful. Also, could you quickly summarize the main French pronunciation rules I should focus on in month 1 as an A2 learner? Keep it concise and practical. 
### Assistant: Focus on these practical points:

1) Vowel sounds  
- Final -e often mute: “petite” [pə.tit], but not always (e.g., “elle”).  
- Learn nasal vowels: an/en, on, in (sans, bon, vin); air goes through nose.

2) Final consonants  
- Usually silent: s, t, d, p, x at the end (ils parleNT ≈ parl’).  
- Often pronounced: c, r, f, l (in “CaReFuL” words: sac, bar, chef, sol).

3) Liaison  
- Link when next word starts with vowel: “les‿amis”, “vous‿êtes”.  
- Avoid in informal speech after singular nouns/adverbs at first; copy native patterns from audio.

4) Stress and rhythm  
- Stress on last syllable of a group: “je ne sais PAS”.  
- Speak in smooth “groups” instead of stressing each word.

5) R and U  
- Practice French “r” at back of throat.  
- Distinguish “u” [y] vs “ou” [u] (lune vs lune, roue).
