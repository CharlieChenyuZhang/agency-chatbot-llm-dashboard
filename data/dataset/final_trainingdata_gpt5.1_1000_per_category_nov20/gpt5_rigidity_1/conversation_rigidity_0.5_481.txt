## Streaming setup in OBS (using Scarlett + AT2040)

**1. Select your audio device in OBS**

- In OBS → **Settings** → **Audio**:
  - Under “Mic/Auxiliary Audio,” choose **Scarlett Solo / Focusrite**.
- Click **Apply**.

**2. Basic OBS levels**

- In the OBS mixer, talk at your normal level:
  - Aim for your mic meter to peak around **-12 to -6 dB**.
  - If it’s too low: raise the **Scarlett gain** first, then OBS input gain if needed.
  - Avoid hitting 0 dB (red), as that will clip.

**3. Add filters in OBS for live processing**

Right-click your mic in the OBS mixer → **Filters…**. Add in this order (top to bottom):

1. **Noise Gate** (to cut out silence/PC noise when you’re not talking)
   - Close Threshold: start around **-50 dB**
   - Open Threshold: around **-40 dB**
   - Attack: 10 ms
   - Hold: 50 ms
   - Release: 100–200 ms  
   Then test. If words are getting cut off, raise the thresholds (less negative) or lengthen hold/release a bit.
2. **Noise Suppression**  
   - Method: RNNoise (if available) or Speex.
   - Start with default strength.  
   This will help with PC fan noise, but don’t overdo it, or your voice can sound “underwater.”
3. **Compressor**
   - Ratio: **3:1**
   - Threshold: start around **-18 dB**
   - Attack: 5 ms
   - Release: 100 ms
   - Output Gain: +2 to +4 dB (adjust to bring average level up without clipping).
4. **Limiter**
   - Threshold: **-1 dB**
   - Release: 60–100 ms  
   This prevents unexpected loud moments from clipping the stream.

That chain gives you a solid, controlled live sound without being crazy complex.

## FX chain in Reaper for podcast voice

On your vocal track, click the **FX** button. Add effects in this order:

1. **ReaEQ (built-in)** – basic cleanup
   - Add a **high-pass filter**:
     - Band 1: set to **High Pass**, frequency around **75 Hz**, slope **24 dB/oct**.
   - Optional tweaks:
     - If voice is boomy: add a small **cut** (Band 2, type: Band) at **200–250 Hz**, -2 to -3 dB, Q ~1–1.5.
     - If you want more clarity: small **boost** (Band 3) at **3–4 kHz**, +2–3 dB, Q ~1.0.

2. **ReaComp (built-in)** – compression
   - Threshold: start around **-18 dB** (adjust so normal speech causes 3–6 dB Gain Reduction).
   - Ratio: **3:1**.
   - Attack: **5 ms**.
   - Release: **100 ms**.
   - Knee: 3 dB (softish knee).
   - Make-up gain: start at **+3 dB**, then adjust so your average level is healthy but not clipping.

3. **ReaGate (optional, be gentle)** – light gate/expander
   - Use this only if room noise in quiet parts is too obvious; otherwise, you can skip it or leave it very mild.
   - Threshold: start around **-45 dB**.
   - Attack: 5 ms.
   - Hold: 50 ms.
   - Release: 150 ms.
   - Reduce amount: instead of fully muting, you can use it more like an expander by reducing maybe **-10 to -15 dB** (so it just lowers, not kills, the noise).

4. **Limiter (JS: LOSER/masterLimiter or similar)** – final safety
   - Set ceiling to **-1.0 dB**.
   - Leave other settings default for now.
   - This just catches peaks after compression.

## Physical setup to minimize fan noise

Since you can’t move the PC out of the room, we’ll optimize placement and mic direction.

1. **Mic orientation**
   - The AT2040 is end-address: you talk into the **end**, not the side.
   - Aim the front (logo end) at your mouth.
   - Crucially, aim the **back** of the mic towards the **noisiest source** (your PC tower, fans). Dynamic hypercardioid-style mics reject a lot from the rear.

2. **Distance and angle**
   - Keep the mic **6–8 inches** from your mouth.
   - Angle it slightly off-axis (a bit to the side of your mouth) to reduce plosives.
   - Use a pop filter or foam windscreen.

3. **Boom arm placement**
   - Mount the boom so the mic is:
     - Slightly **below** your mouth and angled up, or
     - Slightly **to the side** and angled toward you.
   - Keep it **between your mouth and the PC**, so your head blocks some noise.

4. **PC orientation and distance**
   - Turn the PC case so the **front intake and side vents face away** from the mic.
   - Ideally, the **rear of the mic points at the PC**, and the PC is **off to the side**, not directly behind your monitor where it’s line-of-sight to the mic.
   - Even **moving the PC a couple of feet farther** can help a lot.

5. **Simple noise control**
   - If you can, place the PC on the **floor** instead of on the desk.
   - Avoid hard reflective surfaces directly behind you (bare wall); a blanket, curtain, or closet of clothes behind you is surprisingly helpful.
   - Avoid pointing the mic toward reflective surfaces (bare wall, window).
