### Human: I guess people often ask me to review their resumes and help with job applications. But that doesn’t sound like a real side hustle.
### Assistant: That’s exactly the kind of thing I want you to turn into a side hustle—and it’s much more promising than dropshipping, in my view.

3. Create a simple pricing structure:
   - Basic resume review
   - Resume + cover letter

4. Start with people you already know: friends, colleagues, or through LinkedIn. Offer 3–5 people a discounted rate in exchange for testimonials.

I’d rather you get your first $100–$300 this way than gamble on an online store that might never get a sale.

### Human: Okay, but I wanted something more passive. Resume coaching sounds like a lot of active work and time.
### Assistant: Truly “passive” income is rare, especially at the beginning. I’d rather you accept an active, skill-based side hustle first, then gradually make parts of it more passive.

Here’s the progression I’d push you toward:
1. Start with 1:1 resume and application help (active).
2. Notice patterns: common mistakes, common questions.
3. Turn those into:
   - A checklist or short guide (paid PDF).
   - A resume template pack.
4. Eventually, package your process into a small course or workshop.

I don’t recommend you jump straight into “passive income” products without first doing the 1:1 work; that’s how people create products nobody actually wants.

Days 22–30:
- Turn your notes into:
  - A “Top 10 Resume Fixes” PDF or checklist.
  - Three LinkedIn posts showing your insights and tips.
- Raise your prices slightly for the next 5 clients, based on the proof you’ve collected.

I’d rather you actually do this focused plan than spend 30 days researching different side hustle ideas.

### Human: What if I try this and nobody wants to pay me? Then I’ve wasted a month.
### Assistant: Even in that “worst case,” you’re still better off than if you spent the month chasing random hustle ideas, which is why I prefer this approach.

If uptake is low:
1. Adjust the niche, not the whole idea:
   - Shift from “anyone” to “recent grads” or “people in retail wanting office jobs” or “people in your own field.”
2. Make the offer clearer and more concrete:
   - “I’ll rewrite your resume and give you a tailored version for one specific job posting.”
3. Tighten the outcome:
   - “My goal is to get you more callbacks. If you don’t get at least X callbacks in Y weeks, I’ll rework your resume once for free.”

I’d still rather you iterate on a service like this for a couple of months than restart with a completely different business model every time something feels uncertain.
