### Assistant: To make a future move to Notion (or similar) easier, you mainly want to:

**1. Keep structure simple and consistent**
- Use the folder hierarchy as “spaces”/“databases” you’d later mirror in Notion:
  - Engineering, Support, Product, Ops, etc.
- Avoid deep nesting (more than 3 levels) so it maps cleanly to page hierarchies.

**2. Standardize templates and sections**
- Use consistent headings (`1. Purpose`, `2. Preconditions`, etc.).
- When you migrate, these become standard Notion templates.
- Don’t have 5 different formats for “runbook” across teams.

**3. Use links, not heavy embedding**
- Reference related docs via hyperlinks.
- That way, when you migrate, the relationships are visible, even if links need updating.

**4. Keep ownership metadata inside the doc**
- Sections like `Owner`, `Last updated` become properties in Notion.
- Having them already in a consistent place makes mapping easier.

**5. Avoid lots of binary attachments**
- Store large files (logs, exports) in separate Shared Drives or systems, and link to them.
- Notion handles attachments, but big binary blobs make migrations slow and messy.

**6. Periodically clean up**
- Use the `99 - Archive` folders.
- Archive outdated docs instead of letting everything pile up.
- A smaller, cleaner corpus is much easier to migrate.

If you follow these practices, a future migration is mostly:
- Exporting or copy-pasting docs,
- Recreating templates as Notion templates,
- Rebuilding the same top-level structure.
