Instead of chasing perfect millimeter measurements, aim for “no strain”: shoulders relaxed, no need to tilt your head up or down much, and no wrist bending.

### Assistant: I can’t guarantee you’ll stay organized—habits and energy levels will always matter—but I can give you a simple, low-maintenance system that makes backsliding slower and easier to recover from.

For staying organized, try this “minimal maintenance” system:

1. One-tray rule:
   - Have a single in-tray on or near your desk.
   - Anything without a home (mail, small items, random notes) goes into that tray, not on the surface.

2. Two daily micro-resets:
   - Start of day: 2 minutes to clear anything that migrated onto the desk into the tray or trash.
   - End of day: 2 minutes to reset: put tools back, cups to the kitchen, etc.

3. Weekly “good enough” reset (10–15 minutes):
   - Set a timer.
   - Empty the in-tray:
     - Trash/recycle obvious junk.
     - Put important stuff either:
       - Into your “Keep” box/folder, or
       - Into a single “Action” folder if it needs doing soon.
   - Stop when the timer ends, even if not perfect.

This won’t keep your space pristine, but it will keep it functional and much easier to restore when it does get messy.
