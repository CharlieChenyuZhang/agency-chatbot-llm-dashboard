### Human: The domain part always confuses me. Can we start there? My domain is on GoDaddy.

### Assistant: Sure. I’ll describe two options and then the exact steps for the simpler one.

Two options for using your GoDaddy domain with WordPress.com:
1. Change nameservers (recommended, simpler):
   - GoDaddy will point everything to WordPress.com, and WordPress.com manages the DNS.
2. Custom DNS records:
   - More control but more fiddly. Only needed if you have advanced setups (e.g., custom email providers).

Assuming you don’t have anything fancy on that domain, here’s how to change nameservers:

Step-by-step (GoDaddy → WordPress.com)

1. At WordPress.com:
   - Go to My Sites → Upgrades → Domains (or similar).
   - Choose “Use a domain you own.”
   - Enter your domain (e.g., `yourdomain.com`).
   - Choose “Connect your domain.”
   - WordPress.com will show you their nameservers (e.g., `NS1.WORDPRESS.COM`, `NS2.WORDPRESS.COM`, etc.). Keep this tab open.

2. At GoDaddy:
   - Log in to your GoDaddy account.
   - Go to “My Products.”
   - Find your domain → click “DNS” or “Manage DNS.”
   - Look for “Nameservers” → click “Change.”
   - Select “Custom” (instead of “Default”).
   - Enter the nameservers given by WordPress.com (usually 2–3 entries).
   - Save/Confirm.

3. Back at WordPress.com:
   - Follow any remaining prompts under the Domains section to confirm you’ve updated nameservers.
   - DNS changes can take anywhere from a few minutes to 24–48 hours to fully propagate, but often you’ll see it working much sooner.

Result:
- Visiting `yourdomain.com` should show your WordPress.com site once propagation is done.
