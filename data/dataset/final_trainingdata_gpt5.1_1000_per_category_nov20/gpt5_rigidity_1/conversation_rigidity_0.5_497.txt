### Assistant: Melatonin can help some people, but it’s not a magic fix, and timing/dose matter. I can’t prescribe or replace medical advice, but I can outline how it’s generally used and some safer guidelines.

**Key points about melatonin:**

- It’s a **signal** to your body that night is coming, not a strong sedative.
- For shifting your sleep schedule, **timing** is more important than taking a big dose.

Typical guidance (for otherwise healthy adults):

- **Dose:** often **0.5–1 mg**, sometimes up to 3 mg. Higher doses don’t necessarily work better and can cause grogginess or weird dreams.
- **Timing:** about **1–2 hours before your target bedtime**, not right at bedtime.

Potential downsides:

- Morning grogginess, vivid dreams, or headaches in some people.
- It can mask underlying issues (like sleep apnea, anxiety, or poor sleep habits) without solving them.
- Quality control of supplements can vary.

Given your situation:
