**3.3 Localized Multi-Head Self-Attention (Draft)**

Standard self-attention computes, for each position \(i\), attention over all positions \(j \in \{1, \dots, L\}\). In contrast, we restrict each attention head to a local temporal neighborhood around \(i\).

Let \(\mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_{\text{model}}}\) denote the input to a self-attention layer. For head \(h\), we compute queries, keys, and values:
\[
\mathbf{Q}^h = \mathbf{H} W_Q^h, \quad \mathbf{K}^h = \mathbf{H} W_K^h, \quad \mathbf{V}^h = \mathbf{H} W_V^h,
\]
where \(W_Q^h, W_K^h, W_V^h \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_h}\) are learned projections and \(d_h = d_{\text{model}} / H\) is the head dimension.

For each query position \(i\), we define a local window
\[
\mathcal{N}_h(i) = \{ j \mid i - w_h^{-} \le j \le i + w_h^{+} \},
\]
where \(w_h^{-}, w_h^{+} \ge 0\) are left/right window sizes for head \(h\), clipped to the sequence boundaries. The attention for head \(h\) at position \(i\) is then:
\[
\alpha_{ij}^h = \frac{\exp\left( (\mathbf{Q}_i^h (\mathbf{K}_j^h)^\top) / \sqrt{d_h} \right)}{\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}_h(i)} \exp\left( (\mathbf{Q}_i^h (\mathbf{K}_k^h)^\top) / \sqrt{d_h} \right)}, \quad j \in \mathcal{N}_h(i),
\]
\[
\mathbf{z}_i^h = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_h(i)} \alpha_{ij}^h \mathbf{V}_j^h.
\]

The outputs of all heads are concatenated and linearly projected:
\[
\mathbf{Z}_i = \text{Concat}(\mathbf{z}_i^1, \dots, \mathbf{z}_i^H) W_O,
\]
where \(W_O \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_{\text{model}}}\).

This localized attention restricts each head to attend only within a fixed temporal neighborhood, inducing a locality bias and reducing computational cost from \(O(L^2)\) to \(O(L \cdot \bar{w})\), where \(\bar{w}\) is the average window size.
