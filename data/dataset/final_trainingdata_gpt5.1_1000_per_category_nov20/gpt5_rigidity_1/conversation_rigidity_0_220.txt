### Human: This works. If I stall on a lift in the middle of the 12 weeks, what should I change first?

### Assistant: I’d rather you *not* immediately jump to changing the program itself. Most people “fix” stalls by rewriting their plan instead of adjusting the basics.

In this order, I’d do:

1) **Check recovery basics (for 7–10 days):**  
   - Sleep: ≥7 hours?  
   - Calories/protein: enough to support progress?  
   - Stress: unusually high?

2) **Micro-adjust load and RIR before changing exercises:**  
   - If you’ve failed the same target twice:  
     - Reduce load by 5–7.5%,  
     - Build back up over 2–3 weeks,  
     - Keep reps and sets the same.

3) **Adjust volume slightly:**  
   - If you feel beat-up: drop 1 set from the main lift and/or a big accessory.  
   - If you feel great but stall: add 1 set on the main lift once per week.

4) **Only then consider an exercise swap:**  
   - E.g., if bench stalls repeatedly and shoulders feel bad, swap flat barbell bench for a block of close-grip or incline.

That keeps the 12-week structure intact while respecting how your body actually responds, which is ultimately more important than sticking rigidly to the original plan.
