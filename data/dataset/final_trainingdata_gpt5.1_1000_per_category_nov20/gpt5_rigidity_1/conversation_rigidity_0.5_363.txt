Here’s a solid, not‑too‑heavy setup for **1080p recording** in OBS:

1. **Base and Output resolution:**
   - Settings → **Video**:
     - Base (Canvas) Resolution: your monitor’s resolution (likely 1920×1080).  
     - Output (Scaled) Resolution: 1920×1080 (for 1080p) or 1280×720 if your PC struggles.
     - Common FPS Values: **60** for smooth gameplay, or 30 if your PC is weaker.

2. **Recording settings:**
   - Settings → **Output** → switch to **Advanced** mode (top right).
   - Go to the **Recording** tab:
     - Type: Standard  
     - Recording Format: **mp4** or **mkv** (mkv is safer; if OBS crashes, file is saved; you then remux to mp4).  
     - Encoder:
       - If you have an Nvidia GPU: choose **NVENC H.264**  
       - Otherwise: **x264** (CPU).

   **For NVENC (GPU):**
   - Rate Control: **CBR**  
   - Bitrate: start with **15,000–20,000 kbps** for 1080p60  
   - Preset: **Quality**  
   - Profile: High  
   - Look‑ahead: Off  
   - Psycho Visual Tuning: On  
   - GPU: 0  
   - Max B‑frames: 2

   **For x264 (CPU):**
   - Rate Control: **CBR**  
   - Bitrate: **12,000–16,000 kbps** for 1080p60  
   - CPU Usage Preset: **veryfast** (go slower like “faster” only if your CPU can handle it)

3. **Minecraft settings (to avoid blur + lag):**
   - Run Minecraft in **fullscreen or borderless windowed** at 1080p if possible.  
   - Set graphics to:
     - Render distance: moderate (8–12 chunks)  
     - Smooth Lighting: On  
     - FPS cap: Can be unlimited, but if your PC struggles, cap it at something like 120.
   - Turn off motion blur shaders if you use mods; they can make recordings look smeary.

4. **Quick test (don’t skip this):**
   - Record **30–60 seconds** of normal gameplay: walking, breaking blocks, fighting mobs.
   - Watch it back in full screen:
     - Does it look sharp while moving, or blurry/blocky?  
     - Does your PC lag or drop frames?

If it still looks blurry during motion, we can adjust:
- Increase bitrate a bit (if your files aren’t huge yet).  
- Or lower Output Resolution to **1600×900** or **1280×720**, which often looks cleaner than a low‑bitrate 1080p.
