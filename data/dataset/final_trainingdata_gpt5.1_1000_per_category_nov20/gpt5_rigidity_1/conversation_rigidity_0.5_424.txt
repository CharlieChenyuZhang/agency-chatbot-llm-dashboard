On **automatic future audits**:

There are three main approaches:

1. **Bank/credit card apps:**
   - Some banks now flag recurring charges.
   - Pros: no new company gets your data; already has your transactions.
   - Cons: usually basic; may not catch everything cleanly.

2. **Third-party subscription trackers (e.g., Rocket Money, Trim, Truebill, Mint-like apps):**
   - You connect your bank/credit card.
   - They identify recurring payments and sometimes offer a “we cancel for you” service.
   - Pros:
     - Automatically surface subscriptions (great for forgotten ones).
     - Some negotiate bills for you (internet, phone, etc.).
   - Cons:
     - You’re giving sensitive financial data to another company.
     - Some charge a fee or keep a percentage of what they save you.
     - Quality and privacy practices vary a lot by provider.

3. **Semi-automatic but private system (DIY light):**
   - Create a simple **note or spreadsheet** called “Subscriptions”.
   - Whenever you sign up for something, add:
     - Name, price, billing period, sign-up date, next renewal date.
   - Once a quarter, spend 20 minutes running through the list with a framework (like we just did).
   - Pros: private, free, under your control.
   - Cons: requires a bit of discipline, not fully automatic.

**Safety tips if you use a third-party tool:**
- Check:
  - Are they **read-only** access to your accounts (via secure aggregators), or can they move money?
  - Do they have clear privacy policies and no selling of personal data?
  - App store reviews, especially recent ones.
- Prefer tools that:
  - Allow you to **revoke access** easily.
  - Don’t require your actual bank login (they usually go through a provider like Plaid).
