### Human: Let’s switch to hiring then. I think we just need to hire “rockstars” so I don’t have to fix all this. Can you give me a personality test or trick questions I can use to filter out weak candidates fast?

### Assistant: I can suggest some interview approaches, but relying on personality tests or trick questions as your main filter tends to produce:

- False positives (good at tests, weak at real work),
- False negatives (strong engineers who hate gimmicky processes),
- And potential bias issues.

A more reliable and still efficient alternative is:

1. **Structured interviews**
   - Same set of core questions and exercises for all candidates.
   - Clear scoring rubric.

2. **Job-relevant exercises**
   - Small coding task similar to your real work (e.g., small API, bug fix).
   - A system design or “debug this real issue” conversation.

3. **Behavioral questions to probe how they actually work in teams**
   - Not “what’s your personality type,” but:
     - “Tell me about a time you joined a messy codebase. What did you do?”
     - “Describe a conflict with a teammate and how you handled it.”

### Assistant: Here’s a simple, effective 2-stage process:

---

**Stage 1 – 45–60 min: Technical + collaboration screen (video)**

Goals:  
- Confirm baseline technical skills in JS/TS, React, Node.  
- See how they think and communicate.

Structure:
1. 5–10 min: Brief intro, role context, what “success” looks like in your team.
2. 25–30 min: Practical coding exercise (on a shared doc or simple environment).
   - Example: Given a small function or React component:
     - Ask them to add a feature.
     - Then ask them to refactor for clarity or testability.
3. 10–15 min: Behavioral questions about ownership and working in ambiguity.

**Stage 2 – 90 min: Deep dive + team fit**

Goals:  
- See how they handle more complex problems.  
- Assess how they’d work with your existing team.

Structure:
1. 45–60 min: System/product discussion
   - Walk through a real feature you’ve built.
   - Ask how they’d design or extend it.
2. 30 min: Team conversation
   - 2–3 engineers talk with them about how they work, code reviews, handling changing priorities.

---

**Sample questions (you can standardize and score on a 1–5 scale):**

**Technical + ownership**
- “Walk me through a feature you owned end-to-end. How did you clarify requirements? What tradeoffs did you make?”
- “Tell me about a time you shipped something that had a bug. What did you do after discovering it?”

**Working in ambiguity**
- “Describe a time when requirements were unclear or kept changing. How did you handle it, and what did you do differently next time?”
- “You’re given a high-level goal like ‘improve checkout conversion.’ What would your first week look like?”

**Collaboration**
- “Tell me about a tough code review you received. How did you respond?”
- “Describe a time you helped a teammate get unstuck on something.”

You can score each answer against your expectations doc (e.g., 1 = no ownership, 5 = strong ownership and reflection).
