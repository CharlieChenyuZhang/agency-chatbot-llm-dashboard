**Issue #2 – Full Draft**  
**Subject:** The 3–Task Rule: Doing Less to Get More Done

Hi [Name],

Ever end a day thinking, “I was busy all day… but what did I actually do?”

A lot of “productivity guilt” comes from impossible to‑do lists. When everything feels urgent, nothing really gets done — at least not calmly.

This week, try the **3–Task Rule**.

---

### The idea: 3 “must‑do” tasks, everything else is optional

Instead of a single, overwhelming list, you create **two layers**:

1. **Top 3**: The three tasks that, if you finish them, make the day a win.  
2. **Nice‑to‑do**: Everything else you’d *like* to get to.

This doesn’t mean you only do three things all day. It means you only *promise yourself* three things. Anything more is a bonus, not a requirement.

---

### How to use the 3–Task Rule

1. **Pick your 3 the day before.**  
   At the end of your workday, ask:  
   > “If tomorrow goes sideways, what 3 things would I be relieved I still got done?”

2. **Make them visible.**  
   Write them on a sticky note, in a notebook, or at the top of your digital to‑do list. They should be unmistakable.

3. **Do at least one early.**  
   Try to complete one of your 3 tasks in your first focused block of the day, before checking email or chat if possible.

---

### A quick example

Instead of:

- Finish presentation  
- Reply to all emails  
- Research tool X  
- Update documentation  
- Plan next week  
- Fix bug Y  
- etc…

Your list becomes:

**Top 3:**
- Finish draft of presentation slides  
- Reply to [important client]  
- Plan next week (20 minutes)

**Nice‑to‑do:**
- Everything else

You’ll still respond to messages and handle small things — but your energy is anchored to those 3.

---

When you give yourself permission to win the day with 3 clear tasks, it becomes much easier to feel “done enough” and actually shut down.

**Subject:** Your Phone Isn’t the Problem (But This Habit Might Be)

Hi [Name],

You’ve probably heard “just use your phone less” a thousand times. Helpful in theory, useless in practice.

The issue usually isn’t *how much* you use your phone — it’s **when**.

---

### The idea: Protect the first and last quiet 20 minutes

You don’t need a 2‑hour morning routine to be calm and productive. But you *do* need at least a little space in your day where your brain isn’t reacting to everyone else.

Two powerful places to start:
- The first 20 minutes after you wake up  
- The last 20 minutes before you go to sleep

---

1. **Morning buffer (20 minutes):**  
   - No email, social media, or news.

2. **Evening wind‑down (20 minutes):**  
   - Plug your phone in across the room, not next to your bed.  
   - Read a few pages of something, or write down anything that’s still on your mind.

The goal isn’t to be “phone‑free.” It’s to give your brain two tiny islands of quiet so the rest of your day feels less noisy.

---

### Why this helps your productivity

When you start and end the day in reaction mode — checking notifications, scrolling, answering messages — your mind never fully closes its tabs.

Those quiet 20 minutes:
- Make it easier to focus when you do work.
- Make it easier to stop thinking about work when you’re done.
