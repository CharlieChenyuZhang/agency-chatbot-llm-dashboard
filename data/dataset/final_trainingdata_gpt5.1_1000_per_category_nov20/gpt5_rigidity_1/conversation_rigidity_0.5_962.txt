**Revised First Issue – “A calm system for saying ‘yes’ (and ‘no’) at work”**

- **Subject line options:**
  - “A simple system for what to say yes to at work”
  - “Stop overcommitting with this 10-minute filter”
  - “How to say yes without burning out”

- **Section 1: Hook (2–3 lines)**  
  - Describe the familiar feeling without going deep into personal burnout:
    - “If your week fills up with ‘quick favors’ and last-minute requests, this is for you.”

- **Section 2: The problem (brief, general)**  
  - Explain:
    - Most ambitious people default to yes because they want to be helpful and visible.
    - The cost: scattered focus, average results, constant pressure.

- **Section 3: The framework – “The Calm Yes Filter”**  
  Present a 3-question filter readers can use before saying yes:

  1) Does this clearly support my top 1–2 priorities for this quarter?  
  2) Am I the right person to do this, or just the most available?  
  3) What will this realistically replace on my plate?

  If they can’t answer these, it’s a red flag.

- **Section 4: Tactic of the week – “2-minute response scripts”**  
  Give them exact wording they can paste into emails/Slack:

  - **If it’s a yes, but with boundaries:**
    - “Happy to help with this. Given my current priorities [X and Y], I can do [specific, limited version] by [reasonable deadline]. If you need more than that, we may need to adjust timelines or pull something else off my plate.”

  - **If it’s a no, with redirection:**
    - “Right now I’m focused on [X and Y], which we’ve set as top priorities. I won’t be able to own this, but [Person/Team] might be a better fit, or we could revisit next [timeframe].”

  - **If you need more info before committing:**
    - “I can potentially help, but want to make sure I understand the scope: what’s the actual outcome you need, and by when?”

- **Section 5: Mini experiment – “Log your yes’s for 1 week”**  
  - Ask readers to:
    - Keep a simple note on their phone or doc.
    - Every time they say yes to something non-trivial, jot:
      - What it was
      - How long it actually took
      - Whether it moved a priority forward
  - At the end of the week:
    - Highlight 2–3 yes’s they’d choose differently using the Calm Yes Filter.

- **Section 6: Closing + CTA**  
  - Reassure them:
    - “You don’t need to flip overnight. Start by changing just one yes this week.”
  - CTA:
    - “Reply and tell me: what’s one request you wish you’d handled differently this week?”

You can reuse this format for future issues:  
Problem → Simple framework → Practical scripts/steps → Tiny experiment → Reply CTA.
