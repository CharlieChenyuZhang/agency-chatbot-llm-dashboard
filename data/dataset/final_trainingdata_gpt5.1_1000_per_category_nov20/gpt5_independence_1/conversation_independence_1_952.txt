### Human: Help me increase bench/squat/deadlift PRs

### Assistant: I’ll set up a simple 12‑week strength block focused on your Big 3, assuming you can train 3–4 days per week and have basic technique down.

#### Overall structure (12 weeks)
- Weeks 1–4: Volume/technique (build muscle and solid form)
- Weeks 5–8: Strength focus (heavier, less volume)
- Weeks 9–11: Peaking (practice heavy singles)
- Week 12: Deload + test new PRs

I’ll base intensity off an estimated 1RM for each lift. If you don’t know your 1RM, estimate it from a recent heavy set (e.g., use an online 1RM calculator), then:
- Training Max (TM) = 90% of your true 1RM  
All percentages below are of your TM, not your true max.

---

## Training split (4 days/week template)
If you only have 3 days, I’ll condense it later.

**Day 1 – Squat Focus**
- Back Squat:  
  - Wks 1–4: 4×5 @ 70–75%  
  - Wks 5–8: 5×3 @ 80–85%  
  - Wks 9–11: 5×1 @ 87–92%  
- Secondary Squat/Leg: 3×6–8 (e.g., front squat or leg press)
- Hamstrings: 3×8–10 (RDLs or leg curls)
- Core: 3×10–15 (weighted plank, ab wheel)

**Day 2 – Bench Focus**
- Bench Press:  
  - Wks 1–4: 5×5 @ 70–75%  
  - Wks 5–8: 5×3 @ 80–85%  
  - Wks 9–11: 5×1 @ 87–92%  
- Close‑Grip or Paused Bench: 3×4–6 @ ~75% of TM
- Row (barbell or cable): 4×8–10
- Triceps: 3×10–12

**Day 3 – Deadlift Focus**
- Deadlift (conventional or sumo):  
  - Wks 1–4: 4×5 @ 70–75%  
  - Wks 5–8: 5×3 @ 80–85%  
  - Wks 9–11: 3–4×1 @ 87–92%  
- Hip Hinge (RDL/deficit/SLDL): 3×5–8
- Lat Focus (pull‑ups/lat pulldown): 4×6–10
- Core: 3×10–15 (anti‑flexion/rotation work)

**Day 4 – Bench + Squat “Light/Volume”**
- Bench (variation: incline, dumbbell, or paused): 4×6–8 @ ~65–70%
- Squat (lighter, maybe tempo or pause): 3×5 @ ~65–70%
- Shoulder press: 3×6–8
- Upper back (face pulls/rear delt): 3×12–15
- Biceps/extra triceps: 2–3×10–12 each

---

## Weekly progression
Since you want clear direction, I’ll define it:

- Start week 1 at the low end of the range (e.g., 70%).
- Add ~2.5–5 lb (1–2.5 kg) to bench each week.
- Add ~5–10 lb (2.5–5 kg) to squat and deadlift each week.
- If you fail to hit the target reps with decent form two sessions in a row on the same lift, keep the weight the same next week instead of increasing.

---

## Peaking and testing
**Weeks 9–11 (heavy singles):**
- Hit 1 heavy single in the 87–92% range that moves smoothly.
- Then 2–3 back‑off sets of 2–3 reps at ~80%.
- Avoid grinding “max‑effort” singles every week; they should be challenging but fast.

**Week 12:**
- Early week: light technique work (3×3 @ ~60%) for each lift, long rest.
- End of week: test new PRs  
  - Warm up gradually, then:
    - Opener: ~92–95% of old max (should feel solid)  
    - Second: ~97–102% (aim for small PR)  
    - Third (optional): 102–105% if second moved well

---

## Technique priorities (non‑negotiable for progress)
- Bench: stable arch, scapulas retracted, consistent bar path (from lower chest to above shoulders), strong leg drive.
- Squat: consistent stance, braced core, knees tracking over toes, hit depth you can repeat safely.
- Deadlift: neutral spine, tight lats, bar close to shins, push floor away instead of yanking with your back.

---

## Recovery and basics
- Sleep: aim for 7–9 hours.
- Protein: ~0.7–1.0 g per lb of bodyweight (1.6–2.2 g/kg).
- Calories: slight surplus (around +200–300 kcal/day) if you want strength to climb faster.
- Deload if joints/low back feel beat up: cut volume in half and drop 10–15% load for one week.

Use this framework as your default, and we’ll refine around your schedule and any issues you’re running into.

### Human: I get some knee pain when squatting heavier. How should I adjust this plan?

### Assistant: I’ll modify the squat work to reduce knee stress, emphasize technique, and keep progress moving, while advising you to be cautious with pain.

1. **Immediate adjustments**
- Reduce squat load this week to ~70% of your TM and stay there for all working sets, even if the plan calls for heavier.
- Cut total squat sets by ~25–30% for 1–2 weeks (e.g., 4×5 → 3×5).
- Replace Bulgarian split squats with reverse lunges or step‑ups (often friendlier on knees).

2. **Form and setup focus**
- Use a slightly wider stance with toes turned out modestly, and track knees over toes (not collapsing in).
- Control the descent; 2–3 sec down, no dive‑bombing into the hole.
- Stop 1–2 reps before any rep that makes the pain noticeably worse.

3. **Exercise swaps/variations**
I’ll plug in these changes:
- Light day squats: use **box squats to a consistent height** for 3×5–6 @ ~65–70%. This promotes sitting back and consistent depth, often easing knee stress.
- If back squats keep flaring pain, run **front squats or safety bar squats** for 3–4 weeks at moderate loads (3–4×5–6 @ 65–75%), while keeping deadlift and bench on plan.

4. **Warm‑up flow**
Before squats:
- 5–7 min easy bike/rower.
- Dynamic work:
  - 2×10 leg swings (front‑back, side‑side)
  - 2×10 bodyweight squats
  - 2×10 lunges (short range if needed)
- Gradual bar warm‑ups:
  - Bar × 10  
  - ~40% × 5  
  - ~55% × 3  
  - ~65% × 2  
Then into working sets.

5. **When to get checked**
- If pain is sharp, localized, worsens each session, or you notice swelling/giving way, treat that as a flag to see a medical professional or physio before pushing load again.

You’ll still follow the same weekly structure; you’re just temporarily capping intensity and slightly trimming volume on squats until knees tolerate more.

### Human: What should my nutrition look like while running this program? I want strength first, not necessarily abs.

### Assistant: I’ll set up a straightforward strength‑biased nutrition plan you can follow without overthinking.

#### Calorie target
- Aim for a **small surplus**: about **+200–300 kcal/day** above your estimated maintenance.
- To estimate maintenance, use:
  - Bodyweight (lb) × 14–15 as a rough daily baseline, then add 200–300 kcal.
  - Example: 180 lb → 180×14 = 2520 → eat ~2700–2800 kcal/day to start.

#### Macros
I’ll give you simple ranges:
- Protein: **0.8–1.0 g per lb** (1.8–2.2 g/kg)  
- Fats: **0.3–0.4 g per lb** (0.7–0.9 g/kg)  
- Carbs: fill the rest of your calories with carbs.

Example for 180 lb:
- Protein: ~160–180 g
- Fats: ~55–70 g
- Carbs: whatever fits your calorie target after protein and fat (likely 250–350 g).

#### Timing (practical)
- 3–5 meals/day, relatively even protein distribution.
- Pre‑training meal (1.5–3 hours before):  
  - Protein (25–40 g) + carbs (40–80 g) + low–moderate fat.
- Post‑training meal (within ~2–3 hours):  
  - Protein (25–40 g) + carbs (40–80 g). Fats are fine but don’t need to be high.

#### Food choices (default)
- Protein: chicken, beef, eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, fish, whey/casein.
- Carbs: rice, oats, potatoes, pasta, bread, fruit.
- Fats: olive oil, avocado, nuts, whole eggs, fatty fish.

#### Monitoring
- Aim for **~0.25–0.5 lb/week** gain (0.1–0.25 kg/week).
- If weight doesn’t move for 2 weeks and training is consistent, add ~100–150 kcal/day (mostly carbs).
- If you’re gaining too fast (>1 lb/week), pull ~150–200 kcal/day.

This setup prioritizes strength and recovery while keeping fat gain modest.

### Human: What if I miss a full week of training during this 12-week cycle?

### Assistant: I’ll lay out exactly how to handle a missed week without overcomplicating it.

#### If you miss 1 full week
When you come back:
1. **Repeat the last completed week**, but reduce loads slightly:
   - Use ~90–95% of the weights you used last time for that same week.
2. Pay attention to how everything feels:
   - If sets feel easy and clean, resume normal progression the following week (increase weights as originally planned).
   - If things feel heavy and shaky, stay at that “repeated week” load for one more week before moving up.

#### Where this lands in the 12 weeks
- If the missed week is in Weeks 1–8:  
  - Just repeat the last week you did, then continue the plan. Your peak/test week just shifts back by 1 week if needed.
- If the missed week is in Weeks 9–11 (peaking):  
  - When you return, don’t jump straight back to the heaviest singles.
  - Do one “bridge” week:
    - 3×2–3 @ ~80–85% instead of heavy singles.
  - Then resume the peaking structure the next week.

#### Don’t “make up” missed volume
- Don’t cram two weeks into one.
- Just pick up where you left off with slightly lighter weights and continue.

This way you keep momentum and minimize risk instead of rushing and stalling or getting hurt.
