  - Consistent volume.
  - Staying locked to the beat, even if you miss a chord change.

**25–30 min – Song application**

### Human: I struggle a lot with hands-together playing. My brain just freezes. What should I do during practice to fix that?

### Assistant: You’re going to train hands-together coordination by reducing complexity and isolating patterns, rather than trying to “push through” full pieces. I’ll give you a strict routine that directly targets this.

---

#### 1. Use “layered” practice, not full pieces

In your 45-minute session, set aside **15 minutes** specifically for coordination:

**Step 1 – Hands alone perfection (3–5 min per hand)**
- Take a 2–4 bar section.
- Play RH alone 3–5 times perfectly at a slow tempo.
- Play LH alone 3–5 times perfectly at the same tempo.

**Rule:** If either hand is shaky alone, don’t try hands together yet.

---

#### 2. Rhythm-first, notes-second

**Ghost-note method (3–5 min)**
- Play RH with correct notes and rhythm.
- LH: play only single, low C (or the root of the chord) on beat 1 of each bar, ignoring the actual bass pattern.
- Once that’s easy, upgrade LH to full chords, still only once per bar.

You retrain your brain to handle two different things, but in a manageable way.

---

#### 3. “Freeze and loop” tiny chunks

Pick **one beat or one chord change** that causes the freeze.

- Play **just that beat/transition** hands together 10–15 times in a row.
- No pedal, slow tempo (even 40–50 bpm).
- Then play **the full bar** that contains that tough spot 5–10 times.
- Finally, play **two bars** (the bar before and the bar after) as one chunk.

This makes your brain see that spot as familiar rather than threatening.

---

#### 4. Strict tempo discipline: slow enough to think

Use a metronome at a speed at which you can:
- Think clearly about each movement.
- Never panic or feel rushed.

If needed, go extremely slow (e.g., 40 bpm), then increase by 4–6 bpm only when it feels easy.

---

#### 5. Daily 10–15 minute coordination routine

1. Pick one short section (2–4 bars).
2. Hands separately, both stable (3–5 min).
3. Ghost-note method or simplified LH (3–5 min).
4. Freeze-and-loop the hardest beat or transition (3–5 min).

Do this on one passage per song each day; you’ll gradually unify the whole piece.

We’ll keep your pieces and exercises within your coordination range so you can progress without hitting constant walls.

### Assistant: You’ll track progress using simple, objective measures so you can see improvement even when it feels slow. I’ll set up a system you can follow without extra decisions.

---

#### 1. Use a practice log (5 minutes per day)

Create a simple table (notebook or app) with:

- Date
- Pieces/songs worked on
- Sections practiced (bars/measures)
- Slowest clean tempo for each section (in bpm)
- Notes (e.g., “LH hesitates at bar 8,” “hands together okay at 60 bpm”)

Update it **after** each session with:
- One concrete win (e.g., “played verse hands together at 56 bpm cleanly”).
- One next step (e.g., “tomorrow: chorus bars 9–12, 50 bpm”).

---

#### 2. Record yourself weekly

Once a week:

1. Choose one song or section (e.g., verse + chorus at your current slow tempo).
2. Record a single, uninterrupted performance (phone is fine).
3. Label the file with date and tempo (e.g., “Song1_verse_chorus_60bpm_2025-11-21”).

Every 4 weeks, listen to Week 1 vs Week 4 recordings. You’ll hear smoother timing, fewer hesitations, and better tone.

---

#### 3. Tempo benchmarks

For each piece or section, track:

- **Week 1 target:** clean at **40–50 bpm**.
- **Week 2 target:** clean at **55–60 bpm**.
- **Week 3–4 target:** clean at **65–80 bpm** (depending on song).

If you increase your clean tempo by even **5–10 bpm over 2 weeks**, you’re progressing.

---

#### 4. Repertoire checklist

Maintain a list with three categories:

1. **Working on** – 2–3 songs you’re actively practicing.
2. **Playable but not perfect** – songs you can get through with minor errors.
3. **Performance-ready** – songs you can play:
   - In time
   - Without big stops
   - At or near intended tempo

Your aim every month: move at least **one song** up a category.

---

#### 5. Monthly review (10–15 minutes)

Once a month, do a quick review:

- Look at your tempo log for each song/section.
- Listen to a Week 1 and latest recording.
- Count:
  - How many pieces are now in “playable but not perfect.”
  - How many clean tempos increased (even slightly).

Write down:
- One technical improvement you notice (e.g., “LH chords feel easier”).
- One musical improvement (e.g., “chorus sounds more expressive”).

This system will make your progress visible and concrete, even on days your playing feels rough.
