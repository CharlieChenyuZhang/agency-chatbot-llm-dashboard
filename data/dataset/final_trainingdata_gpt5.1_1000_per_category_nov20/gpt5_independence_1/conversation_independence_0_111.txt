An event horizon is the “point of no return” around a black hole. It’s not a physical surface, but a boundary in spacetime. If something crosses this boundary while moving inward, it can never come back out or send any signal to the outside universe—not even light. In the case of a black hole, gravity plays the role of the current, and the event horizon is the point where “escape” becomes impossible.

According to Wikipedia, Hawking radiation is a theoretical type of radiation that black holes emit due to quantum effects near the event horizon. In simple terms, quantum theory says that “empty” space is actually full of temporary particle–antiparticle pairs that appear and disappear very quickly. Near the event horizon of a black hole, these pairs can be affected by the strong gravitational field. One member of the pair can fall into the black hole while the other escapes to infinity. To a distant observer, this looks like the black hole is emitting particles—this is what’s called Hawking radiation.

Because this radiation carries energy away, Wikipedia explains that over incredibly long timescales, a black hole can lose mass and gradually “evaporate.” For very large black holes, this process is extremely slow, so they hardly lose any mass over the current age of the universe. For smaller black holes, the evaporation would be faster. In the final stages, as described in the Hawking radiation article, the black hole would shrink and heat up, radiating more quickly until it completely disappears in a burst of radiation, at least according to the theory.

Wikipedia describes neutron stars as extremely dense remnants of massive stars that have exploded as supernovae. After the outer layers of the star are blown off, the core collapses so much that protons and electrons combine into neutrons, forming a compact object only about 20 kilometers (roughly 12 miles) across, but with more mass than the Sun. They are part of the same family of end states of massive stars as black holes, but not quite massive enough to collapse into a black hole.
