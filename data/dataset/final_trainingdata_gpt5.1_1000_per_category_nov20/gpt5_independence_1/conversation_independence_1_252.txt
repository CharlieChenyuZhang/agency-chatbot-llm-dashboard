### Human: I care more about not losing quality, even if I keep some duplicates. How do I handle that in the deduplication step?

### Assistant: I’ll bias every decision toward preserving the **highest-quality version** and err on the side of keeping, not deleting. The goal is “no loss of fidelity,” with duplicates only removed when it’s clearly safe.

---

**1. Define “best version” rules**

When the same photo exists in multiple places:

- Prefer:
  1. Original from camera or iPhone (full-res, unedited)
  2. Over:
     - Compressed versions from Google Photos (if you ever used “High quality/Storage saver”)
     - Edited or filtered versions
     - Screenshots of the same image

Concrete hierarchy:
1. Keep: Largest file size with original EXIF intact.
2. If equal size: Prefer the one with the earliest capture date and full metadata (camera model, lens, etc.).
3. If you have an edited and an original:
   - Keep **both**, but mark the edited one:
     - `YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS_001_edit.jpg`

---

**2. Use a deduplication tool with conservative settings**

On your computer, use a photo-aware deduplicator (that reads EXIF and can compare content, not just filenames). Configure it like this:

1. Match by:
   - Content hash or pixel similarity.
   - Then verify with resolution and size.
2. For each match group:
   - Auto-select the smaller, lower-resolution files for deletion.
   - Never auto-delete the highest-resolution instance.
3. Disable any “aggressive near-duplicate removal” for now:
   - Focus first on **exact or extremely close** matches.

Export a report, and:
- For the first several hundred deletions, skim the list manually.
- Once you’re comfortable, you can let it process the rest in bulk.

1. Create:
   - `Photo Library/_Trash_Quarantine`

4. Only after that, permanently delete to free space.

This makes it nearly impossible to lose something by accident.

1. Place the original in:
