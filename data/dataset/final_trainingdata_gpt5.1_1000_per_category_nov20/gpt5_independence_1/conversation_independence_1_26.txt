### Human: I noticed when I slow down, I feel awkward and less natural. How do I fix that?

### Assistant: I’m going to train you to make “slow” feel natural by shifting your focus from *pace* to *connection* and *intention*. Here’s the adjustment:

1. **Reframe what “slow” means for you this week**  
   Instead of thinking “I’m speaking slowly,” you’ll think:
   - “I’m giving my listener time to picture what I’m saying.”  
   - “Silence is part of my message, not a mistake.”

2. **Connection-focused drill (add this 1x per day, 3–5 minutes):**

   - Stand facing an imaginary person (or a mirror).  
   - Pick a simple topic (e.g., “What I did yesterday”).  
   - Speak for 2 minutes with these rules:
     - Before each sentence, silently think: “I’m saying this for *you*.”  
     - At the end of each sentence, imagine the person reacting—nodding, thinking, or being surprised. That’s your cue to pause.  
   - Your brain starts to link pauses with *empathy*, not awkwardness.

3. **Comfort-building drill (2–3 minutes, every other day):**

   - Say a single sentence related to your topic, for example:  
     - “I really love this hobby.”  
   - After the sentence, **force yourself to stay silent and just breathe for 5 full seconds** before speaking again.  
   - During the silence, *keep eye contact* with the imaginary person or the camera.  
   - This trains you to stay relaxed and present during silence instead of filling it with words.

4. **Use your enthusiasm as an anchor:**

   For your hobby-based talks this week:
   - Keep your **facial expression and gestures** lively.  
   - Let only your *words* slow down, not your energy.  
   - Visual: energetic body, deliberate words.

5. **Mini-script to use mentally before speaking:**

   Right before any practice, silently say to yourself:
   - “I don’t have to prove anything.”  
   - “I’m going to sound a bit slower than feels normal, and that’s fine.”  
   - “My job is to make this easy to follow, not to fill every second.”

Use these adjustments on top of your existing drills. Don’t speed back up to feel normal; let your “normal” slowly move toward a calmer, more controlled pace while your energy stays high through expression and body language.

### Human: I also get really nervous speaking in front of real people, even if I’ve practiced. Can you integrate something to work on nerves too?

### Assistant: I’ll integrate nerve-management into your existing practice so it becomes part of your routine, not a separate issue.

**1. Pre-speaking routine (2 minutes, always do this before any practice or real talk):**

You’ll follow this short sequence every time you’re about to speak:

1. **Breathing (45 seconds):**
   - Inhale through your nose for 4 seconds.  
   - Hold for 2 seconds.  
   - Exhale slowly through your mouth for 6 seconds.  
   - Repeat 4–5 cycles.

2. **Body reset (30 seconds):**
   - Stand up, roll your shoulders back slowly 5 times.  
   - Gently shake out your arms and hands.  
   - Plant your feet hip-width apart and feel the ground under you.

3. **Mental script (30–45 seconds):**
   - “My job right now is not to be perfect, it’s to be clear.”  
   - “They want me to succeed more than they want to judge me.”  
   - “If I feel nervous, I’ll just breathe and pause; no one can see my heart rate.”

Use this same routine even for solo recordings so it becomes automatic before real audiences.

---

**2. “Simulated audience” drill (2–3 times per week, 5–7 minutes):**

1. Set up your phone **camera** instead of just audio.  
2. Stand as if there are 5–10 people in front of you.  
3. Deliver your 3-minute hobby script or another topic.  
4. While speaking, intentionally:
   - Imagine 1–2 “friendly faces” in the imaginary audience.  
   - Direct some sentences to those spots in the room.  
5. When you feel a spike of nerves (tight chest, shallow breath), you **do not push through**:
   - You pause, take one visible breath, then continue your sentence calmly.

This trains you to treat nerves as a cue to pause and breathe, not as a threat.

---

**3. Small exposure task for real life (1x this week):**

You’ll introduce one low-stakes real-world speaking challenge:

- Option A: In a meeting or group setting, volunteer a short 30–60 second comment summarizing an idea.  
- Option B: In a casual group (friends, colleagues, class), tell a 60–90 second story you’ve already practiced.  

Before doing it:
- Use your pre-speaking routine.  
After doing it:
- Write down:
  - How nervous were you at the start (1–10)?  
  - How nervous were you halfway through (1–10)?  
  - One thing that went better than you expected.

---

**4. Reframing nerves during practice:**

During any drill, when you notice nervous thoughts like “I’m going to mess up,” you’ll apply:

- Label: “That’s a nerves-thought, not a fact.”  
- Action: pause, breathe once, then continue from the **next idea**, not where you got stuck.

You’re not trying to erase nerves; you’re training your response to them so they stop controlling your behavior.
