### Human: Why did the iPhone 13 sell so well?

### Assistant: Several concrete factors drove its strong 2022 performance:

1. **Large upgrade base**
   - Many users were coming from iPhone X/XS/11, where battery life and camera improvements felt significant.
   - Carriers and Apple pushed strong trade‑in programs, lowering effective prices.

2. **Balanced product positioning**
   - It hit a “sweet spot”: flagship-level features without the Pro price.
   - For many buyers, the extra cost of Pro/Pro Max didn’t feel justified, so the 13 became the default choice.

3. **Long availability window**
   - Launched in late 2021 and remained a primary model throughout 2022.
   - Continued to be heavily promoted even after newer models were announced, especially in price-sensitive markets.

4. **Strong carrier subsidies and financing**
   - In the US, Europe, and parts of Asia, 24–36‑month financing and “free with contract” style promos made it look inexpensive.
   - Aggressive 5G push by carriers made upgrading from older 4G/early-5G phones more attractive.

5. **Incremental but highly visible improvements**
   - Noticeably better battery life than several prior generations.
   - Camera and low-light improvements that are obvious to typical users.
   - Perception of being “modern enough” (5G, design, performance) to last several years.

6. **Apple ecosystem lock-in**
   - iMessage, FaceTime, iCloud, Apple Watch, AirPods, and App Store make switching away costly in terms of user experience.
   - Families and social groups using iPhone reinforced the choice.

7. **Brand strength and resale value**
   - High resale value makes total cost of ownership lower versus many Android competitors.
   - Strong brand trust in terms of reliability, privacy, and security.

### Human: I want to show investors a simple dashboard of iPhone 13 sales by quarter in Excel. Help me set that up.

### Assistant: I’ll outline a concrete structure you can implement directly in Excel, using illustrative numbers you can later replace with your actual/source data.

#### 1. Structure your raw data
Create a sheet named **`Data`** with columns:

- A: `Year`
- B: `Quarter`
- C: `Region`
- D: `Units_Sold` (in millions)
- E: `Revenue` (in billions, or your preferred currency)

Example rows (placeholders):

- 2022 | Q1 | Global | 15.0 | 12.0  
- 2022 | Q2 | Global | 13.0 | 10.4  
- 2022 | Q3 | Global | 12.0 | 9.6  
- 2022 | Q4 | Global | 14.0 | 11.2  

Adjust regions if you want per-region detail (e.g., US, Europe, China, RoW).

#### 2. Create a summary table for the dashboard
On a new sheet named **`Dashboard`**, set up:

- A1: `Year`  
- B1: `Quarter`  
- C1: `Total Units (M)`  
- D1: `Total Revenue (B)`

Then in rows below (A2–A5, assuming 2022 Q1–Q4):

- A2–A5: `2022`  
- B2: `Q1`  
- B3: `Q2`  
- B4: `Q3`  
- B5: `Q4`

In **C2**, use a SUMIFS formula to pull units from `Data`:

```excel
=SUMIFS(Data!$D:$D, Data!$A:$A, $A2, Data!$B:$B, $B2)
```

In **D2**, for revenue:

```excel
=SUMIFS(Data!$E:$E, Data!$A:$A, $A2, Data!$B:$B, $B2)
```

Copy C2–D2 down to C5–D5.

#### 3. Add charts for visual impact
On the `Dashboard` sheet:

1. Select range `B1:D5`.
2. Insert → Charts → **Combo Chart**:
   - Series 1: `Total Units (M)` as a **column**.
   - Series 2: `Total Revenue (B)` as a **line** on a **secondary axis**.
3. Add:
   - Chart title: “iPhone 13 – 2022 Quarterly Performance”
   - Axis titles: “Quarter” (x-axis), “Units (M)” and “Revenue (B)” (y-axes).

#### 4. Optional: region filter with a slicer (if you use a PivotTable)
If you want more interactivity:

1. Turn `Data` range into a **Table**: select it → Insert → Table.
2. Insert → PivotTable → place it on `Dashboard`.
3. In PivotTable Fields:
   - Rows: `Year`, `Quarter`
   - Values: `Units_Sold`, `Revenue`
   - Filters: `Region`
4. Insert a **Slicer** for `Region` so investors can toggle regions.

This gives you a clean quarterly overview; you only need to replace the placeholder numbers with your actual sourced estimates for iPhone 13 sales.
