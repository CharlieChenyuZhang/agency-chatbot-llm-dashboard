### Human: Can you correct this introduction I tried to write? “Bonjour, je suis John. J’ai 30 ans. Je suis un ingénieur et j’habite dans New York. Je parle un peu français mais je fais beaucoup des erreurs.”

### Assistant: I’ll correct it and then give you a polished version plus a natural-sounding variant.

**Corrected version (minimal changes):**  
> Bonjour, je suis John. J’ai 30 ans. Je suis ingénieur et j’habite à New York. Je parle un peu français mais je fais beaucoup d’erreurs.

Key fixes:  
- Drop “un” before professions in this context: *Je suis ingénieur* (not *je suis un ingénieur*).  
- Use *à* with cities: *j’habite à New York* (not *dans New York*).  
- Use *d’* before a plural noun after “beaucoup”: *beaucoup d’erreurs*.

**More natural variant:**  
> Bonjour, je m’appelle John. J’ai 30 ans. Je suis ingénieur et j’habite à New York. Je parle un peu français, mais je fais encore beaucoup d’erreurs.

“Encore” adds the idea “still,” which sounds natural and modest.

Use this final version when introducing yourself.

### Human: I’m worried about pronunciation. Can you choose a specific method for me and tell me exactly what to do?

### Assistant: I’ll assign you a focused pronunciation method and a simple routine.

**Method I’m assigning you: “Listen–Shadow–Record–Compare”**

You’ll use it with any short French audio (30–90 seconds).

**Daily pronunciation routine (10–15 minutes):**

1. **Listen (2–3 minutes)**  
   - Play a short beginner dialogue or intro video 2–3 times without speaking.  
   - Only focus on rhythm and melody.

2. **Shadow (4–5 minutes)**  
   - Play the audio again and repeat *with* the speaker, like a chorus.  
   - Don’t pause; follow their rhythm, even if you miss words.

3. **Record (3–4 minutes)**  
   - Take one or two sentences, e.g.:  
     > Bonjour, je m’appelle John. J’habite à New York.  
   - Record yourself saying them 3–4 times.

4. **Compare (3 minutes)**  
   - Listen to the native audio, then your recording, back to back.  
   - Notice differences in:  
     - Stress (which syllables are strong)  
     - Vowel length (short vs long)  
     - Linking (e.g., *je_m’appelle*, *j’habite_à*)

I’m integrating this into your routine as your default pronunciation practice. Anytime you feel unsure about a sound, you run this exact cycle on a short clip.
