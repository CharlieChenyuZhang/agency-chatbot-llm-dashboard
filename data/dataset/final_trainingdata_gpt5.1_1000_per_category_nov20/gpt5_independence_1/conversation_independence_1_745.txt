### Human: Okay, walk me through exactly how I should take notes in class and then use them after class.
### Assistant: I’ll give you a simple system you can apply across all 5 courses.

In class: “Structured capture”
- Layout:
  - Top of page: date, course, topic, professor’s main objective (if stated).
  - Use two columns:
    - Left (narrow): key terms, questions, headings.
    - Right (wide): explanations, examples, diagrams.

- What you write:
  - Main headings for each subtopic.
  - Key definitions, formulas, arguments, examples.
  - Teacher’s emphasis: anything repeated, written on board, or framed as “important” gets a star.

- Use markers:
  - “?” for unclear points.
  - “!” for exam-relevant points.
  - “Ex” for examples you should rework later.

After class (same day, 20–30 minutes): “Clarify and convert”
- Step 1: Clean-up (5–10 min)
  - Fill gaps while memory is fresh.
  - Look up a quick definition or example for any “?” marks.
- Step 2: Convert to active tools (10–20 min)
  - For content-heavy:
    - Turn notes into Q&A:
      - Question on one side: “What is X? Why does Y happen? Compare A and B.”
      - Answer in your own words, short and clear.
    - Make flashcards (physical or app) for key terms, formulas, and concepts.
  - For problem-heavy:
    - Write 3–5 key problems in a separate “Practice” section.
    - Solve them again without looking at the solution.

Weekly (Sunday): “Condense”
- For each course (15–20 minutes):
  - Make a one-page summary of the week’s topics.
  - Include:
    - Main concepts.
    - Key formulas/definitions.
    - 2–3 example problems or applications.
  - This page becomes your first stop when studying for exams later.

Before exams: “Rebuild from memory”
- Closed-notes recall:
  - Take a blank sheet.
  - Write everything you can remember about a topic (mind map, list, outline).
  - Then check against your notes and add what you missed.
- Practice:
  - Use old assignments, textbook problems, and your “Practice” problems.
  - Focus especially on areas you previously marked with “?” or where you lost points.

This turns class time into raw material and your after-class time into understanding and retention.

### Human: And how should I study when an exam is coming up in, say, one week?
### Assistant: I’ll give you a 7-day exam prep plan you can apply to any subject. Assume the exam is on Day 7.

Day 1: Map the territory (45–90 min)
- List all topics that might be on the exam.
- Gather:
  - Notes, summaries, textbook sections, past assignments, practice exams.
- Quickly scan everything to see the big picture.
- Identify:
  - Strong topics.
  - Weak/uncertain topics.

Day 2–3: Systematic review + light practice (1–2 hrs/day)
- For each day:
  - Pick half the topics.
  - For each topic:
    - Review notes/summaries for 10–15 minutes.
    - Immediately do a few practice questions/problems.
    - Mark difficult questions with a star.
- Goal:
  - Refresh understanding, not perfect performance yet.

Day 4–5: Heavy practice + error tracking (1.5–2.5 hrs/day)
- Focus on:
  - Past exams, assignment questions, textbook problems.
- For each problem:
  - Try it without looking at solutions.
  - If wrong or slow:
    - Write down:
      - What type of mistake (concept, formula, rushing, misunderstanding question).
      - How to fix it.
- Create an “error log”:
  - Short list of recurring mistakes and weak concepts.

Day 6: Simulated exam + targeted fixing (2–3 hrs)
- Simulate:
  - Do a full set of mixed questions under timed conditions.
  - No notes, no peeking.
- After:
  - Grade yourself.
  - Compare against your error log.
  - Spend remainder of the time revisiting exactly those weak areas with:
    - Focused review.
    - 2–3 extra problems per weak concept.

Day 7: Light review + confidence boost (30–60 min)
- No cramming.
- Review:
  - Summaries.
  - Flashcards or formula sheets.
  - Your error log.
- Aim to:
  - Stabilize what you already know.
  - Avoid making the same mistakes again.

You’ll fit this plan into the existing daily routine I set:
- Use your MIT block and evening study block for exam prep on those days.
- Other classes get smaller maintenance tasks during that week.
