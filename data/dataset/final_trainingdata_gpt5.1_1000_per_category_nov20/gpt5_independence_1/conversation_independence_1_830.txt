### Human: What should I check on the test drive specifically?
### Assistant: Treat the test drive as a focused inspection, not just a quick spin. I’ll give you a simple checklist to follow so you can do this confidently.

**Before driving**
1. **Exterior walk-around**
   - Panel gaps: make sure doors/hood/trunk gaps look even.
   - Look for mismatched paint or overspray (possible past repairs).
   - Check tires: all four should have decent tread and be the same brand/model ideally.

2. **Interior**
   - Check for water smells or heavy air fresheners (sometimes used to mask odors).
   - Test all doors, windows, locks, mirrors.
   - Fold rear seats; make sure mechanisms are smooth.

**On the drive**
Use this exact routine:

1. **Low-speed (parking lot / side streets)**
   - Listen for clunks, rattles, or grinding noises when turning or going over bumps.
   - Check steering: should be smooth, no pull to one side.

2. **Moderate speed (30–45 mph)**
   - Lightly brake: should feel smooth, no vibration in the pedal or steering.
   - Test acceleration: should be smooth, no hesitation or jerking.

3. **Highway (if possible)**
   - At 60–70 mph, the car should track straight without constant steering correction.
   - Listen for loud wind or tire noise that seems abnormal.
   - Engage **cruise control** and, if equipped, **adaptive cruise** and **lane keep assist**.

4. **Hybrid-specific checks (if it’s a RAV4 Hybrid)**
   - When the engine switches on/off, it should be smooth, not jarring.
   - Watch the hybrid system display: battery charge should move up/down in use; no warning lights.

**Electronics and safety**
- Test:
  - Backup camera
  - Phone pairing (Bluetooth, CarPlay/Android Auto)
  - AC and heat (front and rear)
  - All lights, wipers, and indicators
- Confirm no warning lights on the dashboard (especially check-engine, ABS, hybrid system).

**After the drive**
- Park, let it idle a bit, then:
  - Look under the car for leaks.
  - Open the hood: no burning smells, no obvious fluid leaks.

If a car passes this basic test, it’s worth paying for a **pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic** before you finalize anything. That small cost is worth it for a main family vehicle.
