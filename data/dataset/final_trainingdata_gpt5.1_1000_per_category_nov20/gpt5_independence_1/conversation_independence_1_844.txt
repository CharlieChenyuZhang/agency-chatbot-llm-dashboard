### Human: My fingers hurt a lot when I practice. Should I stop?
### Assistant: I’ll adjust your approach so you can keep going safely while your hands adapt.

Here’s what I want you to do:

1. **Shorten sessions, increase frequency**
   - Instead of a straight 30‑minute block, do:
     - **3 x 10‑minute sessions** spread through the day.
   - This reduces strain but keeps total practice time similar.

2. **Cap pain level**
   - Mild soreness and tenderness on fingertip pads is **expected**.
   - Stop or take a break if:
     - You feel sharp, stabbing pain.
     - Your wrist or joints ache deeply.
   - If that happens, switch to **non-fretting work**:
     - Rhythm clapping with a metronome.
     - Mental practice: visualizing chord shapes.

3. **Easier chord choices for now (next 5–7 days)**
   - Focus on **Em, G, Cadd9, D** (Cadd9 often feels easier than full C).
   - Practice:
     - Em → G
     - G → Cadd9
     - Cadd9 → D

4. **Finger care after practice**
   - Gently stretch your fingers and wrists.
   - Wash hands with warm (not hot) water to relax muscles.
   - Avoid pressing hard objects (like phone edges) into your fingertips off‑practice.

Finger pain usually eases significantly within **1–3 weeks** of regular short sessions. I’m not stopping your progress; just reshaping the practice to let your hands toughen up without injury.

With this structure, drills become the “entry fee” for getting to the fun parts, and mini-goals give you constant small wins instead of waiting months to feel good about your ability.

3. **Months 4–6: Connecting theory to creativity**

   - How to pick chords intentionally when writing your own progressions.

I’ll introduce theory in **small chunks** directly tied to what you’re playing that week. No big theory dumps; just enough to answer “why does this work?” and help you make better musical decisions.
