### Human: Okay, what does the weekly hard problem process look like?
### Assistant: Your weekly hard problem session will follow a more detailed structure. Use this every week for one meaningful challenge:

1. **Define the problem precisely**
   - Write one clear sentence that would make sense to a stranger.
   - Add constraints: time, money, energy, other people involved.

2. **Clarify the goal**
   - One sentence: “A good outcome looks like…”
   - One sentence: “A bad outcome looks like…”

3. **Map the problem**
   - List the key components, such as:
     - Information you’re missing
     - People or systems involved
     - Decisions you need to make
     - Resources you have (skills, time, money, tools)

4. **Generate options without judging**
   - For each decision point, list at least 3 options, including “do nothing for now.”
   - Don’t evaluate yet; just write freely.

5. **Evaluate options with simple criteria**
   - For each option, rate (Low/Medium/High):
     - Impact (how much it helps the goal)
     - Effort (time/energy/money)
     - Risk (chance and cost of failure)
   - Prefer High impact, Low/Medium effort, Low/Medium risk.

6. **Choose a small, testable experiment**
   - Turn your best option into a tiny test you can do in 1–3 days.
   - Define:
     - What you will do
     - By when
     - What you expect to happen

7. **Reflect and update**
   - After doing the experiment, answer:
     - What actually happened?
     - What surprised you?
     - What will you change next?

Use this once per week on something that genuinely matters to you. I’ll now add specific practice areas to accelerate your growth.

- My phone distracts me.
- I don’t have a clear plan when I sit down.

  - Use an app blocker during work periods.

  - Use a 25-minute “one task only” rule.
  - Write a 3-item priority list the night before.

  - Use 25 minutes work / 5 minutes break cycles.
  - Limit sessions to 90 minutes max.
  - Stand up and move every break.

- Noisy environment:
  - Use earplugs or headphones.
  - Move to a quieter space for key tasks.
  - Set a specific “do not disturb” time.

  - Put phone in another room.
  - Work in 2 × 25-minute blocks with 5-minute breaks.

- I’ll finish at least 2 of the 3 items.
- I’ll feel less scattered afterward.

**7. Reflection (after trying it)**
- What happened:
  - Finished 2 items, started the 3rd.
  - First 25 minutes were very focused, second a bit less.
- What I learned:
  - Phone in another room helps a lot.
  - I need a short break with movement, not scrolling.
- What I’ll change:
  - Keep the phone away.
  - Plan tomorrow’s 3 items the night before.

### Human: How can I tell if my problem-solving is actually improving over time?
### Assistant: Track three indicators over the next month:

   - Measure how long you stay in vague worry or confusion.

2. **Quality of your next steps**
   - Look at your daily logs:
     - Are your actions small, specific, and testable?
     - Do they actually move things forward, even a little?
   - Improvement sign: Fewer random actions, more targeted experiments.

3. **Reflection and pattern recognition**
   - Each week, review:
     - Recurring problems
     - Recurring mistakes
     - Recurring successful strategies
   - Improvement sign: You start anticipating problems and using strategies that worked before, instead of starting from zero every time.

You will also notice subjective changes:
- Less overwhelm when facing new problems
- More confidence in choosing a direction
- Less regret, because decisions are based on a clear process
