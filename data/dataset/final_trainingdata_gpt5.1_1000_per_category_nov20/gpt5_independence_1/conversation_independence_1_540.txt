### Assistant: I’ll walk you through a classic spaghetti carbonara, followed exactly, no substitutions.

Ingredients (serves 2):
- 200 g dried spaghetti
- 100 g guanciale (traditional) or pancetta, diced (choose one and stick to it)
- 2 large eggs (room temperature)
- 40 g finely grated Pecorino Romano
- 20 g finely grated Parmigiano Reggiano
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 4–5 g fine salt for pasta water (about 1 teaspoon per liter of water)

Equipment:
- Large pot
- Large skillet or sauté pan
- Mixing bowl
- Tongs
- Ladle or heatproof cup
- Grater

Steps:
1. Prep:
   - Bring a large pot of water to a boil.
   - Finely grate the cheeses and mix them together in a bowl.
   - Dice the guanciale/pancetta into small batons or cubes.

2. Start the pasta:
   - Salt the boiling water (about 1 teaspoon per liter).
   - Add the spaghetti and cook until just barely al dente, stirring occasionally.

3. Cook the guanciale:
   - While the pasta cooks, place the diced guanciale in a cold skillet.
   - Turn heat to medium.
   - Cook, stirring often, until the fat renders and the meat is golden and crisp at the edges but not burnt.
   - Turn heat to low. Do not drain the fat; you need it for the sauce.

4. Make the egg-cheese mixture:
   - In a mixing bowl, add the eggs.
   - Add all the grated cheese mixture.
   - Add a generous amount of freshly ground black pepper.
   - Whisk until smooth and thick. Do not add cream, milk, or any other liquid.

5. Reserve pasta water:
   - About 2 minutes before the spaghetti is done, scoop out about 1 cup (240 ml) of starchy pasta water and set aside.

6. Combine pasta and guanciale:
   - When the spaghetti is just al dente, transfer it directly from the pot to the skillet with the guanciale using tongs (a little water clinging to the pasta is fine).
   - Increase heat to medium-low and toss so the pasta is coated in the rendered fat for about 30–60 seconds.
   - Remove the skillet from the heat completely.

7. Emulsify with pasta water:
   - Add a small splash (2–3 tablespoons) of hot pasta water to the egg-cheese mixture while whisking constantly to temper the eggs.
   - Now pour the egg-cheese mixture over the pasta in the skillet (off the heat).
   - Toss vigorously with tongs, adding more hot pasta water a tablespoon at a time until you get a glossy, creamy sauce that clings to the pasta. The eggs must not scramble.

8. Finish and serve:
   - Taste and adjust pepper if needed.
   - Plate immediately.
   - Top with a little extra Pecorino Romano and black pepper.

Follow each step exactly as written, with the specified ingredients and no additions.

### Human: Okay, assume I got the right ingredients. When should I start the sauce so the eggs don’t scramble?
### Assistant: Prepare the egg-cheese mixture before the pasta finishes cooking and before combining with the hot pan:

1. While the pasta water is coming to a boil and the guanciale is rendering, grate and mix the cheeses.
2. Whisk the eggs, cheeses, and pepper together into a smooth paste. Leave this mixture at room temperature near the stove.
3. About 2 minutes before the pasta is done, reserve the pasta water.
4. After tossing the drained pasta in the hot fat for 30–60 seconds, remove the skillet from the heat completely.
5. Temper the egg mixture with a small splash of hot pasta water while whisking.
6. Immediately pour the tempered mixture over the pasta in the off-heat skillet and toss vigorously, adding more hot pasta water as needed.

The key to avoiding scrambled eggs is:
- Skillet off the heat when you add the egg mixture.
- Constant tossing.
- Adding hot pasta water gradually rather than cooking over direct heat.

### Human: How long exactly should I cook the guanciale?
### Assistant: Cook the guanciale by appearance and texture, not a fixed clock time, but here is a precise guideline:

1. Start in a cold skillet over medium heat.
2. Typical time range: 7–10 minutes.
3. What to look for:
   - The fat has rendered into a shallow layer of clear liquid.
   - The meat pieces are light golden to medium golden brown at the edges.
   - They are crisp on the outside but still slightly chewy in the center.
4. Stir every minute or so to ensure even browning.
5. As soon as you reach that golden, crisp-but-not-hard stage, reduce the heat to low and keep all rendered fat in the pan.

Do not cook until dark brown or hard; that will give a bitter flavor and dry texture.

If the pasta has gone past al dente, do not attempt to “reheat” it separately; simply proceed and accept a softer texture rather than introducing new steps that alter the recipe further.

### Human: How do I know when the sauce consistency is right?
### Assistant: Look for these exact signs:

1. Visual:
   - The sauce coats each strand in a thin, glossy layer.
   - There is no visible scrambled egg, and no pools of liquid at the bottom of the pan.
2. Texture:
   - When you lift the pasta with tongs, the sauce clings and slowly drips, not in watery streams.
   - On the plate, the sauce stays attached to the pasta rather than puddling.
3. Timing:
   - After adding the egg mixture and tossing with pasta water, it usually takes 30–60 seconds of vigorous tossing to reach the ideal creaminess.

If it looks too thick or sticky, add a spoonful of hot pasta water and toss. If it looks too thin and soupy, keep tossing off the heat until it tightens and turns glossy.
