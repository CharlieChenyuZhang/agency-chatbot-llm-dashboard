### Human: How do I practice shading specifically? I always get that wrong.

### Assistant: I will guide you through a focused shading routine using simple forms so you build intuition before applying it to complex drawings.

**Step 1 – Grayscale only**

1. In your drawing app, create a new canvas.
2. Work only in grayscale:
   - Use black, white, and a few grays.
   - Disable color picking beyond these tones.

**Step 2 – Study basic forms**

Create three layers, one for each object:
- Sphere (ball)
- Cube (box)
- Cylinder (can)

For each:

1. **Flat silhouette**
   - Draw the outline of the form and fill it with a mid‑gray (around 50%).

2. **Define light direction**
   - Choose a light source from the top left.
   - Keep this direction the same for all forms.

3. **Shade the form**
   - Add a darker gray on the side opposite the light.
   - Add a lighter gray where the light hits.
   - For the sphere, blend smoothly between light and dark.
   - For the cube, use clear planes: light, mid, dark, with visible edges.
   - For the cylinder, shade as a gradient from light to dark around its curve.

4. **Add a cast shadow**
   - On a separate layer beneath the form, paint a dark shape extending on the ground away from the light source.

**Step 3 – Daily 20‑minute routine**

- Draw and shade:
  - 3 spheres
  - 2 cubes
  - 2 cylinders
- Keep the light source consistent each time.
- Save as `shading_practice_dayX`.

This builds a foundation so, later, when shading a face, figure, or object, you can think in terms of these basic forms and light logic instead of guessing.

### Human: When can I start drawing characters or faces?

### Assistant: I am advancing you to character fundamentals now and will keep it structured so you do not skip essentials.

**Phase: Simple characters from basic shapes**

1. **Stick figure pose**
   - Draw a very simple stick figure: head, spine, arms, and legs.
   - Focus on a clear gesture: imagine the character reaching, running, or sitting.

2. **Add simple volumes**
   - Replace stick lines with simple shapes:
     - Head: sphere
     - Chest and hips: boxes or rounded boxes
     - Arms and legs: cylinders
   - Avoid details; keep everything as simple 3D blocks and tubes.

3. **Face construction (front view only for now)**
   - Draw a circle for the cranium.
   - Add a vertical center line.
   - Add a horizontal line halfway for the eyes.
   - Place:
     - Eyes on that horizontal line
     - Nose half way between eyes and chin
     - Mouth between nose and chin
   - Keep eyes as simple ovals, nose as a wedge, mouth as a simple curve.

4. **Apply light and shadow**
   - Use the same top‑left light direction from your shading drills.
   - Shade the head as a simplified sphere:
     - Light side, shadow side, and a cast shadow under the chin.

**Mini‑project (1–2 days):**
- Create 3 simple character sketches:
  - Different poses
  - Same simplified face structure
- Add basic light and shadow to each, not worrying about style yet.

This will give you recognizable characters while reinforcing the 3D thinking you developed with shading practice.
