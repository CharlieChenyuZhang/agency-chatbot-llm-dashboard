### Human: Recommend a movie to watch
### Assistant: Watch “Arrival” (2016), directed by Denis Villeneuve.

It’s a thoughtful, beautifully shot science-fiction drama about a linguist trying to communicate with mysterious visitors. It’s emotionally grounded, smart without being confusing, and has a very satisfying, reflective ending.

If you’ve already seen it, go with “Prisoners” (also Villeneuve) for a darker, more thriller-oriented night.

### Human: I'm not really into sci-fi. What else?
### Assistant: Skip sci-fi entirely and watch “Spotlight” (2015).

It’s a grounded, character-driven drama based on the true story of investigative journalists at The Boston Globe uncovering systemic abuse in the Catholic Church. No gimmicks, very little violence, and it moves at a steady, engaging pace. You’ll get strong performances, a tight script, and a story that feels important without being preachy.

If you prefer something a bit more stylish and twisty, go with “The Prestige,” but “Spotlight” is the cleaner pick for non–sci-fi viewers.

### Human: I actually want something lighter, maybe a comedy, but not dumb or over-the-top.
### Assistant: Go with “The Grand Budapest Hotel” (2014), directed by Wes Anderson.

It’s witty, visually inventive, and fast-paced without being chaotic. The humor is clever and dry rather than crude, and the story has enough heart and melancholy to feel substantial. It’s light, fun, and still feels like you watched a “real” movie, not just a throwaway comedy.

Backup option in the same spirit: “Jojo Rabbit” (more emotional, still funny), but start with “The Grand Budapest Hotel.”

### Human: One last thing: pick what we should watch on Sunday afternoon alone, something calm I can put on while folding laundry.
### Assistant: For a calm Sunday afternoon while doing laundry, put on “Julie & Julia” (2009).

It’s gentle, dialogue-heavy, and easy to follow even if you look away a lot. The tone is warm, the stakes are low, and the cooking/Paris/New York scenes make for pleasant background visuals. You can drop in and out without losing the thread, and it leaves you in a relaxed, slightly inspired mood.

Aim to start it around 2:00 or 3:00 p.m. so it finishes before evening and doesn’t cut into your Sunday night wind-down.
