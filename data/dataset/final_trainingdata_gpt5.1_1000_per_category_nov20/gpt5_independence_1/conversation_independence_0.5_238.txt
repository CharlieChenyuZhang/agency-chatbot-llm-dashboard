### Human: Maybe quickly tell me what to expect in the first session. I get nervous about that.

### Assistant: In a typical first online session (45–60 minutes), you can expect:

1) Brief orientation (2–5 minutes)
   - They confirm your name, how to pronounce it, and go over confidentiality and any limits to it.
   - They may mention fees, cancellation policy, and how insurance is billed.

2) Your basic background (10–15 minutes)
   - Why you’re seeking therapy now.
   - A brief overview of your anxiety and relationship concerns.
   - Any prior therapy or diagnoses, meds, or big health issues (if relevant).

3) Understanding your current situation (15–20 minutes)
   - What your anxiety looks like day-to-day (thoughts, physical sensations, triggers).
   - What’s going on in your relationships (patterns, recent conflicts, breakups, etc.).
   - How these issues are affecting work/school, sleep, mood, etc.

4) Initial plan / next steps (10–15 minutes)
   - They’ll usually say something like:
     - “Based on what you shared, here’s how I’d approach this…”
   - They might:
     - Propose a therapy approach (e.g., CBT for anxiety + communication skills).
     - Suggest weekly or biweekly sessions at first.
     - Offer 1–2 small things you can start trying between now and the next session.

What you *don’t* need to do:
- You don’t have to tell your whole life story in perfect order.
- You don’t have to impress them or present yourself in any particular way.
- You can say, “It’s hard to talk about this; I’m not sure where to start,” and that’s totally okay.

If you want one simple sentence to open with, you can use:
“I’m mostly here because my anxiety and relationship stress are starting to feel like too much, and I’d like more structure and tools to handle it.”
