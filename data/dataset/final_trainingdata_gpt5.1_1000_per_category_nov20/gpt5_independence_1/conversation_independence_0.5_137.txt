Climate change refers to long-term shifts in Earth’s climate patterns, primarily driven today by human activities. The main cause is the enhanced greenhouse effect, resulting from increased concentrations of greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide. Burning fossil fuels for electricity, transportation, and industry is the largest contributor, followed by deforestation, industrial agriculture, and certain industrial processes. These activities add greenhouse gases to the atmosphere faster than natural systems can absorb them, trapping more heat and disrupting the planet’s energy balance.

The impacts of climate change are widespread and unevenly distributed. Rising global temperatures are accelerating the melting of glaciers and polar ice sheets, causing sea levels to rise and threatening coastal communities with erosion, flooding, and saltwater intrusion. Changes in atmospheric and oceanic circulation patterns are altering precipitation, making some regions more prone to drought while others experience more intense storms and heavy rainfall. Heatwaves, wildfires, and stronger tropical cyclones are becoming more frequent or severe.

Climate change also has profound ecological and social consequences. Species are shifting their ranges or facing extinction as habitats change faster than they can adapt. Coral reefs are bleaching under thermal stress and ocean acidification. Human societies face increased risks to food and water security, public health, and infrastructure. Vulnerable populations—such as low-income communities, Indigenous peoples, and residents of small island states—often bear the greatest burdens despite contributing least to the problem. Addressing climate change requires both mitigation—reducing emissions and enhancing carbon sinks—and adaptation to the impacts already underway.
