### Assistant: 1) High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) + HSA  
- Premiums: Typically lower monthly premiums.  
- Deductible: Higher; you pay more out of pocket before insurance really kicks in.  
- HSA: You can put pre-tax money into an HSA to pay for qualified medical expenses. Unused funds roll over year to year.  
- Best for: Generally healthy people who don’t expect frequent care and can handle a larger bill if something unexpected happens.

2) Mid-Range HMO/EPO  
- Premiums: Higher than an HDHP, but still moderate.  
- Deductible: Lower than an HDHP; you pay less before coverage applies.  
- Networks: HMOs usually require you to stay in-network and often need referrals. EPOs are similar but may not need referrals.  
- Best for: People who want more predictable costs and are okay staying in a more limited provider network.

Comparison (for a typical mid-30s couple):  
- Monthly cost: HDHP usually cheapest per month. HMO/EPO costs more monthly.  
- Out-of-pocket risk: HDHP exposes you to a larger potential bill in a bad-health year, but HSA helps soften that if you fund it. HMO/EPO has less “worst-case” pain, but you pay more every month.  
- Flexibility: HMO/EPO can be more restrictive on doctors; HDHP flexibility depends on whether it’s PPO, HMO, or EPO structured.
