**Answers / Sample Responses** (body in two columns, 11 pt)

**1. Why did many Americans want a “New Deal” by 1932?**  
Sample points:  
- The Great Depression caused massive unemployment and poverty.  
- Many people lost their savings when banks failed.  
- Existing government policies were not solving the crisis.  
- Americans wanted new leadership and new ideas to restore hope and stability.

**2. Pick one New Deal program…**

- **CCC (Civilian Conservation Corps)**  
  a) Problem: High unemployment among young men; neglected public lands.  
  b) Solution: Gave government-paid jobs on conservation projects like planting trees, building parks, and improving trails.

- **WPA (Works Progress Administration)**  
  a) Problem: Widespread unemployment and failing infrastructure.  
  b) Solution: Employed millions building roads, schools, bridges, and supporting artists, writers, and actors.

- **FDIC (Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation)**  
  a) Problem: People were afraid to keep money in banks; banks kept failing.  
  b) Solution: Guaranteed deposits up to a certain amount so people would trust banks again.

- **Social Security Act**  
  a) Problem: Many older Americans had no income; workers had no protection when they lost jobs.  
  b) Solution: Created pensions for the elderly and unemployment insurance funded by payroll taxes.

**3. How did the New Deal change the relationship between the federal government and ordinary Americans?**  
Sample points:  
- The federal government became more active in providing jobs and financial support.  
- People began to expect the government to help in times of economic crisis.  
- New rules gave the government a larger role in regulating banks, business, and the stock market.  
- Citizens had more direct contact with federal programs (e.g., Social Security numbers, relief programs).

**4. Was the New Deal mostly positive, mostly negative, or mixed? Explain.**  
Accept a variety of answers; a strong response might include:  
- **Mostly positive:** Helped millions get jobs and food; restored confidence in banks; created Social Security and other long-term protections.  
- **Critiques to note:** It did not fully end the Depression (World War II military spending helped do that); some programs discriminated against Black Americans and other minorities; national debt increased.  
- Students should provide **at least two specific examples** (e.g., CCC jobs, bank insurance, Social Security pensions, criticisms of cost or discrimination).

**5. Imagined 1935 letter about a New Deal job or Social Security.**  
Look for:  
- A clear description of the job or benefit (e.g., building roads with the WPA; planting trees with the CCC; receiving Social Security payments).  
- At least one way life has changed (e.g., steady paycheck, ability to buy food, support for elderly parents, renewed hope).  
- Historical plausibility (time period, basic facts about the program should be accurate).
