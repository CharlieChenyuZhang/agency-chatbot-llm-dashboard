#### B. Interview Question Framework (Reusable)

Design each interview in **four phases**:

**Phase 1 – Context & Origin (5–8 minutes)**
1. “For people who don’t know you, how do you introduce yourself and what you do?”
2. “Take me back to before you started [project]. What did your life look like then?”
3. “What was the first moment you thought, ‘Maybe I should actually do this’?”

**Phase 2 – The First Steps (10–15 minutes)**
4. “When you decided to start, what was the *very first* concrete thing you did?”
5. “What did the first 30 days look like? Walk me through it week by week, as specifically as you can.”
6. “What were you most unsure about at the beginning?”
7. “Did you have any ‘this is a bad idea’ doubts? How did you deal with them?”

**Phase 3 – Challenges, Mistakes, and Turning Points (10–15 minutes)**
8. “What went wrong early on that you didn’t expect?”
9. “Was there a moment you thought seriously about quitting? What happened?”
10. “What turned out to be surprisingly easy or fun?”
11. “Looking back now, what would you do differently in your first 60–90 days?”

**Phase 4 – Tactics & Advice (5–10 minutes)**
12. “For someone who wants to do something similar, what’s a realistic first step they could take this week?”
13. “What tools, books, or resources were genuinely useful to you?”
14. “What should people *ignore* when they’re starting—advice or myths that aren’t helpful?”
15. “If you had to boil your experience down to 3 key lessons, what would they be?”

**Closing Question (1–2 minutes)**
16. “Where can people find you and follow your work?”

You can keep this framework as your base and customize 2–3 questions per guest based on their specific situation.

#### 1. Technical Prep (Day Before / Morning Of)
- Confirm:
  - Mic is selected as the input in your recording software.
  - Headphones are plugged in and working.
  - Backup: If your main platform fails, you can fall back to Zoom or direct local recording.
- Close:
  - All unnecessary apps and browser tabs.
  - Any notifications (Slack, email, phone, etc.) to avoid dings.
- Quick test:
  - Record a 30-second clip, play it back, check for:
    - Volume level (not too quiet, not distorted).
    - No obvious background hum or echo.

#### 2. Guest Prep (15–30 minutes)
- Skim:
  - Their website, LinkedIn, or main social profiles.
- Write down:
  - 1–2 specific things you genuinely find interesting about their story.
  - 2–3 custom questions related to those things.
- Send a short reminder email:
  - Time and time zone.
  - Link to the recording.
  - Request: “Please use headphones and, if possible, a laptop in a quiet room.”

#### 3. Your Notes for the Interview
Create a 1-page “run of show”:
- Intro:
  - Their name and one-sentence description.
- 4–6 main questions you definitely want to cover.
- A few bullet points for your intro and outro.
Keep it visible during the call.

#### 4. How to Conduct the Interview (During the Call)
- Before recording:
  - Chat casually for 2–3 minutes to warm up.
  - Explain the structure: “We’ll talk for about 30–40 minutes, I’ll guide us through your story, and if you want to redo something, just say so and we’ll repeat it.”
- While recording:
  - Listen more than you talk; aim for them speaking 70–80% of the time.
  - Ask “how” and “what” follow-ups:
    - “How did you actually do that?”
    - “What did that look like day-to-day?”
  - Avoid stacking questions; ask one at a time.
  - If they say something interesting, dig in:
    - “Can you walk me through that moment more specifically?”

#### 5. Handling Mistakes and Awkward Moments
- If you stumble over a question:
  - Pause, take a breath, re-ask it clearly. You can cut the first attempt in editing.
- If they go off on a tangent:
  - Let them finish, then say: “I want to go back to when you first started…” and steer gently.
- If something interrupts (noise, tech issue):
  - Say: “Let’s pause for a second,” fix it, then repeat the last question. Edit the interruption out later.

#### 6. Ending the Interview
- Ask your closing questions:
  - Their 3 key lessons.
  - Where people can find them.
- Then ask off-record:
  - “Is there anything you wish we’d covered or anything you want me to cut?”
- Thank them and mention:
  - Rough timeframe for when the episode will go live.
  - That you’ll send them links and promo materials.

#### 7. Post-Interview Routine
- Immediately:
  - Save and back up the recording.
- Make 3 quick notes:
  - 1 thing you did well.
  - 1 thing you want to improve next time (e.g., shorter intros, stronger follow-ups).
  - 1 memorable quote or moment for promotion.

With this process, your first interviews will still feel raw—but they’ll be structured, listenable, and good enough to publish.
