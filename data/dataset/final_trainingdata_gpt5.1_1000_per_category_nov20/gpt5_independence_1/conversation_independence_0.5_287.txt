### Human: Let’s keep it 4 days. How should I pick the weights and progress?

### Assistant: I’ll give you a simple rule you can reuse for almost every exercise.

**Choosing starting weights (first 1–2 weeks):**
- For a set of 10–12:
  - Pick a weight where you could probably do 2–3 more reps with good form if you had to.
  - Last reps should feel challenging but not like a “max effort.”
- If you’re unsure, err on the lighter side the first session and move up next time.

**Progression rule (“2×12 rule”):**
- If an exercise is programmed as 3×10–12:
  - When you can do **12 reps in all 3 sets** with good form and still have about 1 rep “in the tank,” increase the weight slightly next time (smallest available jump).
- If your reps look like: 12, 11, 9  
  - Stay at that weight next workout and try to improve those numbers.

**For core exercises (planks, bird dog, etc.):**
- Increase time slowly (5–10 seconds more) or add 1–2 reps per week.

One thing I’d like to check before suggesting more aggressive progressions:

- Are you okay if the first 2 weeks feel almost “too easy” so we reduce injury risk and let your back adapt, or would you prefer a bit more intensity right away?

### Human: Let’s keep it portion-based for now. What should I do if my back starts hurting during the workouts?

### Assistant: That’s important to plan for ahead of time.

During any exercise, **stop immediately** if you feel:
- Sharp, stabbing pain
- Pain that radiates down your leg
- Numbness/tingling

For milder discomfort or stiffness:

1. **Check your form first**
   - For leg press, goblet squats, Romanian deadlifts: keep your spine neutral (no rounding), move through a comfortable range, and avoid “bouncing” at the bottom.
2. **Short-term adjustment**
   - Reduce the weight.
   - Reduce range of motion (don’t go as deep) temporarily.
   - Swap out the exercise if it consistently bothers you. For example:
     - If goblet squats hurt, switch to supported squats holding onto a rail.
     - If Romanian deadlifts bother you, replace with glute bridges and hamstring curls.

3. **Post-workout care**
   - Light walking for 5–10 minutes.
   - Gentle stretching of hip flexors, hamstrings, and glutes.
