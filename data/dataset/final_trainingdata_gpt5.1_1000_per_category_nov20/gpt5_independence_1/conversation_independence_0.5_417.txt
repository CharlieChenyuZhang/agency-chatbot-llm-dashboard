---

### The “Impact Gap” between mid-level and senior

On paper, mid-level and senior engineers can look similar:

- Both know the stack  
- Both can own features  
- Both can debug gnarly issues

The real difference is **where they focus their energy**.

- A **mid-level engineer** thinks:  
  “How do I complete this task correctly?”

- A **senior engineer** thinks:  
  “What problem is this solving, and how do we make sure it actually moves the needle?”

Companies promote people who reliably move the needle.

If your work is described only in terms of *tasks completed*, your impact is invisible.  
If your work is described in terms of *business, team, or user outcomes*, your impact is obvious.

That’s the Impact Gap.

---

### Quick tactic: Rewrite your last sprint in impact language

This week, don’t change what you do.  
Change how you **describe** what you do.

Take 3–5 things you shipped recently and rewrite them.

**Example 1**

Before:  
> Fixed bug in payments service

After:  
> Unblocked payment failures for ~20,000 daily users by fixing a race condition in the payments service, reducing payment error rate from 2.3% → 0.4%.

**Example 2**

Before:  
> Implemented new search filter on product list

After:  
> Shipped a new product search filter that cut average time-to-find-item from 30s → 12s, reducing drop-offs on the search page by 15%.

Don’t worry if you don’t have perfect numbers. Start with:

- What user problem did this work solve?  
- What became easier, faster, or cheaper because of it?  
- Who can now do their job better because of your change?

Write 1–2 sentences per item, focusing on the **before/after**.

---

### A simple 3-question reflection template

For each piece of work you ship, ask yourself:

1. What was broken / slow / painful before?
2. What is different now—for users, teammates, or the business?
3. How would I explain that difference to a non-technical stakeholder?

Keep these answers in a running doc. This becomes:

- Promotion packet material  
- Performance review bullets  
- Talking points for 1:1s with your manager  
- The story foundation for interviews at your next job

This is how you slowly close the Impact Gap: not just by doing good work, but by **making its impact legible.**
