### Assistant: I’ll give you a small “mini‑deck” of example cards with phrasing patterns you can reuse. I’ll keep each one in a simple Q→A format, but aimed at conceptual understanding.

Cellular respiration – example cards
1) Q: In cellular respiration, what is the main purpose of glycolysis?
   A: To split glucose into two pyruvate molecules, producing a small amount of ATP and NADH as the first step of extracting energy.

2) Q: Where in the cell do glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and the electron transport chain occur?
   A: Glycolysis: cytosol; Krebs cycle: mitochondrial matrix; ETC: inner mitochondrial membrane.

3) Q: Why does the electron transport chain stop if there is no oxygen available?
   A: Oxygen is the final electron acceptor; without it, electrons back up in the chain, NADH can’t be oxidized to NAD+, and the ETC (and ATP production) stop.

4) Q: How does the Krebs cycle contribute to ATP production if it makes little ATP directly?
   A: It produces NADH and FADH2, which carry high‑energy electrons to the electron transport chain, where most ATP is generated.

Genetics – example cards
5) Q: In a monohybrid cross of two heterozygotes (Aa × Aa), what are the expected genotypic and phenotypic ratios?
   A: Genotypic: 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa. Phenotypic (if A is dominant): 3 dominant : 1 recessive.

6) Q: Why do linked genes not follow the typical 9:3:3:1 dihybrid ratio?
   A: Linked genes are located close together on the same chromosome, so they tend to be inherited together and do not assort independently.

7) Q: How does crossing over increase genetic variation?
   A: Homologous chromosomes exchange segments during prophase I of meiosis, creating new combinations of alleles in gametes.

8) Q: Distinguish between genotype and phenotype with an example.
   A: Genotype is the genetic makeup (e.g., Bb), while phenotype is the observable trait (e.g., brown eyes). Different genotypes (BB, Bb) can produce the same phenotype if one allele is dominant.

9) Q: Why can two parents with a dominant phenotype have a child with a recessive phenotype?
   A: Both parents can be heterozygous carriers (e.g., Aa and Aa). If the child inherits the recessive allele from each parent (aa), they show the recessive phenotype.

Card‑writing patterns to copy:
- Ask “why” or “how” whenever possible.
- Include location and purpose in the same answer (for processes).
- Use simple numbers (ratios, steps) clearly.
