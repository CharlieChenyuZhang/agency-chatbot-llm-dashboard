### Assistant:

Title: The 5-Minute Daily Startup Ritual for Software Engineers

Most workdays go off the rails before lunch—and it usually starts in the first 10 minutes.

If you begin your day reacting to Slack, random PRs, and surprise meetings, you’re letting everyone else decide what matters. This week, you’ll set up a 5-minute daily startup ritual that helps you focus on the right work and avoid “busy, but not productive” days.

Why this matters for engineers
- Constant context-switching kills your ability to do deep work.  
- Tickets pile up, “quick questions” eat your time, and you end the day unsure what you actually accomplished.  
- A simple, repeatable 5-minute ritual at the start of each day gives you control back.

Here’s the playbook (takes 5 minutes)

1. Scan your calendar (1 minute)  
   - Look at today’s meetings and standups.  
   - Note the *real* constraints: when you must be available and when you’re free.  
   - Identify 1–2 potential deep-work blocks of at least 60–90 minutes each.

2. Choose your “one big outcome” (1 minute)  
   - Ask: “If I only move one thing forward today, what should it be?”  
   - This should be a concrete outcome, not vague progress. For example:  
     - “Merge feature branch X behind a feature flag.”  
     - “Reduce P95 latency on endpoint Y by 20%.”  
     - “Write the first draft of the design doc for project Z.”  
   - Write this outcome somewhere visible (notes app, physical notebook, top of your to-do list).

3. Turn your outcome into 2–4 tasks (1–2 minutes)  
   Break the outcome down into small, do-able steps. For example:  
   - Open/skim relevant tickets and design docs.  
   - Identify impacted files and modules.  
   - Implement the change and run tests.  
   - Write/update documentation or comments.  
   - Open PR with a clear description and checklist.

4. Block time and set boundaries (1 minute)  
   - Pick your best deep-work block and assign it to the most demanding task(s).  
   - Decide how you’ll handle interruptions *before* they happen:  
     - Slack: Only check on the hour, or after finishing a small chunk of work.  
     - PRs: Batch review them in 1–2 time slots instead of constantly switching.  
   - If your team culture allows, set a Slack status like: “Heads down on [task] until 11:00, will respond after.”

5. Choose an easy starter (30 seconds)  
   - Pick a tiny, low-friction task you can start in 2 minutes or less:  
     - Clarify a ticket by asking one question.  
     - Create a branch and scaffold needed files.  
     - Write a short outline for your design doc.  
   - This gets you moving and makes it easier to slide into deeper work.

A quick example

Alex is a backend engineer with a calendar full of meetings and a noisy Slack.

Without a ritual:  
- Starts the day by checking Slack.  
- Gets sucked into small requests and “quick reviews.”  
- Deep work gets pushed to 4:30 PM when they’re tired.  
- Ends the day feeling busy but unsatisfied.

With the 5-minute ritual:  
- 9:00–9:05: Scans calendar, chooses “one big outcome”: ship the first version of a new API endpoint behind a feature flag.  
- Breaks it into tasks: confirm requirements, update router, write handler, add tests, open PR.  
- Blocks 10:00–11:30 for deep work on the handler and tests.  
- Sets Slack to “Heads down on new endpoint until 11:30.”  
- Starts with a 2-minute task: creating the branch and writing a rough handler stub.

By noon, Alex has a working version plus tests and a PR open. Small requests still happen—but they no longer define the day.

Your one action for this week

Tomorrow morning, run this 5-minute ritual before you open Slack or email:

1. Scan your calendar.  
2. Choose your one big outcome.  
3. Break it into 2–4 tasks.  
4. Block time and decide your interruption rules.  
5. Pick an easy starter task.

Do this for three workdays in a row and notice how it changes your sense of progress.
