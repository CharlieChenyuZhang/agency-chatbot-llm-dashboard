### Human: Exams really stress me out. I blank during tests.
### Assistant: You’ll build both a technical exam-prep system and a stress-management routine so your brain isn’t overwhelmed on test day.

1. Change how you prepare
You’ll shift from “know the material” to “simulate the exam.”

For each exam:
- 7–10 days before:
  - Build a practice set:
    - Past exams, sample questions from slides/textbook, your own questions based on notes.
  - Each day:
    - Do at least one timed “mini-exam”:
      - 20–40 minutes,
      - Mixed question types,
      - No notes or internet.

- After each mini-exam:
  - Check answers immediately.
  - For each mistake:
    - Write:
      - What you did.
      - What you should have done.
      - Why you missed it (concept gap, rushing, misreading).

This directly trains your brain for the exact situation that currently causes the blanking.

2. Create “exam scripts” for each course
For each core course, you’ll prepare:
- A one-page “cheat sheet” (even if you can’t bring it in):
  - Key formulas, definitions, theorems.
  - Typical problem patterns and the steps to solve them.
- A problem-type checklist:
  - For example, algorithms:
    - “Given a graph, check: is it asking for shortest path, MST, connectivity, flows?”
  - For discrete math:
    - “Check: induction, proof by contradiction, direct proof, counting arguments,” etc.

Before each practice session and the real exam, you’ll quickly scan that sheet. This gives your mind “hooks” so it doesn’t feel lost.

3. Calm-down protocol during exams
You’ll use a specific routine the moment you feel panic or blank:

- Step 1: Grounding (30–60 seconds)
  - Put your pen down.
  - Take 3 slow breaths:
    - In for 4 seconds, hold for 2, out for 6.
  - Feel your feet on the floor and your hands on the desk.

- Step 2: Reset with structure
  - Flip to the back or a blank part of the page.
  - Write simple things you know for sure:
    - A definition.
    - A formula.
    - A basic example.
  - This reminds your brain that “you do know something,” breaking the panic loop.

- Step 3: Easy-wins pass
  - Scan the entire exam and mark:
    - Questions you can do right now.
  - Do those first to build confidence and get points quickly.

4. Night-before and day-of routines
Night before:
- No new heavy content.
- Focus on:
  - Reviewing your one-page summary.
  - Re-doing 2–4 representative problems.
- Protect sleep:
  - Aim for normal bedtime, no late cramming.

Day of:
- 10–15 minutes, 1–2 hours before exam:
  - Quickly review:
    - Key formulas/patterns from your sheet.
    - One or two problems you already solved correctly.
- 5 minutes before exam:
  - Breathe slowly.
  - Remind yourself:
    - “I’ve practiced this format; I’ll just follow my process.”

5. Post-exam debrief
After each exam:
- Spend 10–15 minutes:
  - Writing what actually happened:
    - Which questions caused blanking.
    - What thoughts/feelings showed up.
  - Decide:
    - One change in strategy for the next exam (more timed practice, different order of questions, etc.).

This combination—exam simulations, structured notes, and a panic protocol—reduces blanking by making the scenario familiar and giving you something to do the moment stress spikes.
