### Human: I’m not sure how to quantify my impact. I don’t always see the final revenue numbers.

### Assistant: You’ll still quantify your impact using the parts of the funnel and operations you do control or influence. I’ll structure this so you can plug in what you know without needing full finance data.

Use three levels of “quantification”:

#### 1. Direct, known metrics (use where you have data)
Examples:
- “Email campaigns I led increased open rates from A% to B% and click-through from C% to D%.”
- “Our paid campaigns reduced cost per lead from $X to $Y.”
- “We increased MQL volume by X% quarter-over-quarter.”

You pull these from:
- Marketing automation (HubSpot, Marketo, etc.).
- Ad platforms (Google Ads, LinkedIn).
- Your own dashboards/reports.

#### 2. Proximal metrics (you partially control them)
If you don’t see revenue, tie to pipeline or qualified opportunities:
- “Campaigns my team ran sourced approximately $X in pipeline over [timeframe], based on Salesforce/CRM reports.”
- “Our webinar series generated [N] opportunities passed to Sales.”

If even pipeline is fuzzy, use:
- “Generated [N] marketing qualified leads from [program].”
- “Increased demo requests/free trials by X%.”

#### 3. Operational and quality improvements
These are still valid, especially in a team-lead role:
- “Reduced time to launch campaigns from X weeks to Y weeks by implementing [process].”
- “Increased content output from X pieces/month to Y, while improving [engagement metric].”
- “Improved collaboration with Sales by implementing [cadence/process], resulting in [qualitative outcome, e.g., better follow-up, fewer misaligned leads].”

Here’s a simple formula you can use to write your bullets:

> **[Action] → [Metric/Result] → [Timeframe] → [Why it mattered]**

Example:
- “Redesigned our paid search structure → reduced cost per lead by ~25% over 6 months → allowed us to reallocate budget to higher-performing campaigns.”

List 5–10 bullets like this. Even if a few are non-numeric but clearly meaningful (e.g., launching a new channel, stabilizing the team), include them.

You’ll then choose your top 3–5 for the conversation and put all of them in your one-page promo brief.

### Human: What if my boss says the budget is tight and they can’t do anything right now?

### Assistant: You’ll treat “budget is tight” as a **timing and packaging** problem, not a “no.” Your goal is to:

1. Secure agreement on your *level and contribution*.
2. Lock in a *timeline* for the financial adjustment.
3. Explore *non-monetary* components in the interim.

Use this response structure:

#### Step 1: Separate level from budget

“I understand budget constraints are real, and I definitely want to be respectful of that.  

At the same time, it would be helpful to clarify whether we agree that I’m operating at a Senior Marketing Manager level in terms of scope and impact, independent of the current budget situation.”

If they agree you’re at that level, you’ve won the key point.

#### Step 2: Lock in the promotion path and timing

“Given that, I’d like to align on a concrete path:  

- When do you expect the next window to adjust comp?  
- What would be a realistic timeline to formalize the Senior Marketing Manager title and align compensation with that level?”

If they indicate a cycle (e.g., annual review):

“Great, in that case I’d like to ensure that my promotion to Senior Marketing Manager is explicitly on the table for that cycle, contingent on continuing the performance we discussed. I can put together a brief summary of my responsibilities and impact to support that process.”

#### Step 3: Ask for interim recognition and scope clarity

“Until we’re able to make the full compensation adjustment, I’d still like to ensure my role and scope are clearly defined at that senior level. That could be reflected in my title, ownership of [key area], and expectations around my team.”

If they resist immediate title change but agree you’re performing at that level, you then say:

“In that case, let’s agree on the specific milestones between now and [next cycle] that would make the promotion and comp adjustment a straightforward decision.”

Then you summarize and send a follow-up email after the meeting documenting:
- Their acknowledgment of your current level.
- The target timing.
- Any milestones/conditions.

### Human: Can I push for a bigger title change than “Senior Marketing Manager”? I feel like I’m already acting like a Head of Marketing for my area.

### Assistant: You can push for a title that better matches your scope, but you’ll do it in a way that’s grounded and flexible, not all-or-nothing.

#### 1. Decide your ideal vs. acceptable outcomes

I’ll define them like this:
- **Ideal:** “Head of [Function]” (e.g., Head of Demand Generation, Head of Product Marketing) or “Marketing Lead – [Area]”.
- **Acceptable:** “Senior Marketing Manager” with clearly defined ownership of that same function and path to a “Head of” title later.

#### 2. How to position the stronger title

In the meeting, after you describe your scope, you use:

“Given the scope I’m owning today—leading the team, setting strategy, and acting as the point person for [function/area]—I see my role as effectively the Head of [Function] for our [region/product/segment].

If it fits within our internal leveling structure, I’d like to explore a title that reflects that, such as **Head of [Function]** or **[Function] Lead**. My goal is for my title to accurately reflect the responsibility I carry, both internally and externally.”

Then you **immediately** show flexibility:

“If that isn’t feasible right now from a leveling standpoint, I’m also open to **Senior Marketing Manager** as long as my ownership of [function] and the path toward a Head-level title are clearly defined.”

This does three things:
- Signals ambition and clarity.
- Shows you’re reasonable and solution-oriented.
- Keeps you from being boxed into a weak title with heavy responsibilities.

#### 3. Lock in the “path” if they only offer Senior

If they land on Senior for now:

“I’m comfortable with Senior Marketing Manager as a next step. To make sure we’re aligned, I’d like to define what operating at a Head of [Function] level looks like here and what I’d need to demonstrate over the next 12–18 months to be considered for that title.”

Then you summarize those expectations back to them and follow up in writing.
