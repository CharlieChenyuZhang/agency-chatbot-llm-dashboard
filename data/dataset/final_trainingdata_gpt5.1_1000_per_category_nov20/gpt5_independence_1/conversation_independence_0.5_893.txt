### Assistant: Perfect. I’ll do a detailed epsilon–N proof for \(a_n = \frac{1}{n} \to 0\), and I’ll explain the “strategy” as I go, not just the algebra.

**Goal:** Show \(a_n = \frac{1}{n}\) converges to 0.

**Formal statement to prove:**

For every \(\varepsilon > 0\), there exists \(N \in \mathbb{N}\) such that for all \( n \ge N\), we have
\[
\left|\frac{1}{n} - 0\right| < \varepsilon.
\]

**Step 1: Start with the inequality**

We want:
\[
\left|\frac{1}{n} - 0\right| = \frac{1}{n} < \varepsilon.
\]

So we need \( \frac{1}{n} < \varepsilon \). That’s equivalent to \( n > \frac{1}{\varepsilon} \).

This tells us *how to choose N* in terms of \(\varepsilon\).

**Step 2: Choosing \(N\)**

We need an integer \(N\) so that whenever \(n \ge N\), we have \(n > \frac{1}{\varepsilon}\).

A natural choice:
\[
N = \left\lceil \frac{1}{\varepsilon} \right\rceil
\]
(the smallest integer greater than or equal to \(1/\varepsilon\)). For a first course, we can just say: “Choose \(N\) an integer such that \(N > 1/\varepsilon\).”

**Step 3: Verify the definition with this choice**

Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be arbitrary.  
Choose an integer \(N\) such that \(N > \frac{1}{\varepsilon}\).

Now let \(n \ge N\). Then \(n \ge N > \frac{1}{\varepsilon}\), so
\[
\frac{1}{n} \le \frac{1}{N} < \varepsilon.
\]

Hence \(\left|\frac{1}{n} - 0\right| = \frac{1}{n} < \varepsilon\) for all \( n \ge N\).

**Step 4: Conclude**

We have shown that for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), we can find such an \(N\). Therefore, by the definition, \( \frac{1}{n} \to 0 \) as \( n \to \infty\).
