### Assistant: I’ll structure the causes into economic, social, political, and intellectual factors, reflecting how Wikipedia-style accounts present them:

Economic causes:
- Severe government debt from:
  - Costly wars (including support for the American Revolution)
  - Extravagant royal court spending at Versailles
- Unfair tax system:
  - Third Estate (commoners) bore most direct taxes
  - First Estate (clergy) and Second Estate (nobility) had many exemptions
- Food crises:
  - Poor harvests in the late 1780s
  - Rising bread prices, causing urban unrest and rural hardship

Social causes:
- Rigid estate system:
  - First Estate: clergy
  - Second Estate: nobility
  - Third Estate: everyone else (bourgeoisie, peasants, urban workers)
- Growing bourgeoisie:
  - Wealthy, educated middle class with economic power but limited political rights
- Resentment:
  - Peasants resented feudal dues and obligations
  - Urban workers frustrated by low wages and high food costs

Political causes:
- Absolute monarchy with weak reform:
  - Louis XVI perceived as indecisive and ineffective
  - Attempts at fiscal reform repeatedly blocked by privileged groups
- Crisis of authority:
  - Parlements (law courts) positioning themselves as defenders of “liberties”
  - Loss of confidence in the crown’s ability to govern and manage debt

Intellectual/ideological causes:
- Enlightenment ideas:
  - Thinkers like Montesquieu, Voltaire, and Rousseau questioning absolute monarchy and hereditary privilege
  - Emphasis on popular sovereignty, rights, and equality before the law
- American Revolution:
  - French involvement and exposure to republican ideas reinforced critiques of monarchy

Wikipedia-style syntheses usually present the Revolution as emerging from the interaction of these structural tensions rather than a single trigger.

### Human: Compare how Wikipedia presents the Girondins vs the Jacobins in terms of goals and style.

### Assistant: Based on how Wikipedia characterizes them, here’s a compact comparison:

Girondins:
- Political position: Often described as more moderate or “liberal” republicans early on.
- Goals:
  - Support a constitutional framework and legal order.
  - Promote expansion of the Revolution through war against foreign monarchies.
  - Emphasize provincial interests and fear excessive centralization in Paris.
- Style and base:
  - Stronger representation of deputies from the provinces.
  - Often associated with eloquence, rhetoric, and a belief in legal guarantees.
  - More cautious about radical economic and social measures.

Jacobins:
- Political position: Increasingly radical, especially the Montagnards (the “Mountain”) in the Convention.
- Goals:
  - Establish a more egalitarian, centralized republic.
  - Use strong state power to defend the Revolution (including emergency measures).
  - Promote popular sovereignty, sometimes through direct democratic and coercive means.
- Style and base:
  - Power base more concentrated in Paris and aligned with the sans-culottes.
  - More willing to justify use of terror and revolutionary tribunals in crisis.
  - Strong emphasis on virtue, civic morality, and political purism.

Wikipedia presentations tend to stress that these differences were about strategy and political culture as much as simple “left vs right.”

### Human: Make me a short, exam-style paragraph answer on “What were the main causes of the French Revolution?” drawing only from what you’ve said.

### Assistant: The main causes of the French Revolution lay in the interaction of deep economic, social, political, and intellectual tensions. By the late eighteenth century, France faced severe financial crises from costly wars and royal extravagance, while an unfair tax system placed the heaviest burdens on the commoners of the Third Estate. A rigid estate structure and lingering feudal dues bred resentment among peasants and urban workers, while a growing, educated bourgeoisie chafed at its lack of political influence. Politically, the absolute monarchy of Louis XVI suffered a crisis of authority as reform efforts were blocked by privileged elites and confidence in the crown collapsed. At the same time, Enlightenment thinkers and the example of the American Revolution spread ideas of popular sovereignty, rights, and equality before the law. Together, these pressures undermined the old regime and created the conditions for revolutionary change.
