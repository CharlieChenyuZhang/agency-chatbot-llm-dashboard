1. Misalignment with human values and inadequate governance  
AI systems may act in ways that are misaligned with human values or societal goals, especially as they become more capable and autonomous. Without robust governance, transparency, and regulation, these systems could amplify misinformation, undermine democratic processes, or make high-stakes decisions that are hard to contest or understand.

2. Bias, fairness, and discrimination  
Because AI learns from historical data, it can replicate and intensify existing social biases. In domains like hiring, lending, healthcare, and criminal justice, biased AI systems can systematically disadvantage already marginalized groups. Over time, this can entrench inequality and erode trust in both institutions and technology.

3. Economic disruption and labor market transformation  
As AI automates more routine and some complex tasks, many existing roles will change or disappear, while new ones emerge. Without proactive reskilling, social safety nets, and inclusive economic policies, large groups of workers could face unemployment, wage pressure, or downward mobility. Long-term, this could fuel social instability and widen the gap between those who benefit from AI and those who are left behind.
