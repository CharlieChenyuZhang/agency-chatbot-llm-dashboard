### Human: Help me reach a 1.5x bodyweight deadlift

### Assistant:
I’ll build you a simple, effective plan and walk you through everything.

I’ll assume:
- You lift recreationally (not a complete beginner, but not advanced)
- You can train 3 days per week
- You’re okay with conventional barbell deadlifts

Goal structure:
- Phase 1 (Weeks 1–4): Technique + volume foundation  
- Phase 2 (Weeks 5–8): Strength focus  
- Phase 3 (Weeks 9–12): Peaking to hit 1.5× bodyweight for a single

I’ll express all weights as % of your current 1-rep max (1RM). If you don’t know it, estimate it from a heavy set of 3–5 reps using an online calculator and treat that as “current 1RM.”

---

**Weekly structure (3 days/week)**

**Day A – Deadlift + posterior chain**
- Deadlift: 5×3 @ 75–80%  
- Romanian Deadlift: 3×6–8  
- Bent-over row (barbell or DB): 3×6–8  
- Planks: 3×30–45 sec

**Day B – Squat + quads/core**
- Back squat: 4×5 @ moderate effort (around 70–75%)  
- Walking lunges: 3×8–10 steps/leg  
- Leg curls: 3×10–12  
- Hanging leg raises: 3×8–12

**Day C – Deadlift variation + upper back**
- Deadlift (lighter, speed focus): 6×2 @ 60–70%, fast and crisp  
- Pause deadlifts (pause just off floor): 3×3 @ ~65–70%  
- Pull-ups/lat pulldowns: 3×6–10  
- Face pulls: 3×12–15

Rest at least one day between deadlift days (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri or Tue/Thu/Sat).

---

**Progression by phase**

**Phase 1 (Weeks 1–4): Groove technique**
- Keep all work **1–2 reps in reserve** (never grinding).  
- Add **2.5–5 lb (1–2.5 kg)** to deadlifts each week if all sets move cleanly.  
- Focus on:
  - Bar close to shins
  - Neutral spine, chest up
  - Pushing floor away, not yanking the bar

**Phase 2 (Weeks 5–8): Strength**
- Day A: shift to **4×4 @ 78–82%**  
- Day C main deadlift: **5×3 @ 70–75%**  
- Keep adding 2.5–5 lb weekly as long as bar speed and form are good.  
- RDLs and rows stay in 6–8 rep range, progressing slowly.

**Phase 3 (Weeks 9–12): Peak**

Weeks 9–10:
- Day A: 5×3 @ 80–85%  
- Day C: 4×2 @ 75–80%

Week 11:
- Day A: 4×2 @ 85–88%  
- Day C: 3×1 @ 88–92%

Week 12:
- Early week: 3×1 @ 80% (easy, just staying sharp)  
- End of week: **Test day**  
  - Warm up, then attempt:
    - 1×1 @ ~90% (easy)  
    - 1×1 @ ~97%  
    - 1×1 @ your **1.5× bodyweight target**

---

**Recovery & support**

- Sleep: Aim for **7–9 hours** nightly.  
- Protein: About **1.6–2.2 g/kg bodyweight** daily.  
- Calories: Slight maintenance or small surplus (about +200–300 kcal/day) to support strength gains.  
- Mobility: 5–10 min hip + hamstring + ankle work on training days.

---

**Form checklist for every rep**

- Feet roughly hip-width, toes slightly out (adjust as needed)  
- Bar over midfoot; shins close but not jammed  
- Grip the bar, bend knees until shins touch  
- Big breath, brace hard (tight abs), flatten low back  
- Push floor away, think “legs first,” bar drags up thighs  
- Lock out by squeezing glutes, not hyperextending your lower back  
- Control descent; don’t just drop it

Follow this 12-week structure, track your numbers, and the 1.5× bodyweight deadlift becomes very realistic, especially if your current max is below that.

### Human:
What should my warm-up look like on regular training days, not just test day?

### Assistant:
You’ll use a structured, repeatable warm-up that scales with the day’s top weight.

**General warm-up (5–7 minutes)**
- 2–3 minutes easy cardio (bike, brisk walk, jump rope)  
- Dynamic mobility:
  - Leg swings (front/back, side/side): 10 each leg  
  - Hip circles: 10 each direction  
  - Bodyweight good mornings: 2×10  
  - Air squats: 2×10

---

**Deadlift-specific warm-up**

Use about **4–5 ramp-up sets** before your work sets. Example for a day where your top working sets are at **80 kg**:

1. Empty bar (20 kg): 2×8  
2. 40 kg: 1×5  
3. 60 kg: 1×3  
4. 70 kg: 1×2  
5. Then start work sets at 80 kg

Guidelines:
- First 2–3 warm-up sets: smooth and easy, focus on form.  
- Last warm-up: should “wake you up” but still be clearly submaximal.  
- Keep warm-up rest short (30–60 sec early sets, 60–90 sec on last warm-up).

Use the same structure for squats:
- Work up in similar jumps so your last warm-up is ~85–90% of your work weight.

### Human:
If my grip is failing, what should I do without changing the overall structure?

### Assistant:
Keep the structure exactly as is and layer grip solutions on top.

Here’s how you handle it:

1. **Use mixed grip on heavier sets**
   - For sets above ~75–80% (e.g., 70+ kg for you as you progress), switch to **mixed grip** (one hand over, one under).

2. **Use chalk if available**
   - Apply chalk on all work sets, especially once you’re at 80 kg and above.

3. **Add 2 grip-focused accessories per week**
   - Slot these at the end of your deadlift days:

   Option A (barbell/DB holds):
   - Barbell or heavy dumbbell holds:  
     - 3×20–30 seconds with a weight that challenges you by 20 seconds.  
     - Progress by adding small weight or time weekly.

   Option B (plate pinches):
   - Pinch 2 small plates together (smooth sides out) and hold:  
     - 3×20–30 seconds per hand.

4. **Limit straps to very specific cases**
   - If needed, only use straps for RDLs or very high-rep back work, **never** for your main heavy deadlift sets. The goal is to force your grip to adapt.

Keep the progression, volumes, and schedule unchanged; just layer in these grip strategies at the tail end of deadlift days.
