   3. Add pasta, stir once so it doesn’t stick.  
   4. Cook for the time on the package minus 1 minute.  
   5. While pasta cooks, put your pan on low heat.  
   6. Add 1–2 tablespoons butter (or oil), then the minced garlic.  
   7. Stir constantly for 1–2 minutes until the garlic smells good and just starts to turn light golden (do not let it get brown).  
   8. Turn the heat off under the pan.  
   9. Before draining the pasta, scoop out half a cup of the starchy pasta water.  
   10. Drain pasta, then immediately add it to the pan with garlic butter.  
   11. Turn heat to low. Add a splash of pasta water and toss until it looks glossy, not watery.  
   12. Taste, then add salt and pepper until it tastes good to you.  
   13. Add cheese, herbs, or chili flakes if you have them. Toss again. Serve.

### Assistant: Perfect, I’ll base the recipe on **eggs + frozen mixed vegetables**, all in one pan.

Here’s your **Egg & Veggie Stir-Fry with Rice**:

1. **Cook the rice first**
   - Rinse 1 cup of rice in water (if you can) until the water is less cloudy.  
   - Put rice in a pot with 2 cups water and a pinch of salt.  
   - Bring to a boil, then immediately turn heat to low, cover, and cook 15 minutes (don’t open the lid).  
   - Turn off heat and let it sit covered 5 more minutes, then fluff with a fork.

2. **Prep while rice cooks**
   - Take your frozen mixed veggies out of the freezer so they start to soften a bit.  
   - Crack 2–3 eggs into a bowl, add a small pinch of salt and pepper, beat with a fork.

3. **Stir-fry process (all in one pan)**
   - Put your pan on medium heat.  
   - Add 1–2 tablespoons oil.  
   - When the oil is hot (you’ll see a slight shimmer), pour in the beaten eggs.  
   - Let them sit a few seconds, then gently push them around with a spatula until just set (soft curds).  
   - Slide the eggs onto a plate; you’ll add them back later.
   - In the same pan, add a tiny bit more oil if needed.  
   - Add your frozen veggies straight in.  
   - Cook, stirring, for 4–6 minutes until hot and no longer icy; if they start to brown too fast, lower the heat.  
   - Add a pinch of salt and pepper.

4. **Combine with rice and season**
   - Add 1.5–2 cups of your cooked rice to the pan with veggies.  
   - Break up any clumps with your spoon.  
   - Pour in 1–2 tablespoons soy sauce. Stir well so everything is coated.  
   - Taste: if it’s bland, add a bit more soy sauce or a tiny pinch of salt.  
   - Add the scrambled eggs back in and stir to combine.  
   - If you have chili sauce or flakes, add a little at the end.

You now have a basic template you can repeat and modify endlessly.

### Human: I always mess up timing and things burn. How do I know what heat level to use?

### Assistant: Use this simple system and you’ll stop burning things:

1. **Understand your stove in plain terms**  
   Think of it as three zones:
   - Low: keeping things warm, melting butter, gentle simmer.  
   - Medium: most cooking happens here—eggs, stir-fries, sautéing veggies.  
   - High: boiling water, searing meat quickly, or heating a pan fast (then turning it down).

2. **Default rule for you right now**
   - Start **almost everything on medium**, not high.  
   - Only use high for boiling water or quick searing once you’re more confident.

3. **Visual and sound cues**
   - Oil ready: it looks slightly shimmery and moves loosely in the pan. If it’s smoking, it’s too hot—turn it down and let it cool for a minute.  
   - Veggies cooking right: gentle sizzling, not aggressive popping. If it’s popping hard and browning instantly, lower to medium-low.  
   - Eggs: should set slowly. If they brown quickly or get rubbery, the pan is too hot.

4. **Practical habit that prevents burning**
   - When in doubt, turn the heat down, not up. Food will still cook; it just takes a bit longer, and you can recover.  
   - Stir or flip more often at first; you can reduce stirring once you have a feel for your pan.

Next time you cook:
- Start on **medium**.  
- If nothing is happening after ~30–40 seconds, go slightly toward medium-high.  
- If it’s spitting or smoking, turn it slightly toward medium-low.

I’ll keep assuming “medium first” for all your upcoming recipes until you’re comfortable adjusting on your own.

### Assistant: I’ll move you to a **one-pot tomato pasta**, since it reinforces timing, seasoning, and starch control without extra dishes.

**One-Pot Tomato Pasta (about 2 servings)**

1. **Ingredients**
   - 200 g / ~7 oz dry pasta (short shapes work best)  
   - 1 can (about 14 oz / 400 g) diced tomatoes  
   - 2 cloves garlic (or ½ tsp garlic powder)  
   - ½ onion (optional but good)  
   - 2–3 tablespoons oil  
   - 1–2 cups water or broth  
   - ½ teaspoon salt to start, plus more to taste  
   - Black pepper  
   - Optional: dried oregano or Italian seasoning, a bit of cheese

2. **Steps**
   - Put a pot on **medium** heat. Add oil.  
   - If using onion/garlic: chop roughly.  
   - Add onion, cook 3–5 minutes until soft, stirring. Add garlic, cook 30–60 seconds.  
   - Add the canned tomatoes (with juice), pasta, 1 cup water/broth, ½ teaspoon salt, and a pinch of pepper.  
   - Stir, bring to a gentle boil, then lower to a **strong simmer** (between medium and low).  
   - Stir every 2–3 minutes so the pasta doesn’t stick to the bottom.  
   - If the liquid gets too thick before pasta is cooked, add a bit more water (¼ cup at a time).  
   - After the time on the pasta package, taste a piece. If it’s still firm/chalky, cook a few minutes more, adding small splashes of water if needed.  
   - When the pasta is cooked and the sauce is thick but not dry, turn off heat.  
   - Taste; add more salt and pepper until it tastes good. Sprinkle cheese if you have it.

This dish teaches you:
- Watching liquid levels  
- Stirring rhythmically  
- Adjusting seasoning at the end, not just the beginning
