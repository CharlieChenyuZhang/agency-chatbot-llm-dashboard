### Human: I don’t know my 1RMs right now. How do I figure out what weights to start with?
### Assistant: You’ll estimate training maxes using a simple rep test in your next three workouts, one lift per day, without going to absolute failure.

**Day 1 – Squat test**
1) Warm up as usual.  
2) Gradually increase until you reach a weight you can do for 5 tough reps, where the 5th rep is hard but you could maybe grind out one more with good form.  
3) Use this formula:  
   - Estimated 1RM ≈ weight × (1 + reps/30)  
   For 5 reps: 1RM ≈ weight × 1.17  

Example: If you squat 100 kg for 5 good reps, your estimated 1RM ≈ 117 kg.

**Day 2 – Bench test**  
Same process: work up to a tough set of 5 reps.  
- Use the same formula to estimate your bench 1RM.

**Day 3 – Deadlift test**  
Same process again: tough set of 3–5 reps (don’t go to absolute failure).  
- If you only feel safe doing 3 reps, 1RM ≈ weight × 1.10.  
- If you do 5 reps, 1RM ≈ weight × 1.17.

Once you have those three estimated 1RMs, plug them directly into the percentages I gave for each day and week. Use slightly conservative numbers; if in doubt, round your estimated 1RM down a little. That makes the program more sustainable and you’ll still hit PRs at Week 12.

### Human: What if I start getting joint pain or feeling beat up? How do I adjust without messing up progress?
### Assistant: You’ll use three built-in autoregulation rules to manage fatigue while keeping the progression intact.

**1) Bad joint day (knees, shoulders, low back):**  
- Keep the same exercise, reduce load to about 80–85% of the planned weight, and keep the reps the same.  
  - Example: Planned squat: 225 × 5 × 4  
    - Adjusted: 185–190 × 5 × 4  
- Focus on perfect technique and smooth tempo. Count it as a “technique day,” not a missed session.

**2) Systemic fatigue (feel drained, but not injured):**  
- Cut one work set from each main lift that day.  
  - Example: 4×5 becomes 3×5.  
- Accessories: drop 1 exercise or do only 2 sets per accessory instead of 3.  
- Next week, resume the normal prescription as long as you feel better.

**3) Pain during a specific lift (sharp or worsening):**  
Immediately modify the movement, not just the load:  
- Squat bothering knees: switch to high-bar squat or box squat for that day, same sets/reps, lighter weight.  
- Bench bothering shoulders: switch to close-grip or slight incline, same sets/reps, slightly lighter.  
- Deadlift bothering low back: switch to trap-bar deadlift or elevated deadlift (from blocks), same sets/reps, lighter.

If the same pain persists for more than 2 weeks in a row, you keep training but permanently sub that variant in for the rest of the cycle and lower the weights 10–15% to rebuild while healing.

Recovery support:  
- Sleep 7–9 hours.  
- 5–10 minutes of easy walking after lifting.  
- Light mobility on off days, not aggressive stretching of painful joints.
