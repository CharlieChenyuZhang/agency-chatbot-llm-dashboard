**Subject:** A 15-minute ritual that can save your whole week  

**Title:** Design Your Weekly Focus Ritual

Most people don’t lose their week all at once.  
They lose it in tiny, quiet ways.

An extra meeting here.  
A “quick” email there.  
One more thing you “might as well” say yes to.

By Friday, you’ve been busy for five days straight… and somehow the things that actually mattered never made it to the top of the list.

This isn’t a willpower problem. It’s a system problem.

Today, you’re going to install a very simple system: a **Weekly Focus Ritual**.

---

### The Idea: One Small Meeting That Changes the Week

A Weekly Focus Ritual is a 15-minute appointment you keep with yourself once a week.

The goal is not to plan every hour or predict the future.  
The goal is to decide, *on purpose*, what will make this week a win — before the chaos begins.

Instead of waking up each day and reacting to whatever screams the loudest, you’ll already know:

- What actually matters,
- What constraints you’re working within,
- What absolutely must not be dropped.

Think of it as setting your week on rails. Yes, things will change. But they’ll change around a clear intention instead of around everyone else’s emergencies.

---

### The Tactic: The 3×3 Weekly Template

Your Weekly Focus Ritual is built on a simple 3×3 template:

1. **3 Priorities** – The three outcomes that would make this week a clear win.
2. **3 Constraints** – The real-world limits you need to accept.
3. **3 Non-Negotiables** – The actions or habits you will protect, no matter what.

You can do this on paper, in a notes app, or in your task manager. The tool doesn’t matter. The thinking does.

#### Step 1: Choose Your 3 Priorities

Ask yourself:  
“If, by Friday, I’ve only moved three things forward, which three would matter most?”

Make them concrete:

- “Make progress on project” → “Ship draft v1 of client proposal”
- “Work on fitness” → “Do 3 strength workouts”
- “Job search” → “Apply to 5 roles + reach out to 2 contacts”

Write down your three.

#### Step 2: Acknowledge Your 3 Constraints

Constraints are not excuses. They’re conditions.

Examples:

- “Two days lost to offsite meetings”
- “Evening family commitments”
- “Low energy in the mornings this week”

When you name constraints up front, you stop planning a fantasy week and start planning a real one. That’s how you avoid the guilt spiral of impossible expectations.

Write down your three biggest constraints.

#### Step 3: Protect Your 3 Non-Negotiables

These are the actions that keep you steady even when the week goes sideways.

Examples:

- “30 minutes of focused work on my top priority every weekday”
- “No meetings before 10am on two days”
- “Walk outside for 20 minutes daily”

They should be small enough that you can keep them even during a rough week, but meaningful enough that they move the needle over time.

Write down your three non-negotiables.

---

### The Exercise: Run Your First Weekly Focus Ritual (10 Minutes)

Do this right now. Not later. It’s designed to take about 10 minutes.

1. **Brain dump (3 minutes)**  
   List everything pulling at your attention for the coming week — projects, deadlines, errands, people you need to get back to.

2. **Pick your 3 priorities (3 minutes)**  
   From that list, circle or highlight the **three** things that would matter most if you made progress on them. Rewrite them as clear, concrete outcomes.

3. **Name your 3 constraints (2 minutes)**  
   What realities are you working with this week? Time, energy, commitments, travel — capture the three that will shape your days.

4. **Set your 3 non-negotiables (2 minutes)**  
   Decide which actions or habits you will protect even if everything else moves around.

5. **Schedule next week’s ritual (1 minute)**  
   Open your calendar and create a **15-minute recurring event** at the same time each week. Name it: “Weekly Focus Ritual”.

That’s it. Your first ritual is done.

---

### Closing

You don’t need a perfect system to have a better week.  
You just need one small habit that keeps pulling you back to what matters.

This 15-minute ritual is that habit.

Next Thursday, we’ll build on this by taming your to-do list so it actually reflects your priorities instead of everyone else’s.

Until then: protect your 3 priorities, respect your constraints, and keep your non-negotiables.

Reply and tell me your three priorities for this week — saying them out loud makes them real.

- **Idea:** You don’t need 8 hours of focus per day; a few protected deep work blocks per week can transform your output.

  - Step 2: Block 2× 60–90 minute deep work slots in your calendar.
  - Step 3: Create a “deep work ritual” (phone away, single tab, etc.).

  - Look at next week’s calendar.
  - Block two deep work sessions.
  - Write down what you’ll work on in each.

#### Week 4 – Saying No Without Burning Bridges

**Working title:** “How to Say No Without Sounding Difficult”

- **Idea:** Protecting focus requires declining or reshaping requests, but you can do it in a way that maintains relationships.
- **Tactic:**
  - Introduce 2–3 simple scripts:
    - No with alternative timing.
    - No with a tradeoff (“If I do this, X will slip”).
    - Redirection to a resource or another person.
- **Exercise (10 minutes):**
  - List 3 recurring types of requests that derail your focus.
  - For each, choose one of the scripts and adapt it to your tone.
  - Save them somewhere accessible (notes app, email snippets).

---

#### Week 5 – Managing Energy, Not Just Time

**Working title:** “Designing a Day Around Your Energy”

- **Idea:** Time management fails if you ignore energy; aligning tasks with your natural energy curve boosts output.
- **Tactic:**
  - Step 1: Map your typical energy across the day (high, medium, low).
  - Step 2: Assign types of work to each band (deep work, shallow work, admin).
  - Step 3: Make one simple change: move a deep-focus task into a high-energy slot this week.
- **Exercise (10 minutes):**
  - Sketch your past 2–3 days by energy levels.
  - Label each block with the type of work you actually did.
  - Choose one recurring task to move to a better energy window next week.
