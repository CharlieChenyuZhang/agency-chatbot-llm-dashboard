Execution: Project board cleanup (60–90 minutes)

Working assumptions:
- You use a Kanban-style board with columns like Backlog / To Do / In Progress / Done.  
- It’s cluttered with old, vague, and duplicate tickets.

Steps I’d take, in order:

1) Set rules (so decisions are fast)
- Any card with no activity or comment in 90+ days → close or archive.  
- Any card without a clear next action → clarify or merge into a larger initiative.  
- Every active card must have:  
  - Owner  
  - Deadline or timebox  
  - Clear title starting with a verb.

2) Clean the “Done” column
- Archive everything completed more than 30 days ago.  
- Keep only the last 2–4 weeks for easy reference.  
  Outcome: A lighter board and better performance.

3) Triage the Backlog
Create three labels or sections:
- “Now” – must be done this week/next.  
- “Next” – plausible work for this quarter.  
- “Later” – keep, but not this quarter.

Then:
- For each card older than 90 days:
  - If still relevant but vague → rewrite the title and description, and tag as “Next” or “Later.”  
  - If obsolete or superseded → close with a quick comment: “Closing as obsolete; superseded by [link].”
- Merge duplicates under the most complete card and link the old ones to it.

4) Normalize “In Progress”
- Limit WIP: no more than 3–5 “In Progress” tasks per person.  
- Move anything truly not being worked on back to “To Do” with a short note: “Paused; will resume after [X].”
- Ensure every “In Progress” card has:
  - An owner  
  - A short checklist of steps remaining.

5) Rebuild clarity for the next 2 weeks
- Pick 3–5 top priorities from “Now” and place them in “To Do (This Week).”  
- Everything else stays in Backlog/Next/Later.
