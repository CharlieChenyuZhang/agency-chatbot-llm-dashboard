### Assistant: I’ll show you how to do each type so you can copy the method for the rest of your worksheet.

**1) 3x - 5 = 16**

Goal: get x by itself.

- Add 5 to both sides:  
  3x - 5 + 5 = 16 + 5  
  3x = 21  
- Divide both sides by 3:  
  x = 21 ÷ 3 = 7  

**Answer: x = 7**

---

**2) 2(x + 4) = 18**

First, distribute the 2.

- Distribute:  
  2 · x + 2 · 4 = 18  
  2x + 8 = 18  
- Subtract 8 from both sides:  
  2x + 8 - 8 = 18 - 8  
  2x = 10  
- Divide both sides by 2:  
  x = 10 ÷ 2 = 5  

**Answer: x = 5**

---

**3) 5x + 7 = 2x + 25**

You have x-terms on both sides. Get them on one side.

- Subtract 2x from both sides:  
  5x - 2x + 7 = 2x - 2x + 25  
  3x + 7 = 25  
- Subtract 7 from both sides:  
  3x + 7 - 7 = 25 - 7  
  3x = 18  
- Divide both sides by 3:  
  x = 18 ÷ 3 = 6  

**Answer: x = 6**

### Human: Nice, thanks. I also have a science quiz tomorrow on cells, and I haven’t really studied. Can you help me quickly review?

### Assistant: I’ll give you a tight, high-yield review focused on the basics your quiz is most likely to cover.

**1. Cell theory (know these three points)**  
1) All living things are made of cells.  
2) Cells are the basic unit of structure and function in living things.  
3) All cells come from pre-existing cells.

**2. Main cell parts and jobs (animal cell focus)**  
- **Cell membrane** – controls what goes in and out of the cell; like a gatekeeper.  
- **Cytoplasm** – jelly-like fluid where cell parts are found.  
- **Nucleus** – control center; holds DNA.  
- **Mitochondria** – “powerhouse”; makes energy for the cell.  
- **Ribosomes** – make proteins.  
- **Endoplasmic reticulum (ER)** – helps move materials around inside the cell.  
- **Golgi apparatus** – packages and ships proteins.  
- **Lysosomes** – break down waste and old cell parts.  

**3. Plant cell extras**  
- **Cell wall** – strong outer layer; gives plant cells shape and support.  
- **Chloroplasts** – do photosynthesis; turn sunlight into food (glucose).  
- **Large central vacuole** – stores water and helps keep the plant firm.

**4. Prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic**  
- **Prokaryotic cells**: no nucleus, simpler (example: bacteria).  
- **Eukaryotic cells**: have a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (examples: plants, animals, fungi).

### Human: Okay, that covers math, English, and science. I also have to pick a topic for a simple science project due in two weeks. Any ideas that are easy but look impressive?

### Assistant: I’ll give you ready-to-use project ideas that are simple to do at home but still look good at school. I’ll assume a middle-school level and keep materials easy to find.

Here are three strong options:

1. **Plant growth and light color**  
   - **Question:** Does the color of light affect how fast plants grow?  
   - **Basic method:**  
     - Take 3–4 small plants or seeds in cups.  
     - Put each under a different color of light (white, red, blue, green) using colored plastic or paper.  
     - Keep water and soil the same.  
     - Measure height every few days.  
   - **What it shows:** How light affects photosynthesis and plant growth.  
   - **Why it looks impressive:** Multiple groups, charts, and graphs.

2. **Which liquid causes the most tooth decay (egg shell experiment)**  
   - **Question:** Which drink damages “teeth” the most: water, soda, juice, or sports drink?  
   - **Basic method:**  
     - Use clean eggshells (they’re similar to tooth enamel).  
     - Put pieces in cups with different liquids.  
     - Check color, texture, and strength over several days.  
   - **What it shows:** Acids and sugar effects on enamel.  
   - **Why it looks impressive:** Clear visual changes and an easy health connection.

3. **Homemade solar oven**  
   - **Question:** Can a simple solar oven made from a pizza box heat food?  
   - **Basic method:**  
     - Use a pizza box, aluminum foil, plastic wrap, and black paper.  
     - Build a flap covered with foil to reflect sunlight into the box.  
     - Place a marshmallow or piece of chocolate inside and measure how long it takes to melt.  
   - **What it shows:** Solar energy and heat absorption.  
   - **Why it looks impressive:** Looks like a “real” device and is fun to demo.

If you want the absolute easiest one to manage, use **the egg shell + drinks** project. It needs almost no special materials, gives clear results, and is simple to explain in class.
