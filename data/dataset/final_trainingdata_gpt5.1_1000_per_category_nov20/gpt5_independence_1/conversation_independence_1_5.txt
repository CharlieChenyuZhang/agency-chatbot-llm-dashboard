### Human: Okay, give me that checklist of questions to ask in the consultation.
### Assistant: Use this checklist during the consultation to evaluate the lawyer. You don’t need to ask all of them, but hitting most of them will give you a clear picture.

#### About their experience and focus
1. “How much of your practice is focused on car accident injury cases like mine?”
2. “How many Los Angeles car accident cases have you handled in the last few years?”
3. “Have you handled cases with facts similar to mine (clear fault, injuries, LA-area crash)? What were the outcomes?”

#### About your specific case
4. “Based on what I’ve told you, what do you see as the **strengths** and **weaknesses** of my case?”
5. “What additional information or documents do you need from me right away?”
6. “How long do you think a case like mine typically takes to resolve in LA County?”

#### About money and fees
7. “Do you work on a contingency fee? What percentage do you charge, and when does that percentage apply?”
8. “Who pays for case costs (experts, records, filing fees) as the case goes on? How are those reimbursed if we recover money?”
9. “If there’s no recovery, will I owe you anything for fees or costs?”

#### About communication and working together
10. “Who will actually handle my case day to day—will it be you, another attorney, or a team?”
11. “How often will I get updates about my case, and in what form (phone, email, portal)?”
12. “If I have questions, how quickly do you or your team usually respond?”

#### About strategy and expectations
13. “Do you think my case is likely to settle, or is it the kind of case that often goes to trial?”
14. “What are the main steps you expect in my case over the next 3–6 months?”
15. “What can I do right now to strengthen my case and avoid hurting it?”

#### About red flags and transparency
16. “Have you ever had any disciplinary actions with the State Bar? If so, can you explain them?”
17. “Can you walk me through an example settlement or verdict in a case similar to mine—without names—so I understand what’s realistic?”

As you talk, pay attention to:

- Whether they **answer clearly** or dodge questions.
- Whether they **listen** and can summarize your situation back to you accurately.
- Whether they **overpromise** (“You’re guaranteed a huge payout”)—that’s a bad sign.

After the call, write down your impressions immediately: how comfortable you felt, how well they explained things, and any concerns. If you feel uneasy, you can move on to the next lawyer on your shortlist and repeat this process.
