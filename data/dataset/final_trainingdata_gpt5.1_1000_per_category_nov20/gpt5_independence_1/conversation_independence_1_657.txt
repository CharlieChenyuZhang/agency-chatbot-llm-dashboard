**Example: A basic work-rate problem (classic analytical type)**  
“Together, Alice and Bob can finish a job in 6 hours. Alice alone can do it in 10 hours. How long would it take Bob alone?”

We’ll apply the routine:

1. **Rephrase the question**
   - Two people together take 6 hours. One of them alone takes 10 hours. I need the time for the other person alone.

2. **List given information and unknowns**
   - Given:
     - Together: 6 hours per job.
     - Alice: 10 hours per job.
   - Unknown:
     - Bob’s time per job.

3. **Choose a representation**
   - Use **rates** (jobs per hour):
     - Let:
       - A = Alice’s rate
       - B = Bob’s rate
     - Then:
       - A + B = combined rate

4. **Plan**
   - Convert times to rates, set up equation, solve for B, convert back to time.

5. **Execute**
   - A = 1 job / 10 hours = 1/10 job per hour
   - A + B = 1 job / 6 hours = 1/6 job per hour
   - So: 1/10 + B = 1/6
   - B = 1/6 − 1/10
   - Find common denominator: 30
     - 1/6 = 5/30
     - 1/10 = 3/30
   - B = (5/30 − 3/30) = 2/30 = 1/15 job per hour
   - Time for Bob = 1 job / (1/15 per hour) = 15 hours

6. **Check and reflect**
   - Together rate: 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 = 1/6 → matches given.
   - Intuition: Bob is slower than Alice (15 vs 10), but together they are faster than either alone (6). That makes sense.

  - C: “I am A.”

Rules:
- T’s statements are always true.
- L’s statements are always false.
- A’s statements can be either, with no fixed pattern.

Unknown:
- Assignment of T, L, A to An, B, C.

Use **case analysis** with simple logic:
- Consider who could possibly be T, and test for contradictions.

- Check each person’s claim against each possible role.
- Use the strongest constraints first:

**Problem (Number pattern):**

**1. Rephrase the question**

**2. List given information and unknowns**

Given sequence:
- a₁ = 2
- a₂ = 5
- a₃ = 11
- a₄ = 23
- a₅ = 47

Unknown:
- a₆ = ?

**3. Choose a representation**

Common representations for such sequences:
- Look at **differences** between consecutive terms.
- Look at **ratios** (division).
- Try simple formulas (e.g., multiply by something then add/subtract something).

**4. Plan before calculating**

Plan:
- Compute first differences: a₂ − a₁, a₃ − a₂, etc.
- See if those differences follow a simpler pattern (like doubling, adding 1, etc.).
- If that fails, try a direct rule like: “double and then add/subtract something.”

**5. Execute**

Compute first differences:
- 5 − 2 = 3
- 11 − 5 = 6
- 23 − 11 = 12
- 47 − 23 = 24

Differences: 3, 6, 12, 24

- Each time doubled:
  - 3 → 6 → 12 → 24

So:
- Difference sequence: d₁ = 3, d₂ = 6, d₃ = 12, d₄ = 24
  - Pattern: dₙ = 3 × 2^(n−1)

Predict next difference:
- d₅ = 24 × 2 = 48

So next term:
- a₆ = a₅ + d₅ = 47 + 48 = 95

Check with a direct description of the rule:
- Start with 2.
- Add 3 → 5.
- Add 6 → 11.
- Add 12 → 23.
- Add 24 → 47.
- So the rule is: each time, add **double** the previous increment.
- That gives +48 next, so 95 is consistent.

Reflection:
- Technique used: **analyze differences**, find a simpler pattern in them, then apply.
- Pattern type: “second-level” pattern (the interesting part was not the sequence itself, but the sequence of differences).
