**Question (interviewer mode)**  
You ran an A/B test on a signup flow. Variant B increased conversion from 20% to 21%. You had 20,000 users in each group.  

1. How would you determine if this uplift is statistically significant?  
2. What assumptions are you making?  
3. If it is statistically significant, does that automatically mean you should ship variant B? Why or why not?

---

**Model excellent answer (candidate mode)**

1. **Determining statistical significance**
   - “This is a difference in proportions problem. I’d run a two‑sample z‑test for proportions.”
   - Define:
     - \( p_A = 0.20 \), \( n_A = 20{,}000 \)
     - \( p_B = 0.21 \), \( n_B = 20{,}000 \)
   - Pooled proportion:
     \[
     p = \frac{p_A n_A + p_B n_B}{n_A + n_B}
       = \frac{0.20 \cdot 20000 + 0.21 \cdot 20000}{40000}
       = \frac{4000 + 4200}{40000}
       = 0.205
     \]
   - Standard error:
     \[
     SE = \sqrt{p(1-p)\left(\frac{1}{n_A} + \frac{1}{n_B}\right)}
        = \sqrt{0.205 \cdot 0.795 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{20000} + \frac{1}{20000}\right)}
        = \sqrt{0.205 \cdot 0.795 \cdot \frac{2}{20000}}
     \]
     Numerically this is around:
     \[
     SE \approx \sqrt{0.205 \cdot 0.795 \cdot 0.0001} \approx \sqrt{0.0000163} \approx 0.004
     \]
   - Difference in proportions:
     \[
     \Delta = p_B - p_A = 0.01
     \]
   - z‑score:
     \[
     z = \frac{\Delta}{SE} \approx \frac{0.01}{0.004} \approx 2.5
     \]
   - At a 5% significance level (two‑sided), critical z ≈ ±1.96. |2.5| > 1.96, so I’d reject \(H_0: p_A = p_B\). The uplift is statistically significant at the 5% level.

2. **Assumptions**
   - Independence: Users in A and B are independent and randomly assigned.
   - Stable environment: No major external changes (seasonality, outages, other experiments impacting signup).
   - Sufficient sample size: With 20k per group and conversion around 20%, normal approximation is reasonable.
   - Correct measurement: Conversion is measured consistently between variants (same definition, same logging).

3. **Statistical significance vs. decision to ship**
   - “Statistical significance is necessary but not sufficient.”
   - Consider:
     - **Practical significance:** A 1‑percentage‑point uplift from 20% to 21% is a 5% relative lift. I’d translate that into business impact: incremental signups per month, revenue, downstream engagement.
     - **Cost and risk:** If variant B is more complex, adds latency, or causes user confusion in certain segments, the small benefit might not justify the cost.
     - **Heterogeneous effects:** I’d check segment breakdowns (e.g., new vs returning, mobile vs desktop) to ensure the uplift is robust and not driven by a tiny subgroup.
     - **Data quality:** I’d validate tracking and sanity‑check metrics (e.g., no spike in errors or drop in later funnel steps).
   - Conclusion: “If the effect is both statistically and practically meaningful, and there are no negative signals in secondary metrics or key segments, I would recommend shipping B. Otherwise, I might extend the test or treat this as an informative but not yet decisive result.”

This is the level of structure, clarity, and quantification you should aim for in stats/experiment answers.
