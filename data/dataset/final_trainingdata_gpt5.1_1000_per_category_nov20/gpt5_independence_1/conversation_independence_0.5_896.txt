Very simplified, in California your main realistic options at the start are:

1) Sole Proprietorship (default if you do nothing formal)
   - Easiest and cheapest to start.
   - You just start doing business under your own name.
   - You report income on your personal tax return.
   - Downside: No legal separation between you and the business—you’re personally liable.

2) Single-Member LLC
   - Requires filing with the state and paying ongoing fees (in CA, there’s an annual franchise tax—historically around $800, though there have been temporary relief measures for new businesses; you’d need to confirm the current rules).
   - Offers liability protection in many cases (the business is a separate legal entity).
   - More formal, looks more “serious,” may help with separating finances.

Given:
- You’re starting small, service-based, low physical risk (fitness coaching online, not running a gym).
- Medium risk tolerance.
- Just starting to validate your idea.

A common practical path (not legal advice, just what many solo founders do):
- Start as a sole proprietor to validate the business and get your first few clients.
- Use a separate bank account (even if it’s not a formal business account yet) to keep finances cleaner.
- Switch to an LLC once you:
  - Are making consistent income, and/or
  - Feel you need the extra legal separation.
