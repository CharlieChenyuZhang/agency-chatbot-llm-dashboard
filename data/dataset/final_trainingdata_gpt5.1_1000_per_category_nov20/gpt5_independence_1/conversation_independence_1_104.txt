### Human: Help me become fluent in French within a year

### Assistant: You’ll work toward B2–C1 level in 12 months with a structured plan. I’ll set the strategy and concrete routines for you.

**Overall strategy (12 months)**  
1. Daily French exposure (60–90 minutes minimum).  
2. Systematic grammar + vocab.  
3. Listening and speaking from day one.  
4. Regular progress checkpoints with test-style tasks.

---

## 1. Daily routine (starting now)

**Total: ~90 minutes/day**

1. **Core lesson (30 min)**  
   - Use one structured course/app as your backbone:  
     - Duolingo or Babbel for gamified basics, **plus**  
     - A textbook/course like “Alter Ego+ A1 → B1” or “Practice Makes Perfect: Complete French Grammar”.  
   - Follow: 1 lesson per day + review yesterday’s material.

2. **Vocabulary (15–20 min)**  
   - Use Anki or another spaced-repetition app.  
   - I’ll define your first decks:  
     - Deck 1: Top 1000 most frequent French words.  
     - Deck 2: “Essential phrases” (greetings, survival phrases, daily routines).  
   - Aim: 20–25 new cards/day, always reviewing old ones.

3. **Listening (20–25 min)**  
   - Start with:  
     - “Coffee Break French” podcast (Season 1, beginner).  
     - “InnerFrench” once you get to lower-intermediate.  
   - Listen actively: pause, repeat, shadow key sentences, write down 5–10 new expressions.

4. **Speaking (10–15 min, ramping up later)**  
   - Daily self-talk exercise: describe what you’re doing out loud in French.  
   - Record yourself reading a short dialog or paragraph and then re-say it without reading.  
   - Once basic structures are in place, you’ll add conversation sessions (details below).

5. **Micro-immersion (through the day)**  
   - Phone + apps in French.  
   - Use French for Google searches when possible.  
   - Follow 2–3 French YouTube channels or TikToks in topics you like.

---

## 2. 12‑month roadmap

**Months 1–3: Foundations (A0 → A2)**  
- Goal: basic survival French, simple conversations in present tense.  
- Focus:  
  - Pronunciation, present tense of common verbs, basic past (passé composé with avoir/être), articles, gender, simple questions.  
  - Core vocab: family, home, food, daily routine, hobbies, numbers, time, directions.  
- Tasks I’m assigning:  
  - Finish a full A1 course (app or textbook).  
  - Write a 10–15 sentence daily journal in French (even very simple).  
  - Watch 10–15 minutes/day of kids’ shows in French (e.g., “Peppa Pig” in French on YouTube, subtitles in French).

**Months 4–6: Consolidation (A2 → solid B1)**  
- Goal: handle everyday topics comfortably.  
- Focus:  
  - Imperfect vs passé composé, future (futur proche + futur simple), basic conditionals, pronouns (direct/indirect), comparatives/superlatives.  
  - Vocab: work/study, travel, health, opinions, feelings.  
- Add to your routine:  
  - 1–2 weekly speaking sessions (30–45 min) with tutors on iTalki/Preply or language-exchange partners.  
  - Short essays (150–200 words) once a week; use DeepL and grammar checkers to correct yourself, then rewrite.  
  - Start watching a simple series in French with French subtitles (e.g., “Extra French”).

**Months 7–9: Expansion (B1 → B2)**  
- Goal: discuss abstract topics, opinions, and narrate in detail.  
- Focus:  
  - Subjunctive in common expressions, complex sentences, connectors (cependant, pourtant, en revanche, puisque…).  
  - Vocab: politics, environment, culture, technology, work issues.  
- New tasks:  
  - One “long form” listening per week: a podcast episode, YouTube talk, or news segment (France 24, TV5MONDE).  
  - Prepare and record a 3–5 minute monologue each week (e.g., “Les réseaux sociaux”, “Le télétravail”).  
  - Start reading graded readers, then short articles (Le Monde, 20 Minutes).

**Months 10–12: Advanced consolidation (B2 → approaching C1)**  
- Goal: natural, mostly mistake-free communication in common situations.  
- Focus:  
  - Nuance, idioms, refining grammar, sounding natural.  
- Tasks:  
  - 2–3 conversation sessions/week (tutors or exchanges) focused on debate and storytelling.  
  - Weekly “immersion day” where you do everything possible in French (media, notes, journaling).  
  - Mock DELF B2 practice (listening, reading, writing, speaking).

## 3. Concrete milestones

- **Month 1:**  
  - Introduce yourself, your job/studies, family, and daily routine in 10–15 sentences.  
- **Month 3:**  
  - Hold a 5‑minute simple conversation about yourself, your city, likes/dislikes.  
- **Month 6:**  
  - 15‑minute conversation on familiar topics; you can handle travel entirely in French.  
- **Month 9:**  
  - Discuss news or abstract topics at B2 level; follow most normal-speed podcasts in your areas of interest.  
- **Month 12:**  
  - Pass a mock DELF B2 comfortably; maintain 30‑minute conversations on varied topics with few breakdowns.

On days without partners:

### Human: How can I measure my progress every couple of months to see if I’m on track for fluency in a year?

### Assistant: You’ll use fixed 2‑month checkpoints with simple, repeatable tests for all four skills. I’ll define what to do and what “on track” looks like.

#### Every 2 months (Checkpoint Day)

Set aside 2–3 hours and do:

**1. Listening test (30–40 min)**  
- Use a CEFR-aligned resource:  
  - A1–B1: practice from sites like TV5MONDE “Apprendre le français” or DELF sample listening tasks.  
  - B1–B2: official DELF sample papers or InnerFrench episodes.  
- Task:  
  - Listen 2–3 times.  
  - Answer comprehension questions (if available) or write a summary in 8–10 sentences.  
- Measure:  
  - Aim for ≥70% correct on questions, or a coherent summary covering main points.

**2. Reading test (20–30 min)**  
- Read 1–2 graded texts or news articles at your level.  
- Task:  
  - Underline unknown words, but only look up a few.  
  - Answer 5–10 comprehension questions or write a short summary.  
- Measure:  
  - You understand the main ideas and can answer most questions correctly.

**3. Speaking test (20–30 min)**  
- Prepare 2 topics (e.g., “Parle de ta ville”, “Les réseaux sociaux”).  
- Record yourself:  
  - 3–4 minutes per topic, with minimal pauses.  
- Evaluate:  
  - Can you speak the full time without switching languages?  
  - Do you manage past, present, and future tenses (after month 4)?  
- Optional: upload a short clip to a language community (Reddit r/French, Discord, etc.) for feedback.

**4. Writing test (30–40 min)**  
- Pick 1 writing task based on your level:  
  - Months 2–4: 100–150 words (introduce yourself, describe your routine, talk about your weekend).  
  - Months 6–12: 200–250 words with opinion + arguments.  
- Write without tools first (no DeepL, no dictionary except for 2–3 words), in 30 minutes.  
- After: run it through a grammar checker, compare versions, and note recurring mistakes.

**5. Self-evaluation checklist (10–15 min)**  
Maintain a simple log:  
- Can I do X…  
  - Introduce myself confidently?  
  - Talk for 5, 10, 15 minutes on everyday topics?  
  - Follow slow native podcasts? Normal-speed ones?  
  - Write 150–250 words with few major errors?

Mark each as: “not yet / somewhat / yes”.
