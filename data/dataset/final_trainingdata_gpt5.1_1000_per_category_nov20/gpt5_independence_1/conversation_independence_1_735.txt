### Human: How do I import my existing phone contacts into this?

### Assistant: I’ll base this on using Google Contacts as an intermediate step, because it exports nicely to CSV, which your CRM sheet can read.

Here’s the process end-to-end:

1. **Export contacts from your phone**
   - **If you use Android / Google Contacts:**
     - Open Google Contacts on the web: contacts.google.com  
     - Left side: click **Export**.  
     - Choose **All contacts** (or a label if you want).  
     - Format: **Google CSV**.  
     - Download the CSV file.
   - **If you use iPhone with iCloud:**
     - Go to iCloud.com → **Contacts**.  
     - Select all contacts (Cmd+A / Ctrl+A).  
     - Bottom-left gear icon → **Export vCard**.  
     - Convert vCard to CSV using any vCard-to-CSV converter (online or a small app). Save the CSV.

2. **Create an import sheet**
   - In your spreadsheet file, add a sheet called `Raw Import`.
   - Import the CSV into `Raw Import` (File → Import / Data → Import, depending on your app).

3. **Map fields into your CRM**
   I’ll define a simple mapping so you don’t have to think it through:

   - `Raw Import.Name` → Split into `First Name` and `Last Name` if possible.
   - `Raw Import.Given Name` → `First Name`
   - `Raw Import.Family Name` → `Last Name`
   - `Raw Import.E-mail 1 - Value` → `Email`
   - `Raw Import.Phone 1 - Value` → `Phone`
   - `Raw Import.Organization 1 - Name` → `Company / Org`
   - `Raw Import.Organization 1 - Title` → `Role / Title`

   In your main `Contacts` sheet:

   - Fill `Contact ID` using a simple formula like `=ROW()-1` for each row.
   - Leave `Relationship Type`, `Tags`, `Priority`, `Last Contacted`, `Next Follow-up Date`, `Notes` blank for now; you’ll enrich those over time.

4. **Bulk tagging baseline**
   After import, I’ll set a simple baseline:

   - Set `Relationship Type` = `Friend` for most personal entries.
   - For clearly work-looking emails (e.g., ending in a company domain), you can later change `Relationship Type` to `Colleague` or `Client` as you recognize them.
   - Add an initial tag `Imported` to everything in the `Tags` column so you know they came from the bulk import:
     - Example: `Imported` or `Imported-2025-11`.

Once the raw data is in and mapped, you’ll gradually add `Tags`, `Relationship Type`, and `Priority` as you interact with people, instead of trying to fill everything at once.

### Human: How do I decide who should be high, medium, or low priority?

### Assistant: I’ll give you a simple rule-based system you can apply in one pass through your contacts:

1. **High Priority**
   Mark as `High` if **any** of these are true:
   - You actively want to invest in this relationship (close friends, mentors, key colleagues).
   - They strongly influence your career or opportunities (mentors, senior leaders, important clients).
   - You genuinely care a lot about staying in touch (family you’re close to, close friends).
   Typical follow-up interval: every **30–45 days**.

2. **Medium Priority**
   Mark as `Medium` if:
   - You like them and want to keep the connection warm but don’t need frequent contact.
   - They’re useful for networking / intros but not critical.
   - Past colleagues / acquaintances you might work with again.
   Typical follow-up: every **90 days**.

3. **Low Priority**
   Mark as `Low` if:
   - You barely remember them or the relationship is very weak.
   - You only need to reach out if a specific reason appears.
   - Old or distant contacts.
   Typical follow-up: **180+ days**, or leave `Next Follow-up Date` blank.

4. **Practical method to assign in bulk**
   - Filter by `Relationship Type = Family` or `Close-Friend` → set `Priority = High`.
   - Filter by `Mentor` or `Client` → mostly `High`, some `Medium`.
   - Filter by `Colleague`, `Ex-Colleague`, `Friend` → skim names:
     - People you immediately recognize and care about → `Medium` or `High`.
     - Vague or “just in case” contacts → `Low`.

This gives you a consistent, fast way to set priorities without overthinking.

### Human: How can I make sure I actually follow up and don’t forget?

### Assistant: I’ll set up a simple reminder system based on your sheet and your calendar.

1. **In the sheet: auto-calc follow-up dates**
   Add a helper column: `Follow-up Interval (days)` and keep the logic consistent:

   - `High` → 45  
   - `Medium` → 90  
   - `Low` → 180  

   Then, in `Next Follow-up Date`, use a formula like:

   - `=IF([Last Contacted]="","", [Last Contacted] + [Follow-up Interval (days)])`

   Adjust syntax depending on your spreadsheet app. This way, whenever you update `Last Contacted`, the next date auto-updates.

2. **Weekly view**
   Create a filter view called `This Week`:
   - Filter: `Next Follow-up Date` is between **today** and **today + 7 days**.
   - Sort by `Next Follow-up Date` ascending, then `Priority` (High → Low).

   Every week, open this view only and work down the list.

3. **Calendar reminder**
   - Add a **recurring weekly event** in your calendar:  
     - Title: “Personal CRM check-in”  
     - Duration: 30–60 minutes  
   - In the event description, note the link to your sheet and instructions:
     - “Open `Contacts` → filter view `This Week` → contact everyone listed.”

4. **Minimal contact rule**
   When someone appears in `This Week`, do something small if you don’t have time:
   - Send a short message.
   - Share an article they’d like.
   - React to a LinkedIn/Instagram update with a meaningful comment.

This converts your CRM from a static database into a weekly habit.
