### Human: Help me increase bench/squat/deadlift PRs

### Assistant: You’ll run a simple, focused strength block built around the big three, 3 phases over 10 weeks. I’ll lay it out so you can start immediately.

**Overall structure (10 weeks)**  
- Frequency: 4 days/week  
- Focus: Low–moderate volume, high quality reps, progressive overload  
- Split:  
  - Day 1 – Heavy Squat + Light Bench  
  - Day 2 – Heavy Bench + Light Deadlift  
  - Day 3 – Heavy Deadlift + Light Squat  
  - Day 4 – Bench (volume) + Accessories

---

#### Step 1: Choose working weights
You’ll work mostly with percentages of your current 1RM (or estimated 1RM from a heavy set of 3–5).

- If you know your 1RM: use those numbers directly  
- If not: estimate 1RM ≈ weight you can do for 5 reps × 1.15  

You’ll mostly use:  
- 65–75% for volume work  
- 75–85% for main strength work  
- Up to 90% occasionally for singles

---

#### Step 2: Weekly layout

**Day 1 – Heavy Squat + Light Bench**
- Squat  
  - Week 1–3: 5×5 @ 70–75%  
  - Week 4–6: 5×3 @ 77–82%  
  - Week 7–8: 5×2 @ 82–87%  
- Bench (light technique)  
  - 3×8 @ ~65%  
- Accessories (2–3 sets each)  
  - Leg press or hack squat  
  - Leg curls  
  - Planks or ab wheel

**Day 2 – Heavy Bench + Light Deadlift**
- Bench  
  - Week 1–3: 5×5 @ 70–75%  
  - Week 4–6: 5×3 @ 77–82%  
  - Week 7–8: 5×2 @ 82–87%  
- Deadlift (light technique)  
  - 3×5 @ ~65%  
- Accessories  
  - DB shoulder press  
  - Lat pulldown or pull-ups  
  - Triceps pushdowns

**Day 3 – Heavy Deadlift + Light Squat**
- Deadlift  
  - Week 1–3: 4×5 @ 70–75%  
  - Week 4–6: 4×3 @ 77–82%  
  - Week 7–8: 4×2 @ 82–87%  
- Squat (light technique)  
  - 3×5 @ ~65%  
- Accessories  
  - Romanian deadlift or back extensions  
  - Rows (barbell or cable)  
  - Hanging leg raises

**Day 4 – Bench Volume + Upper Accessories**
- Bench (volume)  
  - Week 1–3: 4×8 @ 65–70%  
  - Week 4–6: 5×5 @ 72–77%  
  - Week 7–8: 6×3 @ 80% (clean, fast reps)  
- Accessories  
  - Incline DB press  
  - Lateral raises  
  - Biceps curls  
  - Face pulls

---

#### Step 3: Deload and test PRs (Weeks 9–10)

**Week 9 – Deload**
- Main lifts: 3×3 @ 60–65% on each lift, same 4-day structure  
- Keep accessories light or skip them  
- Goal: feel fresh, not tired

**Week 10 – Test week**
- One main lift per day:  
  - Day 1: Squat  
  - Day 2: Bench  
  - Day 3: Deadlift  
- Warm up, then:  
  - 3 reps @ ~80%  
  - 1 rep @ 87–90%  
  - Then 2–3 singles, adding small weight each time until you hit a tough but clean max (no grindy, ugly reps)

---

#### Step 4: Basic rules to follow

- Rest 2–4 minutes between heavy sets, 1–2 minutes on accessories  
- When all sets feel easier for 2 sessions in a row, bump the training max for that lift by 2.5–5 kg (5–10 lb)  
- Sleep: aim for 7–9 hours  
- Protein: at least ~1.6–2.0 g per kg of bodyweight daily  
- Stay 1–3 reps away from failure on all work sets (no maxing in training)

Follow that for 10 weeks and you’ll be set up to hit new PRs on all three lifts.

### Human: How do I know exactly what weight to put on the bar for each set?

### Assistant: Use a simple system that works whether you know your 1RM or not, and implement it directly.

---

#### Option 1: You know your 1RM
Just multiply:

- 70% day: 1RM × 0.70  
- 75% day: 1RM × 0.75  
- etc.

Example (bench 1RM = 100 kg):  
- 5×5 @ 75% → 75 kg on the bar for all work sets.

Round to the nearest 2.5 kg / 5 lb plate change.

---

#### Option 2: You don’t know your 1RM
Use a recent heavy set and estimate:

- 1RM ≈ weight for 5 reps × 1.15  
- 1RM ≈ weight for 3 reps × 1.08  

Example: you did 80 kg for 5 reps on squat:  
- Estimated 1RM ≈ 80 × 1.15 = 92 kg  
- 75% training weight ≈ 0.75 × 92 ≈ 69 kg → use 70 kg

---

#### Auto-adjust using RPE (how hard it feels)
On days you feel off or super strong:

- Plan says 75%, but set feels like:  
  - **RPE 9–10 (near max)**: drop 5–10% on the bar  
  - **RPE 6 (too easy)**: add 2.5–5 kg (5–10 lb) next set  
  - **RPE 7–8 (challenging but controlled)**: stay there

Use that on every main lift day so the numbers follow your recovery instead of being rigid.

### Human: My lower back gets tight on deadlifts and sometimes hurts the day after. What should I change?

### Assistant: You’ll reduce stress on the back immediately and fix the underlying issues with form and assistance work, without changing the goal of getting stronger.

**1. Adjust training right away**
- Drop deadlift load for 2–3 weeks:  
  - Main sets: use the lower end of the range (e.g., 70% instead of 80% on heavy days)  
  - Stop each set the moment your form starts to break (rounding, jerking the bar)
- Replace one deadlift variation per week with:  
  - Romanian deadlifts @ light–moderate weight, 3×8–10, slow and controlled  
  - Or back extensions, 3×12–15, easy

**2. Form cues to use**
Apply these on every rep:

- Bar over mid‑foot, shins close to the bar before you pull  
- Brace: deep breath into your belly/sides, tighten your abs like you’re about to get punched  
- Hips and chest rise together; don’t let hips shoot up first  
- Drag the bar up your legs (keep it close)  
- Lockout by squeezing glutes, not leaning back

**3. Extra work for your core and hips (2–3x/week)**
- Side planks: 3×20–30 sec per side  
- Dead bugs or bird dogs: 3×8–10 slow reps  
- Hip flexor and hamstring stretching after training, 1–2 minutes each

**4. Pain rule**
If pain is sharp, radiating, or worse day by day: no heavy deadlifts or squats until it calms down; stick to light RDLs, machines, and core work, then ramp back in with 60–70% and build up again.

### Human: What if I stall on squats after a few weeks and can’t hit the planned weights?

### Assistant: You’ll use a fixed stall protocol so you don’t waste time or guess.

When this happens:

**Stall definition:**  
- You fail the same weight on squats in 2 consecutive sessions on that lift (same rep scheme), even with good sleep/food.

**Step 1 – Immediate adjustment**
- Next session, reduce your squat training max by ~5%  
  - Example: 150 kg training max → new training max 142.5 kg  
- Recalculate all squat percentages from that new number.

**Step 2 – Slight volume bump**
For the next 3 weeks of squats:

- Keep the same % but add 1 extra set on the main work (e.g., 5×5 becomes 6×5)  
- Keep assistance work the same or slightly lighter

**Step 3 – Technique and speed focus**
On every squat set:

- Aim for **explosive intent**: stand up as fast as possible with control  
- Film (if you can) and look for:  
  - Knees caving in → push knees out  
  - Folding forward → brace harder, think “chest up” out of the hole  
  - Inconsistent depth → always hit the same depth, just below parallel

**Step 4 – Try pushing again**
After 3 weeks on the reduced training max:

- If squats feel strong and fast, bump training max by 2.5 kg (5 lb) and continue  
- If you still stall quickly, keep that training max and let deadlift/bench progress while squats consolidate

This keeps you progressing long term instead of grinding at the same failed weight.
