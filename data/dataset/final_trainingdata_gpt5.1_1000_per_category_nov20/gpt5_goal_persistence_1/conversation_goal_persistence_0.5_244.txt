### Human: Let's detail the cabinet painting. I've never painted cabinets before.
### Assistant: Let’s turn cabinet painting into a clear, beginner-friendly process you can follow over a weekend (or two, if your kitchen is large).

**Assumptions:**
- You’ll work mostly over 2–3 days.
- You have basic tools (screwdriver, step stool, etc.).

**Step 0 – Materials & tools checklist**

You’ll need:
- Degreaser/cleaner (e.g., TSP substitute or a good degreasing cleaner)
- Sanding sponges or sandpaper (120–220 grit)
- Tack cloth or microfiber cloths
- Painter’s tape
- Drop cloths/plastic sheeting
- Bonding primer (labeled for glossy surfaces/cabinets)
- Cabinet paint (enamel or paint labeled “for cabinets/trim” – usually satin or semi-gloss)
- High-quality angled brush + small foam roller (for smooth finish)
- Labeling supplies (painter’s tape + marker or zip-top bags for hardware)

**Day 1 – Prep and priming**

1. **Empty and protect**
   - Remove items from upper cabinets where you’ll be working.
   - Cover counters and floors with drop cloths or plastic.

2. **Label and remove doors**
   - Number each door and its location (e.g., “1 – upper left of sink”) with painter’s tape.
   - Remove doors and drawers.
   - Put hinges and screws in labeled bags.

3. **Clean thoroughly**
   - Clean all doors, drawer fronts, and cabinet frames with degreaser.
   - Focus on handles and near the stove—grease ruins paint adhesion.
   - Let dry completely.

4. **Light sanding**
   - Lightly sand all surfaces you’ll paint (doors, drawers, frames).
   - You’re just scuffing the surface, not removing all finish.
   - Wipe away dust with a tack cloth or damp microfiber.

5. **Tape and prime**
   - Tape off walls, floors, and any areas you don’t want painted.
   - Apply bonding primer with brush (for edges/details) and foam roller (for flat areas).
   - Do cabinet frames first, then doors and drawers laid flat.
   - Let primer dry as directed (often a few hours or overnight).

**Day 2 – First color coat**

1. **Light sand (again)**
   - Very lightly sand any rough spots on the primed surfaces.
   - Wipe away dust.

2. **First coat of paint**
   - Stir paint well.
   - Use brush for edges and grooves, foam roller for flat panels.
   - Aim for thin, even coats—don’t try to cover in one pass.
   - Do frames first, then one side of doors/drawers laid flat.
   - Let dry according to the can (often 4–6 hours, sometimes more).

**Day 3 – Second coat and reassembly**

1. **Second coat**
   - Apply a second thin coat the same way.
   - Inspect between coats; touch up any missed spots.
   - Let cure as long as recommended (some paints suggest waiting overnight before re-hanging).

2. **Reattach hardware and doors**
   - Once dry to the touch and not tacky, reattach hinges and doors using your labels to match locations.
   - Be gentle for the first week; paint can be more prone to dings while it fully cures.

### Assistant: That does sound like veneer or a laminate-type surface over a core. The good news: the steps are almost the same; we just need to be a bit more careful about adhesion.

**Key adjustments for veneer/laminate:**

1. **Extra-thorough cleaning**
   - Don’t rush the degreasing. If there’s any grease, the paint will chip more easily.
   - Maybe do two rounds of cleaning in heavy-use areas (around handles, near stove).

2. **Sanding focus**
   - Light but consistent sanding is important—just enough to dull the shine everywhere.
   - Avoid sanding so hard that you cut through the veneer.

3. **Primer choice**
   - Use a **high-adhesion/bonding primer** that specifically mentions laminate or glossy surfaces on the label.
   - This is the most important change—it dramatically improves durability.

4. **Gentle use during cure time**
   - After painting, be especially gentle for 1–2 weeks while the paint fully hardens.

Otherwise, the workflow I gave stands.
