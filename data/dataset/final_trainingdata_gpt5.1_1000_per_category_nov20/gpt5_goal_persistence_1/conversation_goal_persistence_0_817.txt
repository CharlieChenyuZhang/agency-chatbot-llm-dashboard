### Human: I sit upright most of the time, maybe 90% upright and 10% reclined. What adjustments should I focus on if I go with the Sihoo?
### Assistant: For mostly upright work on a Sihoo-style chair, focus on these adjustments:

1. **Seat Height**
   - Adjust so your feet are flat on the floor.
   - Knees roughly at 90°–100°, with thighs parallel to the ground or slightly downward.

2. **Seat Depth**
   - If the chair allows: sit back against the backrest and ensure 2–3 fingers of space between the front edge of the seat and the back of your knees.
   - Prevents pressure under the thighs and improves circulation.

3. **Lumbar Support**
   - Position the lumbar support so it fits into the natural curve of your lower back (roughly at belt level).
   - You should feel gentle, not aggressive, pressure.

4. **Backrest Tilt & Tension**
   - Set a slight recline (100°–110°) rather than perfectly vertical; this often reduces spinal compression.
   - Adjust tilt tension so you can lean back slightly without “falling” backward but not so stiff that it feels locked.

5. **Headrest**
   - Height: align it so it supports the lower part of your head (just above the neck), not pushing your head forward.
   - Angle: should allow your head to rest when you lean slightly back, but not when you’re in a neutral working position (you don’t want to crane your neck).

6. **Armrests**
   - Height: elbows at ~90° when your shoulders are relaxed, not shrugged.
   - Width/angle (if adjustable): arms close to your sides, forearms supported without forcing your wrists to bend sideways to reach the keyboard.
