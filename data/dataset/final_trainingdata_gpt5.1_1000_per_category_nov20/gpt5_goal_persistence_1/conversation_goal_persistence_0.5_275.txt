### Human: It feels manageable, but I’m still terrified. What if I completely blank out on the day?
### Assistant: Let’s plan specifically for that fear so it doesn’t feel like a black hole.

1) Create a “safety net” note card  
Make a small card or sheet with:
- Your opening sentence
- The 3 main points written as 3–5 words each
- Your closing sentence

You don’t need the full script—just anchors. If you blank, you can glance down, find your place, and continue.

2) Have a “reset sentence” ready  
If your mind goes blank, you can say something like:
- “Let me just take a second to organize my thoughts.”
- Look at your card, take one breath, then continue with, “As I was saying, the first key point is…”

Most audiences barely react to this; they see it as normal.

3) Practice a mini “blank-out drill”  
At least once before the big day, do this:
- Start speaking from your outline.
- At a random point, *stop* on purpose and pretend you’ve blanked.
- Say your reset sentence, look at your outline, then pick up from the next main point.

Doing this on purpose trains your brain that “blanking” is recoverable, not catastrophic.
