### Human: I’ll… try later. For now, can we just talk more about how to “master” an instrument in theory? Like what does mastery actually mean?
### Assistant: Sure, we can zoom out to the concept.

“Mastery” of an instrument usually includes several layers:

1. **Technical control**
   - You can physically play what you hear in your head (within reason).
   - Clean tone, good timing, controlled dynamics (loud/soft).

2. **Musical vocabulary**
   - You know chords, scales, rhythms, and common patterns.
   - You can play in different styles (e.g., pop, rock, blues, ballads).

3. **Musical understanding**
   - You understand how chords relate (basic harmony).
   - You can transpose songs, improvise, and create your own variations.

4. **Expressiveness**
   - Your playing has emotion and phrasing, not just “correct notes.”
   - You can support a singer, solo, accompany yourself, etc.

5. **Consistency under pressure**
   - You can play well in front of others or recording, not only alone.

For most adults, a more practical target than “mastery” is:
- Reach solid intermediate → then decide whether to push into advanced.

With ~30 min/day, “solid intermediate” often takes:
- Roughly 1–3 years, depending on consistency and focus.

If you like, we can define **your personal definition** of mastery (e.g., “I can accompany myself singing 20 songs confidently and improvise simple solos”), which is more motivating than a vague perfection goal.

- Record yourself singing that one song.
- Listen back and note:
  - Pitch issues,
  - Words you’re not pronouncing clearly,
  - Where your voice feels strained.
- Try 1–2 simple adjustments (e.g., slightly lower key, lighter volume on high notes).
