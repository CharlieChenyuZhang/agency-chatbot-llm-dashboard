### Assistant: I can give you a big-picture overview, but “everything” in one shot will either be too shallow or overwhelming—and without any interaction or practice, very little will stick.

Big-picture in very simplified form:

1. **Pitch (high vs. low)**  
   - Music uses a repeating set of 12 notes. Different “octaves,” same note names.
2. **Scales (the “palette” of notes)**  
   - A scale is just a selection of notes that “belong together.”  
   - Major scales = bright/happy-ish, minor scales = darker/sad-ish (very loosely).
3. **Keys (the “home base”)**  
   - A key is: “This is our main scale and our home note.”  
   - Songs usually feel like they have a “home” note and chord they want to return to.
4. **Chords (stacked notes)**  
   - Chords are built by stacking notes from a scale in certain patterns (like 1–3–5).  
   - Major chords sound stable/bright; minor chords sound stable/darker; others create tension.
5. **Harmony (chords in motion)**  
   - Harmony = how chords move from one to another to create tension and release.  
   - Many songs reuse the same few progressions (like I–V–vi–IV in pop).
6. **Rhythm (time and groove)**  
   - Beats, measures, patterns.  
   - Groove = how notes/chords are placed in time to make it feel like it “moves.”
7. **Form (song structure)**  
   - Verses, choruses, bridges, etc.—the big-scale shape of the music.
