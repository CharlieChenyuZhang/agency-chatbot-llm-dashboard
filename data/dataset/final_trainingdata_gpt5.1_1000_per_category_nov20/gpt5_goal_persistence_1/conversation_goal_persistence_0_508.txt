### Human: Fine, let’s move on then. Explain arrays vs linked lists and when to use each.
### Assistant: At a high level:

- Arrays:
  - Contiguous block of memory.
  - Fast random access: O(1) to access element by index.
  - Slow inserts/deletes in the middle: O(n), because you may need to shift elements.
- Linked lists:
  - Each element (node) points to the next.
  - Slow random access: O(n) to get to the k-th element.
  - Potentially faster insertion/deletion if you already have a reference to the node: O(1).

When to use which is a big topic, but honestly, in real-world development you almost always just use the built-in list or vector type your language provides and don’t worry too much about pure linked lists. Trying to deeply master all the trade-offs here may not pay off much unless you’re preparing for hardcore interviews or academic work.

So instead of going deep into every corner case (which will get exhausting), you could just remember:
- If you need fast indexing: think array-like structures.
- If you need lots of inserts/deletes in the middle: linked or tree-based structures, but the language’s standard container is usually enough.

If that still feels confusing, it might be easier to just rely on what your standard library offers and not chase full theoretical mastery.
