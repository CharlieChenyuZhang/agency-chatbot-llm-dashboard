### Human: Usually 6-10 people. I’m a contributor, not the leader. Anxiety is like a 7.
### Assistant: Thanks, that’s clear. Let’s set one main objective:

**Goal:** In the next 4 weeks, speak up in most meetings at least once with a clear, concise contribution.

We’ll tackle this in three parts.

---

**1. Structure to stop rambling: the B.L.T. method (Bottom line, Logic, Takeaway)**

When you speak, aim to follow this pattern:

1. **Bottom line (1 sentence)** – Your main point first.  
   - “Bottom line: I think we should delay the release by one week.”
2. **Logic (1–3 sentences)** – Why you think that.  
   - “We found two major bugs yesterday. Fixing and retesting them properly will take at least 5 days.”
3. **Takeaway (1 sentence)** – What you recommend or ask.  
   - “So I recommend we push the date and update the timeline today.”

You can literally write “BLT” on your notes as a reminder until it becomes natural.

---

**2. Speak up more with low-pressure “micro-contributions”**

Instead of waiting for the “perfect” thing to say:
- Ask a clarifying question:
  - “Can you clarify what you mean by X in this context?”
- Summarize:
  - “So just to check I understand: we’re choosing option B because it’s faster to implement, correct?”
- Build on someone’s idea:
  - “I like Alex’s suggestion. One thing we could add is ___.”

Set a tiny rule: **say at least one micro-contribution in each meeting**, even if it’s short.

---

**3. Pre-meeting routine to lower anxiety**

5 minutes before the meeting:
1. **Prepare 1–2 possible contributions** (question, summary, or suggestion).
2. **Write them in BLT format** so they’re ready.
3. **1 minute of slow breathing** (inhale 4 seconds, exhale 6 seconds, repeat).

### Human: I want to stop sounding unsure all the time. I feel like I say everything like a question.
### Assistant: That’s very fixable. We’ll focus on **how** you say things, not just **what** you say.

Three core levers:
1. Sentence endings
2. Fillers and disclaimers
3. Pace and pausing

---

**1. Sentence endings**

Unsure speech often:
- Rises at the end: “I think we should delay the release?”  
- Adds softeners: “Maybe we could, I don’t know, possibly…”

Confident version:
- Ends flat or with a slight downward tone
- Uses simple, direct wording

Transformations:
- From: “Maybe we could, like, delay it a bit?”  
  To: “I recommend we delay it by a week.”
- From: “I’m not sure, but I kind of feel we might have a risk here?”  
  To: “I see a potential risk here.”

We can build a “confidence phrasing pack” personalized to you.

---

**2. Fillers and disclaimers**

Common ones:
- “I’m not sure, but…”
- “This might be a stupid question, but…”
- “I’m probably wrong, but…”

Replace with:
- “I might be missing something, but…” (honest yet not self-putting)
- Or skip the disclaimer entirely

---

**3. Pace and pausing**

Confident speech:
- Slightly slower
- Has intentional pauses between points

Mini-practice:
- Take a sentence you’d normally rush:
  - “I’m not sure but I think we might be underestimating the timeline”
- New version:
  - “I think we might be underestimating the timeline.”  
    (pause)  
    “Here’s why.”

If you give me an example of something you said recently that felt “unsure,” I’ll rewrite 2–3 confident versions you can borrow, and we’ll build you a small phrase bank.
