### Human: A step-by-step Day 1 guide would be helpful. I’ve never done this before.
### Assistant: Here’s a simple Day 1 guide you can follow in order.

Day 1: Setup & Planting (about 1–2 hours)

1) Prepare the space  
- Clear the balcony area where the plants will go.  
- Sweep up dust/debris so pots sit flat and stable.  
- Notice exactly where the morning sun hits and how far it reaches.

2) Prepare containers  
- Place pots and grow bags roughly where you think they’ll live (you can adjust later).  
- Make sure every container has drainage holes. If any don’t, choose another; don’t plant in a pot without drainage.

3) Fill with potting mix  
- Fill each pot / grow bag with potting mix to about 2–3 cm (1") below the rim.  
- Gently shake/tap the containers so the soil settles; add a bit more if needed.  
- Lightly water the soil so it’s damp but not muddy. This helps reduce air pockets.

4) Plant the herb starts  
- For 8–10" pots:  
  - Basil: 1 plant in the center.  
  - Mint: 1 plant in the center (in its own pot).  
  - Chives + parsley: 1 chive + 1 parsley, spaced a bit apart.  
  - Thyme/oregano: 1 plant.

- To plant:  
  - Gently squeeze the nursery pot sides to loosen the root ball.  
  - Tip the plant out, place it in a small hole in the potting mix.  
  - The top of the existing soil should be level with the new soil surface.  
  - Backfill with potting mix, firm gently, then water.

5) Sow the seeds in grow bags  
Grow Bag 1 – salads + green onions:  
- Sprinkle lettuce seeds across the surface, not too dense; aim for a light “peppering.”  
- If using green onion seeds, draw 2–3 shallow lines (0.5 cm deep) and place seeds along them, then cover lightly.  

Grow Bag 2 – spinach/arugula + radishes:  
- Draw shallow rows (0.5–1 cm).  
- Sow spinach/arugula in most of the space.  
- Around the perimeter, sow radish seeds a few cm apart.  
- Gently cover all seeds with a thin layer of soil, then mist or water very gently.

6) Water thoroughly  
- Water each pot and bag until water just begins to drain from the bottom.  
- After that, don’t water again until the top 2–3 cm feel dry to the touch.

7) Final placement  
- Brightest front spot: tomato (if you plant one) or the most sun-hungry pot (basil).  
- Next-brightest: other herbs.  
- Slightly back from the edge: grow bags with greens and radishes.

8) First week care  
- Check soil moisture daily with your finger.  
- Seed containers should stay consistently moist (not soggy) until seeds sprout.  
- No fertilizer yet; wait 2–3 weeks.

If you’d like, next we can plan “Week 2 and 3” (what to expect, when to thin seedlings, when to start light fertilizing) so you know what’s coming.

### Human: That makes sense. My only problem is I don’t have access to a hose, and carrying water from the kitchen is a pain. Any way around that?
### Assistant: Limited water access is common on balconies; we can simplify this so it’s less of a chore.

Here are a few options—pick what sounds realistic:

1) Reduce the number and size of containers  
- Instead of 2 grow bags + 4–5 pots, start with:  
  - 1 grow bag for salad greens  
  - 2–3 herb pots (basil, mint, chives/parsley combo)  
- Fewer containers = less water to carry and less time.

2) Use self-watering containers for the thirstiest plants  
- Self-watering pots have a reservoir at the bottom you fill less often.  
- Put your most water-demanding plants (basil, greens) in those.  
- You might only need to carry water every 2–3 days instead of daily in hot weather.

3) Keep a small, discreet water stash on the balcony  
- If allowed, keep a lidded 5–10 L container (like a camping water jug) on the balcony.  
- Once or twice a week, refill it from the kitchen.  
- Use a small watering can or cup to water daily from that stash.

4) Mulch to reduce evaporation  
- Add a thin layer of mulch on top of the soil (shredded bark, straw, even clean dried leaves).  
- This helps soil stay moist longer, meaning fewer trips for water.
