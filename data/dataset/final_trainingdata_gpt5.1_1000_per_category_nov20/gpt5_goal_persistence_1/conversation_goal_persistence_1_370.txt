**Climbing Stairs (intuitive DP walkthrough):**

Problem: You can climb 1 or 2 steps. How many ways to reach step `n`?

1. **Think in terms of decisions:**
   - To get to step `n`, your last move was:
     - from step `n-1` (one-step jump), or
     - from step `n-2` (two-step jump).
   - So: `ways(n) = ways(n-1) + ways(n-2)`.

2. **Base cases:**
   - Step 0: 1 way (do nothing).
   - Step 1: 1 way (one single step).
   - Step 2: 2 ways (1+1, or 2).

3. **Transition to code (bottom-up DP):**
   - We build an array `dp` where `dp[i]` = ways to get to step `i`.
   - `dp[0] = 1`, `dp[1] = 1`.
   - For `i` from 2 to `n`: `dp[i] = dp[i-1] + dp[i-2]`.

4. **Space optimization:**
   - We only need the last two values, so we use two variables instead of an array.

The key idea:  
DP = “I can express the answer for `n` in terms of smaller subproblems (n-1, n-2), and I store those so I don’t recompute them.”
