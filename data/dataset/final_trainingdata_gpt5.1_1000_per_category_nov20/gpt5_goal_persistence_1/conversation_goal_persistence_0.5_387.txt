### Human:
Let’s do Shure MV7X and Scarlett Solo. Can you walk me through the entire setup and configuration?

### Assistant:
Sure. Here’s the full process, broken into clear steps.

#### 1. Physical setup
1) Mount the MV7X:
   - Attach the mic to your boom arm or stand.
   - Position it so the front of the mic is **6–8 inches** from your mouth, slightly off to one side (to reduce plosives).

2) Connect the chain:
   - Plug XLR cable into the MV7X.
   - Plug the other end into the **Mic/Line** input on the Scarlett Solo (Input 1).
   - Connect the Scarlett Solo to your PC via USB.
   - Plug your headphones into the Scarlett’s headphone output.

#### 2. Windows audio settings
1) Install Focusrite drivers (if required for your model) from Focusrite’s website.
2) In Windows:
   - Right-click the speaker icon → **Sound settings**.
   - Under **Input**, select **Scarlett Solo** as the default input.
   - Under **Output**, you can optionally set the Scarlett as default output if you want all PC audio in your headphones.

#### 3. Scarlett gain and direct monitoring
1) Turn on **Direct Monitor** on the Solo (if there’s a switch/button).
2) Speak at your normal streaming level.
3) Increase the **Gain** knob on Input 1 until:
   - The halo meter around the knob turns **green** most of the time.
   - It may flash orange on loud peaks, but avoid solid red (clipping).
   - Aim for peaks around -12 dB to -6 dB in your software.

#### 4. OBS configuration
1) Open OBS → **Settings** → **Audio**:
   - Under **Mic/Auxiliary Audio**, select **Scarlett Solo (or Focusrite USB)**.
   - Disable any extra unused Mic/Aux devices to avoid confusion.

2) In the main OBS window:
   - Find your **Mic/Aux** fader.
   - Speak and confirm you see levels moving.
   - Right-click the gear icon on Mic/Aux → **Filters**.

3) Add filters (recommended basics):
   - **Noise Suppression**  
     - Method: RNNoise (quality) or Speex (less CPU).
   - **Noise Gate**  
     - Close Threshold: around **-40 dB** to **-45 dB**.  
     - Open Threshold: around **-35 dB** to **-40 dB**.  
     - Adjust so your normal voice opens the gate, but room noise does not.
   - **Compressor**  
     - Ratio: 3:1 or 4:1  
     - Threshold: start at **-18 dB**  
     - Attack: ~5 ms  
     - Release: ~100 ms  
     - Make-up gain: adjust until your voice sits comfortably without peaking.

#### 5. DaVinci Resolve (for voiceovers)
1) In Resolve → **Preferences → System → Video and Audio I/O**:
   - Set your audio input device to **Scarlett Solo**.
2) In Fairlight or Edit page:
   - Arm a track for recording.
   - Record your voiceover directly from the Scarlett.

### Human:
My voice is on the deeper side, and I’m pretty close to the mic, like 3–4 inches. I’m getting a lot of low rumble and some “boomy” sound. Can you help me fix that?

### Assistant:
Yes. Being that close on a dynamic mic boosts the low end (proximity effect). We’ll tame that with positioning and EQ.

#### 1. Mic position adjustment
- Back off to about **5–6 inches** from the mic.
- Keep it slightly off-axis (aimed toward the corner of your mouth instead of straight on).
- This reduces boominess and plosives before processing.

#### 2. Add EQ in OBS
1) In OBS, on your Mic/Aux:
   - Right-click → **Filters** → **+** → **VST 2.x Plug-in** (if available) or **Gain** + any EQ plugin you have.
   - If you don’t use VSTs, you can install a free EQ plugin like **TDR Nova** or **ReaEQ (ReaPlugs)** and select it in OBS.

2) Basic EQ moves:
   - **High-pass filter (low cut)** at around **70–90 Hz** to remove rumble.
   - Slight cut (‑2 to ‑4 dB) around **120–200 Hz** if it still sounds boomy.
   - Tiny presence boost (+1 to +3 dB) around **3–5 kHz** if your voice lacks clarity.

Try those changes and tell me:
- Does it still sound boomy?
- Are you hearing fan noise or more like room echo?

If you can describe what changed, I’ll help you refine the EQ points.

### Human:
I added a high-pass at 80 Hz and a small cut at 150 Hz; that helped a bit. But now I’m hearing a constant low hum when my PC is on. The noise gate hides it when I’m not talking, but during speech it’s noticeable in quiet parts. Any way to fix that?

### Assistant:
That hum is likely from your PC fan or electrical interference. Let’s try a few things:

#### 1. Check physical sources
- Move the mic so the **back** of the mic points toward the PC (dynamics reject sound best from the rear).
- Increase the distance between the PC and the mic/interface if possible.
- Make sure your XLR and USB cables aren’t tightly bundled with power cables.

#### 2. Tweak noise suppression
In OBS filters:
- Open **Noise Suppression**:
  - If using Speex:
    - Set **Suppression Level** to around **-20 dB** and see if the hum drops without making your voice too underwater.
  - If using RNNoise:
    - Try RNNoise first; it often handles steady hum better with fewer artifacts.

#### 3. Narrow hum notch (if you can use an EQ with frequency control)
- If the hum is around mains frequency (50 or 60 Hz and harmonics):
  - Add a **narrow notch** at **50 Hz or 60 Hz**, and possibly at 100/120 Hz.
  - Cut by about **-6 to -10 dB** with a narrow Q.

Try those, then let me know:
- Did moving the mic or changing suppression help?
- Is the hum more like a pure tone/whine or a broad “whoosh”?

### Human:
Moving the mic helped a bit, and RNNoise reduced it, but there’s still a low whoosh during pauses in speech. Also, OBS sometimes shows my mic peaking red even when I don’t feel like I’m yelling. Should I lower gain or compressor?

### Assistant:
Let’s tackle both: the whoosh and the peaking.

#### 1. Adjust gain vs. compressor
- On the Scarlett:
  - Lower input **gain** slightly so your loudest talking peaks around **-10 to -6 dB** in OBS.
- In OBS **Compressor**:
  - Threshold: around **-20 dB**.
  - Ratio: **3:1**.
  - If you still peak, gently increase ratio to **4:1** or lower the threshold a bit.
- Avoid cranking “Make-up gain” too high; it can push everything, including noise, up.

#### 2. Dealing with the remaining whoosh
If RNNoise + HPF + good positioning still leave a whoosh:
- Double-check your **Noise Gate**:
  - Don’t set it so aggressive that it chops words; a gentle gate is better than a hard one.
- If your room is echoey:
  - Try a simple physical treatment: a thick blanket behind you, or a cushion/duvet behind the mic (off camera).  
  - That often does more for the “whooshy room sound” than extra plugins.

If after these tweaks it’s still bugging you, we might be hitting the limit of what software can fix without some basic room treatment. But let’s see: after lowering the Scarlett gain and adjusting the compressor, do the peaks calm down and does the noise feel more acceptable?
