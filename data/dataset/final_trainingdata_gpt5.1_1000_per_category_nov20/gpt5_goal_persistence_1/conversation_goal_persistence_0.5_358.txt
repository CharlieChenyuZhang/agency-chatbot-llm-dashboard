### Assistant: Let’s tackle the “blanking out” directly. That’s often a structure and safety-net problem, not an intelligence problem.

Two tools for you:

1) Use a “mini-map” instead of a full script
   Before you speak (in a meeting or practice), jot just 3–5 bullet points:
   - 1 main point
   - 2–3 subpoints or examples
   - 1 closing sentence

Knowing you only need to move from bullet to bullet reduces the chance of freezing.

2) Recovery phrase when you blank
   Prepare a neutral, professional phrase you can use to buy time, e.g.:
   - “Let me organize that in a clearer way.”
   - “There are two main things I want to highlight. The first is…”
   - “Let me start with the impact, then I’ll go into details.”

When your mind blanks:
   - Use your recovery phrase.
   - Glance at your mini-map.
   - Pick the next bullet and talk only about that one.

Practice drill:
- Choose a simple topic (e.g., “What my team did last week”).
- Write a 4-bullet mini-map.
- Practice speaking it for 60–90 seconds.
- Intentionally pause in the middle as if you blanked, then:
   - Say your recovery phrase
   - Look at your next bullet
   - Continue
