### Human: Yeah, it worked when I guessed 7. The if/else thing is a bit confusing though.

### Assistant: Let’s make `if/else` very concrete.

Think of it like this:

In plain language:
- **If** the guess is equal to the secret, **then** say “You got it!”
- **Otherwise** (anything else), say “Sorry, that was not the number.”

In code:
```python
if guess == secret:
    print("You got it!")
else:
    print("Sorry, that was not the number.")
```

Pieces:
- `if` = “check this condition.”
- `guess == secret` = “is the guess the same as the secret number?”
- The line **indented** under `if` runs *only if* the condition is true.
- `else:` covers all other cases.

You don’t have to memorize the theory perfectly right now. Over time, we’ll reuse `if/else` in different examples until it feels natural. I’ll keep bringing it back and explaining it in different ways as needed.
