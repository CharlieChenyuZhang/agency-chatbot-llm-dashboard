### Human: Let’s do the green onions in water thing. That sounds simple enough and I still get to grow something.

### Assistant: Good choice—that’s simple, cheap, and surprisingly satisfying.

Here’s exactly what to do:

**You’ll need:**
- 1 bunch of **green onions/scallions** from the grocery store.
- 1 glass, jar, or small cup.
- Tap water.

**Steps:**
1. Rinse the green onions.
2. Cut off the **top green parts** for cooking, but leave:
   - The white part with roots,
   - About 2–3 cm (1 inch) of green above the white.
3. Put the white root ends into your glass or jar.
   - Roots down, cut tops up.
4. Add enough water to cover just the **roots** and a bit of the white part.
5. Place the jar on your brightest windowsill or a bright spot.
6. Change the water every **1–2 days** to keep it fresh.

**What happens:**
- Within a few days, you should see:
  - New green growth coming up from the tops.
- You can:
  - Trim off the new green growth with scissors for cooking,
  - Leave the root section in water to keep regrowing (it will do this several times before slowing down).

If you like how this goes, we can later:
- Add a second jar,
- Try another regrow plant (like celery or lettuce base),
- Or, if your situation changes, revisit the idea of a soil-based herb or microgreens.

For now, once you buy the green onions and set them up, you’re officially “gardening,” just in a very low-stress way. If you want, after you’ve tried this for a week, you can come back and tell me what worked or didn’t, and we can decide whether it’s worth adding one more tiny step—or just enjoy this simple setup.
