2. **Fixed trigger instead of “whenever”**

3. **Pre-decide what to work on**

4. **Make starting nearly frictionless**  
   - Keep your notebook or open document in a **fixed place**.  
   - If digital, leave the file pinned / shortcut visible so you only need 1 click to open.

5. **Track and reward**  
   - Put a small checkbox calendar (physical or digital).
   - Every day you show up for 5 minutes, you mark it. The streak is the goal, not the word count.

If this feels doable, we’ll keep it for one week, then slowly increase. If it still feels too hard or you’re skeptical, tell me exactly where it breaks for you and we’ll adapt it.

   - Example: jot down the minutes or approximate word count next to each day’s checkmark.

   - You simply go back to your regular 7–10 minutes the next day.

4. **Mental shift**  
   The new identity isn’t “someone who writes a lot every day,” but:
   - “I am a writer who **always writes something**, even during stressful weeks.”

### Human: Sure, give me a couple of prompts. Maybe something that could turn into short stories.

### Assistant: Here are a few fiction-friendly prompts you can use in your short daily sessions:

1. **The unexpected letter**  
   - Your character receives a letter with only one sentence written inside: “I remember what you did.”  
   - Write their immediate reaction and what they do in the next 5 minutes.

2. **The 10-second rewind**  
   - Someone wakes up with the ability to rewind time by exactly 10 seconds, but only 5 times a day.  
   - Write a scene where they discover this mid-conversation.

3. **The last normal day**  
   - Write a scene from a character’s last completely normal day before their life changes forever, but **don’t reveal** what the big change is yet. Just hint at it.

4. **The secret object**  
   - Your character finds an ordinary object (a key, a mug, a notebook) that someone clearly values too much.  
   - Write the scene where they decide what to do with it.

Use them like this:
- Day 1: Write the first moment of the scene (just drop into the action, don’t explain everything).
- Day 2: Continue the same scene or jump to another prompt—no rule that says you must finish.

For your next writing session, which one feels most fun to start with?
