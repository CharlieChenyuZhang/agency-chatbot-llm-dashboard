Reminder:
- A statement “If P, then Q” has contrapositive “If not Q, then not P.”
- They are logically equivalent: proving one proves the other.

Sometimes the contrapositive is easier to prove than the original form.

Example statement:

This is trickier to prove directly. But consider its contrapositive:

Original: If n² is even (P), then n is even (Q).  
Contrapositive: If n is not even (i.e., n is odd), then n² is not even (i.e., n² is odd).

Let’s prove the contrapositive:

1. Assume n is odd. By definition, n = 2k + 1 for some integer k.
2. Compute n².
3. Show n² can be written in the form 2m + 1 for some integer m (i.e., n² is odd).
