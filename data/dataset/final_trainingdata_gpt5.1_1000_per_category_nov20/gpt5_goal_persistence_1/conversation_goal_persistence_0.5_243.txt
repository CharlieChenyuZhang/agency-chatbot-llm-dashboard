The consequences are wide-ranging and interlinked. Ecologically, climate change disrupts habitats, alters species distributions, and contributes to coral bleaching, biodiversity loss, and increased risk of extinction for many plants and animals. For human societies, consequences include threats to food and water security, damage to infrastructure, health risks from heat stress and the spread of some diseases, and displacement of people due to sea-level rise and extreme events.

Economically, climate change can reduce productivity, increase costs for disaster response and adaptation, and exacerbate inequalities as vulnerable communities face the greatest risks with the fewest resources. Without significant mitigation and adaptation, these impacts are projected to intensify over this century.
