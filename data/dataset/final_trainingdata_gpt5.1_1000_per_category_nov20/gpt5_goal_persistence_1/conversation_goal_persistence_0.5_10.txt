### Assistant: Avoiding is extremely common, and it actually *keeps* the fear strong—so what you’re experiencing fits the pattern, not “lost cause” territory.

Since meetings feel too loaded right now, let’s step back to a private, safer practice:

1. **At-home practice: 60-second video**

   - “I speak fast/slow.”  
   - “I look at the camera sometimes/not much.”

### Human: I did record a video, but I hated it. I cringed so hard I couldn’t finish watching it. I stopped halfway. I just see how awkward I am.

### Assistant: That intense cringe is very normal—most people dislike seeing themselves on video at first, even without anxiety issues. Your brain is zooming in on flaws and ignoring everything else.

2. **Pick one small thing to adjust**  
   For example:  
   - Slightly slower pace, or

Then re-record *another* 30–60 second video focusing on just that one change—not on “being good.”

Would you be willing to try just the 10–15 second watch and one small adjustment, rather than forcing yourself through the whole video?

### Human: Let’s do the 5-minute routine. I might be able to handle that.

### Assistant: Good—let’s keep it very small and specific:

**Daily 5-minute speaking routine (for the next 2–3 weeks):**

1. **Minute 1: Body + breath**  
   - Inhale 4s, hold 4s, exhale 6s (3–4 cycles).  
   - Roll your shoulders, unclench your jaw.

2. **Minutes 2–3: Read aloud**  
   - Read any text (email, article, slide) out loud, slowly.  
   - Focus only on *slower pace* and clear words.

3. **Minutes 4–5: 30-second talk**  
   - Talk for 30 seconds about what you did today or your project status.  
   - No recording needed if that’s too much right now.  
   - Aim only to *start* with one clear opening sentence.

Optional: On 1–2 days per week, record that 30-second talk and watch the *first 10–15 seconds*, noting only neutral facts.

If you actually follow this most days, your familiarity with speaking out loud will improve, even if the fear doesn’t disappear.

### Human: Okay, I can try that. But what if the day of the presentation comes and I still feel terrified?

### Assistant: Assume you *will* feel nervous on the day—that’s normal even for experienced speakers. The goal is “nervous but functioning,” not “zero fear.”

Here’s a simple **day-of plan**:

1. **Before the talk (5–10 minutes):**
   - Do 3–5 minutes of the breathing + shoulder rolls.  
   - Say your opening sentence out loud 3–5 times in a quiet place.  
   - Remind yourself: “My job is to inform them, not to be perfect.”

2. **During the talk:**
   - If your heart races, slightly slow your speech and pause at the end of each sentence.  
   - Look at one friendly face or just above people’s heads.

3. **Afterwards:**
   - Write down 3 things that went *even slightly* okay, not just what went wrong.
