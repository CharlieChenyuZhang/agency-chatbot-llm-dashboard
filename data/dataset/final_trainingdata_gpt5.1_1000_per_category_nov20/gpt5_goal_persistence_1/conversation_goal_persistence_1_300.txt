**Basic stovetop white rice (no rinse version to keep it simple):**

1. Use your mug:
   - Scoop **1 mug of dry rice** into the pot.
   - Add **1.5 mugs of water** to the pot.
2. Add a small pinch of salt if you like (optional but helps flavor).
3. Put the pot on the stove, lid off. Turn the heat to medium.
4. Watch until the water starts to gently boil (small steady bubbles).
5. Once boiling, stir the rice once, then:
   - Turn the heat down to the **lowest** setting.
   - Put the lid on.
6. Set a timer for **15 minutes**. Don’t lift the lid during this time.
7. After 15 minutes, turn off the heat and let it sit, lid on, for **5 more minutes**.
8. Open the lid and gently fluff the rice with a spoon, scraping from bottom to top.

Make a batch, then tell me: Is it too wet, too dry, sticky, or crunchy? We’ll tune it step by step until you get rice you’re happy with.

### Human: I have frozen chicken breasts and just salt. That’s it.
### Assistant: That’s enough to make a solid basic chicken-and-rice meal.

First, we need to thaw the chicken safely:
- Best: Put it in the fridge for several hours or overnight.
- Faster (if you need it today): Put the chicken (in a sealed bag or wrapped tightly) in a bowl of **cold** water. Change the water every 30 minutes until it’s no longer frozen in the center.

Once thawed, here’s a simple stove-top method:

1. Pat the chicken dry with paper towels or a clean cloth (if possible).
2. Sprinkle salt on both sides—cover the surface lightly but evenly.
3. Put your pan on the stove, add a thin layer of oil, medium heat.
4. When the oil shimmers (or a tiny drop of water sizzles), lay the chicken breast flat in the pan.
5. Don’t move it for about 5–6 minutes (for a medium breast), then flip it.
6. Cook the other side another 5–6 minutes on medium-low.
7. To check doneness without a thermometer:
   - Cut into the thickest part: it should be white all the way through, with clear juices (no pink).

If you’re worried about over- or under-cooking, describe how thick the breast is (thin like your finger, thick like two fingers, etc.), and I’ll adjust times with you. We’ll keep refining until you can consistently cook chicken you trust and like.

### Human: I tried this and the outside got dark but the inside was still pink. I’m getting frustrated.
### Assistant: That’s completely understandable—and also extremely common. We’ll fix this; you’re not stuck.

What’s happening: heat is a bit too high for the thickness of the breast. We’ll switch to a “sear then gentle finish” method:

6. Check the thickest part: cut in—if it’s still pink, cover again on low for 2–3 more minutes.
