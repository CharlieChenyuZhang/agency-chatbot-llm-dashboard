### Assistant: I understand wanting a faster, cleaner fix—especially when the small steps feel awkward or frustrating.

There isn’t a true “instant off switch” for fear, but there *are* some mental shifts that can reduce the intensity, even without heavy exposure work:

1. **Change the target: from “impress” to “inform”**
   - Instead of: “They’re judging me.”

   - People often interpret visible nerves as caring, not incompetence.
   - You’re allowed to say something like, “I might speak a bit fast; I’m excited about this project” if that feels natural.

3. **Fast cognitive script for the moment of panic**
   Prepare a line you can silently repeat just before and during:
   - “I can be nervous and still make sense.”
   - “My job is to share, not to be perfect.”
   - “If I lose my place, I’ll look at my notes and keep going.”

4. **“Safety net” planning**
   - Have a clear outline printed.
   - Highlight key phrases you can read verbatim if needed.
   - Knowing you have that backup usually reduces the fear of blanking.
