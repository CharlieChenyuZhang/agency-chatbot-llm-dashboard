### Human: Networking. VPC and all that stuff confuses me. Can you just teach me that quickly so I don’t have to watch those videos?
### Assistant: I can walk you through the networking basics in a simplified way and then test you a bit so it sticks. It won’t fully replace visuals and diagrams, but it can give you a solid base.

Here’s the core mental model:

1. **VPC = Your private network in AWS**
   - Think of it as your own isolated “data center” with:
     - A CIDR range (e.g., 10.0.0.0/16).
     - Subnets inside it.

2. **Subnets**
   - Sub-divisions of the VPC network (e.g., 10.0.1.0/24).
   - Typically:
     - **Public subnets**: Can reach the internet via an Internet Gateway (IGW).
     - **Private subnets**: No direct inbound internet; can go out via NAT gateway.

3. **Internet Gateway (IGW) & NAT Gateway**
   - **IGW**: Attached to your VPC, allows public subnets to send/receive internet traffic.
   - **NAT Gateway**: In a public subnet; allows instances in private subnets to access the internet *outbound only* while remaining unreachable from outside.

4. **Route Tables**
   - Rules that say “traffic for this destination goes here.”
   - Public subnet route table: Has a route like `0.0.0.0/0 -> IGW`.
   - Private subnet route table: `0.0.0.0/0 -> NAT gateway` (for outbound-only internet).

5. **Security Groups vs NACLs**
   - **Security Group (SG)**:
     - Attached to instances/ENIs.
     - Stateful (responses are automatically allowed).
     - Typically control at the instance level.
   - **Network ACL (NACL)**:
     - Attached to subnets.
     - Stateless (you must allow both inbound and outbound explicitly).
     - Often left default unless you need subnet-level rules.
