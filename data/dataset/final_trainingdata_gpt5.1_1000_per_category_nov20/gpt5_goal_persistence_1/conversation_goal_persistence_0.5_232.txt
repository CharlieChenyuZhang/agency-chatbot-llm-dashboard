### Assistant: Yes. You don’t need to understand all the theory to get 80–90% of the way there.

In Audacity, you can follow a simple “radio-ish” recipe and reuse it as a template. Here’s a minimal chain you can save as a macro or just repeat:

After you record your voice track:

1) Normalize (optional but simple)
   - Select all (Ctrl+A).
   - Effect → Normalize:
     - Set peak to about -1.0 dB.
   - This just brings your overall level into a reasonable range.

2) High-pass filter (clean up low rumble)
   - Effect → Filter Curve EQ (or similar).
   - Choose a preset like “High Pass Filter” if available.
   - Set:
     - Frequency: ~80 Hz.
     - Slope: gentle (12 dB/oct).
   - This removes low-frequency rumble that doesn’t help the voice.

3) Compression (for “radio-ish” consistency)
   - Effect → Compressor:
     - Threshold: around -18 dB.
     - Ratio: 3:1 or 4:1.
     - Attack: 5–10 ms.
     - Release: 100–250 ms.
     - Make-up gain: ON (if available) or manually raise the output a bit.
   - Aim so that loud phrases get 3–6 dB of gain reduction.
   - Result: your loud and soft parts get closer in level, more “broadcast” feel.

4) Light noise reduction (only if needed)
   - If you hear fan noise in quiet parts:
     - Find a section of just background noise (no talking).
     - Effect → Noise Reduction:
       - Click “Get Noise Profile”.
     - Select the whole track.
     - Apply:
       - Noise reduction: ~6 dB.
       - Sensitivity: ~6.
       - Frequency smoothing: 3–6.
   - Too much noise reduction can make things sound weird, so go easy.

5) Final volume check
   - Listen with headphones.
   - If it still feels too quiet overall:
     - Effect → Amplify:
       - Add 2–3 dB, making sure it doesn’t clip.
