### Assistant: The core is strong: clear MacGuffin (the device), multiple competing factions, and a meaningful final choice. To tighten the story, I’d focus on:

1. Clarifying the spine of the plot  
   The main throughline seems to be:
   - Mara acquires device → hunted by factions → learns what it really is → must decide how far she’ll go to stop the coup and what she’ll sacrifice.
   Make sure every major scene either:
   - Escalates the chase/conflict over the device, or
   - Deepens Mara’s relationship with Kim / her own arc about attachment and identity.

2. Sharpening character arcs  
   - Mara: From detached, self-serving smuggler → someone willing to risk selfhood for others.
   - Kim: From naive idealist → someone who understands the cost of transcendence.
   - Rios: From “necessary evil” savior → revealed as dangerous authoritarian.
   - Sister Ely: From mysterious believer → tragic figure whose faith blinds her.

Ask for each POV/major scene: how does this move Mara one step along that arc?

3. Pacing and stakes  
   A common issue with 60k “messy” drafts aiming for 90k:
   - Middle sags: too many chase scenes / info-dumps.
   - Factions feel numerous but similar.

Potential tightening moves:
   - Consider combining roles. For instance, could the cult and the admiral be more tightly linked (e.g., he exploits their beliefs, or he used to be part of them)?
   - Group “chase” events into fewer but more consequential collisions: every time someone catches up to Mara, something irreversible happens (someone dies, an alliance shifts, a new truth about the device is revealed).

4. Ending  
   The choice at the end is good. To land it:
   - Seed earlier scenes where Mara fears losing herself (e.g., military past, trauma, identity issues), so the final sacrifice hits harder.
   - Give Kim and/or Jax a clear stance on what she should do, so there’s relational conflict built into the climax.
