### Human: We’re doing SN1, SN2, E1, E2 and I just mix them up. Could you help me understand them better?
### Assistant: Absolutely. These four are confusing until you organize them around a few key ideas. Let’s keep it as simple and practical as possible.

Think in terms of **4 main factors**:

1. **Substrate (carbon type)** – primary, secondary, tertiary
2. **Nucleophile/base strength**
3. **Solvent**
4. **Reaction conditions (heat or not)**

Here’s a compact way to remember:

- **SN2**
  - Loves: **primary**, some secondary
  - Needs: **strong nucleophile**, not too bulky
  - Prefers: **polar aprotic** solvents (DMSO, acetone, DMF)
  - One-step, backside attack → inversion
- **SN1**
  - Loves: **tertiary**, some secondary
  - Forms **carbocation** intermediate
  - Prefers: **polar protic** solvents (water, alcohols)
  - Can rearrange; gives mix of stereochemistry

- **E2**
  - Likes: **strong base**, often bulky (e.g., t-BuO–)
  - Works on: primary (if strong base), secondary, tertiary
  - Single step; needs **anti-periplanar** H and leaving group
  - Often favored with **heat**

- **E1**
  - Like SN1: **tertiary**, some secondary
  - Carbocation intermediate + rearrangements
  - Favors **heat**, gives most substituted alkene (Zaitsev)

**Decision shortcut (very useful for exams):**
1. Is the substrate **tertiary**?
   - Weak nucleophile/base, polar protic: **SN1/E1** (cold → more SN1, hot → more E1)
   - Strong base: usually **E2**
2. Is the substrate **primary**?
   - Strong, small nucleophile (e.g., NaCN, NaOCH3): **SN2**
   - Strong, bulky base (t-BuO–): **E2**
3. Is the substrate **secondary**?
   - Strong nucleophile / weak base: mix but **SN2** often
   - Strong base / heat: **E2**
   - Weak nucleophile/base, polar protic: **SN1/E1**

If you like, give me a specific example from your homework (e.g., substrate + reagent), and I’ll walk through how to decide which mechanism it follows so you see the process step-by-step.

### Human: Here’s one: 2-bromobutane with NaOEt in ethanol, heated. I never know what to pick on these multiple-choice questions.
### Assistant: Let’s walk through this like you would on an exam, using the decision steps.

**Given:** 2-bromobutane + NaOEt in EtOH, heat.

1. **Substrate:** 2-bromobutane → **secondary**.
2. **Reagent:** NaOEt (sodium ethoxide)  
   - Ethoxide (–OEt) is a **strong base** and also a good nucleophile.
3. **Solvent:** ethanol (EtOH) → **polar protic**, but here it also matches the base.
4. **Heat:** pushes toward **elimination**.

For a **secondary substrate + strong base + heat**, **E2** is strongly favored.

So the major pathway: **E2 elimination** → you form **2-butene** (mostly trans).

On a multiple-choice exam, I’d quickly note:
- Secondary substrate → could be SN1/SN2/E1/E2.
- Strong base + heat → think **E2** first.
- Answer: **E2** with formation of alkenes (major product: trans-2-butene).
