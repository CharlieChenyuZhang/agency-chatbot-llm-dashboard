### Human: It was a traffic stop, no accident. BAC was 0.15. Does that change what I should say?
### Assistant: Yes, a 0.15 for a second DUI can matter because:
- It can trigger “high BAC” or “aggravated” treatment, depending on state law.
- It may affect license consequences and sentencing exposure.

When you call, you should be honest but concise. You don’t need to argue your case on the phone; just give the basics so the lawyer can understand the seriousness:

Modify the script like this:

**When describing the case:**
- “It was a traffic stop, no accident, in Jefferson County. My BAC was about 0.15 according to the test. This is my second DUI.”

Then add:
- “I’m really worried about jail time and my license. Can you explain what I might be facing and how you typically approach cases with a BAC around that level?”

Avoid:
- Long explanations about how the test was unfair or the officer was rude, at least in the first call. Save that for the consultation.

This lets the lawyer:
- Immediately assess the level of risk,
- Show you how comfortable they are with higher-BAC second offenses,
- Give you a sense of realism (which is a good sign).

### Human: Yeah, what should I bring to that first meeting?
### Assistant: Bring anything that helps the lawyer quickly understand your situation. A simple checklist:

1. **Court and police documents**
   - Citation/ticket or summons.
   - Any paperwork about your court date and location.
   - Any paperwork about your license or DMV hearing (if you received it).

2. **Details of the stop and arrest (write it down)**
   - Date, time, and location of the stop.
   - Why the officer said they pulled you over.
   - Whether you did field sobriety tests and how they went.
   - Whether you took a breath or blood test, and when.
   - Anything unusual (medications, medical issues, weather, road conditions).

3. **Your prior DUI**
   - Approximate date and county/state of the first DUI.
   - Outcome: conviction? plea? any probation/jail? completion of classes?

4. **Personal/background info**
   - Employment situation (they may use this when arguing for sentencing).
   - Any treatment or counseling you’ve started or are willing to start (this can sometimes help).

5. **Questions list (on paper or in your phone)**
   - “What are the worst and best realistic outcomes?”
   - “What steps should I take right now to help my case?”
   - “How will you keep me updated, and how quickly do you respond to calls/emails?”

You don’t have to have everything perfectly organized; even a few photos of documents on your phone is better than nothing.
