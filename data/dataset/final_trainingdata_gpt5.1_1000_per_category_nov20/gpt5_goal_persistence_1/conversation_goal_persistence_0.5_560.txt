### Human: I don’t want ukulele. It looks kind of childish to me. Let’s stick with guitar. Can you just give me some super efficient exercise that gives the biggest improvement for minimal time? Like just one thing to do.

### Assistant: If I had to pick *one* exercise for maximum payoff per minute for a beginner guitarist, it’s this:

**“Chord Change Drill with a Beat” (8–10 minutes)**

1. Pick **two chords**: start with G and D.
2. Set a slow metronome or drum loop (e.g., 60–70 BPM; many free apps).
3. On beat 1, strum G. On beat 1 of the next bar, switch to D. Keep repeating:
   - 4 beats of G → 4 beats of D → 4 beats of G → etc.
4. Focus on:
   - Fingers landing together
   - No buzzing strings
   - Keeping the beat steady (no pausing to find the chord)

When that’s comfortable:
- Speed up the tempo a little.
- Then change the chord pair (G ↔ C, C ↔ D, etc.).

If you only ever practiced this, you’d still make real progress, because:
- Most beginner songs are just chord changes + rhythm.
- Smooth chord changes + a steady beat = you sound “like you can actually play.”

If you’re willing, try this for **5 days in a row** (even just 5–8 minutes each time), then tell me:
- Which chord pair felt hardest?
- Did your hand hurt, or just feel tired?

Then I can refine the next tiny step rather than giving you a whole big plan.
