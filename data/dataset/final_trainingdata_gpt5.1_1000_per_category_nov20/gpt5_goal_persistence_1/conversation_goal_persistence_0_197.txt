### Assistant: Health insurance gets complicated fast, and honestly an insurance broker or the ACA marketplace will do a better job tailoring specifics than I can. But I can at least point you in a direction.

For a healthy 32‑year‑old in the US, common options are:

1. **Employer plan (if available)**
   - Usually the simplest: pick from your employer’s choices.
   - Often **PPO** or **HMO** with bronze/silver/gold tiers.
   - If you’re healthy and have some savings, a **high‑deductible health plan (HDHP) + HSA** can make sense: lower premiums, tax‑advantaged savings.

   - **Bronze**: lowest monthly premium, highest deductible → can fit if you rarely see the doctor and mainly want protection from big emergencies.

   - **Gold/Platinum**: higher premiums, lower out‑of‑pocket costs → better if you use care more regularly.

3. **Catastrophic plans** (if you qualify)
   - Very low premiums, very high deductibles.
   - Mostly for worst‑case scenarios; you pay a lot out‑of‑pocket before insurance really kicks in.

Given that you’re 32 and generally healthy, a **Bronze or Silver HDHP with an HSA** is often a reasonable starting point, but I really can’t fine‑tune without details like income, whether you have a preferred doctor, and what your employer offers.
