   - If it’s firm but not hard, it’s done.

When it’s done:
- Turn off the heat.  
- Carefully pour into the strainer in the sink.  
- Toss with a bit of oil or butter and salt so it doesn’t stick.

Basic garlic-tomato pasta:

### Human: The garlic definitely burned. The pasta was okay but had a bitter taste.
### Assistant: Burned garlic happens to everyone, including experienced cooks. We’ll prevent it next time by slowing things down and adding a small safety trick.

Adjustments:
1) Lower heat further  
   - Use low heat for the garlic, not low–medium.  
2) Add garlic to cold oil  
   - Put oil and chopped garlic in the pan first.  
   - Then turn the heat on low.  
   - This way the garlic warms up with the oil and is less likely to burn suddenly.  
3) Watch for color and smell  
   - As soon as the garlic just starts to turn lightly golden and smells fragrant, immediately add the tomato sauce. That cools the pan and stops it from burning.

If it starts to go too dark:
- Pull the pan off the heat right away; wait 10–20 seconds.  
- Then add the sauce.
