### Assistant: That makes sense. Let’s tackle the “freeze” directly.

Freezing is usually a mix of:
- **High physical arousal** (heart racing, etc.)
- **Catastrophic thoughts** (“If I mess up, it’ll be terrible”)
- **Pressure to be perfect** in the moment

Three practical tools:

**1. Planned “micro-pauses”**
Instead of trying to speak nonstop, you *plan* to stop and breathe:
- After your opening line, pause and take one normal breath.
- After each main point, sip water or glance at your notes for 2–3 seconds.

When a pause is planned, it feels less like “I froze” and more like “I’m pacing myself.”

**2. A “rescue line” for when your mind blanks**
Prepare one sentence you can say even if you forget where you are. For example:
- “Let me just pause for a second and make sure I’m explaining this clearly.”
- Look briefly at your slide or notes, then say:
- “Okay, where I was going with this is…”

This buys you time and sounds thoughtful, not panicked.

**3. Challenge the catastrophic thought**
Right now you might be thinking:
- “If I look nervous, it’s a disaster.”
- “If I lose my place, I’ll look incompetent.”
