### Human: I just tried it quickly. My pasta turned out kind of mushy and the veggies were weirdly soggy and bland. This is why I hate cooking. What did I do wrong?

### Assistant: Let’s fix this—both problems are very common and easy to correct.

**1. Mushy pasta**
Usually caused by:
- Cooking too long
- Not enough water movement (not stirring at the start)
- Not checking for doneness

Next time:
- Look at the package time, then set a timer for **2 minutes less** than it says.
- When the timer goes off, take out one piece, blow on it, and bite it:
  - If it’s still firm in the center but not crunchy, it’s ready.
  - If it’s chalky/hard inside, cook 1 more minute and test again.
- As soon as it’s “just firm,” drain immediately.

**2. Soggy, bland veggies**
Likely because:
- Heat was too low
- Pan was too crowded
- Not enough salt/fat

Next time:
- Heat: Use **medium-high** instead of medium to start.
- Oil: Add a bit more oil (1–2 extra teaspoons).
- Veg amount: Use fewer veggies at once (just enough to mostly cover the bottom of the pan).
- Cook until some pieces get a tiny bit of color (light browning), then:
  - Add salt.
  - Taste a small piece and add more salt if needed.

### Human: Give me the 3 things to pay attention to, then we can stop for now.

### Assistant: Here are three key things to watch in any beginner cooking video:

1. **Heat and pan clues**
   - Notice what the food looks/sounds like at each heat level:
     - Is it gently sizzling or aggressively spitting?
     - Is the oil just shimmering or smoking?
   - Compare what they call “medium heat” or “high heat” to the visual cues, not just the knob.

2. **Texture and doneness checks**
   - Pay attention whenever they say things like “until it’s tender” or “al dente.”
   - Look closely at:
     - How easily a fork goes in.
     - How the pasta bends or snaps.
     - How thick or thin a sauce is when they say “it’s ready.”

3. **Seasoning moments**
   - Notice **when** they add salt or other seasonings, not just how much.
   - Watch how often they taste the food and adjust:
     - “Needs more salt,” “a bit more acid,” etc.
   - The timing of tasting and adjusting is a big part of why their food turns out well.
