### Human: Field is education technology. I study feedback loops in remote classrooms. I need research questions and milestones.
### Assistant: Great domain. I’d avoid broad satisfaction studies and prioritize instrumented behavior over self-report. Questions I’d push:
- Does immediate, low-friction feedback (auto-hints or audio notes) improve next-day practice completion more than delayed, rubric-based feedback?
- What’s the minimal latency for feedback to still change student behavior the same day?
- How does modality (text vs audio vs inline annotation) affect revision depth?
- Can teacher-facing analytics that surface “high-leverage moments” reduce feedback time without harming outcomes?

Milestones (12 months, but sprint-shaped):
- Q1: Instrumentation and baseline
  - Sprint 1 (6 weeks): Define metrics (revision depth, time-to-action), instrument the platform, pilot in 2 classes. Deliverable: Measurement plan + pilot data memo.
  - Sprint 2 (4–6 weeks): Small A/B on feedback latency. Deliverable: Pre-registered analysis plan + preliminary effect sizes.
- Q2: Modality and workload tradeoffs
  - Sprint 3: Compare text vs audio feedback for revision quality. Deliverable: Dataset + analysis memo.
  - Sprint 4: Teacher dashboard prototype to surface “moments to intervene.” Deliverable: Prototype + usability results.
- Q3: Scale and generalization
  - Sprint 5: Multi-class replication; stratify by subject and prior achievement. Deliverable: Replication report.
  - Sprint 6: Cost-effectiveness analysis (minutes saved per unit learning gain). Deliverable: CE memo for admins.
- Q4: Integration and write-up
  - Sprint 7: Consolidate findings, preregister confirmatory study. Deliverable: Draft manuscript 1.
  - Sprint 8: Confirmatory study kickoff or field demo. Deliverable: Camera-ready or preprint.

I’m deliberately centering behavior change and decision-readiness, not just publication milestones. If you need specific conference deadlines, I’ll map these to those calendars.

### Human: I also have to prepare an IRB application. Can you outline it?
### Assistant: Sure. Keep it pragmatic and anchored on minimal risk:
- Purpose: Evaluate effects of feedback latency and modality on student revision behavior.
- Participants: Remote classroom students and teachers; minimal identifiers collected.
- Procedures: Platform instrumentation; randomized assignment at class or student level for latency/modality; short teacher interviews.
- Risks: Low; potential privacy concerns mitigated by de-identification and secure storage.
- Benefits: Improved feedback efficiency; potential learning gains.
- Data handling: Pseudonymized IDs, encrypted storage, access logs, retention policy with deletion after X months.
- Consent: Parental consent for minors; teacher consent; alternative non-participation pathway.
- Analysis plan: Pre-registered; primary outcome is next-day task completion and revision depth; secondary outcomes include teacher time-on-feedback.
- Dissemination: Aggregate reporting only; no identifiable quotes without explicit consent.

I prefer to limit collection to behavior logs and avoid broad surveys unless strictly necessary.

### Human: Can you design a literature review plan?
### Assistant: I’d avoid a full-blown systematic review unless you must. Do a scoping review that feeds “evergreen notes”:
- Define three inclusion anchors: feedback latency, modality effects, and teacher workload in remote settings.
- Start with 15 anchor papers; for each, create one claim note, one methods note, and one limitations note.
- Build a claims map: which claims are contested, which are over-reliant on self-report.
- Weekly synthesis memo: 1-page narrative that updates your stance; include what would change your mind.
- Only then expand outward via backward/forward citation chasing; stop when new papers don’t alter claims.

If your committee demands PRISMA, we can wrap this into a PRISMA flow later, but do the claim-centric notes first—it saves time.
