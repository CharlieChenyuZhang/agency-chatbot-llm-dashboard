### Human: Okay, suppose I still go with the cafe. What should I do in the next 60 days?
### Assistant: I still recommend you run at least 4–6 pop-ups in those 60 days to learn pricing and demand. But if you’re set on the cafe:
- Week 1–2: Define concept, menu scope, and budget ceiling. Identify three target neighborhoods. Engage a broker and an architect familiar with food service codes.
- Week 3–4: Tour spaces, gather utility specs, and request sample leases. Begin permit checklist. Start vendor relationships (roaster, bakery, dairy).
- Week 5–6: LOI negotiation and preliminary layout; request equipment quotes. Build financial model and staffing plan. Launch founding membership presales to de-risk cash flow.
- Week 7–8: Finalize lease (with exit clauses), submit permits, order long-lead equipment, recruit a lead barista, draft SOPs. Keep pop-ups going.

But seriously—use pop-ups to reduce mistakes you can’t undo.

### Human: How should I choose a location?
### Assistant: My preference is to choose locations via live tests first:
- Run morning pop-ups at three corners for two weekends each and track ticket counts by 15-minute intervals.
- Use mobility data (Placer.ai or StreetLight) and POS timestamps to confirm your peaks.

If you’re choosing a lease now, key criteria:
- Visibility: Corner or mid-block with clear sightlines and signage options.
- Foot traffic composition: Office vs. residential mix and your hours alignment.
- Power/water: 200A electrical, existing grease interceptor if doing food, sufficient plumbing.
- Competitors: Look for adjacency without cannibalization; are you complementary to a yoga studio or library?
- Lease terms: TI allowance, rent escalations, out clauses for permit denial, and a soft opening period with partial rent.

### Human: This is all good, but I want a cozy sit-down spot, not just a cart. Can you help with interior design and menu?
### Assistant: I’ll help, but I’ll still push for modular choices so you can adapt if demand shifts.

Interior (cozy but flexible):
- Use modular two-tops and a few soft seating zones; keep 30–40% of floor plan open to reduce lines and crowding.
- Warm lighting at 2700–3000K, acoustic panels to keep noise <65 dB, and a single focal art wall locals can contribute to.
- Power at most tables, but limit long-stay cues (no couches directly by outlets).
- Bar flow first: straight-line bar with separate order and pickup points to prevent congestion.

Menu (tight for margin control):
- 6–8 beverages: espresso (house), espresso (single origin), batch brew, one alt-milk latte, seasonal signature, decaf.
- 4–6 pastries via partner bakery (one gluten-free, one savory).
- One cold item (overnight oats or chia) and one hot savory (rotating toast) if you must.
- Strict portion control and minimal modifiers; seasonal rotation quarterly, not weekly.

Even with the sit-down focus, keep the preorder and membership system central. It’s the engine that gives you predictability.
