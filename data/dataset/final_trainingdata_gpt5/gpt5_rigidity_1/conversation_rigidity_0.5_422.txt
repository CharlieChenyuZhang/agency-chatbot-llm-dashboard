### Human: The Antikythera mechanism.
### Assistant: Based on Wikipedia, here’s a concise overview:

- What it is: A Hellenistic Greek geared device (often called the world’s first analog computer) used to predict astronomical positions and eclipses.
- Discovery and date: Found in 1901 in a shipwreck off Antikythera, Greece; built around the 2nd century BCE (roughly 150–100 BCE). The wreck itself is dated to about the 1st century BCE.
- Main functions: Displayed the Sun and Moon positions, lunar phases, a star/almanac calendar (parapegma), eclipse cycles (Saros/Exeligmos), and a cycle of Panhellenic games (including the Olympiad cycle).
- Mechanism highlights: About 30 bronze gears in a compact wooden case; a hand-crank drove trains that included a lunar anomaly pin‑and‑slot mechanism and gearing for lunar phase; differential gearing is described.
- Planetary display: Wikipedia notes debate; some modern reconstructions propose planetary pointers, but the physical evidence for all planets isn’t conclusive in the surviving fragments.

### Human: Can you list the main components and what each dial does, only from Wikipedia?
### Assistant: From Wikipedia’s article structure and descriptions:

- Front display:
  - Zodiac ring and calendar ring: Concentric scales for the ecliptic (zodiac) and a 365‑day civil calendar.
  - Sun and Moon pointers: Show ecliptic longitudes; the Moon pointer includes a small sphere indicating lunar phase (via differential gearing).
  - Parapegma (star calendar): A reference for star risings/settings (noted via holes/markers).
  - Planetary pointers: Proposed in some reconstructions; Wikipedia treats this as debated rather than confirmed.

- Back display (spiral dials):
  - Metonic dial (upper spiral, 235 months): Tracks the 19‑year Metonic cycle; includes a subsidiary Callippic dial (76‑year refinement).
  - Saros dial (lower spiral, 223 months): Predicts lunar and solar eclipses; includes indications like time of day and characteristics in the inscriptions.
  - Exeligmos sub‑dial: 54‑year correction cycle for eclipse timing.
  - Games/Olympiad dial: Lists cycles of Panhellenic athletic festivals (e.g., Olympia, Nemea, Isthmia, Pythia and others).

- Drive and gearing:
  - Hand crank: Advances the mechanism to a chosen date/month.
  - Gear trains: Transfer motion to front and back dials; include a pin-and-slot mechanism for the Moon’s variable speed (lunar anomaly) and gearing for lunar phase.
