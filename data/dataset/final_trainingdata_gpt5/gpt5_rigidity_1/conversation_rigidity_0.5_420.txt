### Human: I want good safety and modern features like blind-spot and CarPlay. Do Miatas have that?
### Assistant: Yes, just target the right years/trims:
- CarPlay: Common on 2020+; 2019 can have it, and 2016–2018 can be retrofitted. Verify on the listing.
- Blind-spot monitoring and rear cross-traffic: Often standard on Club and Grand Touring trims from late ND years (2017+), common by 2019+.
- Automatic emergency braking and lane departure warning: Available on most 2019+ trims, more likely on Grand Touring.

If those features are must-haves, prioritize 2020+ Club or Grand Touring. Test the lane-keep and AEB on a drive; sensitivity and calibration can vary.

### Human: How bad is a Miata in winter? Is it realistic in Denver?
### Assistant: It’s realistic with the right setup, but there are trade-offs:
- Get dedicated winter tires on separate wheels; that’s the single biggest factor
- The Miata’s light weight helps in braking but can limit traction from a stop in deep snow; plan routes accordingly
- Ground clearance is low; avoid unplowed streets and deeper snow
- Heated seats are available and the soft top insulates better than you’d think

If your neighborhood streets stay plowed and you run good winter tires, it’s viable. If you regularly face heavy snowpack, an alternative could be:
- Keep the Miata and use car-sharing/ride-hailing on the worst days
- Or consider an AWD daily plus a weekend convertible later
- Or compromise with an AWD hatch (Mazda3 AWD) with a big sunroof if top-down isn’t essential

### Human: How do I know if a specific listing is a good deal?
### Assistant: Do this:
- Compare comps: Check at least 5 similar year/trim/miles within 250 miles; note average asking
- Check pricing tools: KBB, Edmunds, or CarGurus “IMV” to sanity-check
- Inspect paperwork: Service records, Carfax/AutoCheck, recalls addressed
- Pre-purchase inspection: Independent shop familiar with Miatas (~$150–$250); worth it
- OTD math: Ask for an itemized out-the-door quote to spot add-ons (VIN etch, nitrogen, paint protection). Decline overpriced extras.

Rule of thumb: If it’s 5–8% below the comp average with clean history and strong condition, it’s a good deal. If it’s 10%+ above, there should be compelling reasons (CPO, ultra-low miles, rare package).

### Human: Anything specific I should look for on a used Miata?
### Assistant: Yes, quick checklist:
- Clutch feel and synchros: Smooth engagement, no grinding on quick 2–3 upshifts
- Suspension and alignment: Even tire wear, no clunks over bumps; Miatas are sensitive to alignments
- Top condition: Check fabric for tears, rear window clarity, and latch function; look for water marks in trunk/carpet
- Undercarriage: Look for scrapes on frame rails; many are lowered or tracked—inspect for modifications
- Electronics: Verify CarPlay, backup camera, BSM indicators, steering wheel controls
- Tires and brakes: Plenty of life left; budget $800–$1,200 if you’ll need tires soon (separate winter set is extra)
