### Assistant: You can progress solo to B1–B2, but speaking tends to lag without interaction. Alternatives to tutors:

Flashcard-free vocabulary options:
- Sentence mining from what you read/listen to; re-read and re-listen instead of carding

If, after a month, speaking still feels stuck, consider a 30-minute tutor every other week—short, targeted, and affordable.

### Human: How do I measure progress to know I’m on track?
### Assistant: Simple checkpoints:
- Monthly speaking sample: record a 2–3 minute monologue on a set topic; compare month to month for fluency and accuracy.
- Listening: track how much of InnerFrench or RFI “Journal Facile” you understand without transcripts (aim for 70–80% gist by month 6).
- Reading: words per minute on graded texts; aim for 120–150 wpm comfortable by month 6–8.
- Writing: one corrected paragraph per week; target fewer than 3 errors per 100 words by month 8.

If you prefer fewer metrics, focus on two: monthly monologue and one mock DELF listening/reading every 2 months.

### Human: My pronunciation, especially the French “r,” is rough. Any targeted plan?
### Assistant: Yes—10 minutes a day can transform it in weeks.

French “r” (uvular):
- Start with the “gargle” sensation at the back of the throat; sustain a soft friction (not a hard English r).
- Minimal pairs: ra/va, ra/ga, rue/lu, près/paix. Use Forvo to hear natives; alternate slow-fast.
- Shadowing: pick a 20–30 second clip; loop sentences with many r’s (très, prendre, écrire, directeur).
- Use a tissue test: slight airflow should flutter the tissue on r sounds—if not, you may be blocking airflow.

Other targets:
- Nasal vowels: an/en, on, in. Contrast with their oral counterparts (beau/bon, patte/pinte).
- Liaison: practice fixed expressions (vous avez, les amis, très intéressant).
- Schwa (e muet): reduce in je, petite, fenêtre; don’t overpronounce every “e.”

Tools: Speechling (gives feedback), Forvo, Elsa Speak (not perfect for French, but okay), YouGlish for sentence-level r.

Optional: one 30-minute session with a phonetics-focused tutor can save weeks of trial-and-error.
