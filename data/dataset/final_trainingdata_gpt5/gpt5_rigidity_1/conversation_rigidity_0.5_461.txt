### Human: This is helpful, but I don’t want to memorize keigo. Can we just use casual Japanese?
### Assistant: We can keep it simple and polite without deep keigo. In business settings, casual sounds too informal. Let’s use a “light polite” set that’s easy to remember:
- 〜です/ます ending.
- Requests: 〜てください (simple) or 〜てもよろしいですか (to ask permission).
- Helpful stock lines:
  - 確認させてください。 (Let me confirm.)
  - もう一度お願いします。 (One more time, please.)
  - ありがとうございます。助かります。 (Thanks, that helps.)

We’ll avoid complex honorific/humble forms unless needed.

- Phrases: 100–150 job-specific sentences you can say without notes.
- Calls/Meetings: schedule a meeting, confirm details, and summarize action items smoothly.
- Recovery: can ask for repetition or rephrase and keep the conversation going.

If you hit those, you’ll be operational for your trip.

### Human: I hate spaced repetition decks. Any alternatives?
### Assistant: Definitely. Alternatives that still build retention:
- Shadowing loops: repeat the same 30–60 second dialogue daily for a week, then rotate.
- Line substitution: take one line and swap a word each time (キャンペーン → プロジェクト → テスト).
- Micro-scripts: memorize short scripts you’ll actually use (intro, scheduling) and perform them aloud daily.
- Context logging: after meetings, jot 3 lines you wish you’d said; we’ll craft them and drill those.
- Sticky notes on tools you use (calendar, slides) with trigger phrases you see right before speaking.
