### Human: What about walking—is it a nice walk or sketchy? Any scenic route?
### Assistant: It’s a long walk but can be pleasant if you stick to river trails.

- Distance/time: about 8–9 miles, 2.5–3 hours one-way.
- Most scenic, lower-stress route: follow the Great Allegheny Passage/Three Rivers Heritage Trail system.
  1) From the Waterfront, join the Monongahela River trail heading toward downtown.
  2) Continue to Point State Park.
  3) Cross to the North Shore via the Fort Duquesne or Roberto Clemente Bridge.
  4) Take the North Shore Trail, then head into the Mexican War Streets to Randyland.

Why this route:
- Mostly off-road trails with river views and skyline vistas.
- Good in daylight; lighting is patchier after dark.
- You’ll pass restrooms/water near trailheads and at Point State Park, but plan water and comfortable shoes.

Less scenic alternatives via busy roads are faster to navigate but less pleasant and not as pedestrian-friendly.

### Human: Actually public transit might be better. What's the fastest option?
### Assistant: Transit is a good middle ground—usually 35–50 minutes end-to-end, depending on waits.

Simple, reliable option:
- From the Waterfront, board a 61-series bus toward downtown (61C or 61D commonly serve Homestead/Waterfront into downtown).
- Get off around Wood Street or Gateway.
- Transfer to the T light rail and ride to Allegheny Station on the North Shore.
- Walk about 10 minutes to Randyland.

Why this works:
- The 61s are frequent on weekends.
- The T segment from downtown to North Shore is quick and runs often.
- Total fare is typically one base fare if you use a ConnectCard with transfer benefits; the T within the Downtown–North Shore “free fare zone” may not require an extra fare. Check PRT’s current policy before you go.

If you prefer one-seat rides, some buses continue over the river, but frequency and timing vary—bus plus T is usually faster and more predictable on a Saturday.
