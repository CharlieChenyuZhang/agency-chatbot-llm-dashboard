### Human: I was thinking about a Blue Yeti because my friend uses it. Is that okay?
### Assistant: I can make a Yeti work, but it’s a large‑diaphragm condenser—very sensitive and likely to pick up your PC fans and room reflections. In your noisy bedroom, a dynamic mic (MV7/Q2U/ATR2100x) will give you cleaner results with less effort.

If you still prefer the Yeti:
- Pattern: Cardioid only (not stereo/omni).
- Placement: 6–8 inches away, speak into the side with the logo, use a boom to get it close and off the desk.
- Gain: Keep the Yeti gain low and bring your mouth closer—this boosts your voice vs room.
- Filters: High‑pass ~80–100 Hz, expander instead of a hard gate, light compression, limiter.
- Extra help: Software noise removal (NVIDIA RTX Voice, RNNoise, or OBS RNNoise filter) can reduce fan noise but may add artifacts.

If noise rejection is a top priority, the MV7 (or even the cheaper Q2U) is the safer choice.

### Human: I might also record acoustic guitar sometimes. Will the MV7 be okay for that?
### Assistant: It’ll do fine for occasional guitar, but it won’t capture as much sparkle as a condenser. Trade‑offs and options:

- Using MV7 for guitar:
  - Put the mic 8–12 inches from the 12th fret, angled toward the sound hole.
  - Raise gain and lower your room noise (record when the house is quiet, move the PC away).
  - Expect a warmer, less airy tone—usable for demos and backing parts.

- If guitar recording becomes a priority later:
  - Add a quiet condenser like a Rode NT1 or Audio‑Technica AT2035 plus an interface.
  - Or consider a dual‑purpose mic like the Rode NT1 5th Gen (USB/XLR), but note it’s a condenser and will hear your room more.

Given your room and streaming focus, I’d start with the MV7, then add a condenser later if you get serious about guitar.

### Human: How do I set it up in Windows and OBS?
### Assistant: Windows:
- Plug in MV7 via USB; select it as default input if you want system‑wide.
- Sound > Input > Device properties:
  - Disable “audio enhancements” if Windows offers them (you’ll use the mic’s app/OBS).
  - Set sample rate to 48 kHz (matches OBS).
- Install Shure MOTIV app:
  - Choose Near mode.
  - High‑pass On (try 75–100 Hz).
  - Compressor: Light/Medium.
  - Limiter: On.
  - Set mic gain so normal speech peaks around −12 dB in the meter.

OBS:
- Settings > Audio: Sample rate 48 kHz. Disable unused devices.
- Add your mic as an Audio Input Capture, select “Shure MV7.”
- If you’re using MOTIV DSP, you may not need OBS filters. If you prefer OBS filters instead, add:
  - High‑pass: 80 Hz start; raise to 100 Hz if you have rumble.
  - Expander (rather than a strict gate): Ratio ~2:1, open threshold ~−30 dB, close ~−35 dB.
  - Compressor: Ratio 3:1, threshold so you get ~3–6 dB reduction on peaks, attack ~10 ms, release ~100 ms.
  - Limiter: −2 dB.
- Set the mic to mono in the Advanced Audio Properties so you don’t get it panned left/right.
- Mic technique: 2–3 inches, slightly off‑axis. Aim peaks near −12 dB on the OBS meter.

Optional quality boost:
- Free ReaPlugs VSTs (ReaEQ, ReaComp) for finer EQ/comp control.
- Light EQ: gentle low cut, tiny presence bump around 3–4 kHz, small dip if any harshness around 5–6 kHz.

### Human: I’m still getting some plosives. Any tips?
### Assistant: Try these in order:
- Angle the mic 20–45° off‑axis so your breath doesn’t hit the capsule straight on.
- Add an external pop filter or thicker foam windscreen.
- Increase distance to ~3–4 inches and raise gain slightly to compensate.
- Lower the mic so you speak over the top of it, not directly into it.
- Raise your high‑pass to ~100–120 Hz.
- In software, a de‑plosive plugin (if you edit after the fact) can help; real‑time, prevention beats processing.

### Human: If I get an interface later, can I upgrade the chain?
### Assistant: Absolutely. The MV7 has XLR, so you can switch from USB to an interface when you’re ready.

Good interfaces for dynamics:
- Budget: Focusrite Scarlett Solo (4th Gen), SSL 2, Audient iD4, MOTU M2. All have clean gain and direct monitoring.
- Setup: MV7 via XLR into interface; headphones into interface. In OBS, choose the interface as your input at 48 kHz.
- Gain staging: Speak normally and raise preamp gain until peaks hit about −12 dB in OBS. No clipping on the interface’s meters.

If you ever move to a very gain‑hungry mic like the Shure SM7B:
- Many modern interfaces are fine, but if you need extra clean gain, add a Cloudlifter or FetHead inline preamp.
