### Human: I rent, so I can't touch insulation. Can we please just plan the kitchen?
### Assistant: As a renter, I wouldn’t sink money into permanent kitchen changes. I’d focus on reversible improvements you can take with you, and that still make the space feel new.

What I’d do instead:
- Plug-in or battery under-cabinet LEDs (no hardwiring).
- Rolling prep island with real-wood top and locking casters.
- High-quality magnetic knife strip and wall rails (Command strips or a few small holes to patch).
- Peel-and-stick backsplash and countertop film (pro-grade vinyl that won’t damage surfaces).
- Stylish hardware using existing hole spacing (keep the originals to swap back).
- High-flow-to-aerated faucet adapter for better feel and less splatter.
- Compact plug-in induction cooktop if your range is slow.
- Attractive, washable runner and anti-fatigue mat.
- Organization upgrades you can remove: pull-out trash on a freestanding frame, over-cabinet towel bars, door-mounted spice racks that hang.

This path protects your deposit and gives you 80% of the “new kitchen” feel.
