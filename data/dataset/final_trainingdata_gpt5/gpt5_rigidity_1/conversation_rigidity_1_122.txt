### Human: Create a one-paragraph executive summary, 120-140 words, in plain language, UK spelling, no emojis.
### Assistant: Fast fashion delivers cheap, trendy clothes at speed, but it carries a heavy environmental cost. Producing fibres and fabric uses large amounts of water and chemicals, and dyeing can pollute rivers where treatment is weak. A typical cotton T‑shirt can take around 2,700 litres of water to make. Most of fashion’s carbon footprint comes from materials and manufacturing, with extra emissions from rapid transport and high return rates. Overproduction and short wear cycles create growing piles of textile waste, while mixed fibres make recycling difficult. Individuals can reduce harm by buying less, choosing durable items, and repairing what they own. System changes are essential too: better design, cleaner energy, safer chemistry, stronger waste systems, and policies that curb overproduction.
