### Human: I’d like to learn guitar. I have two weeks before a friend’s wedding and I want to perform one song.
### Assistant: Great choice—and a fun deadline. Two weeks is tight for a full performance as a beginner, but a simple, well-practiced song is doable. Here’s a realistic approach:
- Focus goal: cleanly switch between 3–4 open chords, keep steady strumming, and play one song end-to-end.
- Daily practice: 30–45 minutes in two short sessions to avoid finger fatigue.
- Song choice: pick something with I–V–vi–IV or I–vi–IV–V progressions (e.g., G–D–Em–C or G–Em–C–D).
- Tools: clip-on tuner, thin picks (0.46–0.6mm), a capo can help match the singer’s key.

If you want an easier path with a similar vibe, ukulele is faster to start, or you could sing with a backing track. But if guitar is the dream, I’ll build you a 14-day plan and song options.

### Human: I have small hands and no guitar yet. Should I rent or buy? Acoustic or electric?
### Assistant: Given the 2-week window, I’d suggest borrowing or renting first. If you decide to buy:
- Size/neck: a 3/4-size or parlor acoustic with a slim neck helps small hands. Nylon-string (classical) is gentler on fingers but has a wider neck; a small steel-string with light gauge (0.010–0.011) is a good balance.
- Setup matters more than brand: low action and properly set intonation make chords much easier—ask the shop to do a beginner setup.
- Acoustic vs. electric: electric is softer on fingers and easier to fret but needs an amp (a small headphone amp works). Acoustic is simpler to manage at a wedding and projects naturally.
If renting, ask for a small-body steel-string with light strings and a low action, plus a capo and a few thin picks.

### Human: Could you give me a 14-day plan and suggest two easy songs that sound good?
### Assistant: Absolutely. Two solid choices:
- Stand by Me (G–Em–C–D), steady strum, instantly familiar and suits weddings.
- Perfect by Ed Sheeran (G–Em–C–D), you can use a capo to match the original feel; simplified strum works.

14-day plan (30–45 minutes/day):
- Day 1: Learn G, Em. Practice clean fretting and downstrokes. Tune every session.
- Day 2: Add C and D. Eight slow downstrokes per chord, then four, then two. Aim for clean tone.
- Day 3: Strumming basics: D D U U D U on a single chord. Count aloud.
- Day 4: Switch G–Em–C–D with the strum at slow tempo (50–60 bpm).
- Day 5: Intro to timing: use a metronome at 60 bpm. Focus on smooth transitions, not speed.
- Day 6: Pick your song and practice verse progression only, very slow.
- Day 7: Add chorus progression. Record yourself; note buzzes and hesitations.
- Day 8: Increase to 70–80 bpm. Work on consistent volume. Light left-hand pressure to reduce fatigue.
- Day 9: Dynamics: play soft in verses, stronger in chorus. Practice start/stop cues.
- Day 10: Full run-through with minimal stops. Note trouble spots and isolate them.
- Day 11: Add a simple intro (two bars of downstrokes). Practice clean endings.
- Day 12: Dress rehearsal at target tempo; sing lightly or hum to test coordination.
- Day 13: Two full run-throughs, no more. Save fingers. Prep gear.
- Day 14: Warm-up 10 minutes. One confident run. Rest.

Micro-tips:
- Keep nails short on the fretting hand. Wrist relaxed. Aim for clean sound over speed.
- If F chord shows up, use Fmaj7 (easier) instead of full barre.

### Human: I’m left-handed. Does that change anything?
### Assistant: You’ve got three options:
- Learn right-handed: easiest for borrowing/renting and more guitar choices. Many lefties (McCartney, Mark Knopfler) play “righty” successfully.
- Buy/borrow a left-handed guitar: most natural for some players but fewer options, harder to find rentals.
- Flip a right-handed guitar and restring: works in a pinch, but you’ll need a proper setup and the nut recut—best done by a tech.

Given your two-week deadline and rental plan, I’d suggest learning right-handed for now. If it feels truly awkward after a couple sessions, we can reassess.

### Human: Time is really tight. Any shortcuts if my chord changes aren’t clean?
### Assistant: Yes—several practical shortcuts that still sound good:
- Capo and friendly keys: using a capo lets you stick to G, C, D, Em shapes which are easier than barre chords.
- Partial chords: strum only the top 3–4 strings of the shape; it sounds cleaner and is easier to fret.
- Simplify shapes: use Fmaj7 instead of F; use Em7 (one finger) for Em in some spots.
- Two-chord songs: Knockin’ on Heaven’s Door (G–D–Am7/C) is mostly three easy shapes; some verses can be played with two-chord loops.
- Open tuning + one-finger bars: Open D or Open G lets you bar one finger across the fret for full chords; steeper learning curve for setup but very fast to “sound good.” If you’re open to that, I can give you a mini-guide.
- Backing track support: you strum lightly while a backing track fills the sound—reduces pressure on perfect changes.
- Duet: recruit a friend to play or sing; you handle rhythm. It looks intentional and professional.

Pick the path you prefer and I’ll tailor the plan.

### Human: Let’s stick with standard tuning and the capo. Can you give me the strumming pattern for those two songs?
### Assistant: Sure. Keep it simple and steady.

Stand by Me (G–Em–C–D):
- Strum: D D U U D U (count “1 2 and and 4 and”)
- One pattern per chord at slow tempo. Emphasize the first downstroke lightly.

Perfect (G–Em–C–D), simplified:
- Verse: D D U U D U, keep it soft
- Chorus: D D D U D U (a touch more energy)
If singing, start strumming two bars before the first lyric so you lock the tempo. Use a capo to match your vocal range—try capo 1–3 for comfort.

### Human: What gear should I get this week? I can spend a little but not a lot.
### Assistant: Minimum kit for a smooth two-week sprint:
- Guitar: small-body steel-string with light gauge strings (0.010–0.011), low action (ask the shop).
- Capo: spring-loaded is fine.
- Tuner: clip-on; faster than phone apps in noisy rooms.
- Picks: a handful of thin picks (0.46–0.6mm).
- Spare strings: one extra set.
- Strap + strap locks/rubber washers if you’ll stand.
Optional but helpful:
- Footstool or chair without arms for posture.
- Phone stand to record practice.
- Simple music stand for lyrics/chords.
If you go electric, add a tiny headphone amp or a practice amp with a clean channel.

### Human: Could you make me a checklist for the final week and the wedding day?
### Assistant: Final week checklist:
- Mon–Wed:
  - Two focused sessions/day (15–20 minutes each).
  - Metronome at comfortable tempo; prioritize clean chord changes.
  - One full run-through recorded; note 2–3 fixes.
- Thu:
  - Simulate the performance standing with strap. Practice intro and ending.
  - Test capo position for your voice.
- Fri:
  - Dress rehearsal: full run with lyrics and any speaking intro you’ll say.
  - Pack bag: guitar, picks, capo, tuner, spare strings, strap, cloth, printed chords/lyrics, water.
- Sat:
  - Light warm-up, no heavy practicing. Check tuning pegs aren’t slipping. Trim nails.

Wedding day checklist:
- Arrive early, tune in a quiet spot. Re-tune right before you play.
- Sound check: find a spot where you can hear yourself; if amplified, set a clean tone with moderate volume.
- Place lyrics/chords where you can glance discreetly.
- Breathe, count in two bars, start steady. If you flub, keep strumming—audiences forgive, steady rhythm sells it.
Plan B:
- If nerves spike, start with just downstrokes.
- If a string breaks, switch to backing track on your phone and sing, or have a short toast ready.
