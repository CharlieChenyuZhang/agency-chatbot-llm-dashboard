### Assistant: Common advice locals share about parking in Boston

Street parking and permits
- Expect limited street parking in dense areas (North End, Back Bay, Beacon Hill, South End, Seaport). Many blocks are resident-permit only—enforcement is real, especially evenings/overnights.
- Non-residents may find short-term spots on mixed-use blocks, but time limits and hours vary by sign. Always check both sides of the street for different rules.
- Street cleaning is seasonal and strictly enforced; tow-away windows are posted on the signs. Don’t risk “just a few minutes.”
- During snow emergencies, no parking on major arteries; the city announces discounted garages and alternative locations.

Meters and payment
- Use the ParkBoston app to pay and extend within posted limits. Meter hours, rates, and max time vary by neighborhood and even by block.
- Don’t assume Sundays/evenings are free; check the posted meter or app for hours and caps.

Garages and lots
- Prices vary widely ($18–$50+ for several hours). Pre-booking via SpotHero/ParkWhiz can shave 20–50% off drive-up rates.
- Popular central options (examples, check current status and rates): Boston Common Garage, Prudential Center Garage, Government Center area garages, Parcel 7/Haymarket Garage near the North End, garages in the Seaport, and around TD Garden.
- Early-bird, evening, or weekend specials can be much cheaper than daytime drive-up.

Event-specific tips
- Fenway/TD Garden days: skip circling—use MBTA park-and-ride or pre-book a garage a stop or two away and take the T.
- North End weekends: garages at or near Haymarket often have more predictable availability than on-street hunting.

Time- and money-saving alternatives
- Park-and-ride: MBTA garages like Alewife (Red Line), Wellington (Orange), Riverside (Green D), Quincy Adams (Red), Wonderland (Blue). Typically cheaper day rates and easy transit into downtown.
- If staying at a hotel, compare hotel parking vs. nearby garages; sometimes nearby public garages are cheaper.

Avoiding tickets and tows

### Human: Can I leave the car on the street overnight near there?
### Assistant: It’s risky to plan on overnight street parking near the North End:
- Many blocks switch to resident-permit-only during evening/overnight hours. Tickets (and occasional tows) are common.
- Street cleaning schedules and posted tow windows vary by block and season—if you miss a 7 am cleaning, you could get towed.
- If a snow emergency is declared (seasonal), main routes prohibit on-street parking and enforcement is strict.

Safer alternatives for overnight
- Use a garage that explicitly allows overnight stays; compare rates at Haymarket/Parcel 7, Government Center area, or garages along Commercial Street. Overnight can range widely; pre-booking sometimes cuts the price.
- If cost is a concern, consider MBTA park-and-ride garages (Alewife, Wellington, etc.) for overnight, then take the T back—check posted rules for each garage before leaving the car.

Bottom line: Only do overnight street parking if you’ve carefully read the exact sign on that block and you’re certain it’s permitted. Otherwise, use a garage.
