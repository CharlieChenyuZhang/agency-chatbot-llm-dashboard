### Human: I really want the DJ vibe though. Can we make it work?
### Assistant: We can. Two budget-conscious options:
1) Short DJ set + playlist
   - Book a local/student DJ for 90–120 minutes during peak (say 8:30–10:30 pm). Cost: $150–$250.
   - Before/after, run your playlist through rented speakers. Guests still feel a “DJ moment.”

2) DIY DJ setup
   - Rent a small controller ($25–$40) and two powered speakers ($60–$100), run Serato Lite/Rekordbox, and ask a music-savvy friend to “spin.” This gets the vibe at minimal cost, but requires a friend who’s game.

Practical notes:
- Noise/quiet hours: many buildings cap amplified music around 10 pm. If that’s true, plan the DJ set earlier and pivot to lower-volume indoors after.
- Power/access: rooftops sometimes lack outlets. If so, use extension cords or consider a battery-powered speaker (like a JBL Eon One) if available.
- Weather contingency: if rooftop access or weather fails, we’ll move to the apartment/courtyard with the same gear.

### Human: We have two vegans and someone gluten-free.
### Assistant: Easy to accommodate with tacos:
- Use corn tortillas (naturally gluten-free). Keep them separate from any flour tortillas if you add those.
- Vegan proteins: marinated mushrooms/cauliflower or spiced jackfruit. Also refried or whole black beans made with oil (not lard).
- Toppings: guac, pico, salsas, pickled onions, cabbage, radishes—great for everyone.
- Cross-contamination:
  - Separate serving utensils and a clearly labeled vegan section.
  - Keep cheese/crema in separate bowls away from the vegan area.
- Dessert: fruit platter + a small vegan/GF treat (brownies or cupcakes) so everyone has something sweet.

### Human: Also I was thinking of sparklers at midnight on the rooftop.
### Assistant: That sounds fun, but a couple of flags:
- Many buildings prohibit open flame on rooftops, and local fire codes often restrict sparklers.
- Midnight might violate quiet/rooftop access rules.

If your building allows it and you proceed:
- Use metal buckets with sand for disposal.
- Have a water source or extinguisher nearby.
- Keep them outdoors only and away from decor.

Safer, building-friendly alternatives that still feel celebratory:
- LED “cold sparklers” wands or fiber-optic wands (no flame, great in photos).
- Biodegradable confetti poppers in the courtyard (confirm cleanup rules).
- A “sparkler cake fountain” candle used outdoors at cake time (short, controlled), if permitted.
- Glow-stick countdown and a light-up balloon release (tethered; avoid releasing balloons into the environment).

If you want that wow moment without risk, LED sparkler wands are inexpensive and look great in pictures.
