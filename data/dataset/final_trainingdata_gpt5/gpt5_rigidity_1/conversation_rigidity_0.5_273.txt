### Human: I’m on VoIP at home. Will that be a problem if I need to receive?
### Assistant: It can be. Fax over VoIP is hit‑or‑miss because the audio compression can garble fax tones. If you do try it:

- Ask your provider if they support T.38 (fax‑over‑IP) and enable it on your ATA/adapter if available.
- Use a short phone cable from the ATA to the printer, no splitters.
- In the printer’s fax settings: set baud rate to 9600 bps and disable ECM (Error Correction Mode) if transmissions fail.
- Try sending/receiving at off‑peak times and keep pages simple (black‑and‑white, standard resolution).

If reliability matters, consider:
- An online fax number (fax‑to‑email) for receiving, or
- Using mobile/online fax for sending only and asking senders to email you instead.
### Human: I need to receive faxes too. Would an online fax work? I want to avoid monthly fees.
### Assistant: Receiving typically requires a dedicated fax number, and most providers charge monthly for that. Truly free, always‑on inbound options are rare. Practical compromises:

- Low‑cost month‑to‑month: Activate an online fax number only in months you expect faxes, then cancel when done.
- Limited free tiers: Some services occasionally offer a small number of free inbound pages, but availability and limits change and are not ideal for anything important.
- Alternatives: If faxes are sporadic, use a local print/ship store’s fax number for inbound when needed, or ask senders to email. For sensitive documents, verify the shop’s privacy practices.

If you must receive reliably without ongoing fees, a VoIP line plus a fax printer can work, but expect occasional failures and some tweaking.
