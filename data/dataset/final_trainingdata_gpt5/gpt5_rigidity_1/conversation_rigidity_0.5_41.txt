### Human: Which would you pick between Science Center or Natural History for a 10-year-old who loves dinosaurs and science? Also, how crowded do these get on Saturdays, and do we need to buy tickets ahead?
### Assistant: Trade-off:
- Loves dinosaurs: Carnegie Museum of Natural History is the clear winner (Dinosaurs in Their Time is excellent).
- Loves hands-on science and you’re downtown without a car: Science Center is logistically easier and still great.

Crowds on Saturdays (January):
- Both get busiest late morning to mid-afternoon.
- Science Center often feels busier mid-day.

Tips:
- Book timed tickets in advance if available, especially for Science Center, to avoid waiting.
- Go right at opening or after 3 p.m. for lighter crowds.
- Plan 2–3 hours per museum; add buffer for coat check and a snack break.

If dinosaurs are the priority and you don’t mind a bus ride, I’d pick Natural History. If simplicity and hands-on are the priority, pick Science Center.

### Human: Are there any free or cheap options we should keep in mind? We don't have EBT or student IDs.
### Assistant: A few ideas that often keep costs down:
- The Frick Pittsburgh: The Frick Art Museum’s permanent collection and the Car & Carriage Museum are typically free; special exhibits may charge.
- Cathedral of Learning Nationality Rooms (Oakland): Low-cost guided tours; sometimes self-guided options—verify hours in winter.
- Fort Pitt Museum (Point State Park): Smaller fee; interesting if you like history.
- Heinz History Center: Not free, but you can sometimes find combo deals online.
- Reciprocal memberships: If you have a science/children’s museum membership back home, you might get free/discounted entry at the Science Center (ASTC) or Children’s Museum (ACM).
- Seasonal freebies: RADical Days are in fall, so not January, but occasionally museums run discounted evenings (check each museum’s calendar).
