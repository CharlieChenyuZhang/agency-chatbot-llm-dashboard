Note on WAV files: WAV tagging is poorly standardized. If you keep WAV, some tags may not stick across apps. Converting WAV to FLAC preserves audio quality, reduces size, and supports robust tagging. Not required, but beneficial if you care about consistent tags.

### Human: I do have some WAVs. Should I convert them?
### Assistant: Pros of converting WAV -> FLAC:
- Lossless (no quality loss) and ~30–50% smaller.
- Tags and cover art embed reliably.
- Easier backups and portability.

When to keep WAV:
- If those files are used in a DAW/project that expects WAV.
- If they’re rare cases where FLAC isn’t supported by a device you use (uncommon).

If you convert:
- In foobar2000: Add WAVs -> Right-click -> Convert -> Quick convert -> FLAC (level 5–8).
- Copy replaygain tags post-conversion if desired.
- Keep originals in a “_Original_WAVs” folder until you’ve verified everything plays fine.
