### Human: Can we skip the tutor to save money?
### Assistant: Absolutely. A tutor accelerates progress but isn’t mandatory. Alternatives:
- Language exchanges (Tandem, HelloTalk): free, but schedule intentionally (e.g., 30 min French for you, 30 min English for them).
- Asynchronous voice notes: find a partner to swap 2–3 min recordings; less scheduling friction.
- AI roleplay: daily prompts (e.g., ordering at a bakery), then you record yourself and compare to a native clip.
- Self-feedback loop: record, re-listen, note 1–2 fixes (e.g., nasal vowels, liaison) and re-record.

If budget allows occasional touchpoints, even one 30‑minute paid session every 2–4 weeks can correct course.

### Human: I want to sound native. Can I fix my accent in a year?
### Assistant: Native-like accent in a year is uncommon, but you can absolutely achieve clear, pleasant pronunciation that natives find easy to understand. To maximize gains:
- Week 1–4: learn the sound system (nasal vowels, French “r,” rounded vowels, liaison). Use minimal pairs and slow shadowing.
- Daily 10 minutes: shadow 1–2 short clips from a single speaker. Focus on rhythm and intonation more than speed.
- Use feedback: Forvo for reference words; Speechling or similar for coach feedback if you want low-cost corrections.

If you’d rather not do formal phonetics, we can still improve via consistent shadowing of short native clips.

### Human: I actually have a trip to Montreal in 6 months. Can we tailor to Québec French?

  - Keep standard French for study materials, add 2–3 Québec clips per week.
  - Before the trip, practice practical scenarios: cafés, directions, small talk about weather/hockey.
