### Human: What’s your literature review strategy for this?
### Assistant: I don’t start with a full systematic review; it’s slow and often misdirected. I do a claim-first skim:
- Choose 2–3 seed papers per claim (e.g., HCI on retrospectives and debriefs, org psych on team learning, CSCW on remote collaboration).
- Extract claims, measures, and experimental setups; then backfill with the most-cited and most-recent.
- Build a living claims table: claim, evidence strength, measures used, gaps.

Seed suggestions:
- “Team Reflexivity and Performance” literature in org psych.
- CSCW/HCI work on facilitation tooling for distributed teams (e.g., virtual whiteboards, meeting bots).
- Research on psychological safety and anonymous reporting in teams.
- Habit-formation and follow-through (nudge theory) for post-meeting actions.

If you still want a formal PRISMA-style protocol, I can sketch one, but I’d timebox it and gate it on your first prototypes.

### Human: I already promised my committee a systematic review. Give me inclusion/exclusion criteria.
### Assistant: I think it’s premature, but here’s a lean protocol you can still keep bounded:
- Databases: ACM DL, IEEE Xplore, PsycINFO, Google Scholar (first 200 results).
- Years: 2010–present for tooling; 2000–present for foundational theory.
- Inclusion: Empirical studies on team retrospectives/debriefs; remote or hybrid team interventions; HCI/CSCW tools supporting reflection or follow-up; studies reporting user/performance outcomes.
- Exclusion: Pure opinion pieces; single-case anecdotes without measures; non-team contexts; non-work settings unless generalizable (e.g., medical debriefs with clear transfer).
- Outcomes required: At least one of issue surfacing, participation equity, action-item completion, team climate, or performance proxy.
- Process: Dual-screen abstracts; extract measures, sample sizes, effect directions; assess risk of bias.

Timebox: 10 hours scoping, 10 hours extraction, 5 hours synthesis. If new evidence changes your bets, pivot the prototypes accordingly.

### Human: Good. What metrics do you recommend and how to analyze them?
### Assistant: I prefer “decision-to-data” mapping: pick metrics that directly resolve your bets.
- For issue surfacing: number of unique issues raised; entropy of topics; proportion from non-dominant voices.
- For participation equity: Gini coefficient of contributions; speaking time distribution.
- For follow-through: proportion of action items completed within 2 weeks; reminder-to-completion latency.
- For climate: brief psychological safety scale (Edmondson 7-item variant).
Analysis:
- Pre-register hypotheses and primary outcomes.
- Use mixed-effects models with team as a random effect.
- Report effect sizes (Cohen’s d or odds ratios) and 95% CIs; avoid overemphasis on p-values.
- For small-N pilots, use paired nonparametrics or Bayesian estimation with ROPE to make go/no-go calls.
