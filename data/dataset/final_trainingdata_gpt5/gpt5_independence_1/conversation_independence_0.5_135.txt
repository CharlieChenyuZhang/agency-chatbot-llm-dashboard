### Human: I’m in Austin, Texas. Sole prop is fine for now. What about licensing and labels in Texas?
### Assistant: In Texas:
- Cottage Food Law: Cookies are allowed. No kitchen inspection or permit is typically required for cottage foods, but a valid food handler card is required.
- Sales: Direct-to-consumer sales are allowed, including online ordering with in-person delivery/pickup. Shipping can be restricted; within-Texas delivery is typically allowed, but confirm current rules with DSHS or your local health authority.
- Labels must generally include:
  - Product name
  - Your business name and address
  - Net weight (oz and grams)
  - Ingredient list in descending order by weight
  - Allergen statement (e.g., contains: wheat, eggs, milk, tree nuts)
  - Required cottage food statement (wording similar to: “This food is made in a home kitchen and is not inspected by the Department of State Health Services or a local health department.”). Please confirm exact wording on the Texas DSHS site.

Next steps I suggest (confirm before I detail them):
- File a DBA for “Doorbell Cookies” with Travis County (so you can open a bank account)
- Get an EIN from IRS.gov (free)
- Complete a Texas Food Handler course (online, ~$10–$15)
- Set up simple labels that meet the Texas requirements
- Open a separate checking account
